# Glossary

## A
- **Absorption**: process where overhead selling (long liquidation, profit-taking, new shorts) is overcome by demand. At lows = "bag-holding."
- **Apex**: converging trend lines narrowing to a point of equilibrium between supply/demand.
- **Axis line**: a horizontal line that alternates between support and resistance.

## B
- **Bag-holding**: large operators absorbing all selling at a low; price hugs the low before reversing up.
- **Breakout test**: pullback after a breakout that holds above the breakout level on low volume.

## C
- **Cluster**: multiple closes within a narrow price range, indicating equilibrium or absorption.
- **Composite operator**: Wyckoff's term for insiders/pools manipulating markets.
- **Confluence**: multiple line types (support, resistance, trend) meeting at one price area.
- **Contraction/Expansion**: Crabel's principle that narrow ranges precede wide ranges.
- **Crisscross area**: a price level where earlier high-volume struggle occurred, offering future support/resistance.

## D
- **Demand line**: trend line drawn across rising lows in an uptrend.

## E
- **Effort vs. Reward (E/R)**: compare volume (effort) to price progress (reward). Big effort + no reward = reversal warning.

## F
- **Finding-out expense**: holding a position through uncertainty to determine the outcome.
- **Fist**: tight, narrow-range absorption within a few price bars.

## H
- **Hinge (Wyckoff)**: a narrow range (like an NR day) from which a larger swing emerges.
- **Hypodermics (Wyckoff)**: vertical, panic price moves that mark blow-off tops.

## I
- **Ice line**: support line at the bottom of a trading range (Bob Evans metaphor).
- **Inside day**: day whose range is entirely within the previous day's range.

## K
- **Key reversal**: outside day that closes above the previous high (bullish) or below previous low (bearish).

## M
- **Moment of recognition**: the instant you sense a move is about to happen—action is warranted.

## N
- **NR4**: day with range narrower than each of the previous 3 days.
- **NR7**: day with range narrower than each of the previous 6 days.

## P
- **Preparation**: congestion width on P&F charts that determines price projection magnitude.
- **Prime mover**: initial burst of volume that provides impetus for a larger swing.

## R
- **Reverse trend line**: trend line drawn across rising highs (in uptrend) or falling lows (in downtrend) for steep trends.
- **Rising wedge**: converging normal uptrend line + reverse trend line; often indicates tiring rally.

## S
- **Secondary test**: retest of a spring or upthrust low/high with narrowing range and diminishing volume.
- **Shortening of Thrust (SOT)**: diminished progress measured from high to high or low to low; minimum 3 impulses needed.
- **Spring**: failed breakdown below support that reverses upward.
- **Springboard**: price tightening after a spring that precedes a large directional move.
- **Supply line**: trend line drawn across declining highs in a downtrend.

## T
- **Terminal shakeout**: final washout after prolonged trading range that resolves years of congestion.
- **Three Push Method**: three successive impulses with divergence; related to SOT.
- **Tick difference**: net up-volume minus net down-volume per time period.
- **Touch points**: places within a trend where rallies/sell-offs halt against a trend line.
- **True range**: distance from previous close to current high (in uptrend) or low (in downtrend).

## U
- **Upthrust**: failed breakout above resistance that reverses downward.

## W
- **Weis Wave**: wave chart with length (price distance), volume (cumulative contracts), and duration (time).
