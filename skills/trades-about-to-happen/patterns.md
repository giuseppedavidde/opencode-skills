# Patterns & Trading Setups

## 1. The Spring (Failed Breakdown)

**When to use**: Consolidation at support, price breaks below, no follow-through.
**How**: Buy on reversal back into the range. Place stop below spring low.
**Confirmation**: Look for narrow-range secondary test with low volume.
**Trade-off**: Highest win rate in uptrends; in downtrends, springs fail often (bottom picker's nightmare).
**Variants**: High-volume spring → secondary test expected. Low-volume spring → may not test.
**Exit**: Scale out at prior resistance; hold through if P&F projection shows room.

## 2. The Upthrust (Failed Breakout)

**When to use**: Resistance tested, price pushes above, fails to follow through.
**How**: Short on reversal below resistance. Place stop above upthrust high.
**Confirmation**: Narrow-range breakout + succeeding bars show heavy supply.
**Trade-off**: Harder than springs—public loves buying breakouts. Works best in downtrends.
**Variants**: Single-bar upthrust (intraday); multiple upthrusts in a top formation.
**Exit**: Cover at prior support; trail if SOT develops on the down-move.

## 3. Absorption at Resistance (Breakout Setup)

**When to use**: Price presses against resistance; each pullback holds higher.
**How**: Buy when price closes above resistance after absorption clues.
**Confirmation**: Rising supports, volume increasing near resistance, failed down-threats.
**Trade-off**: Requires patience—absorption can take many bars. Risk: upthrust instead.
**Exit**: Measured move from congestion width; trail with wave SOT.

## 4. Absorption at Support (Breakdown Setup)

**When to use**: Price hugs low of range; failed springs keep reversing lower.
**How**: Short when support breaks or when final spring fails to gain traction.
**Confirmation**: Springs that fail one after another; volume building but no lift.
**Trade-off**: The market is trapping buyers who bet on each spring. Emotional setup.
**Exit**: Cover at next major support; watch for climactic volume.

## 5. Secondary Test of Spring

**When to use**: After a spring, price returns to test the area.
**How**: Buy on narrow-range, low-volume test. Stop below the spring low.
**Confirmation**: Range narrows, volume shrinks, close near or above the low.
**Trade-off**: The safest spring entry. May not occur if spring was low-volume.
**Exit**: Initial resistance or P&F projection. Trail with SOT.

## 6. Shortening of Thrust (SOT) Reversal

**When to use**: After 3-4 impulse waves in one direction with diminishing progress.
**How**: Enter on reversal of the final impulse. Stop beyond the extreme.
**Confirmation**: Declining volume on final wave OR heavy volume with no progress.
**Trade-off**: Most common intraday setup. Works on all timeframes.
**Exit**: Opposite SOT develops. Partial at next support/resistance.

## 7. Pullback to Test Breakout (High-Volume Vertical)

**When to use**: A high-volume breakout occurs; price pulls back into the vertical area.
**How**: Buy on low-volume pullback that holds above the breakout level. Stop below base.
**Confirmation**: Volume dries up on the pullback; closes hold near mid-to-high.
**Trade-off**: Combines trend-following with low-risk entry. Weis's favorite overall setup.
**Exit**: Trail with SOT; use P&F projection for target.

## 8. The Lift-Off Spring (Gap Reversal)

**When to use**: Spring occurs with a gap higher the next day (e.g., Cocoa, Deere).
**How**: Buy immediately on gap above previous high. Stop below the gap.
**Confirmation**: Heavy volume on gap day; low volume preceding the break.
**Trade-off**: Rare but powerful. Aggressive entry—no secondary test to confirm.
**Exit**: Quick pop or sustained trend; exit on first narrowing of range at resistance.

## 9. Apex / Coiling Breakout

**When to use**: Price tightens into a narrowing range between converging trend lines.
**How**: Buy/sell when price closes outside the apex with volume confirmation.
**Confirmation**: Tightest range at the point; volume expansion on breakout bar.
**Trade-off**: High reward but ambiguous direction. Earlier clues (trend, volume) favor one side.
**Exit**: Projected from apex width. Trail with wave SOT.

## 10. Weis Wave Compression Setup

**When to use**: Flat, low-angle waves with heavy volume and time (no progress).
**How**: Enter in direction of the next wave that breaks the compression.
**Confirmation**: Several waves going nowhere with cumulative volume building.
**Trade-off**: Indicates absorption or distribution. Use trend context to pick direction.
**Exit**: Opposite compression or SOT develops.
