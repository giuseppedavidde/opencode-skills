# Chapter 4: The Logic of Reading Bar Charts

## Core Idea
Sequential bar-by-bar reading: compare each bar to its predecessor using range, close position, and volume to deduce what comes next.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Ten two-bar sequences**: systematic logic for reading any two consecutive bars
- **Effort vs. Reward (E/R)**: volume = effort, price progress = reward
- **True range**: encompasses gaps; serves as volume proxy when no volume data exists
- **Crabel's patterns**: 2Bar NR, 3Bar NR, NR4, ID/NR4, Principle of Contraction/Expansion
- **Hinge (Wyckoff)**: narrow range that precedes a larger swing
- **Key reversal**: outside reversal that closes above previous high / below previous low

## Key Concepts
- Uptrends perpetuate through repeated failure of threatening bars to produce follow-through
- Narrow range at new highs is bearish (upthrust warning)
- Low volume = exhaustion or lack of interest depending on context
- Volume is best interpreted alongside price range and close position
- True range histogram can replace volume for markets without volume data

## Key Takeaways
Context is everything. The same two-bar pattern means different things depending on position within the trading range, trend, and volume characteristics. Practice bar-by-bar reading until it becomes automatic.
