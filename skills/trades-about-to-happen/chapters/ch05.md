# Chapter 5: Springs

## Core Idea
A spring is a failed breakdown below support that reverses upward. It is Weis's highest-conviction pattern. "You can make a living trading springs and upthrusts."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Spring anatomy**: washout of support → no follow-through → upward reversal
- **Secondary test (retest)**: ideal entry after a spring; narrowing range + diminishing volume
- **Terminal shakeout**: final spring after prolonged trading range that resolves years of congestion
- **Springboard**: price tightening after a spring that precedes a large up-move
- **Failed spring (bottom picker's nightmare)**: springs in downtrends that reverse lower
- **Shortening of the thrust (SOT)**: diminished downward progress from high to high or low to low

## Key Concepts
- Springs in uptrends have highest success rate
- Springs in downtrends are dangerous—they often fail and trap buyers
- Preparation (P&F congestion width) determines spring's profit potential
- High-volume breakdown + low-volume secondary test = ideal setup
- Low-volume breakdown with no secondary test can still produce strong springs

## Key Takeaways
The spring is the foundation of low-risk entries. The trade enters at the danger point—right where a breakdown would prove you wrong. Minimal risk, quick determination of outcome.
