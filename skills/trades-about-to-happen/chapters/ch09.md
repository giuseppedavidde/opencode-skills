# Chapter 9: Tape Reading Part I

## Core Idea
Wyckoff's volume-figure chart adapted into the Weis Wave. Price moves in waves, not equal time periods. Volume tells the true story; time-based volume hides it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Wyckoff's volume-figure chart**: numbers representing shares traded at each price; 1:1 P&F with volume
- **The Weis Wave**: wave chart with length (price distance), volume (cumulative contracts), and duration (time)
- **One-minute tape construction**: recording closing price and volume each minute to build wave data
- **Wave comparison**: comparing successive up-waves and down-waves by length, volume, and duration
- **Composite operator inference**: inferring institutional activity from wave volume patterns

## Key Concepts
- A wave ends when price reverses by the minimum reversal amount
- When buying waves diminish in length/volume/duration and selling waves increase = trend reversal
- "Tape reading is rapid-fire horse sense" (Wyckoff)
- Time-based volume obscures true force; wave-based volume reveals it
- The wave chart with 1/32 scale on bonds produces 400 data points per day

## Key Takeaways
The process of manual tape reconstruction taught Weis more than 42 years of screen time. Wave volume reveals what bar volume hides. The behavior Wyckoff observed in 1909 persists unchanged.
