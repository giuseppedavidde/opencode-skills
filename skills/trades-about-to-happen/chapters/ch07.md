# Chapter 7: Absorption

## Core Idea
The process through which overhead supply (long liquidation, profit-taking, new shorts) is overcome by persistent demand. Absorption is the hardest Wyckoff concept to recognize.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Absorption at highs**: rising supports, volume increases near resistance, prices press against the line
- **Absorption at lows (bag-holding)**: persistent buying absorbs all selling; price hugs the low
- **Upthrust vs. absorption**: same price action but different outcomes—context determines which
- **Greek phalanx / wall of worry**: prices levitate higher, locking out would-be buyers
- **The fist**: tight, narrow-range absorption within a few price bars

## Key Concepts
- Distinguishing absorption from a top/bottom causes confusion
- Failed threats = best indication of absorption
- "Pressing against the resistance line" before breakout
- Failed springs at lows = sellers absorbing buying = breakdown coming
- Absorption on monthly charts can persist for years

## Key Takeaways
Absorption cannot be reduced to rules. It requires reading subtle cumulative behavior. The failure of threatening action to produce follow-through is the most reliable clue.
