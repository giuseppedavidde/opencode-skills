# Chapter 6: Upthrusts

## Core Idea
A failed breakout above resistance that reverses downward. Harder to trade than springs because public buying masks professional distribution.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Upthrust anatomy**: penetration of resistance → no follow-through → downward reversal
- **Narrow-range breakout warning**: low-energy breakouts often fail
- **Secondary test of upthrust**: subsequent rally that fails at same level
- **Multiple upthrusts in a top**: several false breakouts before markdown begins
- **Intraday/high-timeframe distinction**: upthrusts on weekly/monthly charts = larger moves

## Key Concepts
- Trend is paramount: upthrusts in uptrends rarely work; in downtrends they flourish
- Heavy volume on the breakout bar can be misleading if close weak (E/R analysis)
- Upthrusts require evaluation of preceding AND succeeding bars
- An upthrust is "ending action but not necessarily terminal action"
- Intraday upthrusts on 5-minute charts offer daily trading edge

## Key Takeaways
Upthrusts are more nuanced than springs. The S&P is "notorious for its springs and upthrusts." Always consider the larger trend first. Multiple upthrusts can occur before a real breakdown.
