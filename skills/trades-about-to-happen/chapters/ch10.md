# Chapter 10: Tape Reading Part II

## Core Idea
The modern Weis Wave applied to tick bar charts, combining wave length, volume, and duration in a visual format. The automated wave chart makes the tape reader's edge accessible.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Tick bar wave chart**: waves built from tick bar closes (e.g., 250-tick, 500-tick, 1000-tick)
- **Time-scaled wave chart**: wave duration reflected in chart spacing (not equal spacing)
- **Shortening of Thrust (SOT) guidelines**: after 3-4 waves, look for diminished progress + declining volume
- **Compression (flat waves)**: heavy volume + flat angle of decline = absorption or "bag-holding"
- **Dual wave lines**: small wave + large wave superimposed to show micro and macro structure
- **Moment of recognition**: the instant you sense a move is about to happen

## Key Concepts
- Three-wave impulse with SOT + declining volume = trend exhaustion
- Four+ waves with persistent SOT = trend too strong to trade against
- Two waves only = potential spring/upthrust
- SOT measured from price bar highs/lows, not wave turning points
- Wave volume tells the story of strength/weakness of demand and supply
- "When you are striving, struggling, forcing a trade you are out of sync" (Mr. Anonymous)

## Key Takeaways
Wave analysis filters intraday noise and makes holding positions easier. The best trades require no effort—they reveal themselves. Patience + lack of bias = edge.
