# Chapter 3: Will the Real Put/Call Ratio Please Stand Up?

## Core Idea
Multiple put/call ratio formulations exist. The equity-only ratio (purged of index options) best captures the wrong-thinking crowd. Index options are polluted by professional hedging, making the total CBOE ratio less reliable.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Ratio Categories**: CBOE equity-only, CBOE total (equity + index), OEX index-only. Each has different predictive power.
- **Dynamic Thresholds**: Static levels (e.g., 40/65) fail after 1987 crash. McMillan advocates local maxima/minima on charts instead.
- **Detrended Oscillator**: EMA10-250 differencing removes long-term trend, creates stable threshold ranges.

## Key Concepts
- **Dollar-Weighted Put/Call Ratio**: Weights contracts by premium value, not volume. Problematic because cheap OTM options (bought by unsophisticated) get underweighted vs expensive ITM options (hedgers).
- **Hines Ratio**: Incorporates open interest — daily volume relative to open interest for puts and calls separately.
- **ISE Data**: All-electronic exchange dominated by retail; theoretically purer but lacks history for backtesting.
- **Intraday Put/Call Ratios**: 30-minute updates; three consecutive readings above 1.0 near support often precede strong rallies.

## Anti-patterns
- Relying on the OEX index ratio as a contrarian gauge — it has become a smart money indicator.
- Using the total CBOE ratio without separating index options — portfolio hedging distorts the signal.
- Dollar weighting without isolating OTM options — hedgers' ITM trades swamp the sentiment signal.

## Key Takeaways
- Equity-only put/call ratio is the best single measure of speculative crowd sentiment.
- Dynamic threshold methods outperform static levels.
- Intraday ratios above 1.0 near support levels have day-trading value.
