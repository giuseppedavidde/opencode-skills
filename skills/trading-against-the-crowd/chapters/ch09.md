# Chapter 9: Sentiment Squeeze Play II

## Core Idea
Individual stock put/call ratios can power a contrarian system, but the higher noise requires a slower oscillator (EMA50-100) and wider threshold (+5%/-5%). Squeeze Play II applies the sentiment + price trigger framework to single stocks.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **EMA50-100 Squeeze Play II**: Entry when 10-day high of EMA50-100 > +5% (long) or 10-day low < -5% (short). Price trigger: close > 3-day high or < 3-day low.
- **Optimization Across Thresholds**: Testing +2 through +10 / -2 through -10 to verify robustness. All combinations profitable on Merck.
- **Out-of-Sample Testing**: Apply same parameters to IBM, C, MRK, WMT, GE, MSFT without re-optimization.

## Key Concepts
- **Merck Case Study**: Longs-only: +$5,735 (57.36%). Long/short optimized: +$82,602 (826%). Annualized: 140%.
- **Out-of-Sample Group Results**: Aggregate $157,157 profit from $60,000 (47.6% annualized).
- **Non-Optimized Degradation**: Without optimization, group total $71,786 vs $94,874 buy/hold — still profitable but not beating BH.
- **Liquidity Matters**: Only large-cap stocks with liquid options markets qualify — thin markets don't capture true crowd sentiment.

## Anti-patterns
- Using fast oscillators (EMA5-21) on individual stocks — noise overwhelms the signal.
- Expecting every stock to perform equally — stock selection creates significant dispersion.
- Trading stocks without liquid options markets — insufficient volume to represent the crowd.

## Key Takeaways
- Squeeze Play II works on individual stocks but requires slower oscillators.
- Out-of-sample tests show promise but need a larger portfolio for true diversification.
- Non-optimized results beat buy/hold inconsistently — parameter stability across stocks is the challenge.
