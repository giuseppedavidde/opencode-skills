# Chapter 13: Option Implied Volatility and Investor Sentiment

## Core Idea
Option prices, not just volume, reveal crowd psychology. Implied volatility measures how much traders are willing to pay for options — expensive options = high fear (contrarian buy signal), cheap options = complacency (contrarian sell signal).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **VIX/VXO as Fear Gauge**: CBOE's implied volatility indices for S&P 100 (VXO) and S&P 500 (VIX). Inverse relationship with stock prices: high VIX = market bottom, low VIX = market top.
- **Historical vs Implied Volatility**: Historical = actual price speed (annualized std dev). Implied = market's expectation embedded in option prices. The gap between them = sentiment.
- **Butterfly Pattern Confirmed**: VXO and VXN charts exhibit the same inverse butterfly relationship as put/call ratios.

## Key Concepts
- **Black-Scholes Inputs**: Historical volatility is the key variable. Option pricing models work backwards from price to derive implied volatility.
- **VIX. Characteristics**: High VIX = panic/ fear (usually at bottoms). Low VIX = complacency (usually at tops). Not directional but psychological.
- **Contrarian Volatility Signal**: When the crowd pays up for puts (driving IV high), they are fearful — time to be bullish. When options are cheap, the crowd is complacent — time to be cautious.

## Anti-patterns
- Confusing high volatility with high prices — volatility measures speed, not direction.
- Ignoring that VIX works differently for commodities vs equities — commodity futures options don't have the same inverse equity pattern.

## Key Takeaways
- Implied volatility is a pure price-based sentiment gauge with no volume ambiguity.
- VIX/VXO are free, high-quality data with long histories for backtesting.
- High fear (expensive options) = buy signal; complacency (cheap options) = sell signal.
