# Chapter 15: Stock Options Volatility and Sentiment Long Waves

## Core Idea
Individual stock implied volatility can power Squeeze Play II but underperforms put/call ratios. The grand average IV of all options on a stock is noisier than the VIX methodology.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Grand Average Implied Volatility**: Simple average of implied volatility across all options on a single stock, used as sentiment wave input.
- **Sentiment Screen vs No Screen**: Tests demonstrate that without the sentiment oscillator filter, the price trigger alone produces losses. The sentiment screen transforms results.

## Key Concepts
- **No-Sentiment Test Fails**: IBM long-only without sentiment: -$2,085. With sentiment: +$35,957 (61% annualized vs 24% BH).
- **Group Performance**: $223,476 in LEAPS long trades across all time frames. But total $228,654 vs $290,377 BH — underperforms.
- **Put/Call Ratios Superior**: $561,258 vs $228,654 for implied volatility systems. Volume-based sentiment has stronger predictive power for individual stocks.
- **Short Side Struggles**: Only T+30 short trades were profitable with IV. All longer short time frames lost money.

## Anti-patterns
- Using the same grand average IV methodology as VIX — it's coarser and noisier.
- Expecting individual stock IV to match equity-index IV performance — portfolio diversification matters.
- Shorting on IV signals — the bullish bias makes it unreliable.

## Key Takeaways
- Implied volatility works for stock timing but put/call ratios are stronger for the Squeeze Play framework.
- The sentiment screen is essential — without it, price triggers alone lose money.
- Long-only LEAPS is the recommended application of IV-based systems for stocks.
