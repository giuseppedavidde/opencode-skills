# Chapter 8: Squeeze Play I: Pulling the Price Trigger

## Core Idea
Adding a simple price trigger (close > prior day high for longs; close < prior day low for shorts) transforms mediocore sentiment-only results into a robust system. Squeeze Play I with triggers generated 763 S&P 500 points vs 407 buy/hold.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **KISS Philosophy**: Keep It Simple, Stupid — the price trigger is a one-day breakout, not an optimized multi-day window.
- **Price Trigger Logic**: A "squeeze" occurs when price action catches the majority by surprise, confirming the sentiment reversal.
- **Long/Short Synergy**: Combined longs and shorts (reverse signals acting as stops) outperforms either side alone.

## Key Concepts
- **Dramatic Improvement**: Sentiment-only: 312.7 points. With price trigger: 763.7 points (longs/shorts). Win/loss ratio: 1.54.
- **Proprietary Filter Adds Edge**: Additional sentiment screen boosts profit to 1141.4 points with 35 wins vs 7 losses.
- **Cross-Market Robustness**: 951% S&P 500, 900% DJIA, 1222% NASDAQ 100 (7-year returns using futures).
- **OEX Also Works**: +423.54 points vs 208.65 buy/hold.

## Anti-patterns
- Using intraday breakouts instead of closing price confirmation — closes filter false noise.
- Optimizing the trigger window to a specific best-performing day count — simplicity (1 day) is more robust.

## Key Takeaways
- Price triggers are essential for timing sentiment-based entries.
- The basic breakout trigger (close > prev high / close < prev low) is remarkably robust.
- Long/short synergy is significant — reversals act as natural stops.
