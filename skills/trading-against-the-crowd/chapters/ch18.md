# Chapter 18: Trading Against the Advisory Opinion Crowd

## Core Idea
Investment newsletter writers and market advisors — the "experts" — are also prone to crowd psychology. When they become uniformly bullish or bearish, it's time to trade against them. The bearish opinion data has more predictive power than bullish.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Opinion Data Sources**: Investors Intelligence (% bulls/bears), Market Vane Bullish Consensus (0-100 index), AAII Survey (individual investor sentiment).
- **EMA1-4W Squeeze Play II**: Weekly data (not daily). EMA1-4W oscillator with +5%/-5% thresholds. Price trigger: weekly close > prev week high / < prev week low.
- **Bull/Bear Ratio Testing**: Creating a ratio of bulls/bears and testing as alternative input.

## Key Concepts
- **Investors Intelligence**: 489 S&P points vs 383 buy/hold. 1.71 win/loss ratio. Beats BH but modest.
- **Market Vane Bullish Consensus**: 662 points vs 383 BH. 79% annualized on S&P futures. Rules reversed (low consensus = buy, high = sell).
- **AAII Underperforms**: 251 points — did not beat buy/hold of 383.
- **Bear Opinion More Predictive**: Across all sources, bearish opinion levels better predict bottoms than bullish opinion predicts tops.
- **Opinion vs Action Gap**: Surveys measure what people say, not what they do (unlike put/call ratios or short sales which measure actual activity).

## Anti-patterns
- Expecting advisory opinion to match the predictive power of actual transaction data (put/call ratios, short sales) — words vs deeds.
- Ignoring that AAII reflects individual investors (who already show up in options data), not true new information.
- Using advisors as a short-side indicator without adjustment for bullish bias.

## Key Takeaways
- Advisory opinion works but is weaker than volume-based sentiment indicators.
- Market Vane Bullish Consensus (reversed) is the strongest of the three sources.
- Bearish opinion data has more signal than bullish opinion data.
