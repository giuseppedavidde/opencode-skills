# Chapter 12: Adapting Put/Call Ratios to Bond Futures

## Core Idea
The same crowd psychology measured by equity put/call ratios works in bond futures markets. A normalized dollar-weighted put volume ratio replaces the contract-volume ratio for smoother signals.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Normalized Dollar-Weighted Put Volume**: Ratio of long-bond futures put dollar volume to total options dollar volume. Dollar weighting reduces noise in futures markets.
- **Squeeze Play I on Bond Futures**: Same EMA5-21 (entry) / EMA21-50 (exit) framework as equities. Price trigger: close > prev high / close < prev low.

## Key Concepts
- **No Trigger Results**: 24 points profit ($24,000), 480% return on $5,000 account (96% annualized). 71% winners.
- **With Price Trigger**: 27.47 points ($27,470), 549% return (109% annualized). Win/loss ratio improves to 1.65.
- **Short Trades Excel**: 90% accuracy on short trades (9 of 10 winners) — bond futures sentiment is more symmetric than equities.
- **Contract Volume Also Works**: Dollar weighting is preferred but contract-volume put/call ratios also produce good results.

## Key Concepts
- **Futures Specs**: US Treasury bond futures valued at $1,000 per full point. $31.25 per tick. Margin requires ~$2,500-$5,000.
- **Bond Sentiment Waves**: EMA21-50 plot of normalized put volume shows clear butterfly pattern mirroring bond price action.

## Anti-patterns
- Applying equity put/call ratio methodology without dollar weighting — futures options require different data treatment.
- Expecting bond sentiment to mirror equity sentiment — each market has independent crowd dynamics.

## Key Takeaways
- Bond futures are excellent candidates for Squeeze Play I sentiment trading.
- Dollar-weighted normalized put volume outperforms contract-volume ratios in futures.
- Bond market short-side signals are unusually reliable vs equities.
