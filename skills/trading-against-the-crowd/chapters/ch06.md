# Chapter 6: OEX Options Traders — Is This a Smart Money Crowd?

## Core Idea
OEX (S&P 100) index options traders are NOT a good contrarian crowd. They get it right more often than wrong at extremes. Trade WITH this crowd, not against it. The OEX put/call ratio is a smart money indicator.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Smart Money vs Wrong Crowd**: Same EMA10-250 testing methodology, opposite interpretation. When OEX traders are extremely bearish, prices tend to fall; when extremely bullish, prices rise.
- **Oscillator Divergence**: OEX and equity-only ratios often move in opposite directions at emotional extremes, providing confirming/conflicting signals.

## Key Concepts
- **OEX Bearish Bets = Correct**: Expected price change of -0.4% after extreme bearishness (the market declines, which their puts profit from).
- **OEX Bullish Bets = Correct**: 1.47% average price rise after extreme bullishness vs random 0.58%.
- **Probability of Price Rise After OEX Bearishness**: Only 39.29% (vs 77.55% for equity-only) — meaning prices fall 60.71% of the time.
- **Why OEX Is Smarter**: Migration of unsophisticated traders to QQQ/ETF options left OEX to seasoned professionals.

## Anti-patterns
- Using OEX put/call ratio as a contrarian sell signal — trade with OEX bulls, not against them.
- Ignoring the divergence between equity-only and OEX ratios — this divergence itself is information about market health.

## Key Takeaways
- OEX put/call ratio is a smart money indicator. Use it to confirm trends, not fade them.
- When equity-only says "extreme bullish, time to sell" but OEX is neutral, the sell signal is weaker.
- OEX traders' success rate (60%+ correct at extremes) makes them a poor contrarian target.
