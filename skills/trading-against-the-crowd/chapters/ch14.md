# Chapter 14: Testing Option Volatility on Equity Indices

## Core Idea
Implied volatility (VIX/VXO) can replace put/call ratios in Squeeze Play I and II. Results are good but not as strong as volume-based ratios for equity indices. VIX marginally outperforms VXO.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Squeeze Play I with VIX/VXO**: Same EMA5-21/EMA21-50 rules using VIX data (S&P 500 implied volatility). Results: 514.6 points (VXO), 591.1 (VIX) — below buy/hold of 680.6 but with excellent statistics.
- **Squeeze Play II with VIX (EMA21-50)**: Tsunami-style rules with +5%/-5% thresholds and T+30 exits. 848.9 points vs 406.9 buy/hold — more than doubles BH.
- **EMA1-10 Fast System**: Simple 5% threshold penetration system. 488 points (OEX-VXO) and 859.9 (S&P-VIX). 95% annualized return on S&P futures.

## Key Concepts
- **VIX Edge Over VXO**: S&P 500 volatility index (VIX) correlates more closely with S&P 500 than S&P 100 volatility (VXO).
- **Fewer but Better Trades**: VIX Squeeze Play I had only 6-8 trades in 9 years but 2.91 win/loss ratio.
- **Trigger Optimization**: Best results with 1-day high breakout for longs and 8-day low breakout for shorts — all parameter combos beat buy/hold.
- **Reward/Risk Scores**: 96-99 across all configurations — exceptional risk-adjusted performance.

## Key Concepts
- **Comparison with Put/Call Ratios**: Implied volatility systems produce 500-850 points vs 700-1100 for volume-based — volume has stronger predictive power.

## Anti-patterns
- Using VXN (NASDAQ 100 implied volatility) — shorter history and less liquidity distort the signal.
- Expecting implied volatility to perfectly mirror put/call ratio performance — it's a different dimension of crowd psychology.

## Key Takeaways
- Implied volatility Squeeze Play II with EMA21-50 and T+30 exits is a strong standalone system.
- VIX slightly outperforms VXO.
- Fast EMA1-10 5% system is viable for active traders with 95% annualized returns.
