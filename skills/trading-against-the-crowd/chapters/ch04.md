# Chapter 4: The Options Trading Crowd at Extremes

## Core Idea
Quantitative testing of the equity-only put/call ratio EMA10-250 oscillator confirms that extreme bearishness (high readings) reliably predicts rising prices, while extreme bullishness (low readings) shows mixed results for predicting declines.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **EMA10-250 Oscillator**: Difference between 10-day and 250-day EMA of daily put/call ratio, expressed as percentage deviation. Positive = bearish extreme; negative = bullish extreme.
- **Threshold Penetration Testing**: Six percentage thresholds (10%, 15%, 20%, 30%, 40%, 50%) tested across time frames T+5 through T+240.
- **Probability of Price Rise/Fall**: Measured against historical random average to quantify edge.

## Key Concepts
- **Bearish Buy Thresholds Perform Exceptionally**: At T+10, 86.9% probability of price rise with 4.53% average gain vs 52.3% random.
- **Bullish Sell Thresholds Underperform**: Only T+5 through T+20 at 10-15% thresholds show reliable declines. Longer time frames fail due to bullish bias.
- **Pattern of Decreasing Probability**: Less extreme sentiment levels have less uniformity — crowd agreement is strongest at extremes.
- **Best Time Frame**: T+180 produced 15.03% average rise with 66.7% probability.

## Anti-patterns
- Expecting sell signals from extreme bullishness to work as well as buy signals — the market's bullish bias makes short trades inherently harder.
- Overweighting longer time frames for sell signals — performance deteriorates significantly past T+50.

## Key Takeaways
- Extreme bearishness (high put/call) is a powerful buy signal across all time frames.
- Extreme bullishness works only in very short time frames (T+5 to T+20).
- The options crowd is most reliably wrong at market bottoms, less so at tops.
