# Chapter 5: Does the Entire Group of Options Traders Get It Wrong?

## Core Idea
The CBOE total put/call ratio (including index options) also works as a contrarian indicator but is inferior to the equity-only ratio. Index options traders — mostly professional hedgers — dilute the predictive power.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Comparative Performance Testing**: Equity-only vs CBOE total put/call ratio under identical EMA10-250 oscillator methodology.
- **Expected Price Change Metric**: Probability × magnitude, providing a single comparative score.

## Key Concepts
- **Equity-Only Beats Total**: +8.67% expected price change vs +5.61% for bearish thresholds.
- **Index Options Are Pollution**: Portfolio hedging with index puts distorts the ratio, especially during market declines when hedging surges.
- **Probability Scores Are Close**: 71.65% (equity-only) vs 72.61% (total) for bearish thresholds — but total's magnitude per signal is lower.
- **Bullish Thresholds Fail for Both**: Neither ratio reliably predicts downside in time frames past T+40.

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming more data (total = equity + index) means a better signal — the opposite is true.
- Believing that because index traders are professional, their sentiment is irrelevant — their hedging masks, not eliminates, crowd psychology.

## Key Takeaways
- The CBOE total ratio is useful but not as reliable as equity-only for contrarian signals.
- The presence of "smart" index options traders does not destroy the indicator but degrades it.
- For market tops, neither ratio is reliable for longer time frames.
