# Chapter 17: Public Shorts: Still as Good as Ever as Crowd "Sentimeter"

## Core Idea
The Normalized Public Short Sales Ratio (NPSR) — detrended with 4/8-week EMA differencing — produces exceptional trading results across all major equity indices. Despite a 2-week data delay, the indicator has strong predictive power.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **NPSR Proprietary System**: Uses public short sales / total NYSE volume, processed through EMA4-8 oscillator. Entries triggered at extreme oscillator readings.
- **Four-Market Testing**: Applied to DJIA, S&P 500, NASDAQ 100, and Russell 2000 with consistent excellence.

## Key Concepts
- **DJIA Results**: 15,047 points vs 6,850 buy/hold (120% excess). 3.63 win/loss ratio. Reward/risk = 100.
- **S&P 500 Results**: 1,606 points vs 697 buy/hold. 3.70 win/loss ratio. Stable equity growth.
- **NASDAQ 100**: 4,388 vs 1,163. 2.93 win/loss. Largest drawdown -1,099 — requires stamina.
- **Russell 2000**: 876 vs 290. 4.75 win/loss ratio — the highest of all tests.
- **Oscillator Logic**: High NPSR oscillator = too much public shorting = near-term rally. Low NPSR oscillator = too little shorting (complacency) = near-term decline.

## Anti-patterns
- Being spooked by large open trade drawdowns on NASDAQ — the absolute returns merit intra-trade volatility.
- Dismissing the 2-week data delay as fatally lagging — the signal still beats buy/hold decisively.
- Trying to trade only one market — multi-market application smooths equity swings.

## Key Takeaways
- NPSR is among the most powerful sentiment indicators in the book, surpassing most put/call ratio systems.
- Win/loss ratios of 3-4.75 are exceptional.
- Multi-market application is recommended to smooth drawdown volatility.
