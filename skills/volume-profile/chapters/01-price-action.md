# Chapter 1: Price Action & Institutional Activity

## Core Idea
Price Action reveals institutional activity. Markets are dominated by ~10 financial institutions (80%+ of volume). Retail traders are 3.5% of volume. To profit, track what institutions do via naked charts — no indicators needed.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three Signs of Institutional Activity**: Sideways price action, aggressive initiation, strong rejection
- **Support/Resistance Role Swap**: Broken support becomes resistance and vice versa
- **Open-Drive**: One-sided aggressive move from a price channel or session start
- **Strong vs Weak Highs/Lows**: Aggression at swing points determines significance
- **Failed Auction**: Market imperfection that acts as a magnet

## Key Concepts
- **Price moves by aggression, not supply/demand balance**: Market orders from aggressive participants drive price
- **Sideways Price Action**: Institutions accumulate positions slowly over hours/days — NOT boring, but preparation for a move
- **Aggressive Initiation**: After accumulation, institutions push price hard in one direction
- **Strong Rejection**: Price reverses aggressively — clashes of two sides. The rejection level becomes a defended S/R zone
- **Open-Drive**: The open of the first large candle in a one-sided move becomes strong S/R
- **AB=CD Pattern**: Harmonic wave pattern (distance AB = distance CD) using Fibonacci for entry at D
- **Failed Auction**: Multiple participants at swing extreme — price will return to "complete" the auction
- **Weak Highs/Lows**: Price shoots through them; avoid placing entries near them

### Price Action Strategies (Confirmations)
- **Strategy 1: Support becoming resistance (and vice versa)** — Identify strong S/R, wait for breach, enter on return to the broken level. Works standalone or as confluence.
- **Strategy 2: Open-drive** — After sideways action or session open, mark the open of the first aggressive candle. Enter on pullback. The open candle acts as strong S/R.
- **Strategy 3: AB = CD** — Harmonic wave with Fibonacci. Leg AB = Leg CD. Enter at D when C retraces >50% of AB. Uses market psychology.
- **Strategy 4: Session Open** — Asian/EU/US session opens become S/R levels. More confirmations = better.
- **Strategy 5: Daily Open** — NY close (5:00 PM ET). Strongest session open. Price above daily open = support; below = resistance.
- **Strategy 6: Daily/Weekly Highs & Lows** — Wait for breach of previous high/low → then enter on pullback (support/resistance swap).

### Strong/Weak Highs & Lows
- **Strong High/Low**: Fast aggressive rejection visible on multiple timeframes. Long tails, sharp reversal. Strong S/R zones. Safe to place levels near them.
- **Weak High/Low**: Slow reluctant turn, no aggression, many small candles testing the area. Price will shoot through. AVOID placing levels near weak points.
- **Practical use**: If weak low exists below your long level — skip the trade. If strong high near your long — take profit before it.

### Failed Auction Application
- A failed auction (multiple participants at swing extreme with no single winner) acts as a magnetic price target.
- If a failed auction exists below a long entry → price will test lower first (bad trade).
- If a failed auction exists above a short entry → price will test higher first (bad trade).
- Use failed auctions to extend PT by 1+ pip beyond the failed auction level.

## Key Takeaways
- Always identify: where is the sideways action? where is initiation? where is rejection?
- Failed auctions below a long level or above a short level = bad trade, skip it
- Use strong/weak highs/lows to adjust PT and SL placement
- The three signs of institutional activity are the foundation of ALL subsequent volume-based setups
- PA strategies are confirmations for volume-based levels — never trade on PA alone
