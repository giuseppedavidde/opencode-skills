# Chapter 6: Trading Psychology, Backtesting & Common Mistakes

## Core Idea
Mastery requires psychological discipline, systematic backtesting, and avoiding self-destructive behaviors. The "Cycle of Doom" (constant strategy switching) kills more traders than bad strategies.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four Kinds of Trades**: Good win, Bad win, Good loss, Bad loss
- **Cycle of Doom and Despair**: Find strategy → don't master it → switch → repeat → fail
- **5-Phase Backtesting**: Rough backtest → Thorough → Micro trading → Half positions → Full positions
- **10 Common Mistakes**: Root causes of retail trading failure

## Key Concepts
- **Good losing trade**: Most dangerous — can destroy confidence in a working strategy. Stick to rules
- **Bad winning trade**: Encourages rule-bending. A "win" without discipline plants seeds for future disaster
- **Statistics > Feelings**: Use a trading journal with screenshots. Review regularly. Data eliminates emotional decision-making
- **Rule-Fixing**: Track actual results vs. rule-following results. The gap proves discipline pays
- **Cycle of Doom**: Exit by picking ONE strategy and mastering it over months
- **Handling Winning**: Stay humble. Most blow-ups happen after winning streaks (overtrading, oversized positions)
- **Handling Losing**: Check statistics. If within normal drawdown — continue. If excessive — halve positions, don't stop
- **5-Phase Backtest**: Skip demo trading entirely. Go from backtest to micro real account. Scale up gradually
- **Phase 3 (Micro Trading)**: 10-20% of normal size. Test all market conditions before advancing
- **Phase 5 (Full)**: Trial by fire — expect losses immediately. Push through if strategy is statistically sound
- **10 Mistakes**: (1) Indicators (2) Martingale (3) Over-belief in one trade (4) Big positions (5) Can't admit wrong (6) No SL (7) No plan (8) Blindly following others (9) Strategy jumping (10) Bad broker

### Psychology Deep Dive
- **Fear & Greed**: Biggest emotional drivers. Fear causes early exits (taking 8 pips instead of 10). Greed causes oversized positions. Both destroy edge.
- **Rule-Bending**: Track actual vs rule-following results. Data proves bending rules hurts. Review statistics before each trading session.
- **Winning Streak Danger**: After wins, people feel invincible → overtrade → blow up. Stay humble. Keep risk constant.
- **Losing Streak Protocol**: If within normal drawdown → continue. If exceeding historical max → halve position sizes (don't stop). NEVER increase size during drawdown.
- **Recovery Strategy**: When struggling, review screenshots of your best trading period. Trade only your best-performing instrument. Reduce to 1 pair if needed.
- **Getting Back on Track**: Take a screenshot of EVERY trade with commentary. When lost, review the "last known good configuration" — just like Windows backup restore.

### Backtesting Phase Details
- **Phase 1 (Rough)**: 1-2 hours. Ignore news, confluences, exceptions. RRR = 1. Quick pass/fail decision.
- **Phase 2 (Thorough)**: All rules applied. Test multiple markets. Accept that even 70% win-rate in backtest can fail live.
- **Phase 3 (Micro)**: 10-20% of normal size. Real money, real emotions. Test all market conditions.
- **Phase 4 (Half)**: 50% size. Must achieve: (1) similar results to Phase 3, (2) flawless execution, (3) psychological readiness for full size.
- **Phase 5 (Full)**: Expect "cold shower" — initial losses. Push through. Trial by fire. Only revert to Phase 4 if long-term failure.
- **Trading Journal**: Must include: date, instrument, level, P/L, mistakes made, what went well, screenshot.

## Key Takeaways
- Track everything: journal + screenshots + statistics = survival toolkit
- Phase 1-5 is the only safe way to deploy a new strategy
- A good broker with tight spreads is non-negotiable for intraday
- Psychology is learned through real money, not demo accounts
- After a winning streak is when you need to be MOST vigilant
