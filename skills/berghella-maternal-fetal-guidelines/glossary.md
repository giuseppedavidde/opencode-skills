# Glossary — Maternal-Fetal Evidence Based Guidelines

Terms as this book uses them. Values are the book's values.

**A1GDM / A2GDM** — GDM controlled by diet and lifestyle alone / GDM that requires medication.
**Acute chest syndrome (ACS)** — New pulmonary infiltrate with chest pain, fever or hypoxaemia in sickle cell disease. It occurs in about 10% of pregnant patients.
**AFI** — Amniotic fluid index: sum of the deepest vertical pocket in four quadrants. ≤5.0 cm = oligohydramnios; 5.1–24.9 cm = normal; ≥25 cm = polyhydramnios.
**Amniotic fluid embolism (AFE)** — Sudden hypoxia, hypotension and coagulopathy during labour or the puerperium. Management is supportive.
**Anti-D immune globulin** — 300 µg (1 µg = 5 IU) at 28 weeks, then within 72 hours of birth of an Rh(D)-positive infant. Alternative: 100 µg at 28 and 34 weeks.
**Anti-Xa** — Heparin activity 4 hours after injection. Prophylaxis 0.2–0.4 U/mL; therapeutic 0.5–1.2 U/mL (APS), 0.6–1.0 U/mL (VTE).
**Antiphospholipid syndrome (APS)** — At least one clinical and one laboratory criterion, confirmed on repeat testing.
**Asymptomatic bacteriuria** — Significant bacteriuria without symptoms; 20–40% develop pyelonephritis untreated. Screen at the first prenatal visit.
**Biophysical profile score (BPS)** — Five components scored 0 or 2. 8/8 equals 10/10; 6/10 is repeated in about 6 hours; 4/10 indicates delivery.
**Carpenter–Coustan criteria** — 100 g, 3-hour GTT: 95/180/155/140 mg/dL. Two or more abnormal values diagnose GDM.
**Chronic hypertension (CHTN)** — BP ≥140/90 before 20 weeks or pre-existing hypertension. Severe CHTN = SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110 mmHg.
**Congenital CMV** — The commonest viral intrauterine infection, affecting 0.5–1.5% of neonates; 15–20% have sequelae.
**Contraction stress test (CST)** — Three contractions in 10 minutes; decelerations with ≥50% of contractions make the test positive.
**Eclampsia** — Grand mal seizures in a woman with pre-eclampsia and/or HELLP syndrome.
**FNAIT** — Alloimmune thrombocytopenia from maternal anti-HPA antibodies. Intracranial haemorrhage occurs in 10–30%; target platelets >20,000/µL.
**Fetal death** — NCHS: death at ≥20 weeks, or weight ≥350 g if age unknown. WHO stillbirth: no signs of life at ≥28 weeks.
**Fetal growth restriction (FGR)** — EFW or AC <10th percentile. Severe FGR: EFW <3rd percentile, or <10th percentile with abnormal umbilical artery Doppler.
**GDM** — Hyperglycaemia first recognised in pregnancy. Hyperglycaemia before 20 weeks probably represents pregestational diabetes.
**Gestational hypertension** — Sustained BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks, twice 6 hours apart, without proteinuria, severe features or prior hypertension.
**Grade I** — Evidence from at least one properly designed randomised controlled trial.
**Grade II-1** — Evidence from well-designed controlled trials without randomisation.
**Grade II-2** — Evidence from well-designed cohort or case-control analytic studies, preferably from more than one centre.
**Grade II-3** — Evidence from multiple time series, with or without the intervention.
**Grade III (Review)** — Opinions of respected authorities, based on clinical experience, descriptive studies or expert committee reports.
**HELLP** — LDH >600 IU/L or bilirubin ≥1.2 mg/dL, AST or ALT ≥2× upper limit, platelets <100,000/mm³. One or two criteria = partial HELLP.
**HDFN** — Haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn. Rh(D) and Kell are the commonest causes in the United States.
**Hyperemesis gravidarum (HG)** — Vomiting ≥3 times a day with ketones and weight loss >3 kg or >5% of pre-pregnancy weight.
**IADPSG one-step** — 75 g, 2-hour GTT: fasting ≥92, 1-hour ≥180, 2-hour ≥153 mg/dL. One abnormal value diagnoses GDM.
**Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis (IAP)** — Penicillin G 5 million units IV, then 3 million units IV every 4 hours until delivery.
**Iron deficiency anaemia** — Ferritin <15 µg/L with Hgb <11 g/dL; a cutoff of 30 µg/L improves accuracy.
**Kleihauer–Betke test** — Quantifies fetomaternal haemorrhage. It is used to dose anti-D after delivery or trauma.
**LMWH** — Low-molecular-weight heparin, the anticoagulant of choice in pregnancy. Prophylactic enoxaparin 40 mg SC daily; therapeutic 1 mg/kg every 12 hours.
**Macrosomia** — EFW or birthweight ≥4000 g. Grade I 4000–4499 g; grade II 4500–4999 g; grade III ≥5000 g.
**Magnesium sulfate** — Drug of choice for eclampsia; it reduces seizures by 59% versus placebo or no anticonvulsant.
**Maximum vertical pocket (MVP)** — Single deepest pocket without cord or fetal small parts. ≤2 cm = oligohydramnios; 2.1–7.9 cm = normal; ≥8 cm = polyhydramnios.
**MCA-PSV** — Middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity. ≥1.5 multiples of the median (MoM) indicates severe fetal anaemia and triggers fetal blood sampling.
**Mississippi HELLP class** — Class 1 platelets ≤50,000/mm³; class 2 ≤100,000/mm³; class 3 ≤150,000/mm³.
**Modified biophysical profile** — NST plus maximum vertical pocket. A full BPS is needed as backup in 15–30% of cases.
**mWHO class** — Maternal cardiovascular risk I–IV. Class IV includes severe pulmonary hypertension, EF <30%, severe aortic stenosis, Marfan with aortic root >4.5 cm, and bicuspid valve with aneurysm >5 cm.
**NDDG criteria** — 100 g, 3-hour GTT: fasting 105, 1-hour 190, 2-hour 165, 3-hour 145 mg/dL. Two or more abnormal values diagnose GDM.
**Non-stress test (NST)** — Fetal heart rate recording without contraction stimulation. No effect on perinatal mortality has been shown.
**Oligohydramnios** — MVP <2 cm, or AFI ≤5.0 cm.
**Polyhydramnios** — MVP ≥8 cm, or AFI ≥25 cm. AFI is preferred for diagnosis.
**Pre-eclampsia** — New BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks with proteinuria ≥300 mg/24 h, ratio ≥0.3 or dipstick >1+. Also diagnosed without proteinuria when hypertension coexists with organ dysfunction.
**Pre-eclampsia with severe features** — BP ≥160/110 twice >4 hours apart, platelets <100,000/mm³, transaminases ≥2× upper limit, creatinine ≥1.1 mg/dL, pulmonary oedema, or cerebral symptoms.
**Pregestational diabetes** — Diabetes present before pregnancy. Preconception HbA1c <6% prevents most complications.
**Primary CMV infection** — 1–4% of IgG-negative women acquire CMV in pregnancy; about one third transmit to the fetus.
**Prophylactic transfusion in SCD** — Started in the third trimester, every 3–4 weeks, for Hgb 9–12 g/dL and HbS <35%.
**REDV / AEDV** — Reversed or absent end-diastolic velocity in the umbilical artery. Delivery around 30–32 weeks for REDV, 33–34 weeks for AEDV.
**Sepsis hour-1 bundle** — Measure lactate, culture, give antibiotics, give 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L, and start vasopressors for MAP <65 mmHg.
**SGA** — Small for gestational age: birthweight <10th percentile. A neonatal, not an antenatal, descriptor.
**Subclinical hypothyroidism** — Elevated TSH with normal free T4. Screening and treatment have not improved outcomes.
**Superimposed pre-eclampsia** — New proteinuria after 20 weeks in chronic hypertension, or a sudden rise in proteinuria or antihypertensive requirement.
**SVR** — Sustained virological response: undetectable HCV RNA (<25 IU/mL) 12 weeks after therapy; this defines cure.
**Thyroid storm** — Severe acute exacerbation of hyperthyroidism. Treat with PTU, SSKI, dexamethasone and propranolol.
**TORCH** — Toxoplasmosis, other agents, rubella, CMV, herpes. Routine serology is not warranted in isolated FGR without risk factors or markers.
**TPOAb** — Thyroid peroxidase antibodies: >90% of Hashimoto's thyroiditis and 2–17% of pregnant women. A level >50 IU/mL is associated with abruption.
**TSH in pregnancy** — Use population-based trimester ranges when available; otherwise an upper limit of 4.0 mU/L. Hypothyroidism treatment target 0.5–2.5 mU/L.
**TSI / TRAb** — Thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin and TSH receptor antibodies. Values >200–500% indicate higher risk of fetal or neonatal hyperthyroidism.
**VTE** — Venous thromboembolism. Compression ultrasonography is the primary test for DVT; V/Q scan and CTPA are equivalent first-line tests for PE.
**VZIG (VariZIG)** — Varicella zoster immune globulin for seronegative exposed pregnant women: as soon as possible, within 96 hours and up to 10 days after exposure.
**VWD** — von Willebrand disease. Diagnose with factor VIII, vWF antigen and ristocetin cofactor. Keep factor VIII and vWF:RCo >50 IU/dL at delivery.
