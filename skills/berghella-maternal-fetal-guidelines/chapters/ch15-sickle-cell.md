# Chapter 15: Sickle Cell Disease

## Core Idea
Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an autosomal recessive hemoglobinopathy caused by hemoglobin S (HbS), a single valine-for-glutamic-acid substitution at position 6 of the beta chain. It produces chronic hemolytic anemia and vaso-occlusive events in every organ system. Diagnosis is by hemoglobin electrophoresis, and all 50 US states screen newborns. HbSS and HbS-beta-zero genotypes carry the severe phenotype and most adverse pregnancy outcomes. Pregnancy is no longer discouraged, but it requires multidisciplinary care, prophylactic transfusion in selected women, and aggressive treatment of painful crisis and acute chest syndrome (ACS).

## Recommendations and Grades
The chapter states no formal evidence grades; recommendations are narrative.
- Confirm the diagnosis by hemoglobin electrophoresis and counsel on maternal and fetal risks before conception.
- Replace teratogens before pregnancy: ACE inhibitors, iron chelators, and possibly hydroxyurea.
- Prescribe folic acid 5 mg daily plus a prenatal vitamin without iron; add iron only for documented deficiency.
- Continue prophylactic penicillin in pregnancy only if already taken for functional hyposplenism.
- Give pneumococcal, influenza, and meningococcal vaccines; avoid infections and dehydration.
- Obtain serial urine cultures every 4-8 weeks and a complete blood count each trimester.
- Offer first-trimester dating ultrasound, anatomy scan at 18-20 weeks, and growth scans starting at 28-32 weeks.
- Monitor fetal well-being with weekly nonstress tests from 32 weeks.
- Treat painful crisis with morphine or hydromorphone, a patient-controlled analgesia pump for severe pain, intravenous fluids, and stool softeners.
- Add broad-spectrum antibiotics if febrile above 38 degrees C, typically a third-generation cephalosporin plus a macrolide when chest symptoms are present.
- Give oxygen only for ACS or saturation below 95%, and use incentive spirometry in every admission for vaso-occlusive crisis.
- Start prophylactic transfusion in the third trimester every 3-4 weeks to a hemoglobin goal of 9-12 g/dL and HbS below 35%.
- Transfuse for symptomatic or orthostatic anemia, hemoglobin below 6 g/dL or hematocrit below 25%, acute stroke, ACS, or multiorgan failure.
- Use only leukodepleted units matched for Rh and Kell antigens.
- Allow vaginal delivery; reserve induction and cesarean delivery for obstetric indications and do not deliver before 39 0/7 to 39 6/7 weeks without a complication.
- Give prophylactic low molecular weight heparin for 7 days after vaginal delivery and for 6 weeks after cesarean delivery.

## Key Concepts
- Epidemiology: SCD affects 1 in 600 African Americans and 70,000-100,000 Americans; trait affects 1 in 12 African Americans. HbSS accounts for 60-70% of US disease. Over 300,000 infants were born with sickle cell anemia worldwide in 2010.
- Sickled cells live 10-20 days instead of 120, which drives chronic hemolysis. Triggers of crisis are dehydration, infection, low oxygen tension, and acidosis.
- Genotypes: HbSS and HbS-beta-zero are severe; HbSC is intermediate and affects about 1 in 833 African Americans, with 40-60% having the same course as HbSS; HbS-beta-plus, HbS-HPFP, and HbS-HbE are mild.
- Fetal effects: pregnancy loss 7-36% in HbSS and 9% in HbSC; growth restriction 45% in HbSS and 21% in HbSC; preterm birth 45% in HbSS and 22% in HbSC; pre-eclampsia 10% versus 3%.
- Maternal effects: painful crises in 20-50% of HbSS and 19-26% of HbSC pregnancies; urinary tract infection 15%; alloimmunization 24% in HbSS, 6% in HbSC, 4% in HbS-beta-zero; antepartum admission 62% versus 26%.
- Pregnancy effect on SCD: maternal mortality 0.5-2.1%; ACS 7-20%; thromboembolism 2.5%; cerebral vein thrombosis 4.9%; pneumonia 9.8%; sepsis 6.8%; systemic inflammatory response 12.6%; pulmonary hypertension 6.3%; transfusion 22.5%; postpartum infection 1.4%.
- Overall, most women deliver after 28 weeks with more than 80% live births; 50-70% are hospitalized and 75% have a pain crisis.
- Mortality: in-hospital death odds ratio 2.16 (95% CI 1.15-4.04); 30-day readmission odds ratio 1.27 and 90-day 1.92. Stroke affects 24% by age 45 and venous thromboembolism 25% by age 30.
- Transfusion controversy: a randomized trial of 72 women found no perinatal difference but fewer painful crises with prophylaxis (14% versus 50%). A meta-analysis of 12 studies and 1,291 patients found lower maternal mortality (OR 0.23, 95% CI 0.06-0.91), perinatal mortality (OR 0.43, 0.19-0.99), and neonatal death (OR 0.26, 0.07-0.93).
- Transfusion targets: hematocrit above 35%, HbA above 40%, HbS below 35%. For hematocrit below 15% give direct transfusion; above 15% exchange transfusion can be considered. Crisis alone is not an indication.
- ACS: develops in about 10% of pregnant women with SCD, kills 3-10%, and causes neurologic complications in 20%. It is a new pulmonary infiltrate with chest pain, temperature above 38.5 degrees C, tachypnea, wheezing, or cough.
- Iron: patients are usually iron overloaded from hemolysis and transfusion; ferritin above 1,000 ng/mL suggests overload and contraindicates iron.
- Crisis management: fluids about 60 mL/kg/24 hours, or 150 mL/hour; avoid NSAIDs after 28-32 weeks because of ductal constriction.
- Hydroxyurea: recommended outside pregnancy for three or more crises yearly, pain or anemia limiting daily life, or severe recurrent ACS. Case series show no increase in malformations, and 3.4% of the weight-adjusted dose enters breast milk.
- Prenatal diagnosis: chorionic villus sampling at 10-13 weeks or amniocentesis at 15 weeks or later. Delta OD450 is unreliable for fetal anemia; use middle cerebral artery Doppler.
- Neonatal withdrawal occurs in 5-6% of infants after chronic maternal opioid use. Stem cell transplant achieves 92-94% survival but usually causes infertility.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not prescribe routine iron or iron-containing prenatal vitamins; most patients are already overloaded.
- Do not use Delta OD450 to assess fetal anemia in SCD; maternal hyperbilirubinemia invalidates it.
- Do not give oxygen routinely; reserve it for ACS or saturation below 95%.
- Do not transfuse for crisis alone; transfuse for the listed indications or persistent crises.

## Key Takeaways
- Genotype determines risk: HbSS and HbS-beta-zero carry the severe outcomes.
- Start prophylactic transfusion in the third trimester to keep hemoglobin 9-12 g/dL and HbS below 35%.
- Treat febrile crisis as possible ACS with a chest radiograph, cephalosporin, macrolide, and bronchodilators.
- Use folate 5 mg daily without iron, and check ferritin before any iron.
- Plan vaginal delivery at term, with VTE prophylaxis for 7 days after vaginal and 6 weeks after cesarean delivery.
- Offer hydroxyurea continuation in pregnancy when the benefit before pregnancy was clear.
