# Chapter 4: Pregestational Diabetes

## Core Idea
Pregestational diabetes complicates about 0.9% of pregnancies and its complications are proportional to glycemic control; near-normal glucose prevents most of them. Target hemoglobin A1c <6% before conception and throughout pregnancy, with fasting glucose 60–90 mg/dL, preprandial 60–100 mg/dL, 1-hour postprandial ≤140 mg/dL, and 2-hour postprandial ≤120 mg/dL. Multiple-dose insulin is the mainstay; oral agents are used only in selected women already controlled on them. Malformations involve the cardiac and central nervous systems, and the pathognomonic lesion is sacral agenesis. Delivery is at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks when control is good, and earlier when vascular disease, poor control, or prior stillbirth is present.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Achieve and maintain hemoglobin A1c <6% (within 1% of normal), with weight loss, exercise, diet, and insulin as needed, before and during pregnancy. *(Review, guideline)*
- Obtain fasting and 2-hour (or 1-hour) postprandial capillary glucose at least four times daily; add a 3 AM value in women at risk of nocturnal hypoglycemia.
- Perform alpha-fetoprotein screening at 16–20 weeks, a detailed anatomy survey at 18–20 weeks, and fetal echocardiography at 14–16 weeks if HbA1c >8% and again at 20–22 weeks.
- Start antenatal testing with non-stress test or biophysical profile weekly or twice weekly from 32–35 6/7 weeks, then twice weekly until delivery; start at 28 weeks if control is poor.
- Deliver at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks when diabetes is well controlled; at 37 0/7–38 6/7 weeks with vascular disease; and individualize between 34 0/7 and 39 6/7 weeks when control is poor. *(Review, guideline)*
- Offer cesarean delivery when ultrasound estimated fetal weight is ≥4500 g; do not induce labor for macrosomia alone. *(Review, guideline)*
- Maintain intrapartum glucose between 70 and 110 mg/dL with intravenous insulin, hourly glucose checks, and urinary ketone checks.
- Treat diabetic ketoacidosis with aggressive hydration, intravenous insulin, and correction of the precipitating cause.
- Reduce the antepartum insulin dose by half postpartum and restart it with oral intake; add 500 kcal/day during breastfeeding.
- Offer all contraceptive methods, provided hypertension or vascular disease does not contraindicate them.

## Key Concepts
- Non-pregnant diagnostic criteria: fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL, 2-hour 75-g OGTT ≥200 mg/dL, HbA1c ≥6.5%, or random glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms; confirm on a separate day unless hyperglycemia is unequivocal.
- Prediabetes: fasting 100–125 mg/dL, 2-hour OGTT 140–199 mg/dL, HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.
- HbA1c 6% corresponds to a mean glucose of 120 mg/dL; each 1% change equals about 30 mg/dL.
- Nutrition: 30–35 kcal/kg/day for normal weight.
- Fetal echocardiography is indicated for all, and earlier when HbA1c >8%, because cardiac defects and neural tube defects are the most common malformations.
- Diabetic nephropathy affects 5–10% and progresses especially with creatinine ≥1.4 mg/dL or proteinuria ≥3 g/24 h.
- Hypertension coexists in 5–10% and raises the risk of pre-eclampsia, fetal growth restriction, and fetal death.
- Proliferative retinopathy may progress as control tightens, but intensive therapy lowers overall progression; do not avoid tight control.
- A fall in insulin requirement of ≥15% signals risk of pre-eclampsia, SGA, preterm delivery, abruption, and preterm birth.
- Insulin: lispro or aspart 15–30 minutes onset, peak 0.5–3 hours, duration ≤5 hours; regular 30 minutes, 2.5–5 hours, 4–12 hours; NPH 1–2 hours, 4–12 hours, 14–24 hours; glargine 3–4 hours, no peak, 24 hours; detemir 3–4 hours, 3–9 hours, 6–23 hours.
- Regimen: one evening long-acting injection plus three mealtime short-acting injections; glargine cannot be mixed in the same syringe. Insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio starts near 1 unit per 15 g and tightens to as low as 1 unit per 3 g as resistance rises.
- Continuous glucose monitoring may reduce hypertensive disorders (RR 0.58) and neonatal hypoglycemia (RR 0.66), but the evidence is not strong enough for a firm recommendation. *(Meta-analysis of RCTs)*
- Ketoacidosis: glucose usually >250 mg/dL, pH <7.3, bicarbonate <15 mEq/L, elevated anion gap and ketones; occurs in 5–10% of type I pregnancies.
- Intrapartum: give the usual bedtime long-acting or intermediate insulin the night before, withhold the morning dose, start 5% dextrose at 125 mL/hour when active labor begins or glucose <70 mg/dL, and add short-acting insulin when glucose ≥100 mg/dL; run dextrose and insulin as separate infusions, often simultaneously.
- Postpartum: restart half the predelivery dose or the full prepregnancy dose if that achieved euglycemia; treat glucose >140 mg/dL if oral intake is delayed.

## Anti-patterns
- White's classification for management: vascular disease is a better predictor and White's classification is no longer recommended.
- Relying on a positive fetal lung maturity test: it does not preclude infant morbidity; phosphatidylglycerol ≥3% is the accepted least-risk value.
- Delaying indicated delivery before 39 weeks to give corticosteroids or await maturity testing: not required when delivery is truly indicated.
- Assuming oral agents are equivalent to insulin in pregestational diabetes: evidence is insufficient; glyburide or metformin is continued only in women already well controlled.
- Continuing a failing insulin pump regimen without review: pump failure is a ketoacidosis risk.
- Expecting stable insulin needs: requirements usually fall in the first trimester and rise later; falling requirement is a warning sign, not reassurance.
- Missing retinopathy or nephropathy surveillance: ophthalmology and 24-hour urine are part of baseline workup.

## Key Takeaways
1. Normalize HbA1c before conception; this is the single most effective intervention.
2. Screen for malformations with alpha-fetoprotein, detailed anatomy scan, and fetal echocardiography.
3. Start antenatal testing by 32–35 6/7 weeks, or 28 weeks if control is poor.
4. Deliver at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks with good control, earlier with vascular disease or poor control, and by cesarean if estimated fetal weight ≥4500 g.
5. Keep intrapartum glucose at 70–110 mg/dL and halve the insulin dose postpartum.
