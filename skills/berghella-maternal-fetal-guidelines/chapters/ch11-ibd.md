# Chapter 11: Inflammatory Bowel Disease

## Core Idea
Inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) comprises Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis, both chronic relapsing systemic diseases of reproductive-age women. The course of pregnancy follows disease activity at conception: quiescent disease stays quiescent in about two-thirds, while active disease remains active in up to 70%. The single most important intervention is to conceive in steroid-free remission, with optimized nutrition and vaccinations, and to continue maintenance therapy, because the greatest flare risk follows medication discontinuation. Aminosalicylates, thiopurines, and biologics are continued; methotrexate and thalidomide are contraindicated. Delivery mode follows obstetric indications, with cesarean reserved for active perianal disease.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Counsel women to plan conception during steroid-free remission for 3–6 months, with nutritional status optimized.
- Continue prepregnancy maintenance therapy unless it is clearly teratogenic; the greatest flare risk occurs after stopping medication.
- Continue aminosalicylates, azathioprine or 6-mercaptopurine, and anti-TNF agents through pregnancy; use monotherapy where possible.
- Give folic acid 2 mg daily to women on sulfasalazine, starting at least one month before conception and continuing through pregnancy.
- Stop methotrexate at least 3 months, ideally 6 months, before conception; it is contraindicated and teratogenic, as is thalidomide.
- Restrict corticosteroids to flares; they are not maintenance therapy, and avoid them in the first trimester where possible.
- Use MRI or ultrasound rather than computed tomography when imaging is needed, and avoid gadolinium; flexible sigmoidoscopy without sedation is safe, and full colonoscopy with anesthesia and fetal monitoring only when it will change management.
- Perform a single third-trimester growth ultrasound in women in remission; use serial growth scans every 4 weeks and third-trimester antenatal surveillance when disease is active.
- Deliver vaginally unless there is active perianal disease (abscess or fistula), which warrants cesarean, or an obstetric indication.
- Give postpartum thromboprophylaxis with low-molecular-weight heparin during hospitalization, and up to 6 weeks in selected women, especially after cesarean.
- Immunize women on immunosuppressants against influenza and pneumococcal infection, and give tetanus and meningococcal vaccines as appropriate; live vaccines may be given at least 4 weeks before starting and at least 3 months after stopping immunosuppression.

## Key Concepts
- Inheritance: if one parent has ulcerative colitis the offspring risk is 1.6%; if one parent has Crohn's disease it is 5.2%; if both parents have IBD it is 36%.
- Clinical remission means normal stool form and frequency without bleeding or abdominal pain; endoscopic remission means mucosal healing.
- Flare risk during pregnancy is 33%, similar to non-pregnant women; ulcerative colitis flares complicate up to 35% of pregnancies.
- In a meta-analysis, 46% of women with active disease at conception remained active, while only 23% in remission relapsed. *(I)*
- Crohn's disease is associated with first-trimester miscarriage, preterm birth <37 weeks, low birth weight, venous thromboembolism, depression, and anxiety, and possibly stillbirth and SGA. *(I)*
- Ulcerative colitis is associated with preterm birth <37 weeks and possibly congenital anomalies, SGA, and stillbirth.
- Medication risk: aminosalicylates low risk (olsalazine and balsalazide have limited information); azathioprine and 6-mercaptopurine likely low risk; cyclosporine moderate risk; methotrexate and thalidomide contraindicated; infliximab low risk; adalimumab and certolizumab likely low risk; natalizumab, vedolizumab, and ustekinumab limited data, use with caution; tofacitinib not recommended; prednisone low risk but avoid in the first trimester; metronidazole likely low risk but avoid in the first trimester; quinolones moderate risk; vancomycin low risk.
- Biologic timing: ECCO suggests stopping anti-TNF at 22–24 weeks, but evidence of neonatal harm is lacking; many guidelines continue through the third trimester with dose adjustment to the lowest effective concentration at delivery, plus neonatology consultation about live vaccines.
- Ulcerative colitis surgery is indicated for massive hemorrhage, fulminant colitis unresponsive to medical therapy, perforation, suspicion or proof of carcinoma, high-grade dysplasia, or stricture in long-standing disease; surgery should not be delayed because of pregnancy.
- Historical colectomy in pregnancy carried 49% fetal and 22% maternal mortality; series after 1987 report no mortality.
- Ileal pouch–anal anastomosis triples infertility; the laparoscopic approach has lower infertility and is preferred when future pregnancy is desired. *(I)*
- Pouch function may worsen transiently in pregnancy but long-term function is preserved regardless of delivery mode.
- Vaginal delivery is safe with an ileal pouch–anal anastomosis; cesarean may be considered for fecal incontinence risk.
- Venous thromboembolism risk is increased in pregnancy and postpartum in IBD. *(I)*
- Fecal calprotectin is a useful marker outside pregnancy but has not been validated in pregnancy.
- Infliximab is compatible with breastfeeding; breastfeeding may protect against later disease course.

## Anti-patterns
- Stopping maintenance therapy at conception: the greatest flare risk follows discontinuation.
- Using corticosteroids for maintenance: they treat flares only.
- Continuing methotrexate or thalidomide into pregnancy: both are teratogenic.
- Prescribing estrogen-containing contraception without weighing venous thromboembolism risk: consider long-acting reversible contraception.
- Using computed tomography for relapse imaging: prefer MRI or ultrasound and avoid gadolinium.
- Treating every IBD pregnancy with serial growth scans and antenatal testing: women in remission need a single third-trimester growth scan.
- Elective cesarean for all women with IBD or an ileal pouch: vaginal delivery is appropriate without active perianal disease.
- Omitting postpartum thromboprophylaxis, especially after cesarean.
- Terminating pregnancy as a treatment for Crohn's disease: no evidence of improved activity.
- Advising against pregnancy because of inheritance risk: discuss the risk but never discourage pregnancy for this reason.

## Key Takeaways
1. Aim for steroid-free remission for 3–6 months before conception and continue effective maintenance drugs throughout pregnancy.
2. Replace methotrexate and thalidomide before conception, give 2 mg folic acid with sulfasalazine, and update vaccinations.
3. Expect quiescent disease to stay quiescent; active disease usually stays active and needs gastroenterology co-management.
4. Deliver vaginally unless active perianal disease or an obstetric indication dictates cesarean.
5. Give postpartum heparin prophylaxis, especially after cesarean, and support breastfeeding.
