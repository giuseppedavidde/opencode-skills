# Chapter 27: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus

## Core Idea
Pregnancy outcome in systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) is determined by disease activity at conception and by end-organ damage, not by the diagnosis itself. Over 85–90% of women without end-organ disease or antiphospholipid antibodies do well. The goal is to enter pregnancy in remission, on hydroxychloroquine, with teratogens replaced before conception. Cardiac neonatal lupus (C-NLE) is the exception: it occurs in antibody-positive women irrespective of disease activity, and hydroxychloroquine reduces its recurrence.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Continue hydroxychloroquine through pregnancy and postpartum in women stable without recent flares — Grade [II-2] (two comparative studies; review Grade [III]).
- Consider starting hydroxychloroquine if not already taking it — Grade [II-2].
- Give hydroxychloroquine >200 mg/day (usual 400 mg/day) before 10 weeks to women with anti-SSA/SSB and a prior affected child to prevent C-NLE recurrence — Grade [II-1]; earlier non-randomized data Grade [II-2] (64% reduction).
- Start low-dose aspirin 81 mg (range 50–150 mg) at 12–16 weeks for pre-eclampsia prevention — Grade [I] (meta-analysis; pre-eclampsia reduced 24%, RR 0.76, CI 0.62–0.95).
- Screen with fetal echocardiography from 16 to 26 weeks in anti-SSA or anti-SSA/SSB positive women — no grade stated.
- Treat fetal first- and second-degree atrioventricular (AV) block with dexamethasone, alone or with intravenous immune globulin (IVIG), and continue based on response — recommended by the 2020 American College of Rheumatology guideline; grade not stated.
- Do not use IVIG for C-NLE recurrence prevention — ineffective in the cited data.
- Deliver at 39 0/7 to 39 6/7 weeks when no earlier indication exists — no grade stated.
- Repeat laboratory tests each trimester to assess disease activity — no grade stated.

## Key Concepts
- Diagnosis accepts any of three schemes: ≥4/11 American College of Rheumatology (ACR) criteria; ≥4/17 Systemic Lupus International Collaborating Clinics (SLICC) criteria with at least one clinical and one immunologic criterion, or lupus nephritis on biopsy with ANA or anti-dsDNA; or ANA ≥1:80 plus ≥10 additive points (EULAR/ACR).
- SLICC sensitivity 96.7% versus 82.8% for ACR, specificity 83.7% versus 93.4%. EULAR/ACR sensitivity 96.1%, specificity 93.4%.
- Incidence 1:700 to 1:2,000 overall, 1:200 in African Americans, 1:500 in reproductive-age women; 90% of patients are women; 25% meet criteria for antiphospholipid syndrome.
- Maternal risks: hypertension 4–20%, pre-eclampsia 8–20%, eclampsia 0.5–1%, preterm birth 20–50%, cesarean 30–40%, flare 20–30%, nephritis 10–20%, thrombocytopenia 4%, anemia 13%, antepartum bleeding 2%, transfusion 3%, infection/thrombosis/death 1–2%. Indicated preterm delivery occurs in 70%.
- Fetal risks: first-trimester loss 10–20%, fetal death 1–5%, growth restriction 5–20%.
- Independent risk factors for pregnancy loss: proteinuria ≥500 mg/24 h, antiphospholipid syndrome, thrombocytopenia ≤150,000/mm³, hypertension ≥140/90 mm Hg.
- Renal flare rate about 16%. Proteinuria >500 mg–1 g/day and glomerular filtration rate <60 mL/min raise flare risk. Creatinine <1.4 mg/dL predicts success; creatinine >1.4 mg/dL carries 43% worsening and 10% irreversible deterioration. Creatinine >2.5 mg/dL warrants counselling against pregnancy.
- Maternal mortality fell from 442 per 100,000 admissions (1998–2000) to <50 per 100,000 (2013–2015).
- Distinguish flare from pre-eclampsia: C3 and C4 fall and anti-dsDNA rises in flare; red cells, white cells, and cellular casts appear in urine sediment; pre-eclampsia occurs only after 20 weeks.
- Baseline and per-trimester panel: CBC with platelets and differential, transaminases, creatinine, BUN, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, anticardiolipin, lupus anticoagulant or dilute Russell viper venom time, anti-beta-2 glycoprotein-I, anti-dsDNA, C3, C4, CH50, urine sediment, 24-hour urine protein and creatinine clearance or spot protein-to-creatinine ratio.
- Antenatal testing weekly from 32 weeks. Growth ultrasound every 3–4 weeks. Anatomy survey at 18–20 weeks.
- C-NLE risk about 2% in anti-SSA or anti-SSA/SSB positive women; recurrence 18%. Hydroxychloroquine 400 mg/day lowers recurrence from 18% to 7.4%.
- First-degree AV block: an AV interval ≥170 ms may be a more accurate definition; 150–170 ms without extranodal findings is reassuring.
- Steroid-treated second-degree block progresses to third-degree in 52% versus 73% untreated; regression occurs in 25% versus 23%.
- Drug rules: prednisone under 10–20 mg/day maintenance, ≥60 mg/day for 3 weeks for flare, taper if used >7 days; azathioprine 50–100 mg daily, <2 mg/kg/day; NSAIDs safe to 28–30 weeks, non-selective preferred; certolizumab likely safe all trimesters; stop infliximab and adalimumab at 20 weeks and etanercept at 32 weeks; avoid rituximab, mycophenolate, cyclophosphamide, leflunomide; methotrexate contraindicated.
- Fluorinated steroids (dexamethasone, betamethasone) are not for lupus activity; reserve for C-NLE. Their risks are oligohydramnios, growth restriction, and maternal diabetes, reduced by weaning after 30–32 weeks.
- Live vaccines are avoided in infants for at least 6 months after maternal tumor necrosis factor inhibitor exposure.

## Anti-patterns
- Stopping hydroxychloroquine periconception — increases flare risk and removes C-NLE protection.
- Attempting conception during active disease — wait at least 6 months without flares or active disease.
- Continuing mycophenolate, methotrexate, cyclophosphamide, or leflunomide into pregnancy — teratogenic or unsafe.
- Using fluorinated steroids to treat maternal lupus activity — no maternal benefit and significant fetal risk.
- Using hydroxychloroquine to prevent a first C-NLE when the mother has never had an affected child — data insufficient; only conditionally recommended by ACR.
- Giving IVIG to prevent C-NLE recurrence — shown ineffective.
- Relying on weekly echocardiography alone to detect treatable block — weekly imaging is too infrequent to catch first- or second-degree block; ambulatory fetal heart rhythm monitoring is a useful adjunct.
- Treating isolated third-degree AV block reflexively — it is irreversible; decide case by case with the parents.

## Key Takeaways
1. Achieve remission before conception; continue hydroxychloroquine 400 mg/day (max 5 mg/kg/day) throughout pregnancy and breastfeeding ([II-2]).
2. Start low-dose aspirin 81 mg at 12–16 weeks for pre-eclampsia prevention ([I]).
3. In anti-SSA/SSB positive women with a prior affected child, start hydroxychloroquine >200 mg/day before 10 weeks ([II-1]); screen with fetal echocardiography from 16 to 26 weeks.
4. Replace teratogens before pregnancy; keep prednisone under 10–20 mg/day and azathioprine under 2 mg/kg/day.
5. Check complement, anti-dsDNA, and urine sediment each trimester to separate flare from pre-eclampsia.
6. Plan delivery at 39 0/7 to 39 6/7 weeks with stress-dose steroids if indicated, at a center with neonatal pacing capability if C-NLE is present.
