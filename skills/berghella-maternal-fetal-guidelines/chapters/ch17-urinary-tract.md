# Chapter 17: Urinary Tract Disease

## Core Idea
Pregnancy raises renal plasma flow 60-80% and glomerular filtration rate (GFR) 50%, so creatinine falls to a mean of 0.6 mg/dL and normal protein excretion rises to 180-250 mg/day. A creatinine inside the non-pregnant range can therefore hide major renal injury. Prognosis in chronic kidney disease (CKD) tracks the preconception creatinine and worsens with each stage. Acute kidney injury (AKI) is dominated by pre-eclampsia and HELLP syndrome and raises maternal death, stillbirth, and preterm birth.

## Recommendations and Grades
The chapter states no formal evidence grades; recommendations are narrative.
- Work up renal disease with creatinine, blood urea nitrogen, electrolytes, urinalysis, and 24-hour urine for protein and creatinine clearance; target blood pressure below 140/90 mmHg.
- Screen every woman for asymptomatic bacteriuria at the first prenatal visit and each trimester when risk factors exist; treat for 7 days, for 10-14 days if recurrent, and suppress nightly after two infections or one pyelonephritis.
- Admit for pyelonephritis for at least 48 hours after 24 weeks; give intravenous antibiotics until afebrile 24 hours, then oral therapy to 10-14 days.
- Use renal ultrasound first for suspected stones, magnetic resonance urography second, and computed tomography only when both are unavailable.
- Start aspirin before 16 weeks, usually at 12 weeks, in CKD, dialysis, and transplant patients; ASPRE suggests 150 mg may be more effective.
- Stop ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers by 8 weeks at the latest, and replace mycophenolate mofetil, methotrexate, and cyclophosphamide with pregnancy-compatible agents.
- Wait 6 months of quiescent disease before conception in lupus or vasculitis, and complete renal transplant before pregnancy in stage IV-V disease.
- Counsel dialysis patients to consider transplant first and wait 1-2 years before pregnancy; keep all dialysis and transplant patients on contraception.
- See patients every 2-4 weeks to 32 weeks then weekly; obtain monthly growth scans and weekly antenatal testing from 32 weeks.

## Key Concepts
- Normal values: creatinine 0.4-0.9 mg/dL and GFR 117-182 mL/min in the third trimester, with a 15-20% GFR fall near term. Kidney length rises 1 cm and volume 30%; right-sided physiologic hydronephrosis occurs in over 90%. A protein-to-creatinine ratio above 0.3 g/g defines pre-eclamptic proteinuria; 300 mg/day is the upper normal limit.
- AKI: KDIGO stage 1 is a rise of 0.3 mg/dL or 1.5-1.9 times baseline with urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/hour for 6-12 hours; stage 3 is three times baseline, 4 mg/dL or more, or dialysis.
- Prerenal AKI: fractional excretion of sodium below 1%, urine sodium below 20 mEq/L, blood urea nitrogen to creatinine ratio above 20:1, osmolality above 500 mosm/kg, bland sediment. Intrinsic AKI: fractional excretion above 2%, urine sodium above 40 mEq/L, ratio near 10:1, osmolality below 350, granular or cellular casts.
- Pre-eclampsia with severe features and HELLP syndrome cause about 40% of pregnancy AKI; 1% of pre-eclampsia and 3-15% of HELLP cases develop AKI. Other causes are hyperemesis, hemorrhage, sepsis, acute tubular necrosis, interstitial nephritis, glomerulonephritis, and thrombotic microangiopathy.
- AKI outcomes: maternal death odds ratio 4.5 (95% CI 2.7-7.3), stillbirth or perinatal death 3.39 (2.76-4.18), cesarean 1.49, birth weight 740 g lower, delivery 0.7 weeks earlier, intensive care stay 2.13 days longer. Renal recovery is 60-90%.
- Renal biopsy: complications 7% in pregnancy versus 1% postpartum, major bleeding 2% especially in the second trimester, and management changes in 66%. Defer beyond 32 weeks.
- CKD: prevalence 0.03-0.4%. Progression occurs in 7.6% of stage 1, 12.6% of stage 2, and 16.2% of stage 3 patients; 20% of stage 4-5 patients need dialysis. Creatinine 1.4 mg/dL or more predicts 10% deterioration; above 2.3-2.4 mg/dL pregnancy is discouraged; clearance below 70 mL/min predicts poor outcome; proteinuria above 1 g/day with GFR below 40 mL/min accelerates loss.
- CKD outcomes versus controls: pre-eclampsia odds ratio 10.4, preterm delivery 5.7, small for gestational age or low birth weight 4.9, cesarean 2.7, fetal or neonatal death 1.8. Preterm labor reaches 85%, low birth weight 66%, perinatal mortality 10-20%.
- Dialysis: live birth 78% (range 61-89%), mean delivery at 34 weeks, 46% small for gestational age, 18% perinatal death. Intensive hemodialysis above 36 hours weekly improves outcome and 36 hours is the expert minimum. Midweek predialysis blood urea nitrogen above 35 mg/dL has 88% sensitivity and odds ratio 6.4 for adverse fetal outcome.
- Transplant: live birth above 90%. A meta-analysis of 6,712 pregnancies found miscarriage 15.4%, stillbirth 5.1%, pre-eclampsia 21.5%, gestational diabetes 5.7%, gestational hypertension 24.1%, cesarean 62.6%, preterm 43.1%, mean gestational age 34.9 weeks, birth weight 2,470 g, and acute rejection 4.2%. Eligibility requires creatinine below 1.5 mg/dL, GFR above 60 mL/min, proteinuria below 300-500 mg/day, no rejection in the past year, and no recent urinary infection.
- Urinary infection: asymptomatic bacteriuria affects 2-15%, and untreated cases progress to pyelonephritis in 20-40% versus 3% when treated. Treatment lowers pyelonephritis (risk ratio 0.24, 95% CI 0.13-0.41), preterm birth (0.34, 0.13-0.88), and low birth weight (0.64, 0.45-0.93). Escherichia coli causes over 80%, and 20-40% are ampicillin resistant.
- Stones: hospitalization 0.03-0.8%; ultrasound sensitivity 29-95%, computed tomography 96% with 100% specificity; spontaneous passage 23-85%, usually above 65-70%; stent complications reach 16% with exchange every 4-6 weeks; ureteroscopy complications are 4-8%.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not accept a creatinine within the non-pregnant range as normal in pregnancy.
- Do not rely on dipstick alone to exclude proteinuria; it misses about 1 in 11 hypertensive women with true proteinuria.
- Do not use indomethacin tocolysis, postpartum nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, or gentamicin in CKD.
- Do not use shockwave lithotripsy or percutaneous nephrolithotomy in pregnancy; complication rates are higher.
- Do not continue mycophenolate mofetil, ACE inhibitors, or angiotensin receptor blockers into pregnancy.

## Key Takeaways
- Treat a third-trimester creatinine above 0.9 mg/dL as abnormal and calculate GFR.
- Screen and treat asymptomatic bacteriuria at the first visit; untreated, 20-40% develop pyelonephritis.
- Start aspirin by 12-16 weeks in all CKD, dialysis, and transplant pregnancies.
- Target 36 hours weekly of hemodialysis and predialysis blood urea nitrogen below 35 mg/dL.
- Counsel transplant candidates to wait at least 1 year, preferably 2 years, before conception.
- Reserve stone intervention for infection, obstruction, or intractable symptoms.
