# Chapter 26: Respiratory Diseases: Asthma, Pneumonia, Influenza, Tuberculosis, and COVID-19

## Core Idea
Asthma control, not asthma label, determines pregnancy outcome. Inhaled corticosteroids are the mainstay at every step of persistent disease and must not be withdrawn in pregnancy. Pneumonia is confirmed by infiltrate on chest X-ray; prompt, appropriate antibiotics matter more than pathogen identification. Influenza vaccination is recommended for every pregnant and postpartum woman, and antivirals start before laboratory confirmation. Active tuberculosis is always treated in pregnancy; latent infection is usually deferred to 2–3 months postpartum. COVID-19 severity peaks 7–10 days after symptom onset and pregnancy is a risk factor for intensive care and mechanical ventilation.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Use inhaled corticosteroids as the mainstay of long-term control for persistent asthma; they are safe in pregnancy — Grade [I] (systematic meta-analysis); budesonide supported by Grade [I, n = 313].
- Do not change asthma therapy because of pregnancy; aim for maximal symptom relief and best pulmonary function — observational data, no grade stated.
- Add inhaled corticosteroid when a β-agonist is used twice weekly — Grade [I] (meta-analysis of inhaled corticosteroid safety).
- Continue asthma medications through labor; measure peak expiratory flow on admission and every 12 hours — no grade stated.
- Avoid carboprost (prostaglandin F2α analogue) — no grade stated (bronchospasm risk).
- Prefer regional to general anesthesia in asthma — no grade stated.
- Treat low-risk community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) with a macrolide or amoxicillin; treat hospitalized patients with a macrolide plus a β-lactam — guideline-based, no numeric grade stated.
- Do not change antibiotics within the first 72 hours unless deterioration is overt or sensitivities are available — no grade stated.
- Give trivalent inactivated influenza vaccine to all pregnant, postpartum, and breastfeeding women in any trimester, at the start of influenza season — Grade [I, n = 340] for infant benefit; Grade [II-1] for first-trimester safety.
- Start influenza antiviral therapy as soon as possible, ideally within 48 hours, without waiting for laboratory confirmation — no grade stated.
- Treat active tuberculosis in pregnancy without deferral — no grade stated.
- Treat latent tuberculosis with isoniazid 5 mg/kg (max 300 mg) daily for 6–9 months plus pyridoxine — no numeric grade stated; hepatotoxicity data Grade [II-3], ATS hepatotoxicity guidance Grade [III].
- Use interferon gamma-release assay (IGRA) in preference to tuberculin skin testing in BCG-vaccinated patients and those with poor return rates — guideline-based.
- Do not give BCG vaccine during pregnancy — no grade stated.
- Recommend COVID-19 vaccination for women planning conception, pregnant, postpartum, and breastfeeding — no numeric grade stated.
- Use dexamethasone 6 mg daily for up to 10 days in COVID-19 requiring oxygen or ventilation — no numeric grade stated (randomized trial, 29.1% vs 41.4% mortality with mechanical ventilation).

## Key Concepts
- Asthma affects about 8% of pregnant women in the United States. Two thirds improve in pregnancy, one third worsen. Most exacerbations occur between 24 and 36 weeks; symptoms are fewest at term.
- Severity by exacerbation burden: mild disease — 2% hospitalized, 13% exacerbation, 13% symptomatic at delivery; moderate — 7% hospitalized, 26% exacerbation, 21% symptomatic; severe — 27% hospitalized, 52% exacerbation, 46% symptomatic.
- Spirometry: FEV1 <80% predicted or FEV1/FVC <0.7 confirms airflow limitation; ≥12% rise in FEV1 (or ≥10% of predicted) after bronchodilator confirms reversibility.
- Quick-relief dosing: albuterol 2 puffs q4–6h as needed; exacerbation — albuterol nebulizer 2.5–5 mg q20 min × 3, then 2.5–10 mg q1–4h or 10–15 mg/hour continuous; ipratropium 0.5 mg q30 min × 3.
- Systemic steroids for exacerbation: prednisone 40–60 mg/day for 3–10 days; methylprednisolone 60–80 mg/day until PEFR reaches 70% of predicted or personal best.
- Oral steroid in the first trimester raises cleft lip risk from a background 0.1–0.3% by a small excess.
- PEFR 50–80% of predicted: treat immediately at home; <50%: treat and go to the emergency room.
- CAP attack rate is unchanged by pregnancy, about 1.5 per 1,000; Streptococcus pneumoniae and Mycoplasma pneumoniae each cause 5–30%. A pathogen is identified in only 40–60% of cases.
- Hospitalize CAP with respiratory rate ≥30, diastolic BP ≤60, systolic BP ≤90, heart rate ≥125, altered mental status, PaCO2 <60 mm Hg on room air, pleural effusion, hematocrit <30, arterial pH <7.35, or multilobe involvement.
- Blood cultures are positive in 5–11% of CAP. Procalcitonin has no validated cutoff to exclude bacterial pneumonia.
- CAP duration: minimum 5 days, at least 7 days for suspected MRSA or Pseudomonas. Defervescence in 2–4 days is the expected response.
- Influenza: serology-confirmed cases 483–1,097 per 10,000 pregnant women; symptomatic cases 0.1–6.6 per 10,000 pregnancies in the 2009 pandemic. Vaccine efficacy 41–91.5% against laboratory-confirmed influenza.
- Antivirals: oseltamivir 75 mg twice daily for 5 days (preferred); zanamivir two 10-mg inhalations twice daily for 5 days (avoid with asthma or chronic respiratory disease); peramivir 600 mg IV over 15–30 minutes.
- Tuberculosis: 20% of cases are extrapulmonary. IGRA correlates better with recent exposure and is unaffected by prior BCG. PPD is 0.1 mL (5 tuberculin units) intradermal, read at 48–72 hours.
- WHO recommends systematic TB screening in prenatal care only where population TB prevalence is ≥100 cases per 100,000.
- First-line anti-TB daily doses: isoniazid 4–6 mg/kg (max 300 mg), rifampin 8–12 mg/kg (max 600 mg), pyrazinamide 20–30 mg/kg, ethambutol 12–18 mg/kg.
- Isoniazid hepatotoxicity occurs in about 1 per 1,000. Stop if ALT >3× upper limit of normal with symptoms or >5× with no symptoms.
- CDC active-TB regimen: 2 months isoniazid, rifampin, ethambutol, then 7 months isoniazid plus rifampin. WHO and ATS/IDSA use pyrazinamide in the intensive phase. Combination therapy for ≥6 months cures >95%.
- COVID-19: about 15.6% asymptomatic at diagnosis; incubation about 5 days, 99% symptomatic within 2 weeks; 44% of transmission occurs before symptom onset. Preterm delivery is 3 times higher in COVID-positive mothers. Vertical transmission is about 2–3% and rarely symptomatic.
- COVID-19 drugs: remdesivir 200 mg IV once then 100 mg IV daily; dexamethasone 6 mg IV or oral daily up to 10 days; tocilizumab 8 mg/kg actual body weight (max 800 mg) single IV dose.

## Anti-patterns
- Stopping or reducing inhaled corticosteroids in pregnancy — women who decrease therapy deliver lower-birthweight infants at slightly shorter gestational age.
- Using inhaled β-agonists as monotherapy for persistent asthma — they do not control airway inflammation.
- Carboprost for postpartum hemorrhage in asthma — it causes bronchospasm. Ergonovine, methylergonovine, and NSAIDs may also precipitate bronchospasm, although the risk in the general asthmatic population is under 1%.
- Changing antibiotics within 72 hours without overt deterioration or sensitivities — slow radiographic and auscultatory clearing is expected.
- Using procalcitonin to decide on antibiotics — no level reliably excludes bacterial pneumonia.
- Routine follow-up chest X-ray after inpatient CAP — not recommended.
- Deferring treatment of active tuberculosis — untreated mothers infect the population and their infants.
- Giving BCG during pregnancy — theoretical reactivation risk.
- Chloroquine or hydroxychloroquine with or without azithromycin for COVID-19 — not recommended.
- Amantadine or rimantadine for influenza — abandoned for high resistance.
- Delaying influenza antivirals for laboratory confirmation — delayed therapy increases severity and death.
- Universal avoidance of NSAIDs postpartum in asthma — risk is under 1%, so treat if no prior reaction.

## Key Takeaways
1. Never stop inhaled corticosteroid in pregnancy; step up if a SABA is used more than twice weekly ([I]).
2. Order a chest X-ray for suspected pneumonia; both X-ray and CT are safe, far below the 5 Rad fetal threshold.
3. Give inactivated influenza vaccine in any trimester and start oseltamivir 75 mg twice daily for 5 days on suspicion, within 48 hours.
4. Treat active TB regardless of pregnancy; screen only if prepared to treat; check baseline and monthly transaminases.
5. Use dexamethasone 6 mg for up to 10 days when COVID-19 requires oxygen; add remdesivir, and tocilizumab 8 mg/kg (max 800 mg) if pulmonary function declines rapidly.
6. Permit skin-to-skin and rooming-in for a well COVID-positive mother; neonatal transmission is about 2–3%.
