# Chapter 30: Venous Thromboembolism and Anticoagulation

## Core Idea
Venous thromboembolism (VTE) is a leading cause of maternal death in high-income countries, and pregnancy raises risk fivefold, with a 60-fold rise in the first 3 months postpartum. Compression ultrasonography is the primary test for deep vein thrombosis (DVT); ventilation/perfusion (V/Q) scanning and computed tomography pulmonary angiography (CTPA) are equivalent first-line tests for pulmonary embolism (PE). Low-molecular-weight heparin (LMWH) is the anticoagulant of choice. Treatment doses depend on the clinical scenario, and the timing of neuraxial anesthesia must follow fixed intervals after the last dose.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Treat acute VTE with therapeutic LMWH throughout pregnancy, or intravenous unfractionated heparin (UFH) for at least 5 days followed by therapeutic UFH or LMWH for a minimum of 6 months — Grade: not stated (ACOG Practice Bulletin No. 196, review-level).
- Continue anticoagulation for at least 6 weeks postpartum and for at least 3 months after the VTE — no numeric grade stated.
- Use compression ultrasonography as the primary imaging test for DVT — no grade stated.
- Use V/Q scan or CTPA as equivalent first-line imaging for PE; chest radiograph first, then V/Q if normal or CTPA if abnormal — American Thoracic Society/Society of Thoracic Radiology guideline.
- Give prophylactic anticoagulation for prior VTE with low-risk thrombophilia, and therapeutic anticoagulation for prior VTE with high-risk thrombophilia (antithrombin deficiency, homozygous factor V or prothrombin mutation, compound heterozygote) — no grade stated.
- Use therapeutic anticoagulation for recurrent VTE, life-threatening thrombosis, or thrombosis despite chronic anticoagulation; consider inferior vena cava filter in these cases — no grade stated.
- Give prophylactic heparin plus low-dose aspirin to women with recurrent early loss and antiphospholipid syndrome (APS) without prior VTE — no grade stated.
- For mechanical heart valves, use one of three regimens: therapeutic LMWH or UFH between 6 and 10 weeks and near term with a vitamin K antagonist at other times; vitamin K antagonist throughout; or therapeutic LMWH throughout — no grade stated.
- Check platelet counts about 5 days after starting UFH and periodically for the first 3 weeks — no grade stated.
- Consider pharmacological prophylaxis after cesarean delivery with enoxaparin 40 mg daily or UFH 5,000 units every 12 hours when body mass index exceeds 50 kg/m², there is prior VTE, or there are two or more additional risk factors — no grade stated.

## Key Concepts
- DVT accounts for 75–80% of VTE and PE for 20–25%. About 50% of events occur antepartum and 50% postpartum.
- VTE complicates about 1.2 per 1,000 deliveries; absolute risk 1–2 per 1,000 pregnancies. Fatal PE causes 9.8% of pregnancy-related deaths in the United States. Death from PE occurs in 1.1–1.5 per 100,000 pregnancies.
- Risk is increased fivefold during pregnancy and 60-fold in the first 3 months postpartum; it may persist to 12 weeks.
- Unilateral leg pain and edema are the first symptoms in 80% of pregnant patients with DVT. Left leg involvement occurs in 85–90%; proximal femoral or iliac DVT in over 60%.
- Clinical suspicion of DVT is confirmed in 10% of pregnant patients versus 25% of non-pregnant patients; PE is confirmed in only 4% of pregnant patients.
- PE is missed clinically in 70–80% of cases found at autopsy. About 30% of apparently isolated PE has asymptomatic DVT; 40–50% of symptomatic DVT has asymptomatic PE. PE occurs in 15% of untreated DVT (1% mortality) and 4.5% of treated DVT (1% mortality). Most deaths occur within 30 minutes.
- Compression ultrasonography has 97% sensitivity and 94% specificity for proximal DVT in non-pregnant patients.
- D-dimer rises progressively in pregnancy; a low value helps exclude DVT, but a high value always requires confirmatory imaging. Pregnancy-adapted YEARS algorithm: D-dimer threshold 1,000 ng/mL with no YEARS criteria and 500 ng/mL with 1–3 criteria (hemoptysis, PE most likely diagnosis).
- Inconclusive imaging: 14% for V/Q scan and 12% for CTPA.
- Regimen definitions — prophylactic LMWH: enoxaparin 40 mg daily, dalteparin 5,000 units daily, tinzaparin 4,500 units daily, nadroparin 2,850 units daily. Intermediate dose: enoxaparin 40 mg every 12 hours or dalteparin 5,000 units every 12 hours. Adjusted (therapeutic) dose: enoxaparin 1 mg/kg every 12 hours, dalteparin 200 units/kg daily, tinzaparin 175 units/kg daily, or dalteparin 100 units/kg every 12 hours.
- Anti-Xa targets: 0.6–1.0 units/mL 4 hours after a twice-daily therapeutic dose; 0.5–1.2 units/mL therapeutic; 0.2–0.4 units/mL prophylactic.
- Prophylactic UFH: 5,000–7,500 units every 12 hours in the first trimester, 7,500–10,000 units in the second, and 10,000 units in the third, unless the aPTT is elevated. Adjusted-dose UFH: 10,000 units or more every 12 hours targeting aPTT 1.5–2.5 times control 6 hours after injection.
- Enoxaparin 40 mg every 12 hours rather than once daily is suggested for women at or above 90 kg.
- Warfarin is considered safe in the first 6 weeks but causes embryopathy in 4–5% with exposure between 6 and 9 weeks. Start warfarin at 5 mg, 24–36 hours after delivery, with heparin overlap for 5–7 days until INR exceeds 2.0 for 24 hours; target INR 2.0–3.0.
- Heparin-induced thrombocytopenia: suspect with a platelet fall over 50% from baseline or below 100,000/µL, heparin antibodies, injection-site skin lesions, or systemic reaction 5–15 days after starting. Danaparoid or fondaparinux are alternatives; fondaparinux is not recommended in the first trimester because activity is detectable in umbilical cord blood.
- Heparin-induced osteoporosis: symptomatic vertebral fractures in 2–3% of treated patients; bone mineral density reduction up to 30% with long-term UFH; mean bone loss about 5%; LMWH carries lower risk.
- Neuraxial anesthesia timing: prophylactic UFH (7,500 units twice daily or 10,000 units twice daily) — hold 12 hours and check coagulation; adjusted-dose UFH — hold 24 hours; low-dose LMWH prophylaxis — wait 12 hours; intermediate or adjusted-dose LMWH — wait 24 hours.
- Restart anticoagulation 4–6 hours after vaginal delivery, 6–12 hours after cesarean, and no sooner than 2 hours after epidural catheter removal. Leave pneumatic compression devices in place until ambulation and restart.
- Cesarean prophylaxis trials show no reduction in symptomatic thromboembolic events: LMWH/UFH versus no heparin RR 1.30 (95% CI 0.39–4.27; four trials, 840 women). UFH plus physiotherapy increased bleeding versus physiotherapy alone (RR 5.03, 95% CI 2.49–10.18).
- Breastfeeding is safe on UFH, LMWH, or warfarin.
- Thrombolysis for massive PE: 23 reported cases, no maternal deaths, fetal death 2 of 23 (9%), bleeding 9 of 23 (39%), major bleeding 5 of 23 (22%). Embolectomy carries 20–40% fetal loss.
- Inferior vena cava filters are restricted to proven VTE with recurrent PE despite anticoagulation or a contraindication to anticoagulation; place suprarenally.
- Early ambulation with elastic compression does not increase PE or thrombus propagation and speeds resolution of pain and edema.
- Contraception: estrogen-containing pills raise VTE risk 39- to 99-fold in women heterozygous for factor V Leiden or prothrombin G20210A. Progestin-only pills show no increased risk; injectable progestins show a twofold increase. Intrauterine devices and implants are preferred.

## Anti-patterns
- Relying on D-dimer alone to diagnose VTE in pregnancy — values rise physiologically and a positive result always needs imaging.
- Using clinical decision rules derived in non-pregnant patients without pregnancy adaptation.
- Universal heparin prophylaxis after every cesarean in United States practice — not recommended; use risk assessment.
- Universal pneumatic compression plus heparin after cesarean — compression alone gives similar risk reduction with fewer bleeding events.
- Withholding anticoagulation when the prior VTE had a non-recurring cause and no thrombophilia — recurrence was 0% in 44 such untreated women, so prophylaxis may be avoided.
- Starting fondaparinux in the first trimester — placental transfer with detectable activity in cord blood.
- Continuing warfarin beyond 6 weeks without counseling — embryopathy risk of 4–5% at 6–9 weeks.
- Giving estrogen-containing contraception to a woman with prior VTE or a high-risk thrombophilia.

## Key Takeaways
1. Image suspected DVT with compression ultrasound; image suspected PE with V/Q scan or CTPA after a chest radiograph.
2. Treat acute VTE with therapeutic LMWH for at least 6 months total, including at least 6 weeks postpartum.
3. Use the regimen table: enoxaparin 40 mg daily is prophylaxis; 1 mg/kg every 12 hours is treatment, with anti-Xa 0.6–1.0 units/mL.
4. Stop prophylactic LMWH 12 hours and therapeutic LMWH 24 hours before neuraxial anesthesia.
5. Restart anticoagulation 4–6 hours after vaginal delivery and 6–12 hours after cesarean, never sooner than 2 hours after epidural removal.
6. Offer intrauterine devices or implants for contraception; avoid estrogen-containing methods in women at high thrombotic risk.
