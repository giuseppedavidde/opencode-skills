# Chapter 5: Gestational Diabetes

## Core Idea
Gestational diabetes (GDM) is hyperglycemia first recognized during pregnancy; hyperglycemia found before 20 weeks is probably pregestational diabetes. Universal screening at 24–28 weeks is standard, with early screening at the first visit for women with risk factors — about 5–10% of them have early GDM, and these account for 40% of all GDM. Treatment with diet, exercise, and glucose monitoring, adding insulin when targets are not met, reduces pre-eclampsia, birth weight >4000 g, shoulder dystocia, bone fracture, nerve palsy, and perinatal death. Insulin is the preferred pharmacologic agent; metformin is preferred over glyburide if an oral agent is used. Women with GDM need a 75-g glucose tolerance test at 4–12 weeks postpartum and lifelong surveillance.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Screen universally at 24–28 weeks; screen at the first prenatal visit or before conception when risk factors are present, and repeat at 24–28 weeks if the early test is negative.
- Use either the one-step 75-g, 2-hour test with one abnormal value, or the two-step 50-g challenge followed by a 100-g, 3-hour test with two abnormal values; the two-step approach is more cost-effective and is supported by ACOG, while a meta-analysis supports the one-step approach for outcomes.
- Treat to fasting ≤95 mg/dL and 2-hour postprandial <120 mg/dL, or 1-hour postprandial <140 mg/dL. *(Guideline)*
- Start pharmacologic therapy when one or two values exceed the limits during a 2-week monitoring period.
- Prescribe 30 minutes of moderate aerobic exercise 5 times per week; it improves glycemic control with diet and gives a macrosomia rate similar to insulin. *(Guideline)*
- Use insulin as first-line pharmacotherapy: it produces less fetal macrosomia and less neonatal hypoglycemia than glyburide.
- Prefer metformin over glyburide if an oral agent is chosen; it causes less maternal weight gain and lower neonatal birth weight.
- Deliver at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks when medication is required; do not delay truly indicated delivery before 39 weeks for maturity testing.
- Recommend cesarean when estimated fetal weight is >4500 g; avoid operative vaginal delivery when estimated fetal weight is >4000 g with a prolonged second stage. *(Guideline)*
- Screen for diabetes with a 75-g test or standard non-pregnant criteria at 4–12 weeks postpartum, then every 1–3 years.
- Encourage breastfeeding: it reduces progression to type II diabetes, and the benefit rises with duration.

## Key Concepts
- A1GDM is controlled by diet and lifestyle; A2GDM requires medication.
- One-step 75-g thresholds: fasting ≥92 mg/dL, 1-hour ≥180 mg/dL, 2-hour ≥153 mg/dL; one value is diagnostic. This diagnoses about twice as many women as the two-step method.
- Two-step: 50-g non-fasting challenge at 130, 135, or 140 mg/dL. The 130 threshold detects 90% but refers 20–25%; the 140 threshold detects 80% and refers 14–18%. A value ≥200 mg/dL is diagnostic in most practices.
- 100-g, 3-hour test: Carpenter-Coustan fasting 95, 1-hour 180, 2-hour 155, 3-hour 140 mg/dL; NDDG fasting 105, 190, 165, 145 mg/dL. Carpenter-Coustan increases diagnoses by about 50% and identifies real morbidity, so it is preferred.
- Isolated one abnormal value: counsel against excess glucose and repeat fasting and 2-hour values or the 3-hour test in 3–4 weeks; treatment reduces LGA and cesarean delivery.
- Diet: about 30 kcal/kg/day, ±5 kcal/kg for underweight or overweight women; 3 meals and 2–3 snacks; 33–40% carbohydrate, 20% protein, 40% unsaturated fat. About 30–40% fail diet alone.
- Insulin requirements: 0.7–0.8 units/kg/day in the first trimester, 0.8–1.0 in the second, 0.9–1.2 in the third; higher with multifetal gestation, steroids, or betamimetics. Divide one third at each meal, with half as lispro or aspart and half as glargine in the evening.
- Glyburide: start 2.5 mg each morning, maximum 20 mg/day; 10–20% do not reach euglycemia, especially with obesity.
- Metformin: about one third fail; compared with insulin it causes more preterm birth but less gestational hypertension and less maternal and neonatal hypoglycemia; exposed infants weigh about 1 lb more by 18–24 months. Add metformin when the insulin dose is ≥1.12 IU/kg.
- Women who are obese, have high fasting glucose, or need drugs before 24 weeks are better served by insulin, alone or with metformin.
- Intrapartum: 70–120 mg/dL; hourly glucose if on insulin, every 4 hours if diet-controlled.
- Long-term: 30% develop metabolic syndrome; diabetes risk is 10-fold, about 20% at 10 years, 30% at 20 years, 40% at 30 years, 50% at 40 years, and 60% at 50 years.
- A Mediterranean lifestyle intervention reduced glucose disorders within 3 years (42.8% versus 56.8%) in women with normal postpartum testing.

## Anti-patterns
- Routine dietary supplements to prevent GDM: not recommended.
- Glyburide as first-line oral agent: inferior to insulin and to metformin for macrosomia and neonatal hypoglycemia.
- Assuming metformin monotherapy will suffice: one third fail, and obese women with high fasting glucose fail more often.
- Continuing metformin through screening without consideration: it may mask GDM; consider stopping 2 days before testing (half-life 6.2 hours).
- Delivering before 39 weeks without a true indication: perinatal mortality data are insufficient and NICU admission rises.
- Relying on a positive fetal lung maturity test: it does not preclude neonatal morbidity.
- Skipping postpartum testing: about 30% have metabolic syndrome and 10-fold later diabetes risk.
- Expecting equal benefit from a low-glycemic-index diet: larger trials show no benefit over other healthy diets.

## Key Takeaways
1. Screen at 24–28 weeks, and earlier if risk factors are present.
2. Treat to fasting ≤95 mg/dL and 2-hour <120 mg/dL; add insulin if 1–2 values are above limits over 2 weeks.
3. Use insulin first; use metformin over glyburide if an oral agent is needed.
4. Deliver A2GDM at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks, by cesarean if estimated fetal weight >4500 g.
5. Test for diabetes at 4–12 weeks postpartum and every 1–3 years thereafter, and promote breastfeeding.
