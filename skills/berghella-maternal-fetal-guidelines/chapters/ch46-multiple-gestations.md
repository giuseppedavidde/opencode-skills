# Chapter 46: Multiple Gestations

## Core Idea
Chorionicity, determined by first-trimester ultrasound, drives every subsequent management decision, because monochorionicity carries twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), acardiac twinning, and a much worse outcome after single fetal death. Preterm birth is the dominant cause of the excess morbidity and mortality of multiples, and most interventions intended to prevent it — bed rest, pessary, prophylactic tocolysis, home uterine monitoring, history- or ultrasound-indicated cerclage — do not work. What does work: vaginal progesterone for a short cervix in twins, physical exam-indicated cerclage for mid-trimester dilation, low-dose aspirin for preeclampsia, and delivery at a chorionicity-specific gestational age.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Determine chorionicity and amnionicity by early ultrasound, preferably in the first trimester. This is "of paramount importance."
- Offer multifetal pregnancy reduction to all patients with triplets or higher-order multiples. Source: Cochrane review of nonrandomized trials.
- Consider transvaginal cervical length screening at 18–23 weeks in twins; vaginal progesterone reduces preterm birth when cervical length is ≤25 mm before 24 weeks [meta-analysis of RCTs].
- Recommend physical exam-indicated cerclage for diamniotic twins with cervical dilation about 1–4 cm before 24 weeks. Source: small RCT showing 50% reduction in very early preterm birth and 78% reduction in perinatal mortality.
- Give low-dose aspirin 81 mg daily from 12–28 weeks (optimally before 16 weeks) until delivery. Source: ACOG and SMFM.
- Perform growth and amniotic fluid ultrasound after 20 weeks about every 4 weeks; every 3 weeks if discordance or FGR.
- Do not mandate immediate delivery for a single fetal death: maternal DIC risk is probably <2%, and in monochorionic pregnancies the greatest harm to the survivor has already occurred.
- Do not use routine antepartum testing in uncomplicated multiples; test for the same indications as in singletons.
- Deliver by chorionicity (Table 46.3 in the source): uncomplicated DC/DA at 37 0/7–37 6/7 weeks; uncomplicated MC/DA at 35 0/7–36 6/7 weeks; MA/MC at 32 0/7–34 6/7 weeks; DC/DA with one growth-restricted twin and normal umbilical artery Doppler at 36 0/7–36 6/7; abnormal forward flow at 34 0/7–34 6/7; absent end-diastolic flow at 32 0/7–33 6/7; reversed flow at 30 0/7–31 6/7; DC/DA with maternal comorbidity at 32 0/7–34 6/7.
- Deliver multiples with preterm labor or PPROM if ≥34 weeks, or with chorioamnionitis or non-reassuring testing; before 34 weeks manage by cervical length.
- Give antenatal corticosteroids at 24 to 33 6/7 weeks when delivery is expected within 7 days: betamethasone 12 mg every 24 hours ×2, or dexamethasone 6 mg every 12 hours ×4. Source: ACOG.
- Offer magnesium sulfate for neuroprotection at 23 0/7–31 6/7 weeks.
- Treat TTTS stages II–IV with fetoscopic laser coagulation between 16 and 25 6/7 weeks; manage stage I expectantly with at least weekly surveillance.
- Perform cesarean delivery for monoamniotic twins at 32 0/7–34 6/7 weeks, and planned cesarean for triplets and higher-order multiples.
- For twins at ≥32 0/7 weeks with the first twin cephalic, there is no benefit to planned cesarean; attempt vaginal delivery when estimated fetal weight (EFW) >1500 g with experienced staff and anesthesia available.

## Key Concepts
- Monozygotic twinning occurs at a constant 4 per 1000; dizygotic rates vary with race (low in Asians, high in blacks), age, parity, and maternal family history. More than 50% of multiples in developed countries result from assisted reproduction; the United States twin rate was 32.6 per 1000 births in 2018.
- First-trimester chorionicity: two placentas or different fetal sex means DC/DA in 30–40%; otherwise the twin-peak (lambda or delta) sign indicates DC/DA and the T-sign indicates MC/DA. A membrane >2 mm with three or four layers is dichorionic; a thin two-layer membrane is monochorionic.
- Early loss: 20–50% of twins, 53% of triplets, 65% of quadruplets lose at least one sac; serum screening or cell-free DNA after a vanishing twin risks an inaccurate result. The Down syndrome risk of a 35-year-old singleton is reached at about age 31–33 in twins and 28 in triplets.
- Discordance is a 20–25% EFW difference; about 14% of DCDA twins have 20% discordance. FGR (EFW or abdominal circumference <10%), not discordance, predicts adverse outcome: mortality or morbidity 20% in SGA-discordant versus 6% in AGA-discordant twins.
- Single fetal demise occurs in up to 5% of twins and 17% of triplets in the second or third trimester. Monochorionic versus dichorionic: co-twin death 41% versus 22.4% (OR 2.06), preterm birth 58.5% versus 53.7% (OR 1.42), abnormal cranial imaging 43% versus 21.2% (OR 5.41), neonatal death 27.9% versus 21.2% (OR 1.95), neurodevelopmental comorbidity 28.5% versus 10% (OR 3.06).
- Preterm birth: 20.5% of United States twins deliver before 34 weeks versus 82.6% of quadruplets. Mean birth weight 3296/2336/1660/1291 g and gestational age 38.7/35.3/31.9/29.5 weeks for singleton/twin/triplet/quadruplet; delivery <34 weeks 2.1/19.5/63.1/82.6%; very low birth weight 1.09/9.07/33.60/50.44%; infant mortality 5.36/23.62/52.49/96.29 per 1000. IVF twins deliver before 32 weeks more often than spontaneous twins (OR 1.52, 95% CI 1.18–1.97).
- Preeclampsia: 8% in twins, 10% in triplets, 12% in quadruplets, often atypical; assisted-reproduction multiples have a 2.1 relative risk. Abruption 1.2% in twins and 1.6% in triplets. Thrombocytopenia affects up to one third of triplets. Acute fatty liver occurs in up to 7% of triplets versus 1 in 10,000 singletons. Gestational diabetes 22% in triplets.
- Weight gain (IOM): normal BMI 16.8–24.5 kg (37–54 lb), overweight 14.1–22.7 kg (31–50 lb), obese 11.3–19.1 kg (25–42 lb). Calories 300 kcal above singleton intake (600 above non-pregnant), or 40–45 kcal/kg/day. Folic acid 1 mg/day, iron 60 mg/day.
- Screening: nuchal translucency plus maternal age detects 75–85% of trisomy 21 and 66.7% of trisomy 18 at a 5% false-positive rate. First-trimester combined testing in twins has pooled sensitivity 0.893 and specificity 0.946. Cell-free DNA detects trisomy 21 with 98.2% detection and 0.05% false-positive rate; performance for trisomies 13 and 18 is undetermined. MSAFP at 4.5 MoM detects 50–85% of neural tube defects with 5% false positives.
- Amniocentesis loss resembles singletons. Inject indigo carmine or Evans blue into the first sac; never methylene blue (hemolytic anemia, small bowel atresia, demise). In monochorionic gestation sample one sac.
- Cervical length ≤20 mm raises the pretest probability of birth before 32 weeks from 6.8% to 42.4%; >20 mm lowers it to 4.5%. With regular painful contractions at 24–36 weeks, probability of delivery within 7 days by length: >25 mm 0%, 21–25 mm 7%, 16–20 mm 21%, 11–15 mm 29%, 6–10 mm 46%, 1–5 mm 80%.
- Vaginal progesterone for a mid-trimester cervix <25 mm reduces birth before 30 to before 35 weeks, neonatal mortality, and some morbidity; in higher-order multiples it increased mid-trimester fetal loss.
- Prophylactic bed rest increases preterm birth <34 weeks by 84% in uncomplicated twins. Pessary, home uterine activity monitoring, and prophylactic tocolysis have no benefit. Tocolytics in multiples carry a higher risk of pulmonary edema. In PPROM before 34 weeks, tocolysis increases chorioamnionitis without infant benefit.
- TTTS: about 10% of MC/DA pregnancies (1 in 2500). Diagnosis requires maximum vertical pocket <2 cm in one sac and >8 cm in the other. Stage I: >75% regress or remain stable, 86% perinatal survival, manage expectantly with weekly checks. Untreated survival with diagnosis before 26 weeks is 30%. Stage V: 10% co-twin death risk and 10–30% neurologic complication risk. Amnioreduction and septostomy are alternatives when laser is unavailable.
- Monoamniotic twins: 1 in 10,000 pregnancies, up to 17% of IVF multiples with zona manipulation. Fetal loss about 6% overall: 4.3% at 24–30 weeks, 1.0% at 31–32 weeks, 2.2% at 33–34 weeks; 3% with mainly inpatient versus 7% with outpatient management.
- Acardiac (TRAP) twin: 1% of monochorionic twins; pump twin death in 35–50%; intervene when the acardiac EFW exceeds 70% of the pump twin.
- Conjoined twins: 1 in 50,000 to 1 in 10,000 births; 18% survive. Plan cesarean at term.
- Triplets: planned cesarean; attempted vaginal delivery succeeds in 16.7%, with maternal transfusion 20.8% versus 3.6% and neonatal ventilation 26.4% versus 7.7%.
- Delayed interval delivery: only about 25% of second-trimester multiple deliveries are candidates; gain 2–5 weeks, less than 2 weeks after 28 weeks; avoid with monochorionic gestation, abruption, preeclampsia, chorioamnionitis, or need for cesarean.

## Anti-patterns
- Managing multiples without documented chorionicity: every risk estimate and delivery date depends on it.
- Using bed rest to prevent preterm birth in twins: it increases preterm birth <34 weeks by 84%.
- Placing a history- or ultrasound-indicated cerclage in twins expecting benefit.
- Using a pessary, home uterine monitoring, or prophylactic tocolysis in twins.
- Giving vaginal progesterone to higher-order multiples: increased mid-trimester loss.
- Delivering a monochorionic twin pregnancy immediately after a co-twin death: damage has already occurred and prematurity adds harm.
- Injecting potassium chloride for selective termination in a monochorionic pregnancy: it crosses anastomoses and kills both fetuses.
- Using methylene blue for amniocentesis in multiples.
- Judging twins by singleton growth charts after 28–32 weeks.
- Relying on discordance rather than FGR to predict outcome, or testing lung maturity to set delivery timing.

## Key Takeaways
1. Establish chorionicity in the first trimester; it sets surveillance, TTTS risk, and delivery timing.
2. Aspirin 81 mg from 12–28 weeks for preeclampsia prevention in all multiples.
3. Screen cervical length at 18–23 weeks; give vaginal progesterone if ≤25 mm; cerclage only for physical exam-indicated dilation 1–4 cm before 24 weeks.
4. Deliver uncomplicated DC/DA twins at 37 0/7–37 6/7 weeks and MC/DA twins at 35 0/7–36 6/7 weeks; monoamniotic twins by cesarean at 32 0/7–34 6/7 weeks.
5. Survey monochorionic twins with maximum vertical pocket every 2 weeks from 16 weeks; refer stages II–IV TTTS for laser at 16–25 6/7 weeks.
6. A single fetal death does not mandate delivery; manage by chorionicity and gestational age.
7. Trial of labor is appropriate for cephalic/cephalic twins ≥32 weeks and >1500 g; plan cesarean for triplets.
