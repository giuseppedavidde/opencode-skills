# Chapter 36: Chlamydia

## Core Idea
Chlamydia trachomatis genital infection is asymptomatic in 70–90% of women and is an independent risk factor for preterm premature rupture of membranes, preterm birth, low birth weight, endometritis, and reduced perinatal survival. Nucleic acid amplification testing is the gold standard, and a positive screen should be confirmed by a second NAAT when the positive predictive value is low. Azithromycin 1 g orally as a single dose is the preferred treatment in pregnancy. A test of cure at least 3 weeks after treatment is recommended in pregnancy, and partners must be treated.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Screen all pregnant women under 25, and women 25 or older with risk factors, for chlamydia — Grade [Guideline] (CDC 2015 treatment guidelines); ACOG and the American Academy of Pediatrics recommend universal first-trimester screening.
- Repeat screening in the third trimester for women under 25 or with continued risk factors — Grade [Guideline].
- Treat with azithromycin 1 g orally as a single dose — highest efficacy, highest compliance, and fewest side effects in pregnancy — Grade [II-2] (multiple comparative trials and randomized trials).
- Use amoxicillin 500 mg orally three times daily for 7 days as the alternate regimen — Grade [II-2] (two randomized trials showing efficacy in pregnancy).
- Consider clindamycin if azithromycin and amoxicillin are contraindicated or not tolerated — Grade [II-2].
- Do not use doxycycline in pregnancy — risk of permanent fetal tooth discoloration.
- Perform a test of cure at least 3 weeks after treatment in pregnancy, and repeat testing in the third trimester if the positive test was earlier in pregnancy — Grade [Guideline].
- Test for Neisseria gonorrhoeae, syphilis, HIV, and hepatitis B — Grade [Guideline].
- Offer partner notification and patient-delivered partner therapy — Grade [I] (randomized trials and meta-analyses, including 26 RCTs with over 17,000 participants).
- Do not offer expedited partner therapy when intimate partner violence is suspected — Grade [Review; III].

## Key Concepts
- Prevalence in pregnancy in the United States ranges from 2.8% to 19%, highest in urban populations. Global sample rates: Papua New Guinea 26%, India 17%, Cape Verde 13%, Iceland 8%, Tanzania 6%, Thailand 5.7%, United States 5%, Italy 2.7%, Brazil 2.1%.
- The age-specific rate is highest at 15–24 years. The rate in African American females in the United States is five times that in White females.
- The incubation period is 7–21 days. Infection confers little and short-lived protection against reinfection.
- About 70–90% of women with cervical or urethral infection are asymptomatic.
- Transmission: 22–44% of infants born to infected women develop neonatal conjunctivitis; 11–20% develop chlamydial pneumonia. United States neonatal chlamydial conjunctivitis rates were stable at 1.4–2.3 per 100,000 live births from 2014 to 2018.
- Complications: untreated genital chlamydia is an independent risk factor for preterm premature rupture of membranes, preterm birth, low birth weight, endometritis, and decreased perinatal survival compared with treated women.
- Pelvic inflammatory disease and perihepatitis are uncommon after the first trimester.
- Clinical syndromes: mucopurulent cervicitis with discharge, cervical edema and friability, dysuria with urethritis, and low abdominal pain with upper tract infection. Proctitis from serovars D–K causes anal pruritus and mucous discharge; lymphogranuloma venereum strains cause rectal pain, tenesmus, bleeding, fever, and can form rectovaginal fistulas.
- Diagnosis: NAAT (ligase chain reaction or polymerase chain reaction) confirmed by a second NAAT achieves the highest predictive accuracy and is the gold standard. Anti-chlamydia IgG is present in 30–60% of sexually active adults and does not indicate active infection.
- Predictive value example: at 3% prevalence, a test with 80% sensitivity and 99% specificity has a positive predictive value of 70%. The CDC recommends confirming positive screens when positive predictive value is below 90%.
- Do not use a non-NAAT as the confirmatory test after a NAAT, because sensitivity is lower; culture is the exception when an isolate is needed.
- Vaginal swabs perform equivalently to cervical swabs. Patient-collected vaginal swabs have 92% sensitivity and 98% specificity compared with clinician-collected specimens, though only one small trial addressed pregnancy.
- Treatment comparison: doxycycline had a 100% cure rate versus 97% for azithromycin, but noninferiority of azithromycin was not established; doxycycline remains contraindicated in pregnancy.
- Amoxicillin is less expensive than azithromycin, but thrice-daily dosing for 7 days lowers compliance.
- Test of cure must use a NAAT at least 3 weeks after treatment, because nonviable organisms can persist and cause a false-positive result.
- Partner management: inform all partners from the 2 months before diagnosis. Abstain from unprotected intercourse for 7 days after both partners complete treatment.
- Treatment of lymphogranuloma venereum and chlamydial conjunctivitis has not been studied in pregnancy; recommendations extrapolate from non-pregnant adults.
- Concurrent treatment for gonorrhea is not indicated unless gonorrhea testing is positive.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating with doxycycline in pregnancy — permanent discoloration of developing fetal teeth.
- Using a non-NAAT to confirm a positive NAAT — lower sensitivity; culture only when an isolate is required.
- Performing test of cure earlier than 3 weeks — nonviable organisms give a false-positive NAAT.
- Skipping test of cure in pregnancy — recommended after treatment.
- Treating the woman without treating partners — reinfection rates stay high.
- Using expedited partner therapy where intimate partner violence is suspected — disclosure may endanger the patient.
- Screening only women with symptoms — 70–90% are asymptomatic.
- Assuming a positive IgG indicates active infection — seroprevalence is 30–60% in sexually active adults.
- Adding gonorrhea treatment without a positive test.

## Key Takeaways
1. Screen all pregnant women under 25 and older women with risk factors; repeat in the third trimester if risk persists.
2. Diagnose with NAAT; confirm with a second NAAT when positive predictive value is below 90%.
3. Treat with azithromycin 1 g orally once; use amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for 7 days if azithromycin is unavailable or not tolerated.
4. Perform test of cure by NAAT at least 3 weeks after treatment.
5. Treat partners from the prior 2 months, offer patient-delivered partner therapy, and advise 7 days of abstinence after both complete treatment.
6. Test for gonorrhea, syphilis, HIV, and hepatitis B.
