# Chapter 1: Hypertensive Disorders

## Core Idea
Chronic hypertension (CHTN) affects 1–5% of pregnancies and is defined as a history of hypertension or BP ≥140/90 before 20 weeks. The chapter separates CHTN, gestational hypertension (GHTN), pre-eclampsia with and without severe features, superimposed pre-eclampsia, HELLP syndrome, and eclampsia, with explicit thresholds for each. Two interventions change outcomes: low-dose aspirin started before 16 weeks in women at risk, and magnesium sulfate for eclampsia prophylaxis in pre-eclampsia with severe features. Delivery is the only cure; timing is 37 0/7 weeks for GHTN and pre-eclampsia without severe features, 34 0/7 weeks for severe features, and immediate for maternal or fetal indications.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Give low-dose aspirin 75–150 mg/day to women with risk factors for pre-eclampsia; it reduces pre-eclampsia by 17%, preterm birth <37 weeks by 8%, SGA by 10%, and perinatal death by 14%. *(Meta-analysis; 59 RCTs, n = 37,560; I)*
- Start aspirin before 16 weeks when risk is high: this is associated with 90% reduction in severe pre-eclampsia, 69% reduction in gestational hypertension, and 49% reduction in IUGR. *(Meta-analysis of 34 RCTs)*
- Give magnesium sulfate in pre-eclampsia with severe features: loading dose 4–6 g IV, then 1–2 g/hour, for at least active labor and 12–24 hours postpartum. *(Meta-analysis; 15 RCTs, n = 11,444; I)*
- Magnesium reduces eclampsia by 59%, abruption by 36%, and maternal death by 46% (not statistically significant); NNT is 400 for mild pre-eclampsia, 71 for severe pre-eclampsia, and 36 with CNS symptoms. *(Review; III)*
- Deliver at 37 0/7 weeks for GHTN without severe BP and for pre-eclampsia without severe features: induction reduces a composite maternal outcome by 29% versus expectant management. *(RCT, n = 756; I)*
- Deliver at 34 0/7 weeks, or at diagnosis, for GHTN with severe BP and for pre-eclampsia with severe features.
- Treat severe hypertension (SBP ≥160 and/or DBP ≥110) with antihypertensives; target 140–150 mmHg systolic and about 90 mmHg diastolic.
- Do not give magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis in GHTN.
- Give calcium 1.5–2 g/day where baseline calcium intake is low or pre-eclampsia risk is high: 35% reduction in high blood pressure and 55% reduction in pre-eclampsia.
- Do not use vitamin C 1000 mg/day plus vitamin E 400 IU/day for prevention; no reduction in pre-eclampsia was shown.

## Key Concepts
- Severe CHTN: SBP ≥160 mmHg or DBP ≥110 mmHg, on two measurements 4 hours apart.
- High-risk CHTN: secondary hypertension, target organ damage, age >40, previous pregnancy loss, SBP ≥180, DBP ≥110, obesity, diabetes.
- GHTN: BP ≥140/90 on two occasions 6 hours apart after 20 weeks, without proteinuria or other pre-eclampsia features. Severe GHTN: ≥160/110 mmHg.
- Pre-eclampsia: BP ≥140/90 on two occasions 6 hours but not >7 days apart plus proteinuria ≥300 mg/24 h, or protein/creatinine ratio ≥0.3, after 20 weeks. Dipstick >1+ only if other methods are unavailable.
- Pre-eclampsia without proteinuria is diagnosed with new hypertension plus platelets <100,000/mm³, creatinine ≥1.1 mg/dL or doubling, AST/ALT twice normal, pulmonary edema, or persistent cerebral/visual symptoms.
- Severe features: BP ≥160/110 mmHg on two occasions ≥4 hours apart at bed rest; platelets <100,000/mm³; creatinine ≥1.1 mg/dL or doubling; AST/ALT twice normal; persistent epigastric or right upper quadrant pain; pulmonary edema; persistent headache or visual/cerebral disturbance.
- HELLP (Tennessee): LDH >600 IU/L or bilirubin ≥1.2 mg/dL with abnormal smear; AST or ALT twice normal; platelets <100,000/mm³. Mississippi class 1: platelets ≤50,000; class 2: >50,000 to ≤100,000; class 3: >100,000 to ≤150,000.
- Acute severe hypertension: labetalol 20 mg IV bolus, then 40, 80, 80 mg every 10 minutes (maximum 220 mg); hydralazine 5–10 mg IV/IM every 20 minutes (maximum 30 mg); nifedipine 10–20 mg orally, repeat in 30 minutes; sodium nitroprusside 0.25 µg/kg/min to maximum 5 µg/kg/min.
- Oral maintenance: labetalol 100 mg twice daily to maximum 2400 mg/day; nifedipine 10 mg twice daily or 30 mg XL daily to maximum 120 mg/day; methyldopa 250 mg two to three times daily to maximum 2 g/day; hydrochlorothiazide 12.5 mg twice daily to maximum 50 mg/day.
- Magnesium toxicity: loss of patellar reflexes at 8.5–12 mg/dL, respiratory depression at 12–16 mg/dL, altered conduction >18 mg/dL, cardiac arrest >30 mg/dL.
- Regional anesthesia is contraindicated with coagulopathy or platelets <75,000/mm³.
- CHTN delivery: 38 0/7–39 6/7 weeks if off medication, from 37 0/7 if on medication, from 36 0/7 if frequent dose adjustment. No amniocentesis for lung maturity.
- Superimposed pre-eclampsia: preterm birth 50–60%, FGR 15%, abruption 2–5%, perinatal death 5%.
- GHTN before 30 weeks or severe GHTN: 50% progression to pre-eclampsia, usually within 1–3 weeks.

## Anti-patterns
- ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers: contraindicated in pregnancy — fetal malformation, FGR, oligohydramnios, neonatal renal failure and death; postpartum they may cause oliguria and anuria.
- Atenolol and other beta-blockers: associated with 38% increase in SGA and threefold increase in birth weight <5th percentile.
- Labetalol in asthma, heart disease, or congestive heart failure: avoid due to bronchoconstriction and cardiac effects.
- Diuretics in pre-eclampsia or FGR: avoid because uteroplacental perfusion is already reduced.
- Severe proteinuria >5 g: no longer a severe feature; massive proteinuria >10 g/24 h alone should not trigger delivery.
- FGR: removed as a criterion of severe features; it is managed the same with or without pre-eclampsia.
- The "30–15 rule" and edema: eliminated as diagnostic criteria.
- Invasive hemodynamic monitoring (Swan–Ganz): usually unnecessary; associated with complications and no outcome improvement.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use 140/90 as the diagnostic threshold in pregnancy; do not apply the non-pregnant ACC/AHA stage 1 category.
2. Start aspirin by 16 weeks in women with high-risk factors and continue daily until delivery.
3. Treat only severe-range BP in pregnancy, to a target of 140–150/90; lower the threshold when end-organ damage or diabetes is present.
4. Give magnesium sulfate to every woman with pre-eclampsia with severe features, and continue it through cesarean delivery.
5. Deliver pre-eclampsia without severe features at 37 0/7 weeks and with severe features at 34 0/7 weeks; expectant management beyond 34 weeks is not recommended.
6. Screen for postpartum hypertension for 72 hours and again at 7–10 days, because 28% of HELLP presents postpartum.
