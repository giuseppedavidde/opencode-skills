# Chapter 10: Intrahepatic Cholestasis of Pregnancy

## Core Idea
Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy (ICP) is unexplained pruritus in the second or third trimester with total bile acids >10 µmol/L, often with elevated alkaline phosphatase and aminotransferases, resolving spontaneously within 4 weeks after delivery. It is the most common liver disease of pregnancy, affecting 0.2–2% worldwide, and recurs in about 60% of subsequent pregnancies. Severity and management are driven by the peak bile acid level: 11–39 µmol/L is mild, 40–99 is moderate, and ≥100 is severe. Stillbirth exceeds the background rate only at or above 100 µmol/L, where it reaches 3.4% versus 0.3–0.4%. Ursodeoxycholic acid is the treatment of choice, mainly for pruritus, and delivery timing is stratified by peak bile acids.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Diagnose ICP with pruritus plus total bile acids >10 µmol/L after excluding hepatitis A, B, and C, Epstein-Barr virus, cytomegalovirus, autoimmune liver disease, gallstones, hepatobiliary tumors, pre-eclampsia, HELLP, and acute fatty liver.
- Measure bile acids fasting; repeat every 1–2 weeks if pruritus persists with normal biochemistry.
- Classify by peak bile acid level and manage accordingly: mild 11–39, moderate 40–99, severe ≥100 µmol/L.
- Repeat total bile acids serially, about weekly in the third trimester, because levels change with advancing gestation; use the peak value for prognosis.
- Start ursodeoxycholic acid 10–25 mg/kg/day in two divided doses, usually 300–500 mg twice daily. *(FDA category B)*
- Consider delivery at 38 0/7–38 6/7 weeks when peak bile acids are <40 µmol/L; at 37 0/7–37 6/7 weeks when 40–99 µmol/L; and by 35 0/7–36 6/7 weeks when ≥100 µmol/L.
- Offer induction after 37 0/7 weeks for bile acids <100 µmol/L, with counseling about the perinatal morbidity of early-term delivery. *(RCOG)*
- Offer antepartum testing with weekly non-stress tests from diagnosis, usually at or after 32 weeks, plus daily kick counts, while recognizing that no method prevents fetal death.
- Offer continuous fetal monitoring in labor. *(Expert opinion)*
- Give vitamin K 10 mg orally daily from diagnosis of ICP or from 34 weeks to reduce postpartum hemorrhage; use menadiol sodium phosphate 5–10 mg daily if prothrombin time is prolonged. Evidence is insufficient for a strong recommendation.
- Do not use dexamethasone as first-line therapy or outside a trial.

## Key Concepts
- Pruritus starts after 30 weeks in 80–86% of cases, is worse at night, and is often worst on the palms and soles; it resolves within 48 hours of delivery.
- Jaundice occurs in 14–25%, usually 1–4 weeks after pruritus, with mildly elevated conjugated bilirubin; bilirubin rarely exceeds 6 mg/dL.
- Transaminases are elevated in about 60%, gamma-glutamyl transferase in 30%, and bilirubin in about 25%.
- Cholelithiasis is present in 10%; right upper quadrant ultrasound is used when biliary obstruction is suspected.
- Up to 50% of pregnant women report pruritus, but few have elevated bile acids; bile acids may be normal at first and rise about 3 weeks after symptoms begin.
- Complications without treatment: preterm birth 15–44%, meconium 25–45%, non-reassuring fetal heart testing 5–15%, fetal death 2–10%, neonatal death 1–2%, postpartum hemorrhage 20–22%.
- Spontaneous preterm birth occurs mostly at 32–36 weeks and is higher (8–9%) with bile acids ≥40 µmol/L than below 40 (6–7%).
- Fetal death occurs mostly at or after 36 weeks; fetal compromise rises 1–2% for each additional 1 µmol/L of bile acid, with a significant increase at ≥40 µmol/L.
- Bile acids ≥40 µmol/L raise meconium-stained fluid; ≥100 µmol/L raise stillbirth.
- About 20% of ICP cases have bile acids ≥40 µmol/L.
- Ursodeoxycholic acid is a hydrophilic bile acid that inhibits intestinal absorption of other bile acids, improves hepatocyte excretion, stabilizes membranes, and dilutes toxic bile acids; it becomes the dominant bile acid measured after treatment.
- UDCA side effects are headache, diarrhea, and constipation, each under 25%. A Cochrane meta-analysis found a probable reduction in pruritus of small size, possibly below the minimum clinically worthwhile effect, and unclear evidence for fetal outcomes.
- S-adenosylmethionine 500 mg orally twice daily or 800–900 mg IV daily is less effective than UDCA and not commonly used alone.
- Cholestyramine 8 g daily reduces pruritus less than UDCA, has more adverse effects, and binds fat-soluble vitamins A, D, E, and K; it must be separated from other medications. *(FDA category C)*
- Hydroxyzine 25–100 mg every 6 hours may improve tolerance of itching through sedation but has no RCT evidence.
- Risk factors: age over 35, prior ICP, family history, high serum copper, low selenium and zinc, vitamin D deficiency, hepatitis C, and genetic susceptibility; seasonal winter clustering occurs in some countries.

## Anti-patterns
- Using the old terms "benign jaundice of pregnancy" or "idiopathic jaundice of pregnancy": they should no longer be used.
- Dexamethasone as first-line treatment: inferior to UDCA and reserved for trials or explicit discussion.
- Relying on antihistamines for pruritus: they mainly sedate.
- Assuming a reactive non-stress test excludes imminent fetal death: deaths within 24 hours of a reactive test are reported.
- Basing delivery timing on transaminases or bilirubin: bile acids determine management.
- Early delivery for all ICP: preterm morbidity outweighs benefit when bile acids are <40 µmol/L.
- Combining cholestyramine with other drugs at the same time: absorption is impaired.
- Expecting UDCA to prevent stillbirth: it has not been shown to prevent adverse fetal outcomes.
- Missing vitamin K deficiency: subclinical steatorrhea can prolong prothrombin time and cause postpartum hemorrhage.

## Key Takeaways
1. Confirm ICP with fasting bile acids >10 µmol/L and resolution after delivery, and exclude other liver disease.
2. Classify severity by peak bile acid level and repeat levels weekly in the third trimester.
3. Treat pruritus with UDCA 10–25 mg/kg/day in two doses.
4. Time delivery at 38 0/7–38 6/7 weeks for <40 µmol/L, 37 0/7–37 6/7 for 40–99, and 35 0/7–36 6/7 for ≥100.
5. Give vitamin K and plan postpartum hemorrhage precautions; counsel that recurrence risk is about 60%.
