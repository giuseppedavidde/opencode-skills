# Chapter 55: Hemolytic Disease of the Fetus and Newborn

## Core Idea
Red cell alloimmunization is the formation of maternal antibodies to fetal red cell antigens; IgG antibodies cross the placenta and cause hemolysis, anemia, hydrops, and fetal death. Anti-D immune globulin has reduced anti-D alloimmunization to less than 0.2% when given both antepartum and postpartum, but anti-D and Kell remain the commonest causes of hemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn (HDFN). Management is a sequence: genotype the father and fetus, follow titers to the critical value, switch to middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity (MCA-PSV), and perform fetal blood sampling (FBS) with intrauterine transfusion (IUT) when the MCA-PSV is at or above 1.5 multiples of the median (MoM).

## Recommendations and Grades
- Give anti-D immune globulin 300 µg (1 µg = 5 IU) at 28 weeks, and a second identical dose within 72 hours after birth if the neonate is Rh(D) positive; 300 µg covers exposure to 30 mL of Rh(D)-positive fetal whole blood. Alternative regimens such as 100 µg at 28 and 34 weeks exist. Screening for excessive fetomaternal hemorrhage is recommended for all Rh(D)-negative women giving birth to an Rh(D)-positive infant, with dosing adjusted to the result. Grade: ACOG recommendation, not otherwise graded.
- Give anti-D prophylaxis to women with a weak or partial D phenotype (formerly Du). Grade: ACOG recommendation.
- Use a critical titer of 16 for Rh(D) and non-Rh(D) alloimmunization, and 4 for Kell alloimmunization. Most United States laboratories follow American Association of Blood Banks (AABB) recommendations that consider 16 to 32 as critical. Grade: not formally graded; the text notes the ideal threshold has been challenged and that 16 and 32 should continue in use.
- Start MCA-PSV surveillance when a critical titer is reached, or immediately in a previously affected pregnancy, in a Kell-sensitized pregnancy, or when paternity or fetal antigen status is unknown. In a first affected pregnancy start at 18–20 weeks; in subsequent affected pregnancies start at 16–18 weeks. Grade: not stated.
- Perform FBS when MCA-PSV is consistently ≥1.5 MoM, and transfuse for fetal hemoglobin below the 5th percentile (2 SD below the mean for gestational age) or a hematocrit below 30%. Grade: not stated.
- Deliver at 37–38 weeks in alloimmunized pregnancies (SMFM), and at 39 weeks when titers have not reached the critical value and there is no concern for severe anemia. Grade as stated: SMFM clinical guidance; recommendations are based mainly on expert opinion, with high-quality data lacking [115].
- Deliver at a unit able to provide neonatal transfusion, and anticipate neonatal exchange transfusion.

## Key Concepts
- Antibody grading by MCA-PSV (Table 55.2): mild anemia 1.29–1.49 MoM; moderate anemia 1.50–1.54 MoM; severe anemia ≥1.55 MoM. Median MCA-PSV values rise with gestation, from 23.2 cm/s at 18 weeks (1.00 MoM) to 64.4 cm/s at 40 weeks; the 1.50 MoM value is 34.8 cm/s at 18 weeks, 60.7 cm/s at 30 weeks, and 96.6 cm/s at 40 weeks.
- Haemoglobin grading (Table 55.3): normal range is 1.16–0.84 MoM (95th to 5th centile); mild anemia 0.84–0.66 MoM; moderate anemia 0.65–0.55 MoM; severe anemia <0.65 MoM. Median hemoglobin is 10.6 g/dL at 18 weeks and 13.8 g/dL at 40 weeks.
- MCA-PSV performance: sensitivity close to 100% (95% CI 0.86–1.0) with a 12% false-positive rate at 1.50 MoM in the original study [63]; a meta-analysis found 86% sensitivity and 71% specificity for severe anemia in untransfused fetuses [96]. The false-positive rate rises after 35 weeks and can be reduced by reading the trend. Compared with amniocentesis for ΔOD450, MCA-PSV reduces invasive tests by 70–80%.
- Technique: axial head section at the level of the sphenoid bones, Circle of Willis on color Doppler, sample 2–3 mm after the origin of the MCA from the internal carotid artery, at a 0° angle, without angle correction. The proximal MCA has the lowest intra- and interobserver variability.
- Follow-up intervals: using the last three MCA-PSV values, repeat in 2–3-week intervals until 34–35 weeks when the curve stays to the right of the mild anemia curve and MCA-PSV is <1.5 MoM; repeat in 2–3 days when the curve lies to the left of the mild anemia curve with MCA-PSV still <1.5 MoM.
- Liley and ΔOD450: serial amniocentesis with spectrophotometric bilirubin measurement (ΔOD450), introduced by Liley in 1961, has been replaced in clinical practice by MCA-PSV, which is non-invasive and performs better. The chapter does not present the Queenan curve; severity is graded by MCA-PSV and hemoglobin MoM instead. Maternal titers and ΔOD450 correlate poorly with fetal anemia in Kell disease.
- Transfusion thresholds and targets: transfuse for hemoglobin below the 5th percentile or hematocrit below 30%. At 18–24 weeks the target hematocrit should not exceed 25%, or a fourfold increase over the pretransfusion value. After 24 weeks the target hematocrit is 45% (40–50%); a second transfusion 48 hours after the first can reach this target. Hematocrits above 45–50% risk hyperviscosity.
- Volume calculation: (desired hematocrit − fetal hematocrit) / (donor hematocrit − desired hematocrit) × fetoplacental volume. Intraperitoneal volume: (gestational age in weeks − 20) × 10 mL.
- Donor blood: Rh D negative, CMV antibody negative, irradiated with 25 Gy, hemoglobin S negative, leukoreduced, less than 7 days old, washed and packed to a hematocrit of 75–85%. O positive blood may be needed when anti-c antibodies are present.
- Procedure risks (most recent cohort): 3.3% complications per fetus and 1.2% per procedure; fetal demise 1.8% per fetus and 0.6% per procedure; emergency cesarean 1.2%; preterm premature rupture of membranes 0.3%; infection 0.3%; fetal loss 17% when FBS is performed before 20 weeks. Routine fetal paralysis with vecuronium 0.1 mg/kg may prevent 80% of procedure-related loss. FBS is usually not performed after 35 weeks.
- Outcomes: perinatal survival consistently above 90%, and above 80% when hydrops is present. In 291 children after IUT followed a median of 8.2 years: survival 91%, cerebral palsy 2.1%, severe developmental delay 3.1%, bilateral deafness 1%, overall neurodevelopmental impairment 4.8%; hydrops independently associated with impairment (OR 11.2, 95% CI 1.7–92.7).
- After a first IUT, hemoglobin falls about 0.4 g/dL per day, or hematocrit 1% per day. The MCA-PSV threshold for severe anemia after a first transfusion is higher, above 1.69 MoM.
- Antibody titer practice: titers are repeated about every 4 weeks; serial titers are usually not done before 15 weeks except for anti-Kell. In a series of 590 anti-D pregnancies, 70% developed a critical titer, at a mean of 26 weeks and 3 days.
- Fetal genotyping: amniocyte PCR after 15 weeks is >95% accurate. CVS and transplacental amniocentesis are not advised because fetomaternal hemorrhage can worsen alloimmunization. Cell-free DNA for Rh genotyping: sensitivity 99.94%, specificity 97.74%, false-negative rate 0.03%.

## Anti-patterns
- Chasing titers in a previously affected pregnancy with a known antigen-positive fetus: titers are not informative and do not correlate with severity.
- Amniocentesis for ΔOD450 when MCA-PSV is available: it is invasive and less accurate.
- CVS or transplacental amniocentesis for genotyping in an alloimmunized woman: risk of boosting sensitization.
- Over-transfusing: hematocrit above 45–50% risks hyperviscosity; before 24 weeks the target must not exceed 25%.
- Basing delivery decisions on Doppler alone before 32–34 weeks without absent or reversed end-diastolic flow: the GRIT trial showed unnecessary prematurity.
- Relying on MCA-PSV accuracy after multiple transfusions without adjustment: accuracy falls with successive IUTs.

## Key Takeaways
- Confirm paternal zygosity and fetal antigen status before starting surveillance.
- Use titer 16 as critical (4 for Kell), then switch to MCA-PSV.
- Act at MCA-PSV ≥1.5 MoM: FBS, then transfuse if hemoglobin is below the 5th percentile or hematocrit below 30%.
- Target hematocrit 25% before 24 weeks and 45% (40–50%) after 24 weeks; use donor blood at 75–85% hematocrit.
- Quote procedure risk honestly: about 1.2% per procedure, higher before 20 weeks.
- Plan delivery at 37–38 weeks, or 39 weeks if the titer never reached the critical value.
