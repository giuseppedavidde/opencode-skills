# Chapter 58: Antepartum Testing

## Core Idea
The purpose of antepartum testing is to prevent stillbirth and, secondarily, neurological handicap, while avoiding the harm of unnecessary intervention. No randomized trial proves that testing reduces long-term neurological deficits, so most regimens are consensus rather than high-level evidence. Because single tests miss important sources of injury, multi-parameter testing correlates better with fetal condition than any single test. Test choice, start date, and interval should reflect the condition, its severity, gestational age, and the interventions available.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Do not rely on the non-stress test (NST) alone to monitor a high-risk fetus: in randomized data, NST results appear to have no significant effect on perinatal mortality or potentially preventable deaths, and NST is not adequate to exclude major sources of perinatal injury. Grade: meta-analysis of randomized trials, [I]; reported effect not significant.
- Do not use NST criteria derived from term fetuses before 32 weeks: up to 50% of NSTs at 24–28 weeks and 15% at 28–32 weeks are nonreactive, so accelerations of at least 10 bpm for at least 10 seconds are accepted below 32 weeks. A fetus below 32 weeks should not be delivered for a nonreactive NST alone, only for bradycardia or another non-reassuring tracing. Grade: national guideline endorsement.
- Perform umbilical artery (UA) Doppler in fetal growth restriction (FGR), not in normal pregnancies. UA Doppler reduces perinatal deaths (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.52–0.98; number needed to treat 203) with fewer inductions (RR 0.89) and fewer cesarean sections (RR 0.90). Grade as stated: randomized evidence.
- Deliver FGR fetuses with absent UA end-diastolic flow around 34 weeks and with reversed end-diastolic flow around 32 weeks. Grade: not stated.
- Use the full biophysical profile score (BPS) rather than shortened versions in high-risk fetuses with abnormal Doppler, preterm fetuses, postdate pregnancy, anomalies, multiple gestation, arrhythmia, infection, anemia, or diabetic macrosomia. Grade: not stated.
- Rate antenatal testing recommendations as "Level B or even C (i.e., consensus, expert opinion, but no clear evidence)," because no trial has conclusively proven that testing lowers long-term adverse neurological outcome. Grade as stated: Level B or C.
- Start general surveillance at 32–34 weeks for most at-risk fetuses, according to ACOG, and start earlier when viability thresholds, disease severity, or history indicate.

## Key Concepts
**Intervals and start dates (Table 58.9)**
- Chronic hypertension: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Gestational hypertension: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Pre-eclampsia: start at diagnosis, twice-weekly NST.
- Gestational diabetes managed by diet (GDMA1): no testing.
- Gestational diabetes on medication (GDMA2): start 32 weeks, weekly NST with maximum vertical pocket (MVP); at 36 weeks, twice-weekly NST.
- Pregestational diabetes: start 32 weeks, weekly NST; at 36 weeks, twice-weekly NST; if poorly controlled, consider twice weekly from 28 weeks.
- Fetal growth restriction: start at diagnosis, twice-weekly NST with Dopplers.
- Two-vessel cord: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Maternal age ≥35 years at delivery: start 36 weeks, weekly NST.
- Obesity: weekly NST (no start week given).
- Preterm premature rupture of membranes: start at diagnosis, inpatient management.
- Post dates: start 40 weeks, weekly NST; at 41 weeks, twice-weekly NST.
- Dichorionic/diamniotic twins: no testing. Monochorionic/diamniotic twins: start 32 weeks, weekly NST with Dopplers every 2 weeks. Monochorionic/monoamniotic twins: 24–28 weeks, consider hospitalization if not twice weekly, Dopplers every 2 weeks.
- Prior unexplained intrauterine fetal demise: start 1 week before the gestational age of the previous demise, but not before 28 weeks, weekly NST.
- SLE or renal disease: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Organ transplant: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Hypothyroidism or hyperthyroidism: start 32 weeks, weekly NST (for hypothyroidism only if poorly controlled).
- Cholestasis: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Maternal cardiac disease: individualize.
- Oligohydramnios (MVP <2 cm): start 32 weeks, weekly NST with MVP.
- Polyhydramnios (MVP >8 cm): start 28 weeks, weekly NST with MVP.
- Sickle cell disease: start 32 weeks, weekly NST.
- Fetal arrhythmia: start at diagnosis, weekly NST.
- Testing interval can rise to three times daily or continuous monitoring in FGR with the worst UA Doppler pattern.

**Test definitions**
- Reactive NST at ≥32 weeks: two or more accelerations of at least 15 bpm above baseline, lasting at least 15 seconds, within a 20-minute period. Negative predictive value against fetal demise within 7 days is 99.5–99.8%. False-positive reassurance against acidosis can be as high as 15% in FGR with abnormal placental resistance. Up to 85% of nonreactive NSTs are normal on the confirmatory test. Perform the NST semi-Fowler rather than supine.
- BPS: five components — fetal breathing, body or limb movement, tone, cardiotocogram, and amniotic fluid. A score of 8/8 is as reassuring as 10/10. Doing ultrasound parameters first means only 2.7% need an NST. Perinatal mortality within one week rises from <1/1000 for 10/10 or 8/8, to 89/1000 for 8/10 with oligohydramnios, 91/1000 for 4/10, 125/1000 for 2/10, and 600/1000 for 0/10.
- MVP ≥2 cm scores 2 for amniotic fluid; at least four discrete body or limb movements and one episode of breathing of more than 30 seconds are required for full scores.
- Contraction stress test (CST): at least three contractions in 10 minutes; decelerations with 50% or more of contractions make it positive. Negative predictive value 3–4 per 10,000, but high rates of equivocal and false-alarming results; at least 50% of women with a positive CST can safely continue for a week when BPS is used as backup.
- Vibroacoustic stimulation: reduces nonreactive CTG (RR 0.62, 95% CI 0.48–0.81) and shortens testing by about 7 minutes, but in modified BPS it produces a 67% false-alarm rate and 55% of fetuses with later heart-rate abnormalities had a reassuring result. Use cautiously before 32 weeks because of sound pressure up to 130 decibels and hearing concerns.
- Computerized CTG: short-term variability below 3 milliseconds correlates strongly with acidosis; lower perinatal mortality than traditional interpretation (9/1000 vs 4.2/1000, RR 0.20, 95% CI 0.04–0.88) without a difference in preventable deaths.
- Fetal movement counting: the "count to ten" method improves compliance, but trials show no significant reduction in stillbirth and an increase in preterm delivery, induction, and cesarean delivery; the 20% non-significant fall in perinatal mortality may be clinically significant.
- UA Doppler: the pulsatility index remains valid when end-diastolic flow is reversed; abnormal indices rise once the placenta is 60–70% compromised. Absent end-diastolic velocities predict stillbirth, preterm delivery, birthweight below the 10th percentile, and neonatal complications. Cerebroplacental ratio should not be used in isolation; there are no management trials.

## Anti-patterns
- "Testing early and testing often" as a blanket policy: it risks iatrogenic prematurity, which may be more dangerous than the condition being tested.
- Delivering a fetus below 32 weeks for a nonreactive NST alone.
- Using NST as the sole surveillance method in a high-risk pregnancy.
- Using the amniotic fluid index rather than MVP within the BPS.
- Shortening the BPS in high-risk groups where it has not been validated.
- Basing delivery on UA Doppler alone before 32–34 weeks when end-diastolic flow is not absent or reversed (GRIT trial).
- Routine UA Doppler in normal pregnancies.

## Key Takeaways
- Choose the test by condition: UA Doppler for FGR, BPS or NST plus MVP for most other indications.
- Start at 32 weeks for most maternal and placental conditions, 28 weeks for polyhydramnios, 36 weeks for advanced maternal age, at diagnosis for pre-eclampsia, FGR, PPROM, and arrhythmia, and 1 week before the prior demise (never before 28 weeks) after unexplained stillbirth.
- Use twice-weekly testing for pre-eclampsia, FGR, and pregestational diabetes from 36 weeks; weekly otherwise.
- Apply prematurity criteria below 32 weeks and never deliver for nonreactivity alone.
- Remember that glycaemic control, smoking cessation, and treatment of maternal disease contribute as much as testing.
