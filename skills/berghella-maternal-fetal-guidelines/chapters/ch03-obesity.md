# Chapter 3: Obesity

## Core Idea
Obesity, defined as BMI ≥30 kg/m², is the most common modifiable risk factor in obstetric practice and worsens nearly every maternal and perinatal outcome. The preconception visit is the highest-value encounter: record height and weight, calculate BMI, review the class-specific risks, and offer weight loss of 5–10%, which reduces obesity-related comorbidity. Antepartum care targets excessive gestational weight gain, fetal growth, pre-eclampsia, sleep apnea, and anesthesia planning; intrapartum care anticipates a longer, more difficult labor, higher cesarean rate, and higher anesthesia failure; postpartum care targets wound complications and venous thromboembolism. Bariatric surgery is reserved for BMI >40 kg/m², or >35 kg/m² with comorbidity, after lifestyle and drug therapy fail.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Record height and weight at every visit and calculate BMI; review the BMI category and its risks with the patient using patient-centered language.
- Offer preconception weight loss through diet, exercise, and behavior change, with pharmacotherapy or bariatric surgery if needed; a 5–10% loss reduces obesity-related comorbidity.
- Check BP with an appropriately sized cuff, fasting lipids (preconception), blood glucose and HbA1c, thyroid function, and overnight polysomnography if sleep symptoms are present; consider EKG and echocardiogram when BMI ≥35 or multiple risk factors coexist.
- Consider bariatric surgery for BMI >40 kg/m², or >35 kg/m² with comorbidities, when lifestyle and drug therapy have failed; gestational diabetes and hypertension fall after surgery, especially when BMI reaches <30 kg/m².
- Give low-dose aspirin 81 mg daily when obesity is accompanied by additional pre-eclampsia risk factors; ACOG classes obesity as a moderate risk factor.
- Perform serial growth ultrasound every 4 weeks from 28 to 36 weeks, and consider fetal echocardiography at 20–24 weeks when BMI >35 kg/m².
- At cesarean, close the subcutaneous layer with suture when depth is >2 cm, and close the subcuticular layer with suture rather than staples. *(II-3: meta-analysis, 12 RCTs, n = 3112)*
- Give cefazolin 2 g IV at least 30 minutes before skin incision; higher doses are not yet recommended.
- Use early mobilization and pneumatic compression devices, and consider pharmacologic thromboprophylaxis for 7 days postpartum in women with obesity plus additional risk factors, or in all women with BMI >40 kg/m².
- Prefer weight-based enoxaparin 0.5 mg/kg subcutaneously every 12 hours over fixed 40 mg daily. *(I; RCT, n = 84)*
- Perform a 75-g 2-hour glucose tolerance test more than 6 weeks postpartum, and refer to nutrition and behavioral counseling for weight loss.

## Key Concepts
- Classes: class I 30–34.9, class II 35–39.9, class III ≥40 kg/m². Severe obesity is BMI ≥40 kg/m².
- Suggested gestational weight gain: overweight 6–20 lb (2.7–9.0 kg); class I 5–15 lb (2.3–6.8 kg); class II −9 to 9 lb (−4.0 to 4.0 kg); class III −15 to 0 lb (−6.8 to 0 kg). The IOM ranges are 15–25 lb for overweight and 11–20 lb for all obesity classes.
- Pre-eclampsia risk is doubled at BMI 26 kg/m² and almost tripled above 30 kg/m², compared with BMI 21 kg/m².
- Nuchal translucency failure is 7.8% in class II obesity versus 1.0% in normal weight. Anomaly detection with standard ultrasound is 66%, 49%, 48%, 42%, and 25% for normal, overweight, and class I, II, and III; targeted scanning raises these to 97%, 91%, 75%, 88%, and 75%.
- Congenital heart defect odds ratios rise with class: 1.15 for class I, 1.26 for class II, 1.42 for class III.
- Cesarean wound complications reach 30% in obesity versus 3–17% overall, mostly at 8–10 days.
- Initial epidural catheter failure can reach 42% in morbid obesity; ultrasound guidance shortens placement from 9.5 to 6.9 minutes and raises first-attempt success from 52.5% to 87.5%.
- About 1% of maternal deaths are anesthesia related, and 75% of those women were obese.
- Schedule cesarean at 39 weeks if estimated fetal weight is >4500 g in a diabetic woman or >5000 g in a non-diabetic woman.
- Venous thromboembolism risk is up to fourfold higher; additional risk factors are age >35, weight >80 kg, para ≥4, pre-eclampsia, immobility >4 days, major illness, emergency cesarean, infection, antiphospholipid syndrome, prior thromboembolism, family history.
- Bariatric surgery: about 80% of recipients are of reproductive age; nearly 20% need gastric band adjustment or removal for nausea and vomiting; supplement vitamin B12, folate, iron, and calcium if deficient; vitamin D 10 mg daily is suggested by RCOG.
- Metformin in non-diabetic obesity reduced weight gain (4.6 versus 6.3 kg) and cesarean delivery (39.8% versus 62.9%) but is not standard care and does not prevent gestational diabetes.

## Anti-patterns
- Weight-loss pharmacotherapy during pregnancy: contraindicated.
- Negative pressure wound therapy for cesarean wounds in obesity: does not reduce wound complications and increases skin reactions.
- Silver nylon dressings: no benefit over gauze.
- Routine higher-dose cefazolin (3–4 g): data conflict and the recommended dose remains 2 g.
- Elective cesarean for suspected macrosomia alone: does not decrease maternal or neonatal morbidity; it is not recommended.
- Assuming clinical estimation of fetal growth is adequate: growth is hard to assess and ultrasound is required.
- Omitting adjustment of biochemical aneuploidy screening for maternal weight: detection falls without adjustment.
- Treating obesity with homeopathy: no effect on gestational weight gain.
- Recommending more frequent prenatal visits (every 3 weeks from 24–32 weeks) as evidence-based: RCOG suggests it but level I data are insufficient.
- Using the terms "obese patients" or "fatness": use "patients with obesity" and specific BMI terms.

## Key Takeaways
1. Calculate and discuss BMI at the first visit; the preconception encounter is the best opportunity to prevent complications.
2. Expect and screen for sleep apnea, depression, anxiety, and cardiac disease, especially when BMI ≥35 kg/m².
3. Book an anesthesia consult in the third trimester and plan a combined spinal-epidural with ultrasound guidance.
4. At cesarean, suture the subcutaneous layer if >2 cm, suture the skin, give 2 g cefazolin before incision, and mobilize within 4–6 hours.
5. Prescribe postpartum thromboprophylaxis for BMI >40 kg/m² or obesity plus additional risk factors, and recheck glucose at 6 weeks or later.
