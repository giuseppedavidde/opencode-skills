# Chapter 59: Sonographic Assessment of Amniotic Fluid: Oligohydramnios and Polyhydramnios

## Core Idea
Ultrasound estimates of amniotic fluid volume (AFV) correlate well with true volume when the volume is normal but poorly when it is abnormal. The maximum vertical pocket (MVP, also called single deepest pocket) is the best technique for diagnosing oligohydramnios in both singleton and twin pregnancies, because the amniotic fluid index (AFI) overdiagnoses the condition and increases inductions and cesarean deliveries without neonatal benefit. For polyhydramnios, the AFI is preferred because the MVP overdiagnoses it. Management is then stratified by gestational age at diagnosis.

## Recommendations and Grades
- Define oligohydramnios as MVP ≤2 cm, a definition that correlates with abnormal neonatal outcome and has the lowest false-positive rate. Do not use the AFI (for example <5th percentile for gestation or ≤5.0 cm) to define oligohydramnios. Grade: not stated.
- Define severe oligohydramnios as MVP <1 cm. Grade: not stated.
- Define polyhydramnios as AFI ≥25 cm (≥97.5th percentile for gestation) or MVP ≥8 cm; severe polyhydramnios as AFI ≥35 cm or MVP ≥16 cm. Grade: not stated.
- Do not use color Doppler routinely to estimate AFV: it does not identify more dye-determined oligohydramnios and mislabels 21% of normal pregnancies. Grade: not stated.
- Question the woman about possible preterm premature rupture of membranes (PPROM), document normal fetal kidneys, bladder, and fetal weight, and consider amniocentesis for karyotype when oligohydramnios is diagnosed in the second trimester or when severe at any gestation with PPROM excluded. Grade: not stated.
- Recommend increased oral hydration with 2 L of water daily in oligohydramnios. Grade: not stated.
- Do not use indomethacin to treat polyhydramnios: it reduces fetal urine output, but current guidelines advise against it because of neonatal necrotising enterocolitis and intraventricular hemorrhage. Grade: not stated.
- Do not apply increased antenatal surveillance (NST, BPP, or serial ultrasound) or early delivery for idiopathic mild polyhydramnios: there is no evidence of reduced perinatal mortality. Grade: not stated.

## Key Concepts
- Amniotic fluid physiology: fetal urine is the main component in the second half of pregnancy. The urethra is patent at 8–9 weeks; at 18 weeks the fetus produces about 50–100 mL of urine per day; at term, 800 mL per day or 5 mL/kg/hour. After 40 weeks AFV declines by 8% each week. The fetus swallows about 0.5 L per day at term, and the lungs also produce and absorb fluid.
- Measurement technique: MVP is the deepest vertical pocket with a horizontal measurement of at least 1 cm, free of cord and fetal small parts. AFI sums the deepest pockets in four quadrants: ≤5.0 cm is oligohydramnios, 5.1–24.9 cm normal, ≥25 cm polyhydramnios. Use the "to the cord" rather than "through the cord" technique, which identifies more low dye-determined volumes.
- Incidence: oligohydramnios about 0.2% in the second trimester and 3–5% in the third trimester, lower when defined by MVP ≤2 cm. Polyhydramnios 1–5% of singleton pregnancies, with <1% severe.
- Accuracy: all three ultrasound methods (subjective, AFI, MVP) identify normal volumes well (sensitivity 70–98%) but identify oligohydramnios and polyhydramnios poorly.
- Oligohydramnios management by gestation:
  - 16–24 weeks: consider amniocentesis if feasible.
  - 28 to <36 weeks: modified BPP (NST plus MVP) or BPP; if reassuring, continue weekly or biweekly testing depending on fetal status. If MVP ≤2 cm, at least twice-weekly NST with AFI. If MVP >2 cm on two consecutive scans, return to routine care. Consider delivery only for substantial signs of fetal compromise.
  - ≥36 weeks: consider induction or delivery for idiopathic oligohydramnios with persistent MVP ≤2 cm despite hydration.
  - ≥39 weeks: proceed with induction or delivery.
- Hydration evidence: intravenous isotonic infusion of 1500 mL/day raised mean AFI from 39.7 to 77.7 mm; home oral hydration of 2500 mL/day produced higher volumes at delivery than 1500 mL/day (112.45 ± 14.92 vs 86.21 ± 16.89 mm, p <0.001). A 2015 meta-analysis confirmed that maternal hydration increases AFV in isolated oligohydramnios, with a reduction in cesarean delivery in three studies where volume improved.
- Amnioinfusion: transabdominal infusion of lactated Ringer's raises adequate visualization of fetal structures from 51% to 77% and reduces non-reassuring fetal heart tracing from 42% to 5% and cesarean for that indication from 25% to 5%. Transcervical infusion of about 500 mL of normal saline in term oligohydramnios reduces cesarean for non-reassuring tracing by 77%, overall cesarean by 48%, umbilical artery pH <7.20 by 60%, non-reassuring tracing by 76%, and Apgar <7 at 5 minutes by 48%. Prophylactic and therapeutic amnioinfusion do not differ in one randomized trial.
- Polyhydramnios: 50–60% of cases, especially mild, are idiopathic. Risk of major anomaly on prenatal ultrasound is 8% with AFI <30, 12% with AFI 30–34.9, and 31% with AFI ≥35; at birth after a normal scan the risks are 1%, 2%, and 11%. Aneuploidy risk is ≤1% with normal ultrasound but about 10% with a major anomaly. Perinatal mortality is <5% with normal anatomy and 10–80% with an anomaly.
- Polyhydramnios classification: mild AFI 25–29.9 cm or MVP 8–11 cm; moderate AFI 30–34.9 cm or MVP 12–15 cm; severe AFI ≥35 cm or MVP ≥16 cm.
- Polyhydramnios workup: detailed ultrasound for anomalies, growth, hydrops, and TTTS; umbilical and MCA Doppler (pulsatility index and peak systolic velocity); diabetes screening, repeat if more than 4 weeks since the original test; personal and family history; antibody screen and RPR; parvovirus, toxoplasmosis, and CMV IgM and IgG. Strongly consider amniocentesis for severe polyhydramnios, an anomaly, growth restriction, or diagnosis before 24 weeks.
- Polyhydramnios management by gestation:
  - <24 weeks: strongly consider amniocentesis.
  - 24 to 38 6/7 weeks: consider amniocentesis, especially with severe polyhydramnios, FGR, or anomaly; for moderate disease, weekly AFI or MVP with or without NST or BPP and growth ultrasound every 3–4 weeks; for severe disease with discomfort or dyspnea, consider amnioreduction to normalize AFV. Amnioreduction carries a 1.5% complication rate (preterm labor, PPROM, chorioamnionitis, abruption, membrane detachment), and risk rises if more than 2 L is removed at once.
  - ≥39 weeks: offer induction or delivery to all, particularly with maternal discomfort from moderate or severe polyhydramnios; there is no medical indication for induction before 39 weeks for mild polyhydramnios; cesarean only for obstetric indications.
- Twins: MVP is the most consistent method; measure the deepest pocket in each sac after identifying the dividing membrane. Colour Doppler decreases both AFI and MVP by about 20% compared with grey-scale, which contributes to overdiagnosis.

## Anti-patterns
- Using the AFI to diagnose oligohydramnios: it overdiagnoses and increases induction and cesarean delivery without improving neonatal outcome.
- Using the MVP to diagnose polyhydramnios when the AFI is available: it overdiagnoses without benefit.
- Routine color Doppler for AFV estimation.
- Indomethacin for polyhydramnios: neonatal necrotising enterocolitis and intraventricular hemorrhage.
- Antenatal testing or early delivery for mild idiopathic polyhydramnios.
- Removing more than 2 L at amnioreduction.

## Key Takeaways
- Use MVP ≤2 cm for oligohydramnios and AFI ≥25 cm (or MVP ≥8 cm) for polyhydramnios.
- Check for PPROM and fetal anatomy before attributing low fluid to placental insufficiency; offer karyotype in second-trimester or severe cases.
- Advise 2 L of oral water daily, and consider amnioinfusion at or near term.
- In polyhydramnios, screen for diabetes, anomalies, and infection, and reserve amniocentesis for severe disease, anomaly, FGR, or diagnosis before 24 weeks.
- Do not intensify surveillance or deliver early for mild idiopathic polyhydramnios; deliver at 39 weeks or later.
