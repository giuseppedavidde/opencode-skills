# Clinical Patterns

The book grades references, not recommendations: **[I]**, **[II-1]**, **[II-2]**, **[II-3]**, **[III]**.

## Severe hypertension and pre-eclampsia with severe features
**When you see it**: BP ≥160/110 mmHg twice >4 hours apart, or severe-range BP with headache, visual change, platelets <100,000/mm³, creatinine ≥1.1 mg/dL or pulmonary oedema.
**How to confirm**: Repeat BP with the correct cuff. Check platelets, AST/ALT, creatinine and urine protein.
**Management**: Labetalol 20 mg IV then 40/80/80 mg every 10 minutes (max 220 mg). Give magnesium sulfate and deliver at 34 0/7 weeks. Magnesium reduces eclampsia by 59% **[I]**. Delivery at 37 weeks for non-severe disease cut composite maternal outcome by 29% **[I]**. Acute agent choice lacks Level 1 data.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Hydralazine causes more side effects. Target 140–150/90–100 mmHg.

## Cardiac disease in pregnancy
**When you see it**: Known congenital, valvular or cardiomyopathic disease, pulmonary hypertension, or new dyspnoea, orthopnoea or chest pain.
**How to confirm**: ECG, echocardiogram and BNP. Assign an mWHO class.
**Management**: Refer to a cardio-obstetrics team. Class IV disease (severe pulmonary hypertension, EF <30%, severe aortic stenosis, Marfan with aortic root >4.5 cm) carries extremely high risk. Use epidural anaesthesia and left lateral labour. Prefer vaginal delivery. Endocarditis prophylaxis is not recommended for delivery. Evidence is expert opinion **[III]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Pregnancy symptoms mimic decompensation. Avoid strict bed rest because of VTE risk.

## Diabetic pregnancy
**When you see it**: Pregestational diabetes, or GDM at 24–28 weeks with macrosomia or poor glucose logs.
**How to confirm**: HbA1c and glucose logs. Screen with a 50 g GCT at 135 mg/dL then a 100 g GTT (Carpenter–Coustan 95/180/155/140 mg/dL, two abnormal), or a one-step 75 g GTT (92/180/153 mg/dL, one abnormal).
**Management**: Preconception HbA1c <6%. Target fasting <95 mg/dL and 2-hour postprandial ≤120 mg/dL. Insulin is first-line. Deliver well-controlled pregestational diabetes at 39 0/7–39 6/7 weeks and offer cesarean if EFW ≥4500 g. Treating GDM improves outcomes **[I]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: One-step screening diagnoses about twice as many women as two-step. Oral agents in pregestational diabetes lack evidence.

## Thyroid disease
**When you see it**: Known or suspected thyroid disease with an abnormal TSH, goitre, or compatible symptoms.
**How to confirm**: TSH and free T4. Check TPOAb if TSH >2.5 mU/L. Use population trimester ranges, otherwise an upper TSH limit of 4.0 mU/L.
**Management**: For overt hypothyroidism, target TSH 0.5–2.5 mU/L, increase levothyroxine 20–30%, and check every 4 weeks until normal, then each trimester. For hyperthyroidism, use PTU through 16 weeks then methimazole, keeping FT4 high-normal. Radioiodine is contraindicated.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Subclinical hypothyroidism treatment has no proven benefit. Do not test TSH in hyperemesis.

## Intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy
**When you see it**: New pruritus in the second or third trimester, often on palms and soles, without rash.
**How to confirm**: Fasting total bile acids >10 µmol/L, with relief within 4 weeks of delivery. Exclude hepatitis, gallstones, pre-eclampsia and HELLP.
**Management**: Ursodeoxycholic acid is first-line. Classify 11–39 µmol/L as mild, 40–99 as moderate and ≥100 as severe. Deliver at 35 0/7–36 6/7 weeks if ≥100, 37 0/7–37 6/7 if 40–99, and 38 0/7–38 6/7 if <40.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Stillbirth exceeds background risk only above 100 µmol/L. Sudden death has occurred within 24 hours of a reactive NST. 

## Sickle cell crisis
**When you see it**: HbSS or HbSβ0 disease with pain, fever or dyspnoea.
**How to confirm**: Haemoglobin electrophoresis establishes the diagnosis. For acute chest syndrome, obtain a chest X-ray, cultures and oxygen saturation.
**Management**: Give morphine and IV fluids. Add antibiotics if febrile or with acute chest syndrome, and oxygen to keep saturation ≥95%. Transfuse prophylactically in the third trimester every 3–4 weeks for Hgb 9–12 g/dL and HbS <35%. Transfuse for Hgb <6 g/dL, Hct <25%, stroke or acute chest syndrome. Evidence is observational **[II-2]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Hydroxyurea data are limited, but continue it if previously beneficial. Acute chest syndrome needs a cephalosporin, a macrolide and bronchodilators.

## Thromboprophylaxis decision
**When you see it**: Prior VTE, thrombophilia, cesarean delivery, obesity or immobility.
**How to confirm**: Risk assessment early in pregnancy, repeated on admission.
**Management**: Low-risk thrombophilia with family history: surveillance. Prior unprovoked VTE: prophylactic or intermediate LMWH. High-risk thrombophilia with prior VTE: therapeutic LMWH. Continue postpartum prophylaxis 6–8 weeks. Prophylactic enoxaparin is 40 mg SC daily; therapeutic 1 mg/kg every 12 hours. Anti-Xa targets are 0.2–0.4 and 0.5–1.2 U/mL.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Several scenarios have no Level 1 evidence, and the book says so for APS with placental insufficiency. Anti-Xa monitoring is not prospectively validated.

## Sepsis and septic shock
**When you see it**: Fever or hypothermia, tachycardia, hypotension, oliguria, altered mentation, or qSOFA ≥2.
**How to confirm**: Blood cultures before antibiotics, lactate, and source imaging.
**Management**: Use the Surviving Sepsis hour-1 bundle. Measure lactate, culture, give broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour, give 30 mL/kg crystalloid for hypotension or lactate ≥4 mmol/L, and start vasopressors for MAP ≥65 mmHg. The book states there is no specific evidence for these guidelines in pregnancy **[III]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Each hour of delay increases mortality. Delay in recognition or management occurred in 70% of UK sepsis deaths.

## Suspected venous thromboembolism
**When you see it**: Unilateral leg pain and oedema, or dyspnoea, tachypnoea or chest pain.
**How to confirm**: Compression ultrasonography is the primary test for DVT. V/Q scan and CTPA are equivalent first-line tests for PE.
**Management**: Give therapeutic LMWH throughout pregnancy, or IV UFH for at least 5 days then LMWH. Continue a minimum of 6 months and at least 6 weeks postpartum. Evidence is largely cohort data **[II-2]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Suspicion is confirmed in only 10% of DVT and 4% of PE. Most events are iliofemoral and left-sided.

## Lupus flare and anti-SSA antibodies
**When you see it**: SLE with active disease, nephritis, hypertension, cardiac or pulmonary disease, or anti-SSA/SSB antibodies.
**How to confirm**: ACR, SLICC or EULAR/ACR criteria, plus C3, C4, anti-dsDNA and protein/creatinine ratio.
**Management**: Conceive in remission. Continue or start hydroxychloroquine. Start aspirin 81–150 mg between 12 and 16 weeks for pre-eclampsia prevention. For anti-SSA, perform fetal echocardiography from 16 to 26 weeks. Cardiac neonatal lupus risk is about 2%, with 18% recurrence. Evidence is observational **[II-2]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Do not stop hydroxychloroquine in stable disease. Complications are driven by active disease and organ involvement at conception.

## Preterm infection screening (GBS and PPROM)
**When you see it**: Preterm labour, preterm rupture of membranes, intrapartum fever ≥38°C, or unknown GBS status at delivery.
**How to confirm**: Anovaginal culture at 36 0/7–37 6/7 weeks. If status is unknown in preterm labour, perform NAAT and treat while awaiting the result.
**Management**: Penicillin G 5 million units IV, then 3 million units every 4 hours. If allergic without anaphylaxis risk, cefazolin 2 g then 1 g every 8 hours. Universal screening is more effective than a risk-factor strategy **[I]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: GBS bacteriuria at any colony count and a prior affected infant receive prophylaxis without screening. Screening does not prevent late-onset disease.

## Red cell alloimmunization
**When you see it**: Rh(D)-negative or Kell-sensitized pregnancy, or a positive antibody screen.
**How to confirm**: Antibody titre (16 is critical for Rh(D), 4 for Kell) and MCA-PSV from 15 weeks.
**Management**: MCA-PSV ≥1.5 MoM indicates fetal blood sampling. Transfuse for Hgb below the 5th percentile or Hct <30%. Give anti-D 300 µg at 28 weeks and within 72 hours of an Rh(D)-positive birth. Evidence is cohort data **[II-2]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: MCA-PSV misses severe anaemia in 1–10% of cases. Kell disease can cause anaemia at lower titres.

## Fetal growth restriction
**When you see it**: EFW or AC <10th percentile. Severe disease is EFW <3rd percentile, or <10th percentile with abnormal umbilical artery Doppler.
**How to confirm**: Confirm dating with a first-trimester ultrasound. Perform umbilical artery Doppler.
**Management**: Growth ultrasound every 3–4 weeks. Umbilical artery Doppler weekly, 2–3 times weekly with absent end-diastolic flow, and daily with reversed flow. Deliver at 38–39 weeks for EFW 3rd–10th with normal Doppler, 37 for decreased diastolic flow, 33–34 for absent flow, 30–32 for reversed flow. Doppler reduces perinatal death **[I]**.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Up to 70% of fetuses below the 10th percentile are constitutionally small. MCA and ductus venosus Doppler are not routine.

## Abnormal antepartum testing
**When you see it**: Non-reactive NST, BPS ≤6/10, or positive contraction stress test.
**How to confirm**: At ≥32 weeks, follow a non-reactive NST immediately with a full BPS. A contraction stress test is positive with decelerations in ≥50% of three contractions in 10 minutes.
**Management**: BPS 8/8 equals 10/10 and needs no acute action. For 6/10, repeat in about 6 hours and deliver if persistent at term. For 4/10, deliver. For 0–2/10, deliver by cesarean if needed.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Up to 50% of NSTs at 24–28 weeks and 15% at 28–32 weeks are non-reactive. Do not deliver before 32 weeks for non-reactivity alone. No randomised data show that testing reduces cerebral palsy.
