# Chapter 5: Basic Cryptocurrency Analysis

## Core Idea
Before diving deep into TA, this chapter covers foundational metrics for evaluating any cryptocurrency: teams, whitepapers, events, competition, market cap, activity, volatility, and supply mechanisms.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Big-Brother Technique**: New projects resembling successful "big brother" coins with slight improvements often go parabolic
- **Supply Mechanism Classification**: Fixed/deflationary vs unlimited/inflationary; burning; halving
- **Activity Metrics Trifecta**: Volume | Active Addresses | Average Holding Time
- **Market Cap Analysis**: Price × Circulating Supply = Total market value; determines upside potential and volatility

## Key Concepts
- **Teams**: Research background, track record, advisors (e.g., Storj's Harvard-affiliated CEO)
- **Whitepapers**: Three types—backgrounder, numbered list, problem/solution
- **Events**: CoinMarketCal, CoinEvents—coins often pump 10-20% on event day
- **Competition**: Niche markets support multiple projects; Big-Brother explains how clones thrive
- **Utility**: Real use cases (BTC = digital gold, ETH = smart contracts, STORJ = cloud storage)
- **Market Cap**: Low cap = high volatility/upside potential; high cap = stability/lower upside
- **Sentiment Tools**: Crypto Fear & Greed Index, Bulls & Bears Index, Santiment
- **Volatility**: Crypto is the most volatile asset class; drives both massive gains and losses
- **Supply**: Maximum supply, circulating supply, fixed vs unlimited
- **Burning**: Sending coins to inaccessible addresses (eater address) reduces supply, increases value
- **Halving**: Mining rewards cut in half every 4 years (Bitcoin); scarcity drives price appreciation

## Crypto-Specific Considerations
- Supply mechanisms directly affect investment thesis—deflationary coins reward long-term holding
- Market cap determines realistic upside: 10x-ing Bitcoin requires $11T, 10x-ing OVR requires $1B
- Average holding time (AHT) reveals whether a coin is a short-term flip or long-term hold
- Burning and halving are crypto-specific supply events with no stock-market equivalent

## Key Takeaways
- Market cap and supply analysis are essential before any trade
- Utility + team quality + reasonable supply = long-term potential
- Big-Brother technique helps identify which new projects might explode
- Events calendars provide actionable short-term trading catalysts
