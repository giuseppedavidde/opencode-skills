# Chapter 2: Technical Analysis

## Core Idea
Technical analysis uses historical price patterns to predict future movements. The chapter covers the philosophical foundation ("The Key Three") and introduces major indicator categories used throughout the book.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Key Three**: History repeats itself | Market discounts everything | Prices move in trends
- **Leading vs Lagging**: Leading indicators predict (fast, less accurate); lagging confirm (slow, more accurate)
- **Alert → Predict → Confirm**: Three-step system for combining indicators into a trade decision
- **Behavioral Trading**: Psychology affects prices (round numbers as S/R, emotional trading patterns)

## Key Concepts
- **Support & Resistance**: Concentrated buying (support) and selling (resistance) zones; breakouts flip roles
- **Volume Indicators**: Measure trade activity combined with price (ADX, ROC, OBV, Volume RSI)
- **Momentum Indicators**: Rate of change shows strength/weakness of moves
- **Oscillators**: Vary within upper/lower bounds (0-100); overbought = sell, oversold = buy
- **Moving Averages**: Lagging indicators for trend, S/R; crossovers signal momentum shifts
- **Volatility Indicators**: Measure risk via standard deviation, ATR, Bollinger Bands

## Crypto-Specific Considerations
- Crypto is more trend-based than other markets (less efficient = stronger trends)
- 24/7 trading means no gap openings/closings; S/R levels form differently
- Round numbers have stronger psychological effect in crypto (e.g., Bitcoin at $50K, $100K)
- Higher volatility means oscillator overbought/oversold thresholds may need adjustment (e.g., RSI 40/60 instead of 30/70)

## Key Takeaways
- MAs alone can outperform buy-and-hold by ~30% in crypto
- Combine leading + lagging indicators for best results
- Psychology is a legitimate price-influencing factor in crypto
