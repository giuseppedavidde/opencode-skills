# Chapter 6: Types of Charts

## Core Idea
Charts are the foundation of TA. Different chart types reveal different information. Choosing the right chart for your trading style is as important as the indicators used.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Chart Type Selection**: Match chart type to trading style (candlestick for detail, Renko for trend clarity, Heikin-Ashi for noise reduction)
- **Charting Resources**: TradingView (best overall), CoinMarketCap (simple), CryptoWatch (bots), GoCharting (free)
- **Chart Pattern Classifications**: Bullish, Bearish, Candlestick-specific, Continuation, Breakout, Reversal

## Key Concepts
- **Line Chart**: Simplest; single line connecting closing prices; less information but clearest overview
- **Candlestick Chart**: Most popular; shows open, high, low, close (OHLC) per period; real body (green/red) + wicks
- **Renko Chart**: Bricks instead of candles; ignores time/volume; shows only price movement; smoother trends
- **Point & Figure Chart**: Xs (rising) and Os (falling) in columns; ignores time; identifies entry/exit points
- **Heikin-Ashi Chart**: Smoothed candlestick variant; averages data across two periods; reduces false signals
- **Risk Management**: Stop-loss, trailing stop-loss, long vs short trading

## Crypto-Specific Considerations
- 24/7 market means continuous data for all chart types
- Renko and Heikin-Ashi particularly useful for crypto's choppy, high-noise price action
- Point & Figure helps identify support/resistance in volatile crypto conditions
- TradingView is the industry standard for crypto charting; most crypto-native
- Always account for volatility when setting stop-loss distances

## Key Takeaways
- Candlesticks are standard; experiment with Heikin-Ashi for trend clarity
- Renko removes time-based noise—useful for crypto's erratic movements
- Match chart type to your strategy: detail (candlestick) vs clarity (Renko/Heikin-Ashi)
- Always use stop-losses; trailing stops protect profits in volatile moves
