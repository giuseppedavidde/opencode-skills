# Cheatsheet — Trading in the Zone (Mark Douglas)

Quick-reference decision tables and the load-bearing lists to keep on the desk
while trading.

---

## If You Feel X Before Entry → Do Y

| Feeling / impulse before entry | What it signals | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Fear of being wrong | A belief that the market must confirm you | Predefine risk; reaffirm: *I don't need to know what happens next* |
| Fear of losing money | Risk not actually accepted, only intellectually known | Reduce size until loss is genuinely comfortable; skip trade if it isn't |
| Fear of missing out | Belief that every perceived opportunity must be captured | Wait for the next edge; reaffirm: *anything can happen, including that I miss this one* |
| Fear of leaving money on the table | Belief that the market will run forever in your favor | Plan profit-taking in thirds; scale out as the market pays |
| Euphoria / "I can't lose" after winners | Euphoria state — denial of risk | Stop sizing up; reaffirm the five truths aloud before the next trade |
| Compulsion to "get my money back" | Revenge agenda active; not available | Stand down; you are no longer trading the market, you are trading the past |
| "I've seen this pattern before, I know what happens" | Association mechanism overriding uniqueness | Invoke uniqueness belief; treat the triggered feeling as a notification, not a forecast |
| Hesitation at the trigger | Conflict between knowing probabilities and a stronger belief arguing against | Refocus on the seven principles; cut the trade down to minimum size to execute it |
| Tightening the stop closer to entry | Pain-avoidance trying to reduce an already-accepted cost | Keep the stop at the technically optimal level; the predefined risk is a due bill, not negotiable |
| Wanting to skip the next trade after a loss | Belief that one loss predicts the next | Remind: *random distribution* — every loss edges you closer to a win within the sample |

---

## The Five Fundamental Truths

1. **Anything can happen.** One trader anywhere can negate your edge.
2. **You don't need to know what's going to happen next to make money.**
3. **There is a random distribution between wins and losses** for any set of variables that define an edge.
4. **An edge is an indication of a higher probability** of one outcome over another — not a certainty.
5. **Every moment in the market is unique.**

---

## The Seven Principles of Consistency

1. I objectively identify my edges.
2. I predefine the risk of every trade.
3. I completely accept the risk, or I am willing to let go of the trade.
4. I act on my edges without reservation or hesitation.
5. I pay myself as the market makes money available to me.
6. I continually monitor my susceptibility for making errors.
7. I understand the absolute necessity of these principles and therefore never violate them.

---

## The Failure Triad (Ch. 6)

A trader who hasn't accepted the uncertainty principle falls into one of three
mutually reinforcing failure modes:

- **Refuse to define a loss** — won't predefine risk *before* entry.
- **Refuse to take a loss** — won't exit when the predefined risk is hit.
- **Refuse to take a profit** — won't pay themselves as the market makes money
  available, then watch the winner become a loser.

Each refusal is a manifestation of the four primary fears (being wrong, losing
money, missing out, leaving money on the table) operating through the
pain-avoidance mechanism. Breaking the triad requires only accepting the five
fundamental truths at a functional level.

## The Casino Edge — Mindset Swap

| Slot-machine player | Casino operator (the trader's target) |
|---|---|
| Predicts each pull | Doesn't predict any individual hand |
| Selects which pulls to take | Participates in every edge in the sample |
| Wins/loses trade-by-trade | Wins over a *series* of events |
| Needs the market to make him right | Needs only the odds in his favor up front |
| Suffers the four fears | Has nothing to protect against |

Blackjack house edge: ~4.5% per hand. The casino nets consistently over the
year *because* it plays every hand. Replace "casino rules" with "your edge
variables" and "every hand" with "every occurrence of your edge in your sample
size," and the trader owns the game the same way.
