# Chapter 6: Falling Off the Edge

## Core Idea
Trading edges exist because of divergences between market perception and reality — caused by cognitive biases. Support and resistance are not magical lines but the battlegrounds between buyers and sellers. The edge comes from entering when one side wins and the other capitulates.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Cognitive Basis of Support & Resistance
Support and resistance emerge from three specific biases:
1. **Anchoring**: Recent highs/lows become psychological reference points. A $1.13 low makes $1.15 feel "cheap."
2. **Recency Bias**: The most recent high/low weighs heaviest in trader decisions. Recent reference points are the strongest anchors.
3. **Disposition Effect**: Traders who bought at $1.02 and missed selling at $1.14 will sell when price returns to $1.14 — creating resistance.

S/R becomes a **self-fulfilling prophecy**: belief in its existence reinforces its reality.

### The Points of Price Instability Framework
Prices near support/resistance edges are **unstable** — they are unlikely to stay there. One side wins, the other capitulates:
- If support **holds** → countertrend traders win (price bounces)
- If support **breaks** → trend followers win (price cascades lower)
- In both cases, the **cost of being wrong is low**: a tight stop near the level limits losses

### The Battle Analogy
- Mid-range = armies not engaged, outcome unclear
- Near S/R = armies committed, one side will lose
- After breakout = winning side advances, losing side panics → price moves far
- **"It is easiest to tell who will win a battle when it is nearly over"** — wait for confirmation

## Key Concepts
- **Trend followers buy resistance breakdowns** (long entries) and sell support breakdowns (short entries).
- **Countertrend traders buy at support** and sell short at resistance, expecting bounces.
- **The gap in human perception at S/R breakdown** is the source of the trend follower's edge: people hold old beliefs too long while the market moves.
- **No new buyers below support**: Why buy when the price is dropping? Wait until it stops. This absence of buyers accelerates declines.
- **Breakout definition**: Price exceeds a previous high/low that had served as resistance/support.

### The Heating Oil Example
Support at $2.10 held once. Second test: price broke through and fell to $1.73. A 10-contract long entered at $2.10 would have lost $115,500. Countertrend traders who exited on the close of the breakdown day survived. Those who stayed lost everything.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating S/R as precise levels**: Prices don't bounce at exact numbers — use zones, not lines.
- **Averaging down**: Buying more as price falls "because it's cheaper" (anchoring on the old entry price).
- **Hoping instead of acting**: "If it gets back to $X, I'll sell" — the market doesn't care about your breakeven.
- **Fighting the breakout**: When support/resistance clearly breaks, don't argue — exit.
- **Over-relying on S/R alone**: S/R is probabilistic, not deterministic. Always know where you're wrong.

## Key Takeaways
1. Support and resistance are psychological phenomena, not mathematical truths.
2. The best trades happen at **points of price instability** — where the distance between right and wrong is smallest.
3. Trend followers make money when S/R **breaks**; countertrend traders when it **holds**.
4. Exit immediately when the market proves you wrong at an S/R level — small losses, big winners.
5. The edge is in the **gap between perception and reality** when prior levels break.
