# Chapter 7: By What Measure?

## Core Idea
Traders obsess over returns but underestimate the four risks that destroy accounts: drawdowns, low returns, price shocks, and system death. Proper risk/reward assessment requires metrics beyond CAGR—including MAR ratio, Sharpe ratio, and the psychological reality that simulated drawdowns feel different from live ones.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four-Risk Taxonomy**: Drawdowns (strings of losses), Low Returns (extended flat periods), Price Shocks (sudden catastrophic moves), and System Death (permanent regime change) represent distinct threats requiring different defenses.
- **Log-Scale Deception**: Equity curves plotted on logarithmic scales visually compress drawdowns. A 38% drawdown on a log chart looks manageable; on a linear scale with actual dollar amounts, it triggers fear and premature strategy abandonment.
- **MAR Ratio**: CAGR / Maximum Drawdown. A quick measure of return per unit of pain—the higher the MAR, the smoother the equity curve relative to returns.

## Key Concepts
- **Drawdown Psychology vs. Mathematics**: Most traders overestimate their drawdown tolerance. A backtest showing 38% max drawdown seems acceptable in analysis; experiencing 42% in real-time with live capital triggers doubt, selective trade-skipping, and eventual strategy abandonment.
- **The 1987 Crash as a Price Shock Case Study**: Faith lost ~$11M on a $20M account overnight when eurodollars gapped 2+ points (no opportunity to exit), gold dropped $25, and silver dropped $1+. An entire year's profits vanished in one session.
- **Richard Dennis's Edge as a Capital Allocator**: Dennis distinguished between drawdowns from giving back profits vs. drawdowns from string losses. He tolerated profit retracement as "part of the game for trend followers"—a rare quality that enabled Turtle-level returns.
- **Institutional Reality Check**: Successful former Turtles running outside money trade at greatly reduced risk levels compared to Turtle days. 100%+ annual returns require 70% drawdown tolerance; institutional capital cannot accept this.
- **CAGR Consistency Premium**: A system returning 30% every year is preferable to one returning 5%, 5%, then 100% over three years—even with same terminal CAGR—because consistent returns indicate higher probability of future outperformance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Log-Scale Self-Deception**: Using logarithmic charts to evaluate systems because they make drawdowns look smaller. Always examine drawdowns on linear scales with actual dollar amounts.
- **Planning for Historical Max Drawdown**: Assuming future drawdowns won't exceed historical maximums. The worst drawdown is always in the future, and it usually arrives shortly after you start trading.
- **Ignoring CAGR Distribution**: Evaluating systems on terminal CAGR alone without examining year-by-year consistency. Erratic returns mask regime-dependency and survivorship risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Drawdowns that look small on a log chart feel catastrophic with real money on a linear scale.
2. The worst historical drawdown will be exceeded by a future one—plan for worse than worst-case.
3. Price shocks (1987, 9/11) can erase years of profits in hours with no opportunity to exit.
4. Institutional capital requires dramatically lower risk than what produced Turtle-level returns.
5. MAR ratio and return consistency matter more than raw CAGR for live trading sustainability.
