# Chapter 4: Think Like a Turtle

## Core Idea
Good trading is not about being right — it's about trading right. Success comes from thinking in probabilities over the long run, ignoring the outcomes of individual trades, and taking full responsibility for your results.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The Probabilistic Thinking Model
Trading is like running an insurance company: you don't know which trade will win, but you know the distribution of outcomes. The key shift is from **prediction** ("will this go up?") to **probability** ("what is the likely distribution of outcomes if I take 100 trades like this?").

### The R-Multiple Framework
- **R** = the amount risked on a trade (entry price − stop price)
- A 5R trade means profit = 5 × amount risked
- Trend-following systems: many small losses (≤1R), few large wins (4-5R, sometimes 12R-30R)
- The system is profitable because winners are large enough to cover losers + profit

### The Cocoa Case Study
A real-world example of recency bias: 17 consecutive losing trades in cocoa (April-October 1998), totaling −$16,750. Then 4 winning trades generated +$78,258 (+$55,903 net). Traders who quit after loss #17 missed the entire move. This is typical — trends often appear just after everyone gives up.

### The Distribution-Based Mindset
- Build histograms of trade outcomes (like women's heights)
- Know the general shape: skewed right with fat tails
- Most trades will be losers — that's expected and irrelevant
- Each trade is just one draw from a known distribution

## Key Concepts
- **Turtles don't care about being right**: They care about making money. Expecting to lose on any given trade frees you from outcome bias.
- **Think in the present**: Don't dwell on past mistakes or try to predict the future.
- **Avoid the future tense**: Think in possibilities and probabilities, never in predictions.
- **10 losing trades in a row while sticking to your plan**: This means you are trading WELL.
- **Take responsibility**: Don't blame the market, broker, or missing "secrets." You make the trades.

### Dos and Don'ts
1. **Trade in the present** — past is counterproductive, future is impossible.
2. **Think probabilities, not predictions** — focus on methods with favorable long-run odds.
3. **Take responsibility** — learn from mistakes, don't pretend they didn't happen.

## Anti-patterns
- **Needing to be right**: The ego-driven urge to predict. This causes rule-breaking when wrong.
- **Recency-trapping**: Avoiding a market because of recent losses (like the friend who missed the cocoa trend).
- **Conspiracy thinking**: Believing "they" gave others secret rules. Real reason for failure is usually internal.
- **Blame-shifting**: Blaming brokers, markets, specialists instead of examining your own process.
- **Overcomplicating**: Curtis traded only the simple 10-week breakout system — simpler was better.

## Key Takeaways
1. You can be wrong most of the time and still make money — trend followers win ~35-40% of trades.
2. Build histograms of your trading results to internalize the distribution of outcomes.
3. R-multiples let you compare trades across markets and systems: focus on R, not dollars.
4. If you can't take responsibility for losses, you'll never learn from them.
5. The simplest system (100% allocated to 10-week breakouts) outperformed complex approaches.
