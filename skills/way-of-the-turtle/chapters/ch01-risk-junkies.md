# Chapter 1: Risk Junkies

## Core Idea
Trading is fundamentally about the transfer and assumption of risk. Markets exist because different participants have different relationships with risk: hedgers seek to eliminate it, scalpers trade its liquidity, and speculators profit from assuming it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Tripartite Market Model**: Hedgers → Scalpers → Speculators form the three legs of every market transaction, each with distinct risk profiles and time horizons.
- **Price Discovery via Collective Perception**: Prices move not because of fundamental news but because the collective perception of all market participants—scalpers, speculators, and hedgers—shifts simultaneously, changing bid/ask willingness.

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity Risk vs. Price Risk**: Liquidity risk is the inability to buy or sell at a desired price (bid-ask spread risk). Price risk is the possibility of significant directional movement. Scalpers trade liquidity risk; speculators trade price risk.
- **Bid-Ask Spread Mechanics**: The spread is the scalper's profit margin. Floor traders attempt to capture the spread repeatedly by buying at the bid and selling at the ask milliseconds later.
- **Market Impact and Panic Cascades**: When large orders hit a thin market, scalpers caught with adverse positions panic-exit, causing rapid price movement. Experienced traders exploit this by reversing positions early.
- **Southwest Airlines Hedging Case**: Southwest hedged 85% of its oil at $26/barrel for years, insulating its P&L from price fluctuations. This exemplifies the hedger role: using futures markets to transfer price risk to speculators.
- **The Death of the Pits**: Electronic exchanges are replacing pit trading across all markets. While execution costs are lower, the psychological signals visible in traders' faces are lost. The players (hedgers, scalpers, speculators) remain the same behind screens.

## Anti-patterns
- **Confusing Trading with Investing**: Believing that buying undervalued assets is the same as trading. Traders deal in risk transfer, not valuation.
- **Ignoring the Other Participants**: Trading as if you're the only actor. Every trade has a counterparty with opposite intentions and possibly superior information.
- **Over-Leveraging in Panic**: When caught on the wrong side of a fast market, inexperienced traders freeze or double down instead of cutting losses immediately.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every trade involves three participant types—know which one you are at any given moment.
2. Price is a function of collective perception, not objective value.
3. The transition from pit to electronic trading doesn't change human psychology; the same fear and greed operate behind screens.
4. Professional speculators make money by assuming the price risk that hedgers pay to eliminate.
