# Glossary — Way of the Turtle

Alphabetical reference of all key terms, frameworks, and concepts from Curtis M. Faith's *Way of the Turtle*.

---

## A

**Anchoring**: Cognitive bias where traders fixate on a specific price (e.g., recent high) and evaluate all subsequent prices relative to it. Leads to holding losers hoping to "get back to even" and selling winners too early. One of three biases (with recency bias and disposition effect) that cause support/resistance levels.

**Arbitrage**: Trading the liquidity of one market for another. Arbitrageurs buy crude in London and sell in New York, or buy a basket of stocks against short index futures. Edge is typically small, making arbitrage especially vulnerable to trader effects.

**Ask Price**: The price at which a seller is willing to sell. Traders buying at market pay the ask. The difference between bid and ask is the spread, which scalpers attempt to capture.

**Average True Range (ATR)**: See N.

---

## B

**Bid-Ask Spread**: The difference between bid and ask prices. Represents the scalper's profit margin and the immediate cost of trading for speculators. Spreads widen during panic or fast markets as scalpers demand higher compensation for liquidity provision.

**Breakout**: A price movement exceeding a previous N-day high (bullish) or low (bearish). The Turtles used 20-day (System 1) and 55-day (System 2) breakouts. Breakouts have negative short-term edge (E5 < 1.0) due to false breakouts and countertrend pressure, but positive medium-term edge (E70 ≈ 1.20).

**Building Blocks**: The five elemental components of any trend-following system: breakouts, moving averages, volatility channels, time-based exits, and simple lookbacks. Any block can be tuned to react faster or slower; block choice matters less than consistent execution.

---

## C

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)**: The year-over-year growth rate of an investment assuming profits are reinvested. Used as the primary return metric in system evaluation. Must be assessed alongside drawdown (MAR ratio) and year-by-year consistency, not in isolation.

**Cognitive Biases**: Systematic distortions in human perception and decision-making. Key biases affecting traders: anchoring, recency bias, sunk cost effect, disposition effect, outcome bias, law of small numbers, gambler's fallacy, survivorship bias, availability bias.

**Complete Trading System**: A system that specifies all six components: markets to trade, position sizing, entry rules, stop-loss rules, exit rules for winning positions, and tactical execution mechanics. Systems missing any component delegate critical decisions to emotion.

**Countertrend Trading**: Strategies that profit from price reversals rather than trend continuation. The initial adverse movement after a breakout creates a genuine short-term edge for countertrend traders betting on breakout failure.

**Curve Fitting (Overfitting)**: Tuning a system's parameters or adding rules so specifically to historical data that the system loses predictive power out-of-sample. Complex systems with many parameters are especially vulnerable.

---

## D

**Disposition Effect**: Cognitive bias where traders sell winners too early (to lock in gains) and hold losers too long (hoping to break even). Caused by anchoring on entry price. One of three biases creating support/resistance levels.

**Diversification**: Spreading risk across multiple uncorrelated markets and/or trading systems. Market diversification increases the probability that at least one market is trending favorably. System diversification ensures some systems perform regardless of market regime.

**Donchian Channel Breakout**: Entry rule: buy when price exceeds the highest high of the previous N days, sell short when price drops below the lowest low of the previous N days. Named after Richard Donchian. The Turtles' primary entry mechanism.

**Drawdown**: A peak-to-trough decline in account equity. The primary psychological risk for traders. Most traders overestimate their drawdown tolerance—a 38% backtest drawdown becomes a 42%+ live drawdown shortly after starting, triggering fear, doubt, and strategy abandonment.

---

## E

**E-Ratio (Edge Ratio)**: E-ratio = average volatility-adjusted MFE / average volatility-adjusted MAE over N days. E-ratio > 1.0 indicates positive edge; ≈1.0 is random. Subscript indicates measurement horizon (E10 = 10 days, E70 = 70 days). Used to quantify entry signal edge across markets and time frames.

**Edge**: A systematic statistical advantage. In trading, positive expectation that persists over the long run. An edge can come from entries, exits, or portfolio filters. The Turtle edge was the combination of breakout entries + trend portfolio filter + volatility-normalized position sizing + disciplined execution.

**Electronic Exchanges**: Computer-based trading platforms replacing physical trading pits. Offer lower costs and faster execution but remove the psychological signals visible in traders' faces and body language.

**Entry Signal**: The specific price and market conditions that trigger a trade. Entry signals need edge over the system's intended holding period, not all periods. A medium-term trend system's entries may have negative short-term edge (normal and expected).

**Exit Signal**: Rules determining when to close winning positions. The Turtles used breakout-based exits: System 1 exited on a 10-day breakout against the position; System 2 on a 20-day breakout against the position. Exits determine what portion of favorable excursion is captured.

**Expectation**: The average amount won per dollar risked, computed from historical trade data. Positive expectation means the system wins over the long run. The Turtles were trained to focus on expectation, not individual trade outcomes, to avoid outcome bias.

---

## F

**Front-Running**: Buying or selling ahead of known large orders to profit from the price movement those orders will cause. A form of trader effect. Faith used fake orders in the opposite direction to disguise true intentions and prevent front-running.

---

## G

**Gambler's Fallacy**: The erroneous belief that past random outcomes influence future ones (e.g., "I've lost five times, so I'm due to win"). Leads traders to increase position size after losses in the mistaken belief that a win is "due."

---

## H

**Hedger**: Market participant seeking to eliminate price risk (e.g., Southwest Airlines hedging fuel costs, a farmer locking in crop prices). Hedgers pay a premium to transfer risk to speculators and provide the liquidity that speculators profit from.

---

## L

**Law of Small Numbers**: Cognitive bias where people draw statistically invalid conclusions from small sample sizes. In trading, manifests as judging a system after a handful of trades or assuming a short track record proves skill.

**Liquidity Risk**: The risk of being unable to buy or sell at the desired price—no buyer when you want to sell, no seller when you want to buy. Scalpers trade liquidity risk, capturing the bid-ask spread as compensation.

**Log-Scale Deception**: The tendency of logarithmic equity curves to visually compress large drawdowns, making them appear manageable. Always evaluate drawdowns on linear scales with actual dollar amounts.

---

## M

**MAR Ratio**: CAGR / Maximum Drawdown. Measures return per unit of pain. A higher MAR indicates smoother returns relative to risk. Used alongside Sharpe ratio for system evaluation.

**Maximum Adverse Excursion (MAE)**: The maximum price movement against a position from entry to a specified time horizon. Used in E-ratio calculation as the denominator—represents the "bad" price movement.

**Maximum Favorable Excursion (MFE)**: The maximum price movement in favor of a position from entry to a specified time horizon. Used in E-ratio calculation as the numerator—represents the "good" price movement.

**Mechanical Trading System**: A complete rule-based system that removes discretion from trading decisions. The Turtle system was fully mechanical, which Faith identifies as a major success factor: rules prevent fear and greed from overriding judgment.

**Money Management**: The science of controlling risk of ruin by sizing positions appropriately. The Turtles used N-based position sizing: unit = 1% of account / (N × dollars per point).

**Moving Average (MA)**: A continuously calculated average of price over N periods. Simple MA: average of N prior values. Exponential MA (EMA): blend of previous EMA with current price, responding faster. Crossovers (e.g., fast MA above slow MA) generate entry signals.

---

## N

**N (Average True Range, ATR)**: The 20-day exponential moving average of true range. N represents the average daily price range accounting for gaps. Used as the universal volatility yardstick for position sizing: N normalizes dollar volatility across all markets. Formula: N = (19 × PDN + TR) / 20.

**Negative Expectation**: A system where the average loss exceeds the average gain over time. Trading with negative expectation guarantees ruin, regardless of occasional large wins. Casino games like roulette have negative expectation for players.

---

## O

**Optimization Paradox**: The act of selecting specific parameters (e.g., 25-day vs. 30-day moving average) from historical data reduces the backtest's predictive value. Optimization fits noise as well as signal, creating results that won't repeat out-of-sample.

**Outcome Bias**: Judging a decision's quality by its outcome rather than by the quality of the decision process at the time it was made. The Turtles were explicitly trained to avoid this: a losing trade made correctly is better than a winning trade made recklessly.

**Overfitting**: See Curve Fitting.

---

## P

**Portfolio Filter**: A rule that determines which markets or trades to include based on market state. The Donchian Trend system's 50-day > 300-day MA filter eliminates counter-trend breakouts, improving E70-ratio from 1.20 to 1.33. Portfolio filters add adaptability without adding fragility.

**Position Sizing**: Determining how many contracts or shares to trade. The single most important trading decision. The Turtles used N-based sizing: 1 unit = 1% of account equity / (N × contract point value), pyramiding up to 4 units.

**Positive Expectation**: A system where the average gain exceeds the average loss over time. Trading with positive expectation is profitable over the long run. The Turtles believed in and trained to rely on positive expectation despite inevitable losing streaks.

**Price Risk**: The risk of adverse price movement. Speculators assume price risk from hedgers in exchange for the opportunity to profit. Farmers, airlines, and manufacturers hedge price risk; traders speculate on it.

**Price Shock**: A sudden, rapid price movement caused by catastrophe, political event, or economic disaster. Examples: the 1987 stock market crash and the September 11, 2001 attacks. Price shocks can erase years of profits in hours with no exit opportunity.

**Pyramiding**: Adding to winning positions as the trend progresses. The Turtles added up to 4 units total, each at ½N intervals from the prior entry. This layers into trends gradually rather than committing full size at breakout.

---

## R

**Random Effects**: The degree to which luck influences trading results. Simulations show 100 identical systems with the same edge produce returns from +17.5% to +53.3% CAGR through random variance alone. Track records cannot distinguish skill from luck with certainty.

**Recency Bias**: The tendency to overweight recent events when making decisions. After a string of losses, traders become overly cautious; after wins, overly aggressive. Trend followers who avoid recency bias continue trading through drawdowns while others capitulate.

**Risk of Ruin**: The probability of losing all trading capital through a string of losses. Increases disproportionately with position size—doubling bet size can triple or quintuple ruin probability. The primary consideration in position sizing.

**Robust Trading Program**: A trading program designed to perform well regardless of future market conditions. Built on two pillars: diversity (multiple uncorrelated markets/systems) and simplicity (few rules tied to durable concepts). Assumes the future is unknowable.

---

## S

**Scalper**: Short-term trader who captures the bid-ask spread repeatedly rather than speculating on directional moves. Provides liquidity to the market. Scalpers are the first leg of the hedger → scalper → speculator transaction chain.

**Sharpe Ratio**: A risk-adjusted return measure: (return – risk-free rate) / standard deviation of returns. Measures return per unit of volatility. Used alongside MAR ratio for comprehensive system evaluation.

**Simple Lookback**: A trend-following mechanism comparing current price to price N days ago. Buy if current > price[N days ago] + K × ATR. Trend following reduced to its purest, simplest form.

**Speculator (Position Trader)**: Market participant who assumes price risk in exchange for potential profit. Trades directional moves over days, weeks, or months. Speculators are the ultimate counterparty to hedgers.

**Sunk Cost Effect**: Cognitive bias where past investments influence current decisions. Traders hold losing positions because they've already "invested" time or drawdown; businesses continue failing projects because of money already spent. The rational decision ignores sunk costs entirely.

**System Death**: Permanent change in market dynamics causing a previously profitable system to stop working. Caused by structural market changes, regulatory shifts, or widespread adoption eroding the edge. Mitigated through diversification and simplicity.

---

## T

**Time-Based Exit**: Exiting a position after a predetermined number of days regardless of price action. The simplest possible exit type. Often reduces drawdowns by exiting before trend-following indicators signal the reversal.

**Trader Effect (Observer Effect)**: The phenomenon where trading a strategy changes the market conditions on which the strategy's edge depends. When too many traders exploit the same pattern, the edge dilutes or reverses. Proprietary systems have a structural advantage over widely-traded ones.

**Trend Following**: A trading approach that buys at the start of uptrends and sells short at the start of downtrends, exiting when the trend goes sideways. Based on the observation that markets exhibit sustained directional movement due to human behavioral biases.

**Trend Portfolio Filter**: A rule allowing long trades only when the 50-day MA > 300-day MA and short trades only when 50-day MA < 300-day MA. Eliminates counter-trend trades, substantially improving the edge ratio. The filter alone provides more edge than the breakout entry.

**True Range**: max(High − Low, High − PreviousClose, PreviousClose − Low). Accounts for overnight gaps in daily range calculation. The foundation of N (ATR) computation.

**Turtle**: A member of Richard Dennis and Bill Eckhardt's experimental trading group (1983-1988), trained to trade using a complete mechanical trend-following system. The experiment proved that trading could be taught to novices with the right rules and discipline.

---

## U

**Unit**: The Turtles' position sizing quantum. One unit = 1% of account equity / (N × dollars per point). Positions were built from 1 to 4 units, with each additional unit added at ½N intervals beyond the prior entry. Stop-loss was placed at 2N from the most recent entry.

---

## V

**Volatility Channel**: A band plotted above and below a moving average at a distance proportional to market volatility (ATR or standard deviation). Price breaking above the upper channel signals an uptrend; below the lower channel signals a downtrend. Channel width adapts to market conditions.

**Volatility Normalization**: Adjusting position sizes so that the dollar volatility of each position is equal across all markets. The Turtles used N to achieve this: positions in high-volatility markets get fewer contracts; low-volatility markets get more. Makes cross-market diversification genuinely effective.
