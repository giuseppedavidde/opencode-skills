# Way of the Turtle
**Author**: Curtis M. Faith | **Chapters**: 14 + Epilogue + Bonus | **Generated**: 2026-07-27

## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Cognitive Edge Framework
Markets are irrational because people are irrational. Eight cognitive biases create systematic price distortions that traders can exploit: **Loss Aversion** (losses hurt 2× gains), **Sunk Cost Effect** (can't release committed capital), **Disposition Effect** (sell winners, ride losers), **Outcome Bias** (judge by result not process), **Recency Bias** (overweight recent events), **Anchoring** (fixated on reference prices), **Bandwagon Effect** (follow the crowd), **Law of Small Numbers** (conclusions from insufficient data). Every bias is both a trap for the unaware and an opportunity for the disciplined.

### The Four Market States Model
Markets exist in one of four states defined by two axes: trend (none/strong) × volatility (quiet/volatile). **Trend followers** thrive in "trending & quiet"; **countertrend traders** in "stable & volatile"; **swing traders** in volatile markets regardless of trend. States shift — the Turtle does not predict the shift, only observes and adapts.

### The Probabilistic Thinking Model
Trading is like running an insurance company. The outcome of any single trade is unknowable, but the **distribution** of outcomes across many trades IS knowable. Turtles think in **R-multiples** (profit ÷ amount risked). A typical trend-following distribution: 65-70% small losses (≤1R), 20-25% medium wins (4-5R), 5% large wins (12-30R+). The system works because the right tail covers all losses. You don't need to predict; you need to survive long enough for the distribution to play out.

### The Points of Price Instability Framework
The best trades occur at edges where **support and resistance** — psychological battlegrounds created by anchoring and recency bias — are about to break. At these points, the distance between being right and wrong is minimal, making the cost of a stop small. Trend followers enter when S/R **breaks** (catching the cascade of capitulation); countertrend traders enter when S/R **holds** (catching the bounce).

### The N Factor — Volatility-Based Position Sizing
The Turtle innovation that equalizes risk across all markets. **N = ATR(20)** in dollar terms. **1 unit = 1% of account ÷ N**. Hard limits: 4 units/market, 6/correlated group, 10-12/direction. This automatically reduces exposure in volatile markets and survived the 1987 crash — stop-distance-based sizing would have blown up.

### The Robust Testing Framework
Standard metrics (CAGR%, MAR, Sharpe) are fragile — a 3-month endpoint shift can change CAGR% by 3+ points. Use **RAR%** (regressed annual return, 30× less sensitive), **R-cubed** (risk/reward accounting for drawdown severity AND duration), and **Robust Sharpe** instead. Validate with parameter scrambling (±25%), rolling optimization windows, and Monte Carlo with 20-day equity curve chunk scrambling (preserves autocorrelation of bad days).

### The Four Turtle Commandments
1. **Trade with an edge** — positive expectation over the long run
2. **Manage risk** — stay in the game to realize the edge; ruin is permanent
3. **Be consistent** — execute every signal; the plan means nothing without action
4. **Keep it simple** — simple systems hold up; complexity invites curve-fitting

### The Ego Is the Enemy
The ego wants to be right, predict, and know secrets. Trading requires accepting being wrong most of the time, not predicting, and following simple rules. **Systematic trading removes ego from decisions.** Humility is the essential trader trait. "Having secret knowledge makes us feel special; possessing simple truths does not."
