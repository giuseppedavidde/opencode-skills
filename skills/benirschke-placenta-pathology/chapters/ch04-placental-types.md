# Chapter 4: Placental Types

## Core Idea
Placental classification is comparative and descriptive, not hierarchical. Three independent axes are used: shape of the chorionic sac, type of maternal-fetal interdigitation, and tissue layers of the interface. These axes do not form a phylogenetic ladder. Grosser's categories were once read as a progression from primitive to advanced; molecular phylogenetics shows the epitheliochorial state is derived and arose by convergence in at least three clades. Layer count does not predict exchange efficiency, because capillary indentation and transporter density matter more.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Shape.** Diffuse (placenta membranacea), with interdigitations over the whole sac; cotyledonary, restricted to discrete caruncles; zonary, an equatorial ring; discoid, restricted to one or more poles.

**Interdigitation.** Folded (simple ridges); lamellar (branched parallel lamellae); trabecular (between lamellar and villous); villous (treelike branches); labyrinthine (trophoblast block penetrated by channels, greatest surface area per unit volume).

**Interface (Grosser, extended by Enders).** Epitheliochorial with six layers; endotheliochorial with trophoblast apposed to maternal endothelium; hemochorial with trophoblast bathed in maternal blood, subdivided into hemotrichorial, hemodichorial, and hemomonochorial.

**Blood flow interrelations.** Concurrent (theoretically most inefficient, absent in mammals); countercurrent (rodents, lagomorphs); simple crosscurrent (carnivores); double crosscurrent (lower primates, pig); multivillous (ruminants, higher primates, human).

**Nutrition and membrane type.** Histotrophic uptake of secretions or cells versus hemotrophic exchange. Choriovitelline placentas are vascularized by yolk sac vessels and are older; chorioallantoic placentas are vascularized by the allantois and are definitive in eutherians.

## Key Concepts
- Epitheliochorial interface: maternal endothelium, endometrial connective tissue, endometrial epithelium, trophoblast, chorionic connective tissue, fetal endothelium — six layers.
- Hemochorial interface: maternal blood directly bathes syncytiotrophoblast. The human placenta is hemodichorial in the first trimester and hemomonochorial at term.
- Fetal-to-placental weight ratios: human 6:1; pig 9:1; cat 8:1; rabbit 6:1; sheep 10:1; rat 10:1; Tupaia 18:1; guinea pig 20:1; chinchilla 30:1.
- Species with labyrinthine placentas have gestation lengths about 44% of those of similarly sized species with villous or trabecular placentas.
- Human placenta at term: discoid, villous, hemomonochorial, multivillous flow.
- The human interface changes during gestation. This appears unique to humans and possibly the great apes. Oxygen concentration rises threefold and may precipitate complications specific to human pregnancy.
- Placental mammals probably arose after the Cretaceous-Paleogene extinction, 66–65 million years ago; large placental mammals expanded in the Eocene, about 40 million years ago.
- Imprinting evolved with placentation. Paternally expressed Igf2 raises surface area, thins the exchange barrier, and raises fetal demand. Maternally expressed H19 and Igf2r reduce surface area and fetal demand.
- Elevated IGF in Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome is associated with placentomegaly and chorangiomas. Silver-Russell syndrome has been associated with villous hypoplasia.
- The human intervillous space contains endometrial gland secretions in the first trimester and maternal blood in the second and third.

## Anti-patterns
- Reading Grosser's scheme as an evolutionary ladder with epitheliochorial as primitive.
- Assuming a six-layered barrier is functionally thicker than a three-layered one; capillaries indent deeply into both epithelia.
- Ranking placental types by efficiency. All support healthy offspring.
- Treating human shape variants as homologues of diffuse or duplex placentas in other species; in humans they are disordered development from abnormal implantation.
- Extrapolating transport data across species without checking interface type and transporter density.
- Interpreting altered imprinting as the cause of most growth restriction; methylation at imprinting control regions is usually unchanged.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify any placenta on three axes: shape, interdigitation, interface.
2. Use the human baseline: discoid, villous, hemomonochorial at term, multivillous flow.
3. Do not infer function from layer number; use surface area, membrane thickness, and flow geometry.
4. Treat epitheliochorial placentation as derived, not primitive.
5. Check species-specific interface before applying animal data to human pathology.
