# Chapter 31: Innovations in Placental Pathology

## Core Idea
New methods now let pathologists and scientists profile the placenta at molecular, cellular, and functional levels rather than by morphology alone. Genome-wide RNA profiling, single-cell methods, circulating biomarkers, extracellular vesicles, stem-cell and organoid models, advanced imaging, and spatial omics each answer a different question. The value lies in integrating these data with conventional histopathology, not in replacing it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transcriptomic strategy ladder:** whole-tissue microarray or RNA sequencing; cell-type purification by sorting; laser-capture microdissection (LCM) of selected regions; single-cell RNA sequencing of thousands of cells per sample.
- **Circulating biomarker classes:** placental proteins (hCG, PAPP-A, AFP, sFlt1, PlGF), cell-free fetal DNA (cffDNA) and fetal fraction, placental microRNAs, and extracellular vesicles (EVs).
- **In vitro model hierarchy:** 2D primary cells and lines; human trophoblast stem cells (hTSC); human pluripotent stem cell (hPSC) and induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) derivatives; expanded potential stem cells (EPSC); placental organoids; placenta-on-a-chip; STEMbryos.
- **Imaging toolkit:** 3D and 4D ultrasound with power Doppler, shear-wave elastography (SWE) with acoustic radiation force impulse (ARFI), and functional MRI (BOLD, arterial spin labeling, dynamic contrast enhancement, diffusion-weighted imaging).
- **Spatial omics:** digital spatial profiling (DSP), imaging mass cytometry (IMC), multiplex ion beam imaging (MIBI), and mass spectrometric imaging (MALDI-MSI, DESI-MSI), plus high-resolution in situ hybridization.

## Key Concepts
- Bulk placental transcriptomics in preeclampsia and SGA identifies two clusters: a "canonical" cluster with hypoxia-associated gene expression and maternal vascular malperfusion, and an "immunological" cluster with perivillous fibrin deposition, chronic deciduitis, and higher fetal vascular malperfusion scores. Known genes include HTRA4, FLT, LEP, plus TET3 and ZNF175.
- Gestational diabetes shows altered metabolic and immune pathways; intrahepatic cholestasis of pregnancy shows altered G-protein-coupled receptor and VEGF signaling with hypovascular villi and chronic inflammation.
- sFlt1 is higher and PlGF lower in preeclampsia, and because these analytes change across gestation their clinical use is restricted to specific gestational ages. hCG rises rapidly from 4 weeks. cffDNA derives largely from trophoblast apoptosis and its fetal fraction rises with gestation.
- MicroRNAs: the chromosome 19 miRNA cluster (C19MC) is the largest human miRNA cluster and may confer viral resistance. In severe preeclampsia 51 miRNAs were differentially expressed (22 up, 5 down); miR-141, miR-144, miR-221, and miR-29a were confirmed by RT-PCR. miR-376c, miR-377, miR-154, and miR-411 are implicated in trophoblast proliferation, invasion, and apoptosis.
- EVs: exosome markers are tetraspanins CD63, CD9, and CD81. Syncytiotrophoblast microvesicles (STBMs) measure 100–1000 nm and are increased in preeclampsia. Syncytial nuclear aggregates contain about 60 nuclei and are released from 6 weeks' gestation. Placental alkaline phosphatase (PLAP)-positive EV counts differ in early-onset preeclampsia, and the placenta-derived to total EV ratio correlates with birth weight percentile.
- Fetal central nervous system EVs positive for contactin-2 (CNTN-2) are detectable in maternal plasma in the first and second trimester and may report fetal brain development.
- hTSC: mouse TSC require FGF4 and TGFβ/Activin. Human TSC derive from blastocysts or first-trimester placenta before 10 weeks, but not later, using TGFβ plus histone deacetylase inhibition with WNT, EGF, and HGF activation; they express TP63, ELF5, GATA3, and TEAD4 and differentiate into syncytiotrophoblast and extravillous trophoblast. hTSC have also been derived from complete hydatidiform mole, showing reduced contact inhibition and aberrant imprinted gene expression.
- iPSC-derived cytotrophoblast-like cells are generated with a BMP4-based protocol; EPSC derive directly from 4- to 8-cell embryos and differentiate into trophoblast independently of BMP4.
- Placental organoids from first-trimester CTB form an outer CTB layer and an inner STB core — an inverted chorionic villus. Placenta-on-a-chip uses multi-channel microfluidics with hydrogel support. STEMbryos model peri-implantation development and raise unresolved ethical and regulatory issues.
- Elastography: ARFI displaces tissue 1–20 µm and reports shear-wave speed (Vs, m/s); higher Vs means stiffer tissue, and growth-restricted placentas have higher Vs. SWE has high intra- and interobserver reliability, and ARFI produced no thermal or mechanical damage in studied placentas.
- MRI: placentas with infarction, hemorrhage, or infection have lower apparent diffusion coefficient (ADC) values (146 vs 177 sec/mm²). Adding diffusion-weighted MRI to umbilical artery Doppler raises sensitivity for placental insufficiency from 73% to 100% with 99% specificity. BOLD T2* signal falls with placental dysfunction.
- DSP images more than 20 protein or RNA targets in a region of interest using UV-photocleavable oligonucleotide barcodes counted on nCounter. MSI maps lipids and metabolites directly in tissue sections.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not interpret whole-tissue expression data without accounting for mixed cell types; cellular heterogeneity inflates variance.
- Do not assume a molecular cluster maps one-to-one onto a histologic lesion; the correlations are probabilistic.
- Do not treat 2D trophoblast lines as equivalent to villous trophoblast in vivo.
- Do not overstate EV biomarker readiness; isolation and quantification methods differ and generate functionally different populations.
- Do not ignore the ethical limits of embryo-like models, including the 14-day rule.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pair every molecular or imaging innovation with the histologic lesion it is meant to explain.
2. Use sFlt1/PlGF, cffDNA fetal fraction, and EV or miRNA panels as research tools until validated for the specific population.
3. Prefer hTSC or organoids when the question concerns trophoblast differentiation; prefer whole tissue when the question concerns villous architecture.
4. Apply SWE and diffusion-weighted MRI as adjuncts to Doppler, not replacements.
5. Reserve DSP and MSI for spatially defined questions that bulk assays cannot answer.
