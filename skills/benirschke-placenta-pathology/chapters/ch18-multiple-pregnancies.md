# Chapter 18: Multiple Pregnancies

## Core Idea
Chorionicity, not zygosity, determines outcome. Monochorionic placentas share a circulation through placental anastomoses, and every major complication of twinning — twin-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), twin anemia-polycythemia sequence (TAPS), acardiac twinning, and monoamniotic cord entanglement — follows from that shared circulation. The pathologist's decisive act is therefore to establish chorionicity and amnionicity by membrane and vascular anatomy, and then to map the anastomoses. Zygosity is a genetic question that the placenta can only estimate.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Chorionicity and amnionicity classification.** Diamniotic-dichorionic (DiDi), diamniotic-monochorionic (DiMo), and monoamniotic-monochorionic (MoMo). The type follows the timing of monozygotic splitting: DiDi at 4 weeks, DiMo at 5 weeks, and MoMo versus DiMo at 7 weeks' gestation. DiDi dividing membranes are opaque and thick; DiMo membranes are thin and translucent with no blood vessels, and a "T section" shows two amnions only.

**Anastomosis-based classification of monochorionic placentas (Bermúdez et al.).** Four categories with corresponding TTTS frequency: (a) no anastomoses, 0% TTTS; (b) deep arteriovenous (A-V) anastomoses only, 100% TTTS; (c) superficial anastomoses only, 5.6% TTTS; (d) deep plus superficial anastomoses, 79.12% TTTS.

**Quintero staging of TTTS.** Stages I–IV assigned from sonographic and clinical parameters, widely used to time and compare therapy; the text notes frequent local modification.

**TAPS definition.** Significant hemoglobin difference between twins *without* amniotic fluid discordance, defined as a hemoglobin discordance greater than 8 g/dL.

**Acardiac twin criteria.** Diagnosis of acardiac fetus rests on defined structural criteria; organisation should not be used as a criterion because it occurs in other malformations.

## Key Concepts
- Twinning rates: 31 per 1000 in mid-Europe, 45 per 1000 in west and east Europe, 6.1 per 1000 in Japan. Monozygotic twinning is about 1.2% of births versus 0.45% in an earlier era; dizygotic twinning is about 51.4% of twin pairs in one genotyping series.
- Of 668 pairs, 80% had DiDi placentas and 90% of those were dizygotic; among same-sex dichorionic twins, 8% proved monozygotic.
- Velamentous cord insertion is found in up to 45% of monochorionic twin placentas, far above the singleton rate.
- In 126 cases of severe TTTS, A-V anastomoses were present in all, 31% also had artery-to-artery (A-A) anastomoses, and 12% had vein-to-vein (V-V) connections. A-A anastomoses protect against severe TTTS and predict higher double survival; V-V connections reduce perinatal survival.
- Persisting anastomoses remain after laser in 32%, most inconsequential.
- Monoamniotic twins: nearly 40% perinatal mortality overall, falling significantly after 24 weeks; no deaths after 32–35 weeks in several series. A meta-analysis found intrauterine demise in 4.3% at 24–30 weeks, 1.0% at 31–32 weeks, and 2.2% at 33–34 weeks. Pooled series: 304 of 458 twins survived (66%), 154 died (34%).
- Cord entanglement is characteristic of MoMo pregnancy and is documented even at 12 weeks.
- TAPS may occur spontaneously (usually after 26 weeks) or after laser surgery for TTTS, presenting within 1–5 weeks of the procedure.
- Acardiac twins: holoacardius amorphous forms exist; examples include a 40 g acardiac with a 2910 g co-twin, and a 1210 g acardiac delivered at 28 weeks. About 10% of co-twins are themselves malformed. Acardiacs may occur in MoMo and in higher-order multiples (an acardiac quintuplet is described).
- Fetus papyraceus and vanishing twin: a subamnionic sac containing debris is the remnant of a DiMo placenta; a separate embryo may be found within the membranes.
- Discordance of relative cardiac size is described as the most important means of diagnosing transfusion syndrome, and can be recognised sonographically even in dead twins.

## Anti-patterns
- **Equating monozygosity with monochorionicity.** Same-sex dichorionic twins include monozygotic pairs, and 8% is a realistic figure.
- **Diagnosing chorionicity from membrane thickness alone at term.** Thin membranes can be DiMo, but confirm with a T section and vessel pattern.
- **Relying on postnatal hematology to diagnose TTTS.** Rapid blood shifts after delivery, and after one twin dies, negate hematologic values.
- **Diagnosing TTTS from hemoglobin difference alone.** TAPS is defined by hemoglobin discordance without fluid discordance, and the two entities differ.
- **Assuming a monochorionic placenta with no visible anastomoses is safe.** Absence of anastomoses predicts no TTTS; the danger lies in deep A-V connections.
- **Calling an acardiac a teratoma.** Organisation is not a diagnostic criterion; the lesion is a malformed fetus.

## Key Takeaways
1. Establish chorionicity and amnionicity first, and state the evidence: membrane layers, T section, and vascular equator.
2. Map anastomoses in every monochorionic placenta, distinguishing deep A-V from superficial A-A and V-V, because the pattern predicts TTTS.
3. Apply Quintero staging language when reporting TTTS placentas so that pathology aligns with clinical management.
4. Use the greater-than-8 g/dL hemoglobin discordance without fluid discordance to identify TAPS.
5. In monoamniotic twins, document cord entanglement and cord knots, and counsel by the 66% pooled survival.
6. Look actively for fetus papyraceus, membrane-embedded embryos, and acardiac co-twins in every twin placenta.
