# Chapter 3: Microscopic Survey

## Core Idea
The chapter sets the normal histologic baseline at the two ends of gestation, using a 6-week in situ specimen and a 40-week delivered placenta, so that later pathology is read against a reference. It treats the placenta as compartments in vertical order: chorionic plate, intervillous space with villi, basal plate, plus cell islands, septa, anchoring villi, and endometrial glands. Low-power pattern recognition carries the diagnosis, not high-power detail.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Vertical survey section.** One section from chorionic plate, through intervillous space, to basal plate. Obtainable in second and third trimester and from first-trimester hysterectomy specimens. Products of conception are usually unsuitable, because basal plate, septa, anchoring villi, and cell columns are destroyed or mixed with villi.

**First-trimester inventory.** Chorionic plate; intervillous space; cell islands; septa; anchoring villi with cell columns; basal plate; endometrial glands; spiral arteries.

**Terminologic precision.** "Syncytial cells" and "syncytiotrophoblasts" are wrong: the syncytiotrophoblast is one continuous, uninterrupted, postmitotic layer.

**Villous type identification.** Mesenchymal, immature intermediate, mature intermediate, terminal, and stem villi, distinguished by caliber, stroma, and vessel type.

## Key Concepts
- At 6 weeks p.m., intervillous pores are several hundred micrometers wide because villous growth is sparse.
- The first-trimester chorionic plate usually lacks amnion, because the coelom still separates the amniotic sac from the plate. Its fetal surface carries incomplete mesothelium over thick chorionic mesoderm; toward the intervillous space, syncytiotrophoblast is later replaced by fibrinoid.
- Villous cytotrophoblast (Langhans' cells) is the proliferating stem population that fuses with syncytiotrophoblast.
- Nucleated red cells fill fetal villous vessels for the first 2 months and create high resistance, so effective fetal flow begins only near the end of the first trimester.
- Before 8 weeks p.m., even the largest villi have homogeneous mesenchymal stroma and no arteries or veins.
- Mesenchymal villi measure 60–200 μm, have dense cellular stroma, few vessels and little collagen, and end in syncytial sprouts.
- Immature intermediate villi are bulbous with reticular stroma, and Hofbauer cells lie in stromal channels.
- Decidua basalis measures 5–6 mm at 6 weeks p.m. and about 1 mm at 20 weeks.
- Endometrial glands are active for 3 months, then regress to epithelial remnants.
- Endovascular trophoblast plugs the spiral artery mouths during the first trimester; channels open at the start of the second trimester and begin the maternal circulation.
- At term, intervillous width varies from subchorionic lakes to capillary-dimension pores.
- Amnion is a single cuboidal or columnar layer; amniotic mesoderm measures 15–30 μm; nearly 50% of mature placentas show squamous metaplasia up to 15 cell layers.
- Villous trees measure 1–4 cm. Stem villi range from 80 μm to several thousand μm and contain muscular arteries and veins. Mature intermediate villi measure 60–100 μm and lack muscular vessels. Terminal villi measure 40–80 μm and contain sinusoidally dilated capillaries.
- Immature intermediate villi persist at term below 10% of villous volume and are absent in hypermature placentas.
- Only a small percentage of third-trimester syncytial knots are true protrusions or bridges; most are tangential sections.
- Perivillous fibrinoid replaces the trophoblastic cover; intravillous fibrinoid lies beneath an intact surface.
- Cell islands enlarge through pregnancy and may develop central cysts by degeneration and liquefaction.

## Anti-patterns
- Using termination specimens to study normal villous architecture.
- Reading tangential sections of syncytiotrophoblast as syncytial knots.
- Confusing cross sections of mature intermediate villi with terminal villi; longitudinal sections are diagnostic.
- Calling reticular stroma edema.
- Attributing wide intervillous pores to disease without allowing for delivery mode and postpartum blood loss.
- Expecting an amnion on a first-trimester chorionic plate.

## Key Takeaways
1. Read the placenta vertically: plate, intervillous space, basal plate, then adnexal structures.
2. Use the 6-week and 40-week pictures as anchors and interpolate maturity.
3. Identify villous type by caliber plus stroma plus vessel type.
4. Expect most term syncytial knots to be artifact.
5. Remember that the maternal arterial circulation starts only in the second trimester.
