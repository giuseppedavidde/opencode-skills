# Chapter 15: Anatomy and Pathology of the Placental Membranes

## Core Idea
The fetal membranes are a load-bearing, self-repairing composite whose normal histology is dominated by maturation artefacts, so most "lesions" found at the membrane are variants rather than disease. Rupture is a programmed biomechanical event, not a purely inflammatory one: the membranes remodel continuously through microfractures and fail when remodeling fails. The pathologist's central discipline is therefore to separate four categories — normal maturation, mechanical consequence, marker of an intrauterine environment, and true pathology.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Layer anatomy of the membranes.** Amnion comprises epithelium, basement membrane, compact layer, fibroblast layer, and spongy (intermediate) layer; chorion comprises trophoblast, chorionic mesoderm, and decidua. Tensile strength resides in the amount and arrangement of fibres in the compact layer of amnionic mesenchyme; the amnion is stronger than the chorion.

**Extrachorial placenta classification.** *Circumvallate*: a complete circumferential ring with plicated, folded membranes inserting inward from the margin, leaving "naked" placental tissue at the periphery (placenta extrachorialis). *Circummarginate*: the same inward insertion but with no plication, the protruding edge covered only by fibrin. The two blend, and partial circumvallation is common.

**Amnion nodosum versus squamous metaplasia (the key normal-versus-pathology pair).**
- *Amnion nodosum*: small granules on the placental surface, most pronounced centrally, occasionally extending onto membranes; composed of vernix and hair embedded in amnionic epithelium; result of prolonged deficient amnionic fluid. Associated with renal agenesis, prolonged amniorrhea, the "stuck twin" of twin transfusion, diamnionic acardiac twins, and sirenomelia. "Vernix granuloma" is a misnomer — they are not granulomas.
- *Squamous metaplasia*: focal keratinization of amnionic epithelium, visible as tiny concentric hydrophobic elevations, most prominent around cord insertion. It betrays maturity and is absent in immature placentas.

## Key Concepts
- Membrane surface area by ultrasound: 1037 cm² at 25–29 weeks, 1376 cm² at 30–34 weeks, 1876 cm² at term. Expelled membranes measure only 737, 855, and 1115 cm², showing that membranes are stretched in utero.
- Average membrane thickness 0.56 mm; the amnion contributes one-third. Average tensile strength 205 g/cm, range 50–500 g/cm.
- Membranes can expand to twice normal size during pregnancy and labour. Minimal stretch beyond the elastic limit causes an acute rise in tension and catastrophic failure.
- Adhesion between amnion and choriodecidua (T-peel) ranges 0.017–0.262 N/cm, and is significantly greater adjacent to the placental disc than remote from it.
- Amnions from pregnancies with preterm premature rupture have higher, not lower, tensile strength than those with timely rupture; membranes thin appreciably at the rupture site.
- Microfractures are defined by amnion cell puckering, basement membrane and collagen matrix degradation, and tunnels into the matrix with migrating cells. They increase with preterm premature rupture and oxidative stress.
- Squamous metaplasia is present in at least one-fourth, and up to 60%, of term placentas.
- Circumvallate frequency across series ranges 0.62–18.3%; circummarginate 1.8–25.5%. Circumvallation occurs in less than 2% of deliveries in some series.
- Circumvallate associations: preterm delivery 64.1%, placental abruption 10.9%, emergency cesarean 45.6%, small for gestational age 36.9%, neonatal death 8.9%, NICU admission 55.4%, chronic lung disease 33.9%. Sensitivity for detecting circumvallation antenatally is 28.8% with specificity 99.9%.
- Meconium staining: 15.3% at term, 27% at 1–2 weeks past term, 31.5% beyond 42 weeks. Microscopic meconium in 17.81% of 12,951 placentas; 19% in another series.
- A 405 nm absorption peak, traditionally read as meconium, is often methemoglobin; it is not correlated with fetal outcome and is more frequent with circumvallate placenta and abruption.
- Hemosiderin forms within cells after about 24 hours; hematoidin crystals follow in about 7 days. Neither can be reliably distinguished from bilirubin by light microscopy or spectrophotometry.
- Vernix caseosa is 81% water, 9% lipid, 10% protein. Vernix can dissect beneath the amnion and embolize.
- Amniotic fluid embolism caused maternal death in 24.8% of cases; 70% occurred during labour, 11% after vaginal delivery, 19% during cesarean section.
- About 80% of subchorial hematomas recognized before 20 weeks resolve.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling squamous metaplasia "amnion nodosum."** Metaplasia marks maturity and sits near the cord insertion; nodosum marks oligohydramnios and sits on the placental surface. The term "vernix granuloma" is also wrong.
- **Treating meconium as proof of hypoxia.** Meconium is common, gestational-age dependent, and its pigment peak is unreliable.
- **Reading every subamnionic cyst, cartilage nodule, or yolk sac remnant as disease.** These are normal vestiges; an epidermoid cyst or omphalomesenteric remnant is a curiosity, not a lesion.
- **Assuming ruptured membranes are mechanically weak.** Amnions from preterm premature rupture are stronger; failure reflects failed remodeling, not global weakness.
- **Confusing circumvallate placenta with extramembranous pregnancy.** Both show circumvallation, but extramembranous pregnancy follows early membrane rupture with the fetus outside the sac.
- **Diagnosing amniotic fluid embolism on a smear alone.** The finding of squames in pulmonary vessels is not by itself absolutely diagnostic.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use the amnion nodosum versus squamous metaplasia pair as the template for variant-versus-lesion calls throughout the membranes.
2. Report amnion nodosum as evidence of prolonged oligohydramnios and prompt a renal and twin-transfusion review.
3. Describe extrachorial placentas as circumvallate (plicated) or circummarginate (non-plicated), and state the proportion of chorion frondosum remaining.
4. Interpret meconium with gestational age and do not infer hypoxia from pigment alone.
5. Recognize microfracture biology as the mechanistic explanation for preterm premature rupture.
6. Treat amniotic fluid embolism as a clinical diagnosis with supportive histology, given a 24.8% maternal mortality.
