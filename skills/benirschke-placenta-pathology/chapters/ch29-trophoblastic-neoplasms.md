# Chapter 29: Gestational Trophoblastic Neoplasms

## Core Idea
Gestational trophoblastic tumors are rare neoplasms derived from defined trophoblast subpopulations, classified by the 2020 WHO scheme into choriocarcinoma, placental site trophoblastic tumor (PSTT), and epithelioid trophoblastic tumor (ETT). Each recapitulates a different stage or site of trophoblast: villous trophoblast for choriocarcinoma, implantation-site intermediate trophoblast for PSTT, and chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast for ETT. Diagnosis is difficult because the tumors are rare, criteria for precursor lesions are uncertain, and persistent disease is often treated clinically without tissue. Immunohistochemistry and genotyping are essential.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **WHO 2020 classification by putative cell origin:** chorionic villous trophoblast gives hydatidiform mole (complete, partial, invasive) and atypical villous lesions; villous intermediate trophoblast gives gestational choriocarcinoma; implantation-site intermediate trophoblast gives PSTT and exaggerated placental site reaction (EPS); chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast gives ETT and placental site nodule/atypical placental site nodule; mixed intermediate trophoblast gives mixed trophoblastic tumors.

- **FIGO/WHO prognostic scoring (2002):** age 40 years or more = 1; antecedent mole 0, abortion 1, term 2; interval less than 4 months 0, 4–6 months 1, 7–12 months 2, more than 12 months 4; hCG less than 10³ 0, 10³–10⁴ 1, 10⁴–10⁵ 2, more than 10⁶ 4; tumor size less than 3 cm 0, 3–5 cm 1, more than 5 cm 2; metastases lung 0, spleen or kidney 1, GI tract 2, brain or liver 4; number of metastases 1–4 = 1, 5–8 = 2, more than 8 = 4; prior failed single-agent chemotherapy 2, two or more agents 4. Low risk is 6 or less; high risk is 7 or above.
- **EPS versus PSTT criteria:** absence of a mass, presence of chorionic villi, mononuclear cells with evenly distributed multinucleated forms, no mitotic activity, and low Ki-67 favor EPS.

## Key Concepts
- **Choriocarcinoma:** mean age 30 years; incidence 1 in 40,000 pregnancies in the United States and Europe, 3–9 per 40,000 in Southeast Asia. Risk after complete mole 2–3%, after partial mole 0.1–0.5%, and 1 in 24,000–50,000 normal pregnancies. Serum hCG invariably exceeds 10,000 mIU/ml. Postmolar diagnosis at a mean of 13 months (1–48 months); after term pregnancy at 1–3 months.
- Choriocarcinoma gross: bulky, dark red, shaggy, hemorrhagic and necrotic uterine masses with deep myometrial invasion; it can arise in cervix, tube, ovary, or as an intraplacental tumor.
- Choriocarcinoma histology: biphasic or triphasic proliferation of cytotrophoblast, syncytiotrophoblast, and villous intermediate trophoblast, with no intrinsic tumor stroma or vasculature, extensive hemorrhage and necrosis. Atypia is marked.
- Choriocarcinoma immunohistochemistry: syncytiotrophoblast diffusely positive for hCG and HSD3B1; intermediate trophoblast positive for CK18, CK8, AE1/AE3, CAM 5.2, Mel-CAM, HLA-G, p63, CEA, MUC-4, hPL, Sall4, GATA3; PLAP negative or weak; Ki-67 40–90%.
- Choriocarcinoma genetics: majority have an XX genome; may be androgenetic or biparental. STR genotyping identifies the causal pregnancy and separates gestational from non-gestational choriocarcinoma, which is chemoresistant.
- **PSTT:** age 20–63 years, mean 31; interval 2 weeks to 17 years, median 12–18 months; hCG below 1,000 mIU/ml; expansile to infiltrative solid mass. Tumor cells replace the vascular wall and split myometrial smooth muscle fibers at the periphery. Atypia moderate to marked.
- PSTT immunohistochemistry: diffuse hPL and Mel-CAM, scattered multinucleated cells positive for hCG and inhibin, strong cytokeratin AE1/3 and CK18, GPC3 positive; p63, SALL4, and p40 negative; Ki-67 5–30% (10–30% in the text). Prominent paranuclear filaments on electron microscopy.
- PSTT outcome: recurrence or metastasis in 25–35%; overall survival 70% and recurrence-free survival 73% at 10 years. Clear cytoplasm is an independent poor prognostic factor. PSTT is not chemosensitive and requires hysterectomy; FIGO scoring does not apply.
- Poor-prognosis factors in a 326-patient PSTT/ETT analysis: age over 40, interval over 4 years, FIGO stage, lymphovascular invasion, deep myometrial invasion, hCG over 10,000 IU/ml, tumor cell necrosis; only interval and stage remained significant on multivariate analysis.
- **ETT:** age 15–48 years, average 36; interval 1–25 years, average 6.2 years; hCG below 3,000 mIU/ml; cervix, lower uterine segment, or corpus; pushing border; sheets, nests, and cords with geographic necrosis, dystrophic calcification, keratin-like material, and colonization of mucosal epithelium. Chorionic-type intermediate trophoblast with mild to moderate atypia. Diffuse p63 with rare hPL and Mel-CAM positivity; Ki-67 10–25%.
- Choriocarcinoma responds to chemotherapy with over 90% survival even with widespread disease; PSTT and ETT do not.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not diagnose PSTT from a curettage showing many intermediate trophoblast cells and infiltrative growth; EPS shows no mass, retains villi, and lacks mitoses.
- Do not apply FIGO/WHO risk scoring to PSTT or ETT; it guides choriocarcinoma chemotherapy only.
- Do not rely on morphology alone to separate gestational from non-gestational choriocarcinoma; use STR genotyping.
- Do not expect high hCG in PSTT; values are usually below 1,000 mIU/ml.
- Do not treat a p63-positive trophoblastic tumor as choriocarcinoma; diffuse p63 with low Ki-67 indicates ETT.

## Key Takeaways
1. Assign a trophoblastic tumor to its cell of origin first, then confirm with the marker profile.
2. Use Ki-67 to separate choriocarcinoma (40–90%) from PSTT (5–30%) and ETT (10–25%).
3. Order STR genotyping whenever a gestational versus non-gestational origin changes therapy.
4. Treat PSTT surgically; chemotherapy response cannot be assumed.
5. Document interval from antecedent pregnancy and FIGO stage, the two independent prognostic factors in PSTT/ETT.
