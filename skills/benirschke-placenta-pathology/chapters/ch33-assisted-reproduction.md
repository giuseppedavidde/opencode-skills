# Chapter 33: Assisted Reproductive Technologies

## Core Idea
Assisted reproductive technology (ART) is now used widely: an estimated 8 million children have been born by ART, and up to 6% of newborns in Europe and 1.5% in the United States are conceived this way. Singleton ART pregnancies carry a consistent but modest excess of preterm birth, low birth weight, small-for-gestational-age, and perinatal mortality. Part of the excess reflects subfertility itself, but sibling comparisons show that ART manipulations also contribute. The placenta is the likely mediator, yet no single histopathologic signature separates ART from natural conception.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Outcome comparison designs:** ART versus natural conception (NC); fresh versus frozen-thawed embryo transfer (FET); donor-oocyte versus autologous-oocyte ART; sibling comparisons that control for parental factors.
- **Placental assessment domains:** clinical outcomes, placental weight and weight ratio, 3D power Doppler vascular indices, histopathology (maternal vascular malperfusion, fetal vascular malperfusion, syncytial knots, fibrin), angiogenic markers, serum biomarkers, placental metabolism, and epigenetics.
- **Maternal vascular malperfusion (MVM) lesion set:** villous infarction, accelerated villous maturity, distal villous hypoplasia, increased syncytial knots, decidual vasculopathy, and fibrinoid deposition.
- **Fetal vascular malperfusion (FVM) lesion set:** avascular villi, villous edema, stem villus vasculopathy, and intervillous thrombus.

## Key Concepts
- Adjusted risks in ART singletons versus NC: preterm birth 1.41–2.04; very preterm birth 1.68–3.07; low birth weight 1.6–1.7; very low birth weight 1.8–3.0; small for gestational age 1.4–1.6; perinatal mortality 1.7–2.0.
- FET lowers the risk of preterm birth, low birth weight, and SGA, but raises the risk of large for gestational age, high birth weight, and hypertensive disorders of pregnancy.
- **Placenta previa:** a sibling comparison of 845,384 pregnancies found a threefold higher risk after ART. Endometrial thickness greater than 9 mm before transfer is a strong risk factor.
- **Placenta accreta:** women after ART are about two to three times more likely to develop accreta. A meta-analysis found a threefold to fourfold increase after FET (48,158 FET versus 69,486 fresh transfers).
- **Cord insertion:** in a multicenter study of 100 placentas, marginal insertion was present in 15% and velamentous insertion in 14%, against referral frequencies of about 5% and 1%.
- **Placental weight:** one study found higher weight (536 ± 122 g versus 489 ± 119 g) and placental-to-fetal weight ratio (0.17 ± 0.04 versus 0.15 ± 0.03) in ART; several larger cohorts found no difference.
- **MVM:** among 1140 placentas (929 fresh, 211 frozen), 69.5% of fresh and 73.0% of frozen-transfer placentas showed MVM, a nonsignificant difference.
- **Infarction:** 8% versus 6% extended infarcts in one controlled study; 11.1% versus 8.9% in another. In twins, infarcts occurred in 21.8% of first-born ART twins versus 6% of non-ART first-born twins.
- **Syncytial knots** (defined as more than 30% of villi containing knots): 15.6% in ART versus 8.9% in non-ART singletons; 47.3% versus 32% in twins. Donor-oocyte preterm placentas showed knots in 96.2% versus 72.7% of non-donor placentas.
- **Fibrin deposition or fibrinoid necrosis** occurs in 2–20% of placentas in both groups, with no consistent difference. Donor-oocyte pregnancies show massive fibrin deposition in up to 40–70%.
- **FVM:** no difference in fetal vascular lesions between 33 fresh ART and 39 non-ART pregnancies. Intervillous thrombi occurred in 16.7% of fresh versus 24.2% of frozen-transfer placentas, possibly from epigenetic effects of freezing or from the absent corpus luteum altering uterine vascularization.
- Donor-oocyte pregnancies show pathological immature villi in 75% versus 15.4% of autologous-oocyte pregnancies, without a difference in stem villus vasculopathy.
- **Biomarkers:** ART pregnancies have a higher false-positive rate for aneuploidy screening because hCG, unconjugated estriol, and PAPP-A are placenta-derived and altered. Second-trimester PAPP-A multiples of the median in ART range from 1.04 to 1.22, compared with 2.06 in Down syndrome.
- **Angiogenesis:** sFlt-1 was unchanged in first trimester in most studies; one study found increased sFlt-1 from 18 weeks to at least 35 weeks, not confirmed by others. Placental GLUT1 and GLUT3 expression is increased in ART, with unclear functional consequence.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not attribute every adverse outcome to ART; subfertility itself raises risk, and sibling studies are needed to isolate the technique.
- Do not expect a specific "ART placenta" histology; most studies find no significant difference in MVM or FVM.
- Do not generalize donor-oocyte findings to all ART pregnancies; the allograft relationship is a distinct biological setting.
- Do not compare studies uncritically; definitions of infarction, syncytial knots, and fibrin vary widely.
- Do not interpret altered first-trimester biomarkers as aneuploidy risk without adjusting for ART status.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat ART as an independent risk factor for placenta previa and accreta, and document cord insertion carefully.
2. Expect intervillous thrombi more often after frozen embryo transfer.
3. Interpret first-trimester screening markers with ART-specific reference ranges.
4. Examine the placenta of every ART pregnancy for MVM and FVM, recognizing that absence of a lesion is common.
5. Counsel that most of the perinatal risk is modest and multifactorial, not a fixed placental defect.
