# Chapter 10: The Chorionic and Basal Plates

## Core Idea
The chorionic and basal plates are the fetal and maternal boundaries of the intervillous space, and both are composite, dynamic structures rather than inert connective tissue. Their layers, fibrinoid subtypes, and cellular content change with gestation, and both merge at the margin to become the smooth membranes. The basal plate is the plane of separation: Nitabuch's fibrinoid and the maternal endothelium lining its inner surface allow detachment without tearing into the myometrium.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Chorionic plate layers at term.** Amnion (epithelium, compact layer, fibroblast layer); spongy layer with residual mesothelium; chorionic mesoderm; cytotrophoblast; Langhans' fibrinoid with extravillous trophoblast; syncytiotrophoblast facing the intervillous space.

**Basal plate layers at term.** Inner lining (syncytiotrophoblast, cytotrophoblast, maternal endothelium, fibrinoid); Rohr's stria; extravillous trophoblast with remnants of anchoring villi and columns; Nitabuch's fibrinoid; separation zone with a few decidual layers and a fibrous network containing a vascular plexus.

**Fibrinoid subtype localization.** Chorionic plate: matrix-type on the compact layer facing mesenchyme, fibrin-type on the reticular layer facing the intervillous space. Perivillous fibrinoid is mostly fibrin-type; intravillous is mixed. Cell islands and cell columns have superficial fibrin-type and central matrix-type. Rohr's stria and superficial basal plate are fibrin-type. Deep basal plate is mixed, with fibrin-type near Nitabuch's stria. Membranes have matrix-type within trophoblast and fibrin-type at the trophoblast-decidual junction.

## Key Concepts
- Chorionic plate thickness is usually 200 μm or less where no villi are incorporated, and may reach several millimeters where villi are enmeshed in fibrinoid.
- Amnion is avascular. Near the cord insertion there may be gray nodules 3–4 mm in diameter made of keratinized squamous cells from fetal skin; the underlying epithelium is often degenerate.
- Spongy layer: coil-like collagen bundles about 11 μm in diameter; a shear plane.
- The primary plate forms on day 8 p.c. The amnion contacts it at 12–14 weeks, when the coelom is obliterated; the definitive plate then forms.
- Chorionic vessels begin to function at about 8–10 weeks.
- The subchorial closing ring is complete in 71% of placentas, incomplete in 21%, and absent in 8%. It is about 1 cm wide.
- A circumvallate placenta results when the ring lies closer to the cord insertion, so membranes arise from the chorionic surface and the intervillous space extends beyond the marginal zone.
- The marginal lake accounts for about one third of intervillous venous drainage; it is an extension of the subchorial lake, not a separate sinus.
- Basal plate thickness at term ranges from 100 μm to 1.5 mm.
- Inner surface composition at term: maternal endothelium 61%, trophoblast 19%, fibrinoid 20%.
- Decidua basalis measures 5–6 mm at 6 weeks p.m. and about 1 mm at 15 weeks. It is thick over a "column of Streeter" and attenuated elsewhere.
- The basal plate becomes irregular by the end of the first trimester and more uniform by 20 weeks, when septa begin to subdivide the intervillous space.
- Septa first appear at 12 weeks and share the basal plate composition. Early septa may contain endometrial glands, veins, and spiral arteries, supporting an origin by buckling.
- Separation occurs just deep to Nitabuch's stria. This layer is absent in accreta spectrum.
- Calcification before 34 weeks is associated with abruption, postpartum hemorrhage, and stillbirth, and correlates with umbilical artery pulsatility index.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating the basal plate as one homogeneous layer.
- Reporting fibrinoid as automatically pathologic; it has defined normal locations.
- Using maternal floor infarction for ordinary perivillous fibrin.
- Describing septa as complete partitions.
- Assuming the marginal lake is the sole venous drainage.
- Ignoring an absent separation zone, the histologic signature of accreta spectrum.

## Key Takeaways
1. Report plate thickness, layering, and fibrinoid type separately.
2. Use 61%/19%/20% as the normal inner-surface composition at term.
3. Record the state of the subchorial closing ring.
4. Treat calcification before 34 weeks as a marker of malperfusion.
5. Confirm that a separation zone is present.
6. Identify septa by their basal plate composition.
