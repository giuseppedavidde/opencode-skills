# Chapter 17: Placental Shape Aberrations

## Core Idea
Placental shape is not decorative: the gross outline is established in the first trimester and is determined largely by the pattern of chorionic plate vascular development and by where the blastocyst happened to implant. Shape aberrations are therefore markers of early developmental and vascular events, and they travel with cord insertion variants and functional deficits. The chapter separates three explanatory theories, then works through the named shapes — bilobate, succenturiate, multilobate, fenestrate, membranacea, and previa — each with its own frequency, mechanism, and clinical risk.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Three theories of abnormal placental shape.**
1. **Trophotropism / dynamic placentation:** the placenta grows where decidua is well vascularized and atrophies where it is not, so the apparent position can "migrate" during gestation. Seen with implantation over leiomyomas, scars, and the lower uterine segment.
2. **Eccentric fetal pole:** the fetal pole is eccentrically placed at implantation, so the placenta does not develop uniformly.
3. **Defective first-trimester branching of chorionic plate vessels:** areas deficient in vascularization persist into the third trimester. Supported by mathematical modelling of chorionic plate vasculature, and extended to abnormal cord insertion.

**Placenta previa ultrasound grading (transabdominal).**
- Grade 1 — "minor previa": lower edge inside the lower uterine segment.
- Grade 2 — "marginal previa": lower edge reaches the internal os.
- Grade 3 — "partial previa".
- Grade 4 — "complete previa": placenta completely covers the cervix.
Grades 1 and 2 are often combined as "minor" previa; grades 3 and 4 as major.

**Distance-based definition.** Low-lying placenta = placental edge less than 20 mm from the internal os. Normal = edge more than 20 mm from the internal os.

## Key Concepts
- "Error in outline" is the descriptive term for shape variants. The placenta is rarely truly circular; the basic gross morphology is typically established in the first trimester.
- Bilobate (bilobed, duplex, bipartite) placenta: two lobes separated by a segment of membranes; membranous vessels between lobes must be examined.
- Succenturiate (accessory) lobe frequency is 0.3–0.9% in published series, but 5–6% in routinely examined placentas. More than two lobes is termed multilobate.
- Placenta fenestrata: a central defect devoid of normal villous tissue with only membranes remaining; attributed to focal deficiency of villous anchoring over a uterine wall abnormality such as leiomyoma or overlying the tubal ostia.
- Placenta membranacea (diffusa): all or nearly all of the fetal sac circumference is covered by villous tissue. Estimated at 1 in 3300 to 1 in 25,500 pregnancies, with about 50 reported cases; the placental mass is thin, 1–2 cm. Many, but not all, are accreta. It is not a variant of extensive succenturiate lobe formation because there is never separation or segregation of tissue from a main disk.
- Placenta previa prevalence: 0.3–1.0% across series; 0.33%; 0.4% and 0.56%; 0.4–0.5%; 0.6% overall with 0.12% total/complete in a survey of 31,070 pregnancies.
- Midtrimester low-lying placenta was present in 5.3% and converted to non-previa by term in 90.4%; overall term previa incidence in that cohort was 0.58%.
- Migration is asymmetric: 77% of initially low-lying placentas moved upward, while 68% of fundal implantations extended downward.
- Previa is strongly associated with placenta accreta, and the combination is the reason for the highest maternal morbidity.
- Placenta previa is a major cause of third-trimester bleeding; the maternal surface often shows old marginal clot with a disrupted, torn edge.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating an irregular outline as incidental.** Shape reflects first-trimester vascular development and predicts cord insertion variants and functional deficit.
- **Assuming a low-lying placenta at midtrimester will persist.** Over 90% resolve by term, so management should not be fixed at the midtrimester scan.
- **Calling placenta membranacea an extreme succenturiate lobe.** There is uniform thinning without segregation of a disk.
- **Ignoring membranous vessels between lobes.** They are vulnerable to compression, thrombosis, and rupture.
- **Confusing placenta fenestrata with a missing cotyledon.** The defect is a focal failure of villous development, not avulsion.
- **Assigning previa by grade alone.** The 20 mm distance rule is the operationally important cut-off.

## Key Takeaways
1. Describe shape precisely, and record the number of lobes and any bridging membranous vessels.
2. Use the 20 mm internal-os distance to define low-lying placenta, and apply the four-grade scheme for previa.
3. Expect resolution of most midtrimester low-lying placentas, but document the 90.4% conversion figure in counselling.
4. Search for accreta whenever previa is present, especially with prior cesarean delivery.
5. Consider trophotropism when shape is distorted over a leiomyoma or scar.
6. Report succenturiate lobes because of retained-lobe risk postpartum.
