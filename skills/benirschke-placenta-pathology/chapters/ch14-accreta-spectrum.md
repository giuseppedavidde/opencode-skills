# Chapter 14: The Placenta Accreta Spectrum

## Core Idea
Placenta accreta spectrum (PAS) is the loss of the decidual layer at the placental–myometrial interface, so that chorionic villi and extravillous trophoblast lie directly against myometrium. It is best understood as a largely iatrogenic disease of uterine injury: its incidence rose from 1 in 4027 pregnancies in the 1970s to 1 in 533 in 1982–2002, parallel to cesarean delivery rates. The chapter's central move is to replace the three-word vocabulary (accreta, increta, percreta) with a graded, reproducible system that can be applied both to hysterectomy specimens and to delivered placentas.

## Frameworks Introduced

**PAS grading system (hysterectomy and myometrial resection), built to parallel the FIGO classification.** Grades are assigned by degree of myometrial disruption:
- **Grade 1 — noninvasive:** smooth placental/myometrial interface without myometrial thinning or thickening.
- **Grade 2 — superficial invasion:** irregular placental/myometrial interface, invasion not exceeding 75% of the myometrium; uterine wall thinned below the placenta but at least 25% of wall thickness preserved.
- **Grade 3A — deep invasion:** invasion over 75% of the myometrium without breach of the serosa.
- **Grade 3D — deep invasion with breach of the serosa.**
- **Grade 3E — deep invasion with invasion into extrauterine structures.**

**Basal plate myofibers (BPMF) staging for delivered placentas.** Because most placentas are delivered, not resected, BPMF is quantified instead:
- **Stage 1:** decidua present on sections.
- **Stage 2:** decidua absent.
- Record the **size in mm** of the largest focus as a linear dimension along the basal plate.
- Record the **number of foci**.
- Stage 1 is more likely incidental than stage 2; a comment should recommend clinical correlation.

**Reporting framework.** The PAS consensus panel (Society for Pediatric Pathology, Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine, International Society of Gynecologic Pathology, International Federation of Placenta Associations, International Society for Abnormally Invasive Placenta, FIGO) endorses a shared classification so that pathology and imaging speak one language.

## Key Concepts
- Diagnostic feature of PAS is loss of the decidual layer, with villi apposed to myometrium. Adherence to a scar without myometrial invasion is not PAS, so sections away from the scar are essential.
- Risk with placenta previa: one prior cesarean 11%, two prior cesareans 40%, three prior cesareans 61%.
- Eighteen percent of PAS occurs in primiparous women. In primiparous women, prior uterine procedures carry relative risks of 1.5 for one procedure (99% CI 1.1–1.9), 2.7 for two (99% CI 1.7–4.4), and 5.1 for three or more (99% CI 2.7–9.6).
- Other risk factors: dilatation and curettage, abortion, intrauterine device insertion, myomectomy, and assisted reproductive technology (confounded by maternal age, uterine anomaly, and prior procedures).
- BPMF in a prior placenta predicts accreta: odds ratio 4.8 (95% CI 1.8–13.0). Recurrence of BPMF was 100% in one series, often with increasing degrees of adherence.
- BPMF is found in normal placentas but may mark subclinical morbid adherence, especially when abundant. Occult placenta accreta had an incidence of 0.6% at one institution.
- In one study, 20.1% of cases of retained placenta had BPMF. Associated clinical signs include delayed third stage, need for manual removal, increased bleeding, and placental fragmentation.
- Retroplacental clot, or separate clot in the specimen container, may indicate PAS rather than abruption.
- Extravillous trophoblast must be distinguished from decidua; keratin immunohistochemistry separates them. CD146 immunostaining shows greater thickness and number of extravillous trophoblast cells at BPMF sites, with unchanged density.
- Intravascular villi may be seen in increta. Chorion laeve accreta is described as another precursor lesion.
- Morbidity: severe hemorrhage, coagulation disorders, transfusion complications, infection, reoperation, acute respiratory distress syndrome, amniotic fluid embolism, and injury to adjacent organs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling adherence to a scar "accreta" without myometrial invasion.** Scar adherence alone fails the diagnostic requirement.
- **Sampling only the scar.** Sections from areas away from the scar are essential or the grade will be wrong.
- **Ignoring isolated BPMF.** Isolated BPMF does not by itself signify PAS, but it is under-reported and often represents small focal accreta; it belongs in the comment.
- **Treating PAS as a purely obstetric event.** The prior placenta may already carry the signal, in the form of BPMF foci, so review prior specimens.
- **Assuming assisted reproduction is the cause.** The excess risk may reflect age, uterine anomaly, and prior procedures rather than the technology.
- **Believing a prior uterine injury is mandatory.** Eighteen percent of cases occur in primiparous women.

## Key Takeaways
1. Grade PAS from the hysterectomy specimen with the five-tier system, and state the percentage of myometrial thickness invaded.
2. When only a delivered placenta is available, report BPMF stage, largest focus size in mm, and number of foci.
3. Always add a comment linking BPMF to possible noninvasive PAS and recommend clinical correlation, noting that stage 2 is more significant than stage 1.
4. Sample away from the scar before excluding PAS.
5. Treat previa plus prior cesarean as the dominant risk pair, and counsel by the 11%, 40%, and 61% figures.
6. Use keratin immunohistochemistry whenever trophoblast and decidua cannot be separated on H&E.
