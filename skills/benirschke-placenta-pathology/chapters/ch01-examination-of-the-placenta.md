# Chapter 1: Examination of the Placenta

## Core Idea
The placenta records the whole pregnancy, and it must be examined in a fixed sequence so that no compartment is skipped. The authors reject the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists view that routine placental study is not warranted. Storage for one week at 4 °C preserves morphology and allows retrieval when a neonate deteriorates. Freezing, and primary formalin fixation, destroy the most useful gross and histologic information and prevent culture. Weight is meaningful only after cord, membranes, and retroplacental clot are removed.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Storage and triage.** Labeled plastic containers at 4 °C, seven shelves labelled Monday to Sunday. Discard normal placentas from one shelf when the same weekday returns. Never freeze.

**Gross sequence.** Odor, shape, completeness of the sac, fetal surface and vessels, cord, net weight, maternal surface, serial parallel cuts. Blood escapes and surfaces dry once the organ is cut.

**Membrane roll (Zeek and Assali).** Amnion inward, roll started at the rupture site and carried to the placental edge, so that the rupture sits at the center and decidua capsularis is maximized.

**Twin placenta protocol.** Record the number of dividing membranes, two or four or none, and the anastomotic types. Prepare a T section through the junction, identify the vascular equator, and dissect the halves at the equator for separate weights.

**Sampling set.** Two or more cord blocks, one or two membrane-roll blocks, three villous blocks including both surfaces. H&E is routine; special stains on indication.

**Research tissue.** Use unlabored Cesarean deliveries only, because labor alone changes oxidative-stress and cytokine gene expression. Freeze within 10 minutes, sample ten or more placentas per group, wash in buffered saline, store at −80 °C, and confirm arrays by qRT-PCR with three housekeeping genes.

## Key Concepts
- Term averages: diameter 22 cm, central thickness 2.5 cm, weight 470 g.
- Storage loss is mostly blood and serum leakage; a 740-g hydropic placenta lost 180 g of fluid in one day.
- Formalin raises placental weight by 0.7–23.0%, mean +9.9%.
- Normal term thickness is 2.0–2.5 cm; pale, friable, hydropic, or exsanguinated placentas measure 3–5 cm.
- Meconium: macrophages in the amnion after 1 hour and in the chorion after 3 hours (Miller); other studies give 24–48 hours. A very immature fetus cannot pass meconium.
- Green-brown discoloration in an immature placenta usually means hematoidin or hemosiderin, not meconium. Hemosiderin stains with Prussian blue; meconium stains poorly with bile stains.
- Amnion is avascular. Arteries cross over veins; the reverse occurs in about 3%.
- Hyrtl's anastomosis joins the two umbilical arteries near the insertion and may extend 10 cm from it. Count vessels away from the insertion.
- Squamous metaplasia near the cord insertion is normal. Amnion nodosum correlates with oligohydramnios.
- Intervillous thrombi displace villi and look shiny and striated. Infarcts are based on the maternal surface, rectangular or triangular, and granular. Fresh infarcts are red, older ones yellow to white.
- Retroplacental hemorrhage: fresh clot cannot be separated from adherent postpartum clot; within hours it dries, firms, then turns brown and green. The underlying placenta is infarcted or compressed. Most are clinically silent and marginal.
- DiDi membranes are opaque with vessel remnants and old decidua; DiMo membranes are transparent amnion only; MoMo has no dividing membrane.
- About one third of monozygotic twins are DiDi, and DiDi placentas never show vascular confluence.
- Monochorionic placentas almost always have artery-to-artery, vein-to-vein, and artery-to-vein anastomoses. Artery-to-vein anastomoses cause twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome, and an unpaired chorionic artery identifies one.

## Anti-patterns
- Freezing, or fixing before gross study: both impair evaluation and prevent culture.
- Weighing the intact organ with cord, membranes, and clot.
- Counting vessels at the insertion, where Hyrtl's anastomosis mimics a single artery.
- Equating DiDi placentation with dizygotic twins.
- Diagnosing placenta previa when intact membranes extend beyond the tear.
- Sampling only abnormal areas; infarcts look alike, while normal tissue carries chronic villitis.
- Diagnosing from one block, or calling a fresh retroplacental clot artifactually adherent blood.

## Key Takeaways
1. Store every placenta at 4 °C for one week.
2. Report net villous weight, shape, cord insertion, cord length and coiling, membrane insertion, and percentage of parenchymal involvement.
3. Confirm three vessels at a site away from the insertion.
4. Center the rupture site in the membrane roll and sample both surfaces.
5. For twins, record membrane number and anastomoses, block the T section, and weigh each half.
6. Reserve gene-expression work for unlabored Cesarean deliveries, frozen within 10 minutes.
