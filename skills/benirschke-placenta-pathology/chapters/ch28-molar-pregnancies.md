# Chapter 28: Molar Pregnancies

## Core Idea
Hydatidiform moles are genetically abnormal gestations divided into complete (CHM) and partial (PHM) moles, with different genetics, morphology, and trophoblastic disease risk. CHM is diffusely hydropic with cisterns, absent fetal vessels, circumferential trophoblast proliferation, and androgenetic diploidy. PHM is focal, triploid, and retains vascular remnants and fetal parts. Morphology alone is unreliable, so p57 immunohistochemistry, ploidy, and genotyping are needed for classification.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **CHM / PHM / hydropic abortus algorithm:** p57 immunostain plus ploidy (flow cytometry DNA index) plus short tandem repeat genotyping. Hydropic abortus: diploid with positive p57. PHM: triploid with positive p57. CHM: diploid with negative p57.
- **DNA index by flow cytometry:** normal placenta 1.00; partial mole 1.45; complete mole 1.00.
- **p57kip2 logic:** p57 is paternally imprinted and maternally expressed, so a conceptus with no maternal genome loses nuclear staining in cytotrophoblast and villous mesenchyme. Extravillous trophoblast retains expression in CHM.
- **Invasive mole versus choriocarcinoma:** distinguished only by molar chorionic villi scattered among the trophoblast.
- **Clinical risk stratification:** CHM carries persistent GTD in 8.9–20%, PHM in 0.2–4%; neoplasia follows about 15% of CHM and 0.5% of PHM.

## Key Concepts
- Incidence: up to 2 per 1000 pregnancies in China and Japan; 12 per 1000 in Indonesia, India, and Turkey; up to 1.1 per 1000 in North America, Europe, Australia, and New Zealand. Risk is greater at maternal age 14 years or less and 45 years or more.
- CHM gross: diffusely enlarged, edematous placenta with cistern-like cavities, absent vessels, and often no embryo. Ultrasound shows a "snowstorm" or "speckled" pattern without a fetus; theca lutein cysts, hyperthyroidism, pulmonary edema, hyperemesis, and preeclampsia may occur.
- CHM histology: swollen villi with empty cisternae from dissociation of loose stroma; circumferential trophoblast proliferation rather than polar capping; absent vessels or vascular remnants; abundant Hofbauer cells; frequent intervillous thrombi. Villi are linked by thin strands of former mainstem villi.
- CHM genetics: most are homozygous 46,XX androgenetic from one sperm; about 8% arise by dispermy (two male pronuclei); 5–10% are heterozygous 46,XX or 46,XY. Heterozygous moles required chemotherapy in 25% versus 17.6% in one series.
- PHM: mixed normal and distended villi, irregular swelling, less and focal trophoblast hyperplasia, scalloped villous contour, trophoblastic inclusions and pseudoinclusions, and villous capillaries often containing fetal nucleated red cells.
- PHM epidemiology: incidence as high as 1 in 512 pregnancies and 1 in 41 spontaneous abortions; triploidy occurs in about 1% of recognized pregnancies; 85% of triploid and hypertriploid embryos show hydatidiform degeneration. Most PHM are diandric triploids (69,XXX or 69,XXY); digynic triploids are not molar.
- Early CHM is subtle: villous edema and trophoblast proliferation are less marked, so the differential is CHM versus hydropic abortus, not CHM versus PHM. Absent vessels do not exclude a prior capillary bed, and fetal membranes or nucleated red cells in a first-trimester specimen do not rule out CHM.
- Grading nuclear atypia does not predict persistence, recurrence, or malignancy.
- Recurrence: CHM 1–2% in Western countries, higher in the Middle East; PHM about twice the background risk; CHM about ten times background; overall reported 0.60–2.57%. NALP7 and KHDC3L mutations cause familial biparental moles with p57 negativity.
- A normal twin coexisting with CHM occurs in 1 in 22,000–100,000 pregnancies; survival of the twin is up to 40%, and 60% of women who do not terminate lose the pregnancy.
- Invasive mole (chorioadenoma destruens) invades myometrium like placenta increta; it is almost always a CHM. Incidence is difficult to establish because persistent GTD is often treated clinically without hysterectomy.
- Surveillance: serial hCG with contraception until hCG is normal, then 6 months of follow-up.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not classify moles by morphology alone. Interobserver variability is significant, and p57, ploidy, or genotyping is required in difficult cases.
- Do not use p57 to separate PHM from hydropic abortus; both are p57 positive. Ploidy is needed.
- Do not diagnose PHM when a normal twin and a separate complete mole coexist; each conceptus must be classified separately.
- Do not expect uniform p57 loss in every CHM. Retention of maternal chromosome 11 or 6, and tetraploid 92,XXYY moles, cause aberrant expression.
- Do not grade trophoblast atypia for prognosis.
- Do not assume an early CHM will show florid cisterns and proliferation.

## Key Takeaways
1. Send molar tissue for p57 and ploidy when morphology is ambiguous; do not rely on H&E alone.
2. Report CHM versus PHM explicitly because persistent GTD risk differs by an order of magnitude.
3. Recognize early CHM as a subtle lesion and distinguish it from hydropic abortus.
4. Screen all terminations for unsuspected GTD when early moles cannot be excluded.
5. Arrange hCG surveillance for 6 months after normalization, with contraception.
