# Chapter 13: Extrauterine Implantation and Involution of Implantation Site

## Core Idea
Ectopic implantation is not a single entity but a family of site-specific diseases, each defined by its own anatomic criteria and each behaving as a form of placenta accreta because the implantation site lacks decidua. Tubal pregnancy implants interstitially and invades rapidly through a thin, non-decidualized wall, so it ruptures into the retroperitoneum rather than the lumen. The second half of the chapter reframes the postpartum uterus as a wound that heals on a defined timetable, so that "subinvolution" becomes a diagnosis of exclusion rather than a description.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Site-based classification of ectopic pregnancy.** Fallopian tube (interstitial, isthmic, ampullary, fimbrial), ovarian, cervical, abdominal, and heterotopic. Frequency in 1800 population-based cases: interstitial 2.4%, isthmic 12.0%, ampullary 70.0%, fimbrial 11.1%, ovarian 3.2%, abdominal 1.3%, cervical 0%.

**Diagnostic criteria sets.** Each site has a formal definition:
- **Cervical pregnancy (two criteria):** (1) cervical glands lie opposite the site of placental attachment; (2) part or all of the placenta lies below the uterine vessels or the peritoneal reflection.
- **Ovarian pregnancy (Spiegelberg, 1878; four criteria):** both fallopian tubes normal with no evidence of prior tubal implantation; histological evidence of trophoblast; gestational sac connected to the uterus by the ovarian ligament; sac entirely surrounded by ovary.
- **Primary abdominal pregnancy (three criteria):** both tubes and ovaries normal without evidence of primary implantation at either site; no uteroperitoneal fistula; pregnancy related principally to the peritoneum.

**Postpartum involution timeline (Table 13.1).** Staged structural change from 1–3 days (early necrosis) through 7–8 days and onward, used as the reference against which subinvolution is judged.

**Placental site lesion differential.** Subinvolution of the placental site, placental polyp (placental polypoid mass), and placental site nodule.

## Key Concepts
- Ectopic pregnancy occurs in 1–2% of all pregnancies; more than 100,000 cases per year are reported in the United States, and only surgically managed cases are counted.
- Non-tubal ectopics as a share of all ectopics: cervical <1%, cesarean scar 6.1%, interstitial 2–11%, cornual 2%, ovarian 1–6%, abdominal 0.9–1.4%. Cervical pregnancy is about 1 in 9000 deliveries.
- Tubal nidation sites in 112 tubes: fimbriae 7.6%, plicae 11.4%, purely mural or plicomural 81%. Placentation occurs on the mesosalpingeal aspect closest to the major blood supply.
- Decidual reaction is rare in tubal mucosa and, when present, is much less than in the pregnant uterus. Absence of decidua plus a thin wall lets trophoblast reach the subserosa rapidly; most conceptuses develop between tube and peritoneum.
- Tubal histology in 227 excised tubes: normal 74%, chronic inflammatory change 24%, endometriosis 1%. Another series found chronic salpingitis in 95.5% of 148 gravid tubes.
- Heterotopic pregnancy: spontaneous incidence about 1 in 30,000; about 1 in 100 when conception follows assisted reproductive technology. Roughly 50% present with acute rupture.
- Abdominal pregnancy outcome: 83% perinatal mortality in 23 cases managed to 1984 in Zimbabwe; a 1965–2012 review of 26 late (over 20 weeks) cases found 23 surviving infants (88.5%) and 7 maternal deaths (26.9%).
- Involution: uterus of about 1000 g after term delivery shrinks to less than 100 g, usually within 2 months. Placental site measures 18 cm at delivery, 9 cm after detachment, and 4 mm in thickness; 4.5 cm at 8 days; 2 cm at 8 weeks. Site vessels lose staining qualities for 25–30 days.
- hCG disappears from maternal blood within 2 weeks; free DNA clears in about 1–2 days; endometrium regenerates by about 16 days; plasma cells may persist 6 weeks postpartum in 50% of cases and 3–4 months in 37%.
- Subinvolution: uteroplacental arteries remain large, widely patent, tortuous, and filled with red cells or organizing thrombus. Intraluminal endovascular trophoblast may persist. Diagnose only in the absence of retained placental tissue.
- Placental site nodule: well-circumscribed, extensively hyalinized, eosinophilic "fluffy" plaques, paucicellular with persistent extravillous trophoblast; found in less than 0.25% of pregnancies and 6% of placental site lesions. Trophoblast is positive for cytokeratin, PLAP, HPL, and focally hCG and EMA.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming tubal pregnancy grows inside the lumen.** Implantation is interstitial, penetrating surface epithelium into tubal stroma; luminal distension is not the mechanism.
- **Reading chronic inflammation into a gravid tube.** Plasma cells and expanded, fused tubal plicae favor true chronic salpingitis; CD56+ natural killer cells favor the normal response to placentation.
- **Diagnosing subinvolution without excluding retained products.** The diagnosis requires absence of retained placental tissue.
- **Calling a retained placental fragment a "polyp."** The preferred term is placental polypoid mass.
- **Mistaking subinvolution after molar evacuation for persistent trophoblastic disease.** Both cause continued bleeding; the vessel morphology separates them.
- **Treating cervical or ovarian implantation as ordinary ectopic disease.** Both are effectively placenta accreta because neither site decidualizes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Apply the formal criteria sets before assigning a site; ovarian and abdominal diagnoses are exclusionary and anatomic.
2. Expect retroperitoneal rather than intraluminal rupture in tubal pregnancy, and anticipate placenta accreta-like adherence at every extrauterine site.
3. Use the involution timetable to date postpartum change and to avoid overcalling retained tissue in the first 6 weeks.
4. Diagnose subinvolution only after excluding retained products, and look for intraluminal trophoblast in the vessels.
5. Consider heterotopic pregnancy in every patient after assisted reproduction with pelvic pain, and remember that half present with rupture.
6. Recognize placental site nodule as a benign legacy lesion of a prior pregnancy, not a trophoblastic neoplasm.
