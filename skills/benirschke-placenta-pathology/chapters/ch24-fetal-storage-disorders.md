# Chapter 24: Fetal Storage Disorders

## Core Idea
Inborn errors of metabolism produce inclusions or vacuoles in tissues, and the placenta is often affected in the same way. Inborn errors cause about 1% of nonimmune hydrops, so every hydropic placenta deserves careful histologic scrutiny. Vacuolation may be absent or subtle, and the storage material is often water and lipid soluble, so routine processing can erase it. Precise diagnosis now depends increasingly on enzyme studies, electron microscopy, and targeted gene panels rather than on morphology alone.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Broad disease groups:** errors of carbohydrate metabolism, protein metabolism, fatty acid oxidation, and glycogen storage.
- **Site-of-vacuolation rule:** the compartment that vacuolates — syncytiotrophoblast, cytotrophoblast, endothelium, or villous stroma/Hofbauer cells — historically predicted the defect class. The chapter notes this approach may become obsolete as gene panels replace it.
- **Fixation protocol for suspected storage disease:** tissue for electron microscopy in glutaraldehyde, and snap-frozen tissue for enzyme assay. Many lysosomal inclusions are highly water and lipid-solvent soluble and are lost in routine processing.

## Key Concepts
- GM1 gangliosidosis (GLB1, 3p22.3): "empty vacuoles" in syncytial cytoplasm, extravillous trophoblast, and Hofbauer cells; visible even in paraffin sections. β-galactosidase absent by amniocentesis at 14 weeks; zebra bodies in fetal ganglion cells.
- GM2 gangliosidosis type I / Tay-Sachs (HEXA, 15q23–q24): vacuolated syncytiotrophoblast with occasional myelin bodies in villous stromal cells; absent hexosaminidase in CVS. Classic infantile form is fatal by age 2–3 years.
- GM2 type II / Sandhoff (HEXB, 5q13.3): multiple parallel arrays in lysosomes of stroma, vacuolated syncytium, myelin bodies in trophoblast and endothelium; PAS-positive diastase-resistant granules may be seen.
- Fabry disease (GLA, X-linked): lamellar lysosomal inclusions in decidual cells; fetal placental cells normal in a heterozygous carrier; globotriaosylceramide deposits reported in placenta, cord, and membranes. Enzyme replacement therapy may prevent placental storage.
- Mucolipidosis I / sialidosis (NEU1, 6p21.33): vacuoles in Hofbauer cells and villous syncytium, trophoblast, and placenta X-cells; villous maturational delay; acid phosphatase staining marks vacuolated trophoblastic cells. Amniocytes may look normal by EM.
- Mucolipidosis II / I-cell disease (GNPTAB, 12q23.2): vacuolated syncytium; confirmed ultrastructurally.
- Mucolipidosis IV (MCOLN1): vacuolated stromal Hofbauer cells with lamellar inclusions.
- Mucopolysaccharidoses: MPS I Hurler (vacuolation of fibroblasts); MPS IV Morquio (villous edema, vacuolated cells); MPS VII Sly (fetal hydrops with vacuolation of Hofbauer cells).
- Sialic acid storage disease (SLC17A5): Hofbauer cells, endothelium, and syncytium packed with clear membrane-bound vacuoles; also extravillous trophoblast and amnionic epithelium.
- Galactosialidosis (CTSA): striking vacuolation of villous syncytiotrophoblast, extravillous trophoblast, and Hofbauer cells; electron-lucent lysosomes on EM.
- Gaucher disease (GBA): hydrops with erythroblastosis-like gross appearance and numerous nucleated red cells in villous capillaries; calcifications may occur in the placenta.
- Niemann-Pick type A: vacuolization and laminated myelin-body inclusions in Hofbauer cells, fibrocytes of umbilical cord, and chorion laeve. Niemann-Pick type C produces no visible placental storage; cultured CVS cells accumulate laminated inclusions under special conditions. About 95% of NP-C cases are NPC1, 5% NPC2.
- Glycogen storage disease: vacuoles full of glycogen on EM; glycogen deposits mainly in extravillous trophoblast, PAS positive and diastase resistant in two unrelated patients.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not assume absence of vacuoles excludes storage disease. Placental vacuolation is inconsistent, and many inclusions dissolve during processing.
- Do not interpret vacuolated stromal/decidual cells as edema or postmortem change. Fetal death and edema do not create vacuolation; abundant foam cells with irregular calcification indicate an unidentified storage disorder.
- Do not rely on amniocyte morphology alone. Sialidosis amniocytes have been reported normal by EM.
- Do not treat a hydropic placenta as diagnostic of a specific enzyme defect; the same gross pattern occurs across many disorders.
- Do not omit glutaraldehyde fixation and frozen tissue when hydrops of unknown cause is autopsied.

## Key Takeaways
1. Consider inborn errors of metabolism in every case of nonimmune hydrops; they account for about 1%.
2. Sample the placenta for EM and freeze tissue before routine fixation if storage disease is in the differential.
3. Use the vacuolated compartment (syncytium, trophoblast, endothelium, stroma) to guide, not to conclude, the diagnosis.
4. Recognize the erythroblastosis-like pattern in Gaucher disease and the Hofbauer-cell vacuoles in MPS VII and sialic acid storage disease.
5. Remember that Niemann-Pick type C leaves no visible placental storage; functional cell studies or genetics are needed.
