# Chapter 21: Other Maternal Disorders Complicating Pregnancy

## Core Idea
Beyond hypertension and diabetes, a long list of maternal diseases reaches the placenta — connective tissue disease, renal and cardiac disease, hemoglobinopathies, thrombophilias, endocrine disease, malignancy, drug exposure, and environmental exposures. Their placental signatures are mostly non-specific and converge on two final common pathways: maternal vascular malperfusion and placental hypoplasia. The chapter's implicit argument is that the placenta rarely names the maternal disease; it grades the adequacy of the intrauterine environment. Interpretation therefore requires the clinical diagnosis, and reverse diagnosis from the placenta alone is unreliable.

## Frameworks Introduced

**System-based organisation of maternal disease effects.** Autoimmune and connective tissue disease; renal, liver, and heart disease; hematological disorders including inherited thrombophilia; endocrine disorders; non-hematological and hematological neoplasms; dermatoses of pregnancy; drugs (illicit and therapeutic); smoking and wildfire smoke; irradiation.

**Two final common pathways.** Nearly every entity in the chapter produces either maternal vascular malperfusion (infarction, atherosis, distal villous hypoplasia) or placental hypoplasia (low weight, reduced dimensions), often both.

**Thrombophilia screening framework.** Screening is generally not recommended in pregnancy as a blanket policy; it should be considered in women with a significant personal or family history, and testing should be done at least 10 weeks postpartum.

## Key Concepts
- Systemic sclerosis: miscarriage odds ratio 1.6 (95% CI 1.22–2.22), IUGR 3.2 (2.21–4.53), preterm birth 2.4 (1.14–4.86), low birth weight 3.8 (2.16–6.56). Placentas may show massive perivillous fibrin deposition mimicking maternal floor infarction, and infarcts.
- Ehlers–Danlos syndrome: prematurity OR 1.47 (1.18–1.82), cervical incompetence OR 3.11 (1.99–4.85), antepartum hemorrhage OR 1.71 (1.16–2.50), placenta previa OR 2.26 (1.35–3.77), maternal death OR 9.04 (1.27–64.27). Vascular EDS carries additional prematurity (OR 1.55) and IUGR (OR 1.81) risk. Placentas may show MVM with atherosis and abnormal coiling (e.g. 1 coil per 2 cm).
- Rheumatoid arthritis: the described placental finding was hypoplasia (356 g, 165 × 150 × 27 mm) without MVM.
- Renal transplantation: preeclampsia 21.5% (95% CI 18.5–24.9; US mean 3.8%), cesarean section 62.6% (57.6–67.3; US mean 31.9%), preterm birth 43.1% (38.7–47.6). About 30% of women with successful transplantation have a pregnancy complication. Maternal mortality in renal disease has fallen from 11% (1960s–1970s) to 0–2.4% (1980s–1990s) to 0–0.1% (2000s).
- Sickle cell disease: frequency 9% in the US Black population and up to 45% in Central Africa. Placental sickling was found in 9.4% of placentas in one series; mean sickling rate 95.2% in the maternal circulation, 8.4% in umbilical or chorionic veins, and 1.9% in arteries. Maternal mortality relative risk 5.98 (95% CI 1.94–18.44); stillbirth RR 3.94 (95% CI 2.60–5.96). MVM is present in about 40%, most commonly infarction.
- Thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura: maternal and fetal mortality reach 80% and 89% without therapy; relapse in pregnancy is high.
- Thrombophilia: factor V Leiden is found in approximately 1% of the general population. Inherited thrombophilias are associated with MVM (infarcts, atherosis) at OR 3.36, and with a further adverse outcome at OR 2.75 (95% CI 1.05–7.23), but not with inflammation.
- Thyroid dysfunction affects at least 2–3% of pregnant women: hyperthyroidism 0.2–0.4%, hypothyroidism 0.5–3.5%. Hashimoto thyroiditis is the commonest cause of hypothyroidism.
- Diabetes insipidus: a placenta of 990 g is recorded in a pregnancy with induced diabetes insipidus diagnosed at 38 weeks and delivery at 39 weeks.
- Melanoma complicates about 0.002% of pregnancies; fetal mortality was 53% (23.3% stillborn, 12.4% abortions) and maternal mortality 40.3% in reported cases. Placental metastases are documented and always accompanied by at least microscopic vascular involvement.
- Leukemia in pregnancy has a prevalence below 1 in 10,000 pregnancies; placental infiltration by leukemic cells is described.
- High altitude: placentas at 3100 m are smaller than those at lower altitude, and villous vascularity is altered.
- Anemia and folate deficiency are associated with altered villous maturation, and folate deficiency relates to neural tube defects detectable at 11–13 weeks.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reverse-diagnosing a maternal disease from the placenta.** Findings such as MVM, hypoplasia, or perivillous fibrin are shared across many maternal conditions.
- **Screening every pregnant woman for thrombophilia.** Screening is not generally recommended; restrict to a significant history and test at least 10 weeks postpartum.
- **Attributing placental hypoplasia to the maternal disease alone.** Fetal, infective, and other maternal causes must be excluded.
- **Assuming a normal-sized placenta excludes disease.** Several conditions produce normal weight with abnormal villous maturation.
- **Ignoring drug and environmental exposure.** Alcohol, illicit drugs, therapeutic cytotoxics, cigarette smoke, wildfire smoke, and irradiation all have placental consequences that mimic intrinsic disease.
- **Treating sickle cell placental change as thrombosis alone.** Sickling, infarction, and MVM coexist, and the sickling rates differ sharply between maternal and fetal circulations.

## Key Takeaways
1. Interpret placental findings against the named maternal disease, and grade MVM and hypoplasia rather than assigning a disease label.
2. Use the odds ratios for scleroderma and EDS in counselling, and expect MVM rather than a disease-specific lesion.
3. In sickle cell disease, look for infarction and MVM, and remember that placental sickling is far less frequent than maternal sickling.
4. Restrict thrombophilia testing to women with a significant history and defer testing until at least 10 weeks postpartum.
5. In maternal malignancy, examine the placenta and cord for metastatic involvement, which implies hematogenous spread.
6. Record altitude, smoking, alcohol, and drug exposure in the placental report because they alter villous maturation and weight.
