# Chapter 5: Early Development of the Human Placenta

## Core Idea
Human placentation is a staged process with dated events, from compaction on day 4.5 to onset of the maternal arterial circulation at the end of the first trimester. The first trimester is histotrophic and low in oxygen, not hemochorial: the placenta is better called deciduochorial until the second trimester. Maternal flow begins at the periphery, where plugging is least complete, and causes local oxidative stress with villous regression. That regression creates the chorion laeve and determines the shape of the disc.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Carnegie staging of implantation.** Stage 5a prelacunar; stages 5b and 5c lacunar; stage 7 early villous with shell formation.

**Sequential villous maturation.** Primary villi: cytotrophoblast core with syncytial cover, from about day 13. Secondary villi: extraembryonic mesoderm invades the core about 2 days later. Tertiary villi: fetal capillaries differentiate in the mesenchyme from days 18–20.

**Trophoblast lineage criteria (Lee et al.).** KRT7, TFAP2C, and GATA3 protein markers; microRNAs from the C19 cluster; and additional transcriptional features. No single marker is specific.

**Two extravillous routes.** Interstitial trophoblast migrates through endometrial stroma. Endovascular trophoblast enters spiral arteries and migrates down the lumen; these cells express CD56.

## Key Concepts
- Compaction and blastocyst formation occur by day 4.5 p.c. CDX2 appears in future trophectoderm on day 5; POU5F1 (OCT4) becomes restricted to the inner cell mass by day 6.
- The conceptus enters the uterus on day 3–4 and apposes to the epithelium around day 5.5–6.
- The earliest implanted specimen has an ovulation age of no more than 7.5 days and measures 0.1 × 0.3 × 0.5 mm.
- Implantation is intrusive: trophoblast migrates between endometrial cells and displaces them without necrosis.
- The lacunar stage runs from day 8 to day 13. Lacunae begin at the implantation pole; the syncytial lamellae between them are trabeculae.
- By day 12 the uterine epithelium closes over the site. Cytotrophoblast columns reach the trabecular tips about 2 days later, spread laterally, and form the cytotrophoblastic shell.
- The shell is "thick" at 14–18 days, "attenuated" at 20–30 days, and "markedly thinned" from 37–38 days onward.
- First fetal capillaries appear between days 18 and 20. A narrow placental-fetal communication exists at the start of the fourth week; a complete circulation by about the sixth week.
- Intervillous oxygen is below 20 mmHg in the first trimester. The ATP/ADP ratio stays constant and the early placenta relies on glycolytic and polyol pathways, so this is low oxygen without energy failure.
- Maternal-fetal diffusion distance falls from 50–100 μm in the second month to 4–5 μm at term.
- Villi cover the entire sac until the eighth week p.c., forming the chorion frondosum; villi over the superficial pole then regress to form the chorion laeve.
- Onset of maternal flow raises intervillous oxygen threefold. Syncytiotrophoblast has low superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione peroxidase, so oxidative stress causes syncytial degeneration, capillary regression, apoptosis, and collagenous ghost villi.
- In miscarriage, onset of flow is premature and disorganized. The shell is deficient in two-thirds of cases, plugging is incomplete, and oxidative damage is widespread.
- Decidua basalis is 5–6 mm at 4 weeks. Endometrial glands show the Arias-Stella reaction and open through the shell; communications persist to at least 10 weeks.
- Terminal villous volume rises steeply from about 20 weeks, producing an exponential rise in surface area and progressive membrane thinning.
- Excessive villus regression produces a small placenta in early second trimester and an abnormal growth trajectory, and explains irregular disc margins and eccentric cord insertion.

## Anti-patterns
- Describing the first-trimester placenta as hemochorial.
- Calling the low-oxygen first trimester hypoxic injury; energy flux is maintained.
- Assuming maternal arterial blood enters the intervillous space at implantation; only a slow venous ebb and flow exists until late first trimester.
- Treating extraembryonic mesoderm as a simple delamination product; it most likely arises from hypoblast, or from hypoblast and epiblast jointly.
- Interpreting an irregular placental margin or eccentric cord as random variation.
- Using chorionic villus sampling aneuploidy as a direct measure of embryonic aneuploidy.

## Key Takeaways
1. Convert to post-conception age before applying the chapter's dates.
2. Expect no maternal arterial inflow before 10–12 weeks.
3. Interpret first-trimester oxygen tension as physiologic, not pathologic.
4. Read villous regression at the superficial pole as the normal mechanism creating the chorion laeve.
5. Suspect deficient shell formation when miscarriage shows widespread oxidative villous damage.
6. Use the diffusion-distance trajectory, 50–100 μm to 4–5 μm, as the maturity index.
