# Chapter 6: Basic Structure of a Placental Villus

## Core Idea
Every villus shares one five-layer blueprint: syncytiotrophoblast, cytotrophoblast, trophoblastic basement membrane, stromal core, and fetal vessels. Function is partitioned by region rather than by villous type alone. Two syncytial structures with similar names have opposite meanings: the syncytial sprout is proliferative and transcriptionally active, while the true syncytial knot is a collection of effete, oxidatively damaged nuclei. Most structures reported as knots are tangential section artifacts.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Five-layer blueprint.** Syncytiotrophoblast (continuous, no cell borders); cytotrophoblast (Langhans' cells, stem population); trophoblastic basement membrane; stroma (connective tissue cells, Hofbauer cells, fibers, ground substance); fetal vessels.

**Sprout versus true knot.**
- Timing: sprouts commonest early and still present at term; true knots rare before 32 weeks and commonest postmature.
- Meaning: proliferation versus maturation and aging.
- Shape: pedunculated with a long stalk versus a gentle platelike elevation.
- Nuclei: normal size, euchromatic, nucleoli present, versus smaller, condensed, nucleoli absent.
- RNA polymerase II: positive versus negative.
- Perfusion: reduced maternal perfusion versus reduced fetal perfusion.
- Pre-eclampsia: sprouts more frequent, true knots minimally increased.

**Fibrinoid classification.** Fibrin-type derives from clotting and is mainly fibrin; matrix-type is secreted by trophoblast and contains laminins, collagens, and heparan sulfate. Perivillous fibrinoid surrounds recognizable villi; intravillous fibrinoid replaces the interior beneath an intact surface.

**Hofbauer cell identification.** CD14 and CD68 positive, bind Maclura pomifera lectin, abundant Fc receptors, large vacuolated cytoplasm.

## Key Concepts
- Syncytiotrophoblast surface area at term is 12–14 m².
- Microvilli amplify the apical surface five- to sevenfold. First trimester: 0.75–1.25 μm long, 0.12–0.17 μm diameter. Term: 0.5–0.7 μm long, 0.08–0.14 μm diameter.
- Fluorescein-dextran of 10,000–70,000 MW spreads along a whole terminal villus in 5 minutes; 150,000 MW spreads only about 20 μm in 5 minutes. The syncytium is one continuous compartment.
- The trophoblastic basement membrane is 100–250 nm thick and duplicated in about 50% of placentas. It contains collagen IV, laminin isoforms, perlecan, and collagen XVIII.
- Villous macrophages measure 10–35 μm, appear in villi by day 18 p.c., and are the only fetal immune cells in healthy villous stroma.
- Collagen fibers measure 20–60 nm with 64.9-nm cross-striation. They are dense in stem villi, scarce in terminal villi, and notably absent around capillary sinusoids.
- Vasculosyncytial membranes become common after 32 weeks; trophoblast over capillaries in mature intermediate villi falls below 2–3 μm.
- True syncytial knots occur on 10–30% of villi in normal term placentas. A frequency above 33% marks accelerated villous maturation, as in growth restriction, pre-eclampsia, and preterm labour.
- Nuclei positive for 5-hydroxymethylcytosine make up 20–50% in the first and second trimesters and increase toward term; damaged nuclei are sequestered into knots.
- About 80% of intervillous bridges in paraffin sections are sectioning artifacts.
- Deported syncytial sprouts are estimated at 100,000 per day at term.
- Intravillous fibrinoid replaces up to 3% of villous cross sections in normal pregnancy and increases in maternal diabetes, Rh incompatibility, and pre-eclampsia.
- Perivillous fibrinoid is present in every term placenta; deposits range from a few hundred micrometers to a few millimeters. Deposits of a few centimeters usually represent intervillous hematomas that displace villi.

## Anti-patterns
- Using "syncytial cell" or the plural "syncytiotrophoblasts".
- Reporting syncytial knots as degeneration or infarction without separating false from true knots.
- Diagnosing villous edema where the stroma is reticular and merely lacks collagen.
- Treating any perivillous fibrin as pathologic; only amount and distribution matter.
- Counting Hofbauer cells as an inflammatory infiltrate.
- Measuring villous membrane thickness on immersion-fixed, delayed-clamped tissue.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use the five-layer blueprint to describe any villous abnormality.
2. Distinguish sprouts from knots by chromatin, nucleoli, and RNA polymerase II.
3. Report knotting only with a stated method.
4. Quantify fibrinoid by compartment: perivillous, intravillous, or basal.
5. Accept up to 3% intravillous fibrinoid as normal.
6. Identify Hofbauer cells by CD14, CD68, and MPA lectin.
