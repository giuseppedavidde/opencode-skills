# Chapter 26: Placental Disorders of Uncertain Etiology

## Core Idea
A group of chronic inflammatory and fibrinoid placental lesions shares an undefined cause and a strong association with adverse obstetric outcome. They are recognized by the compartment they occupy — chorioamnion, villous parenchyma, or basal floor — and by the cell type that dominates. Infection must be excluded where possible, but most cases remain "of unknown etiology." These lesions recur and predict growth restriction, preterm birth, and fetal death.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Compartment-based classification:** chronic chorioamnionitis (CC) and chorionic histiocytic hyperplasia (CHH) in the chorion; chronic villitis (CV) and chronic villitis of unknown etiology (CVUE) in villi and basal floor; chronic intervillositis (CIV) in the intervillous space; chronic deciduitis (CD) in decidua; eosinophilic/T-cell chorionic vasculitis (ETCV) in chorionic vessels.
- **CVUE grading:** low-grade CV has fewer than 10 affected villi per focus, either focal or multifocal; high-grade CV has more than 10 affected villi per focus, and patchy or diffuse involvement in which more than 5% of all distal villi are affected. High-grade CVUE with obliterative vasculopathy carries the neurologic risk.
- **Obliterative fetal vasculopathy (Redline):** terminal CVUE extending to stem villi with stem villous vasculitis, vascular occlusion, and loss of vessels in the downstream distal villous tree.
- **CC grading (Jacques and Qureshi):** 1+, a few scattered foci; 2+, up to half of the membrane roll; 3+, more than half of the membrane roll involved.
- **PVFD threshold:** fibrinoid material between chorionic villi involving at least 25% of the slide examined.
- **MPFD threshold:** PVFD in more than 50% of at least one slide. MFI is PVFD confined to the basal plate.

## Key Concepts
- CVUE occurs in 5–15% of term placentas; recurrence risk is 10–17%. Associations: preterm birth, IUGR, neurologic impairment including cerebral palsy, and IUFD.
- CV histology: lymphocytic infiltrate in villi, villous agglutination, and perivillous fibrin. Plasma cells suggest infection or basal CV with adjacent chronic deciduitis. Infection work-up covers toxoplasmosis, syphilis, CMV, and HSV; Steiner stain for Treponema pallidum.
- Basal chronic villitis (BCV) may occur alone or as part of multifocal CV; it is linked to preeclampsia and accreta spectrum. Concurrent chronic deciduitis must always be excluded.
- **CIV (chronic histiocytic intervillositis, massive chronic intervillositis):** patchy intervillous histiocytes with red cells and sometimes lymphocytes, adjacent to agglutinated villi, often with fibrinoid. Associated with IUGR, third-trimester fetal death, recurrent spontaneous abortion, and a likely autoimmune etiology.
- **CC:** lymphocytes in the chorioamnion, distinct from native histiocytes; often involves free membranes only. A marker of CVUE and CHH, more common than acute chorioamnionitis in late preterm birth, and associated with maternal anti-HLA class I seropositivity.
- **CD:** plasma cells in decidua; single plasma cells are easily missed among lymphocytes.
- **ETCV:** mixed lymphocytic and eosinophilic infiltrate marginating from one chorionic vessel toward the fetal surface; incidence about 0.2%.
- **CHH:** band of CD68-positive histiocytes at the base of the chorion, shown by X and Y in situ hybridization to be fetal in origin; may show skip regions.
- **PVFD/MPFD:** MPFD placentas are small and firm; the parenchyma shows a "tigroid" pattern. Calcification is common and may resemble woven bone, but ossification has not been reported. Mandsager described ultrasonographic criteria.
- **MPFD associations:** elevated AFP with poor outcome; autoimmune disease including antiphospholipid syndrome and polymyositis; acquired or inherited coagulopathy (though MPFD was not linked to PAI-1, TAFI, u-PA, or t-PA polymorphisms); LCHAD deficiency; and a syndrome of chronic intervillositis, osteopenia, renal tubular dysgenesis, oligohydramnios, growth restriction, and high AFP. MPFD also occurs in first-trimester miscarriage with C4d deposition and angiogenic imbalance.
- **MFI:** originally "Gitterinfarkt"; the maternal floor is thick, stiffened, and yellow, with focal or multifocal change. IUGR and recurrence are associated, more strongly with MPFD than MFI.
- **LMN:** band of necrosis in the decidua (not the chorion) of the membrane roll, with neutrophils or karyorrhexis, indicating ascending infection.
- **Thrombi:** intervillous thrombus with adjacent infarction — infarcted villi next to an intervillous thrombus are usually secondary; intramembranous thrombi may contain hemosiderin; subchorionic thrombi can sit beside subchorionic PVFD.
- **Chorionic cysts:** homogeneous pink fluid, distinguished from vestigial chorionic villi, which retain stromal cells; septations and calcification may occur.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not call avascular villi with karyorrhexis chronic villitis; fetal vascular malperfusion mimics it.
- Do not overdiagnose CV in second-trimester villi, which are diffusely hypercellular rather than patchily inflamed.
- Do not mistake agglutinated villi for CV when adjacent vascular villi are normal.
- Do not mistake basophils or mast cells for plasma cells in chronic deciduitis.
- Do not dismiss perivillous fibrinoid in abortus specimens as post-miscarriage degeneration; first-trimester MPFD recurs.
- Do not equate a negative infection stain with proven absence of infection; Ureaplasma and other organisms are not routinely screened.

## Key Takeaways
1. Assign each chronic lesion to its compartment and cell type before naming it.
2. Grade CVUE and CC: grade drives the neurologic and recurrent-loss risk.
3. Apply the 25% PVFD and 50% MPFD thresholds literally when measuring involvement.
4. In CIV, expect recurrence and poor outcome; document it for future pregnancy planning.
5. Look for accompanying chronic deciduitis whenever basal chronic villitis is present.
