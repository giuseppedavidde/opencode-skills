# Chapter 20: Maternal Diabetes and Obesity

## Core Idea
Diabetes and obesity act on the placenta through two separate but overlapping mechanisms: glucose-mediated fetal hyperinsulinemia and the inflammatory, lipotoxic, and vascular burden of excess adipose tissue. The result is not one lesion but a set of adaptations — a larger, heavier, often hypercoiled placenta with villous hypervascularization, delayed villous maturation, and thickened trophoblast basement membrane. Crucially, roughly 40% of third-trimester placentas from diabetic women are histologically normal, so the diagnosis is probabilistic and must be made against gestational-age reference standards.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Diagnostic thresholds for hyperglycemia.**
- Diabetes diagnosed outside pregnancy: fasting plasma glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L, or 75 g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) 2-hour plasma glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L, or HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol.
- Gestational diabetes (GDM): fasting plasma glucose 5.1–6.9 mmol/L, or 75 g OGTT 1-hour plasma glucose ≥10.0 mmol/L, or 2-hour plasma glucose 8.5–11.0 mmol/L.
- Hyperglycemia first recognised in pregnancy that meets pre-pregnancy criteria is managed as preexisting diabetes, not GDM.

**Pedersen hypothesis.** Maternal hyperglycemia drives fetal hyperinsulinemia, which drives macrosomia and the characteristic fetal/neonatal complications.

**White classification.** The historical diabetes-in-pregnancy classification across which placental findings must be compared; the chapter cautions against comparing cohorts across different White classes.

**Distal villous immaturity (relative immaturity).** Diagnosed only when definite immature features are present in at least 30% of villi at term, and over 80% of villi at 32 weeks.

**Villous capillary development sequence.** Looped capillaries (9–25 weeks), then non-branching angiogenesis (up to about 32 weeks), then moderately branched capillaries in more mature villi.

## Key Concepts
- GDM outcomes: large for gestational age above the 90th centile 53%, preterm birth below 37 weeks 38%, macrosomia ≥4000 g 23%, neonatal hypoglycemia requiring intravenous dextrose 25%, NICU admission 25%, respiratory distress 9%, early preterm birth below 34 weeks 5%, hypertensive disorders 18%, cesarean section 63%.
- Tight glycemia matters: HbA1c ≥64 mmol/mol compared with <53 mmol/mol is associated with lower risk of large-for-gestational-age infants, adjusted odds ratio 0.20.
- Preeclampsia occurs in 8% of pregestational diabetes and 8.6% of GDM, against background rates of 0.5% and 1.0%.
- Single umbilical artery is reported in 3–5% of diabetic progeny versus about 1% overall.
- Diabetic placentas are larger: about 22% increase in weight, 33% increase in diameter, and 85% increase in volume. Placental weight is higher than in non-diabetic pregnancies even when the fetus is not macrosomic.
- Cord changes include a thicker "edematous" cord, longer cords, and abnormal coiling; one example was 975 mm long, hypercoiled at 3–4 coils per 5 cm, with a diameter of 21–25 mm.
- Villous hypervascularization is characteristic, with capillaries in a peripheral subtrophoblastic location rather than a central position. Persistent CD15 expression in fetal vessel endothelium in the third trimester is reported.
- Cytotrophoblast persists beneath 50–100% of the syncytiotrophoblast in diabetic villi, compared with 14% in normal term villi; there is a thicker syncytiotrophoblast, increased numerical density of cytotrophoblastic mitoses, and thickened basement membrane.
- Basement membrane thickening, endothelial "blebs", and glycogen deposits greater than normal are described, more marked in type 1 diabetes.
- About 60% of third-trimester placentas show some abnormality, and about 40% show normal villous maturity for gestation; about half of those with abnormal maturity show a specific pattern.
- Distal villous immaturity has a reported prevalence of 14% in pregestational diabetes.
- Obesity is defined by BMI, with underweight below 18.5 kg/m²; lower BMI cut-offs apply to Asian and South Asian populations.
- Diabetic and obese placentas can show increased syncytial knots, fibrinoid necrosis or atherosis in maternal spiral arteries (up to a reported proportion), and mural hypertrophy of decidual arteries.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming a large placenta proves diabetes.** Placental weight overlaps widely, and growth restriction can coexist with a normal or increased placental weight.
- **Diagnosing delayed villous maturation loosely.** It requires immature features in at least 30% of villi at term; otherwise the pattern is normal variation.
- **Treating hypervascularization as specific.** Excessive non-branched and excessive highly branched capillaries both occur in diabetes and obesity.
- **Ignoring obesity as an independent variable.** Obesity exerts its own effects on villous maturation and maternal vessels, so diabetic and obese cohorts cannot be pooled.
- **Comparing series across White classes.** Placental findings differ by class and by glycemic control.
- **Over-reading CD15 endothelial persistence.** It is reported as a marker, not a diagnostic criterion.

## Key Takeaways
1. Report placental weight, disc dimensions, cord length, diameter, and coiling against gestational-age norms, because the diabetic phenotype is dimensional.
2. State villous maturation explicitly, using the 30% threshold at term for distal villous immaturity.
3. Record cytotrophoblast persistence, syncytiotrophoblast thickness, and basement membrane change as a coherent triad.
4. Look for single umbilical artery, and note that the diabetic rate is 3–5%.
5. Interpret maternal spiral artery fibrinoid necrosis and mural hypertrophy in the context of the 8–8.6% preeclampsia rate.
6. Use HbA1c, not placental size, as the index of glycemic control when correlating pathology with clinical management.
