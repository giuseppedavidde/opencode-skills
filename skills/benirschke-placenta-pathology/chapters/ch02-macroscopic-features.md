# Chapter 2: Macroscopic Features of the Delivered Placenta

## Core Idea
The delivered placenta presents two surfaces with different diagnostic logic. The fetal surface is intact amnion over the chorionic plate, so its vessels, opacities, and deposits are read directly. The maternal surface is an artificial plane created by cleavage through the junctional zone, so its grooves reflect the decidua and the placental bed rather than a capsule. Shape and cord insertion are not decorative descriptors: both reflect the quality of implantation and correlate with placental efficiency.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Surface-by-surface reading.** Fetal surface, then maternal surface, then terminology. Each surface has its own normal structures, artifacts, and lesions.

**Shape index methods.** Multiple radial markers from the cord insertion yield a round placenta; the longest axis plus the longest perpendicular axis yields an elliptical one. Reported shape prevalence therefore depends on method, not biology.

**Lobe-to-villous-tree correspondence.** At term, 60–70 villous stems arise from the chorionic plate, each branching into one villous tree. Each maternal lobe holds one to several trees. A small marginal lobe occupied by a single tree is the placentone of Schuhmann.

**Critique of "fetal placenta" and "maternal placenta".** The terms come from species with noninvasive placentation, where fetal and maternal components separate cleanly at delivery. Human placentation is invasive and has no such cleavage plane.

## Key Concepts
- More than 90% of term placentas are disklike, flat, and round to oval. Nearly 10% are abnormal: bilobata, duplex, succenturiata, membranacea.
- Average diameter 22 cm, central thickness 2.5 cm, weight 470 g. All vary with mode of birth, timing of cord clamping, and interval to examination.
- The fetal surface is glossy because the amnion is intact and avascular; it covers plate, chorionic vessels, membranes, and cord.
- Chorionic vessels branch centrifugally from the insertion. Arteries are superficial and cross over veins; the reverse occurs in about 3% (Wentworth).
- The plate is opaque near large vessels, where collagen accompanies them, and transparent between them, appearing dark lilac to black from maternal blood.
- Opaque spots (bosselations) and larger independent opaque areas indicate subchorionic fibrin.
- Near the margin, an incomplete opaque subchorial closing ring of cytotrophoblast and collagen connects placenta to membranes.
- A circumvallate or circummarginate placenta results when the ring is overgrown peripherally by villous trees; membranes then insert superficially on the fetal surface.
- Increased shape variability lowers efficiency, measured as the placental-to-fetal weight ratio. Eccentric cord insertion lowers the fetoplacental weight ratio and raises umbilical resistance.
- Cord insertion is most commonly off-center. Excessive late first-trimester villus regression can move it toward the margin of the surviving disc.
- The maternal surface is opaque because it is an artificial cleavage plane in the junctional zone.
- Separation divides the junctional zone into the basal plate, which stays on the placenta, and the placental bed, which stays in utero.
- An incomplete groove system divides the surface into 10–40 maternal lobes. Internally the grooves correspond to placental septa.
- Septa are folds of the basal plate: irregular pillars or short sails that trace lobar borders. They do not reach the chorionic plate and do not divide the intervillous space into chambers.
- The basal plate is a heterogeneous mixture of trophoblast, decidua, extracellular debris, fibrinoid, and clot.
- No structure is of purely fetal composition except possibly the central chorionic plate. The marginal plate, cell islands, and septa may contain maternal cells.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating round versus elliptical as a fixed property rather than a method-dependent result.
- Describing septa as complete partitions of the intervillous space.
- Using fetal placenta and maternal placenta for human tissue; the terms mislead biochemical work.
- Dismissing shape and cord insertion as functionally unimportant.
- Assuming marginal cord insertion is always a normal variant rather than asymmetric villus regression.
- Reading the subchorial closing ring as a membrane abnormality.

## Key Takeaways
1. Record three dimensions, shape, cord insertion site, and the state of the subchorial closing ring.
2. Do not compare shape data across studies without knowing the shape-index method.
3. Treat 10–40 cotyledons as normal and inspect the grooves for missing tissue or excess fibrin.
4. Expect one to several villous trees per lobe; a single tree in a small marginal lobe is a placentone.
5. Avoid fetal placenta and maternal placenta; name the compartment instead.
6. Read eccentric cord insertion as a marker of reduced placental efficiency.
