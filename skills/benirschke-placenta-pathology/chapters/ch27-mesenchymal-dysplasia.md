# Chapter 27: Placental Mesenchymal Dysplasia

## Core Idea
Placental mesenchymal dysplasia (PMD) is a rare stromal disorder producing placentomegaly and abnormal mesenchyme of stem villi and chorionic plate vessels, while trophoblast is typically spared. It mimics partial hydatidiform mole on imaging and gross examination but has different genetics and different fetal implications. PMD is often mosaic, so abnormal and normal villi are intermixed. It carries substantial risks of growth restriction, fetal death, and Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome.

- **p57kip2 triage:** normal placenta and PHM show nuclear positivity in stroma and trophoblast; CHM is uniformly negative in stromal and cytotrophoblast cells; PMD shows an intermediate pattern with stromal loss in a subset of villi and retained trophoblast staining.
- **Androgenetic-biparental mosaicism/chimerism (ABM/C):** the dominant genetic mechanism, with one normal biparental (M1P1) lineage and one androgenetic lineage; also BWS imprinting-center alterations at 11p15.5 and mosaic UPD.
- **BWS consensus scoring:** PMD has been proposed as a cardinal pathologic feature in the international consensus scoring system for BWS.

## Key Concepts
- PMD definition: placentomegaly with mesenchymal abnormality variably affecting stem villi and chorionic plate vessels; the trophoblast component is spared.
- Epidemiology: more than 130 reported cases; incidence about 0.02% in some series, 0.002% in another; female predominance about 4:1. Discordance occurs in diamniotic dichorionic twins.
- Gross: enlarged, often massively so; tortuous, poorly ramified chorionic vessels; aneurysmal dilation and thrombosis; a magistral vascular pattern especially with BWS; long or abnormally coiled cord; cut surface shows enlarged stem villi, redundant vessels, edematous stroma, and mucinous cysts with a "grape-like" appearance.
- Temporal pattern: multicystic parenchymal lesions predominate in early to mid-second trimester; proximal chorionic plate vascular ectasia predominates at term. Small parenchymal cysts without large vessel aneurysm were reported at 15 weeks.
- Histology: variably thick-walled tortuous chorionic plate and stem villous vessels with perivascular cuffs of loose mesenchyme; a patchwork of enlarged edematous villi among normal villi; cisternae in stem and intermediate villi.
- Stroma: rich in hyaluronic acid (strong Alcian blue); loss of alpha smooth muscle actin relative to age-matched controls, with preserved desmin and vimentin, suggesting arrested mesenchymal development.
- Associations: BWS in 15–25% of cases; IUGR in 30–50%; IUFD in 18–36%; neonatal death 7%; normal neonatal outcome only 9–14%. Of IUFD and neonatal deaths, 44% had IUGR.
- Other associations: hepatic mesenchymal hamartoma (perinatal death 45% in a review of 9 cases), fetal hematologic abnormalities (anemia, thrombocytopenia, hemorrhage), chorangioma and chorangiomatosis, gastroschisis, pleuropulmonary blastoma, skeletal dysplasia, CHARGE syndrome.
- Maternal serum: AFP elevated in a large subset; hCG usually normal but mildly elevated in up to 38%.
- Proposed mechanisms of fetal harm: vascular steal through aneurysmal vessels with high-output cardiac failure, thrombosis with occlusion, rupture with exsanguination, and chronic hypoxia.
- Doppler: color Doppler may show a "stained-glass" appearance with flow in cystic spaces, unlike CHM, which shows no flow.

## Anti-patterns
- Do not diagnose partial hydatidiform mole from a cystic placenta with a live fetus. In one referral series, 15 cases initially called PHM proved to be PMD.
- Do not expect uniform villous abnormality. Mosaicism makes the lesion patchy, with normal parenchyma in between.
- Do not interpret p57kip2 loss as complete. PMD shows partial stromal loss with trophoblast retention; uniform negativity indicates CHM.
- Do not restrict the diagnosis to third trimester. Early second-trimester cases show only small cysts without vascular ectasia.
- Do not ignore the fetus when the placenta looks molar; BWS and IUGR change management.

## Key Takeaways
1. Consider PMD whenever a cystic, enlarged placenta accompanies a live fetus, especially a female fetus with growth restriction.
2. Confirm with p57kip2: patchy stromal loss with trophoblast retention supports PMD; uniform loss supports CHM.
3. Examine the chorionic plate and cord for ectatic, thrombosed, or aneurysmal vessels.
4. Report PMD explicitly so the neonate is screened for BWS and watched for hematologic and growth complications.
5. Measure AFP and counsel for recurrence risk and possible underlying imprinting disorder.
