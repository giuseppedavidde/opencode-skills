# Chapter 12: Abortion and Chromosomal Anomalies

## Core Idea
Pregnancy loss is not a single disease but an age-dependent, U-shaped risk curve whose dominant cause shifts with gestational age: chromosomal errors drive most first-trimester loss, implantation and maternal vascular disease drive mid-trimester loss, and fetal anomalies and maternal malperfusion dominate near term. Because roughly half of early losses carry a cytogenetic error, the abortus specimen is a diagnostic tissue, not waste. The pathologist's task is to separate the secondary degenerative changes of a dead conceptus from true molar disease and from a specific aneuploid phenotype. Most villous changes in abortuses are consequences of fetal demise, not causes of it.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Terminology framework.** "Abortion" is ambiguous and should be replaced. Use *miscarriage* for previable loss and *stillbirth* for loss after viability. Modifiers retain defined meanings: *missed* = retained more than 8 weeks after embryonic or fetal death; *habitual/recurrent* = varyingly more than two or three consecutive or nonconsecutive losses; *induced* = elective termination.

**Causes of fetal loss by gestational age (Table 12.2).** Three columns:
- 8–20 weeks: genetic, chorioamnionitis, disorders of implantation, unknown, fetal anomalies.
- 20–28 weeks: disorders of implantation, fetal growth restriction, maternal malperfusion, immune, immune villitis, unknown, fetal anomalies.
- 28 weeks–term: fetal anomalies, maternal malperfusion, fetal growth restriction, immune, unknown.

**Aneuploid placental phenotype scheme.** Assign villous morphology to specific trisomies only where a pattern exists: trisomy 16 (avascular, hydropic, giant cytotrophoblast in up to 30% of villi), trisomy 13/15 (variable maturation, reduced vasculature, giant cytotrophoblast in 50% of villi), trisomy 18 (large irregular villi, cytotrophoblastic inclusions), trisomy 21 (no characteristic villous pathology; nonpolar trophoblastic hyperplasia), monosomy X (nonspecific).

**Confined placental mosaicism (CPM) types.** Type 1 = trophoblast only; type 2 = mesenchymal villous core only; type 3 = both. Associated with reduced PAPP-A.

**p57kip2 imprinting test.** p57kip2 protein is absent from villous trophoblast and villous stroma in complete mole (dipaternal genome) but retained in partial moles and hydropic abortuses; it remains expressed in intermediate trophoblast and decidua.

## Key Concepts
- Embryo = specimen with crown–rump length up to 30 mm, i.e. less than 9 weeks; fetal stage begins at 8 weeks postconceptional age.
- About 25% or more of conceptions are lost in the first trimester; only about 10% of losses follow maternal recognition of pregnancy. Around 80% of all losses occur in the first trimester. Loss rate at 6–10 weeks is about 7.5%.
- Loss rate rises from 10–15% in young women to over 80% at maternal age 45.
- WHO stillbirth definition: from 28 weeks gestational age, or birth weight >1000 g when dating is uncertain. Stillbirth rate 3.4 per 1000 births in developed countries versus about 30 per 1000 in Sub-Saharan Africa.
- Chromosomal errors: 70% of losses in the first 6 weeks and 50% in the first 10 weeks. Trisomies 56%, triploidy 18%, monosomy X 15%; remainder double trisomies. A later series: 44.5% trisomies, 24.2% monosomy X, 15.1% triploidy. Perhaps 60% or more of first-trimester losses are chromosomal.
- Hydatid (hydropic) change: present in up to 81% of instrumented (induced) specimens but excessive in only 6.9%, versus 41% of spontaneous abortions. Focal hydatid change in 21% of legal abortions, diffuse in 2%.
- Breus mole (massive subchorionic thrombohematoma): up to 1 cm thick, or occupying over 50% of the placental surface. Found in 20.2% of 237 abortion specimens in one series, alongside molar change in 41.3%.
- Digynic triploidy gives a small placenta; diandric triploidy gives a large placenta with partial molar change, large hydropic villi, and large tortuous chorionic plate vessels.
- Paternal age raises miscarriage risk: odds ratios 1.06, 1.31, and 1.80 for paternal age 30–34, 35–39, and 40–64 years with maternal age held at 20–29.
- CVS-related loss is generally 1–2%. Septic abortion after CVS is reported from 10 to 20 weeks.
- Recurrent loss: cytogenetic abnormality found in about 4.1% of couples; a live birth after three losses occurs in about 80%.

## Anti-patterns
- **Calling hydropic abortus a mole.** Villi in the chorion laeve are normally edematous, so sampling site determines the appearance. Excess hydatid change is present in only 6.9% of induced specimens versus 41% of spontaneous ones.
- **Diagnosing molar pregnancy on histology alone in the first trimester.** Features overlap between complete mole, partial mole, and trisomies; even experts disagree, and villous morphology alone is not diagnostic.
- **Diagnosing a trisomy from villous morphology.** Trisomy 21 has no characteristic villous change; trisomy 13 and 18 changes mimic partial mole and are not diagnostic.
- **Reading basement membrane mineralization as viral change.** Finely granular calcification along the trophoblastic basement membrane is a degeneration artifact of ceased villous circulation.
- **Treating degenerative villous changes as the cause of loss.** Hydrops, fibrosis, avascularity, perivillous fibrin, and mineralization are secondary to fetal demise.
- **Ignoring an unreconstructed maternal artery in the decidua basalis.** This suggests impaired trophoblastic implantation and links early loss to later preeclampsia and growth restriction.
- **Using obsolete embryo terms** such as *Windei* (blighted ovum), nodular, cylindrical, stunted, or disorganized embryo; they carry no specific diagnostic meaning.

## Key Takeaways
1. Submit tissue for cytogenetics whenever an embryo is not available; embryonic tissue is preferred, and success rates of 82% and 76% are reported for short-term mitotic study.
2. Interpret hydropic villi against the sampling site and against the 6.9% versus 41% baseline before raising molar disease.
3. Use p57kip2 to separate complete mole from partial mole and hydropic abortus.
4. Report gestational-age-specific cause patterns, not a single differential, in perinatal autopsy and placental conference.
5. Treat Breus mole and massive perivillous fibrin as recurrence-risk lesions, and document chronic histiocytic intervillositis because it recurs.
6. Screen for antiphospholipid antibodies in recurrent loss, while accepting that the placental features are neither specific nor diagnostic.
