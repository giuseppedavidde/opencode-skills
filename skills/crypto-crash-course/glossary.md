# Glossary — Beginner Crypto Terms

## A
**Address**: A unique alphanumeric identifier (26-35 characters for Bitcoin, starting with 1 or 3) used to send and receive cryptocurrency. Case-sensitive, single-use recommended for privacy.

**Altcoin**: Any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin (e.g., Ethereum, Litecoin, Ripple).

**ASIC (Application-Specific Integrated Circuit)**: Specialized hardware designed specifically for mining cryptocurrencies, particularly Bitcoin.

**Atomic Swap**: A feature allowing direct exchange of one cryptocurrency for another without using an exchange.

## B
**Bitcoin**: The first and most well-known cryptocurrency, created by Satoshi Nakamoto in 2009. Digital gold, capped at 21 million coins.

**Block**: A group of verified transactions linked together and added to the blockchain. Contains a timestamp, hash, and reference to the previous block.

**Blockchain**: A decentralized, distributed ledger that records all transactions across a network of computers. Transparent, immutable, and secured by cryptography.

**Block Reward**: New bitcoins awarded to miners for successfully adding a block to the blockchain. Halves every 4 years (currently 6.25 BTC).

## C
**Centralized**: A system controlled by a single entity (e.g., a bank or government). Opposite of decentralized.

**CFD (Contract for Differences)**: A financial contract that allows trading price movements without owning the underlying asset. Includes margin calls to prevent negative balances.

**Cold Wallet**: An offline wallet not connected to the internet. Immune to remote hacking. The safest place for long-term storage.

**Consensus**: Agreement among network participants (>50% of nodes) that a transaction or block is valid.

**Cryptocurrency**: A digital currency secured through cryptography, operating on a decentralized network with no central authority.

**Cryptography**: Mathematical techniques used to secure communications and transactions in cryptocurrency networks.

## D
**Dapp (Decentralized Application)**: An application that runs on a blockchain network rather than a single server. Open source, no central point of failure.

**Decentralized**: A system with no single point of control. Authority and responsibility distributed across all participants.

**Double-Spend**: A flaw in digital cash where the same coins could be spent twice. Bitcoin's proof-of-work solved this problem.

## E
**Ethereum**: A blockchain platform for building decentralized applications (dapps) using smart contracts. Its currency is called Ether (ETH).

**Exchange**: A platform for buying, selling, and trading cryptocurrencies for other digital assets or fiat money.

## F
**Fiat Currency**: Government-issued currency not backed by a physical commodity (e.g., USD, EUR, GBP). Can be printed without limit.

**FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)**: Emotional reaction driving hasty investment decisions during price surges.

**Fork**: A split in a blockchain network. Hard forks create a new, separate cryptocurrency (e.g., Bitcoin Cash from Bitcoin).

## H
**Halving**: An event occurring every ~4 years where the Bitcoin block reward is cut in half. Reduces the rate of new coin creation.

**Hard Wallet**: See Cold Wallet.

**Hash**: A cryptographic code added to each block, protecting transaction data while keeping the transaction publicly viewable.

**Hot Wallet**: An internet-connected wallet used for active trading. Convenient but vulnerable to hacking. Keep only small amounts.

**HODL**: A meme-turned-strategy for long-term holding through market volatility. Originated from a typo for "hold."

## I
**ICO (Initial Coin Offering)**: A fundraising method where new cryptocurrency projects sell tokens to early investors before launch. Very high risk (96% failure rate).

**Immutability**: The property that once data is recorded on the blockchain, it cannot be altered or deleted.

## K
**KYC (Know Your Customer)**: Identity verification process required by most exchanges to comply with anti-money laundering regulations.

## M
**Masternode**: A full node that provides additional network services (e.g., InstantSend, PrivateSend in Dash). Requires a collateral stake (e.g., 1000 Dash).

**Miner**: A network participant who dedicates computing power to verify transactions and add blocks to the blockchain, earning new coins as reward.

**Mining**: The process of solving cryptographic puzzles to validate transactions and create new blocks. Secures the network.

## N
**Node**: Any computer connected to the cryptocurrency network that maintains a copy of the blockchain and relays transactions.

## P
**Paper Wallet**: Physical paper with printed private/public keys and addresses. Secure from hacking but vulnerable to physical theft/loss.

**Private Key**: A secret alphanumeric code that proves ownership of cryptocurrency. Never share it — anyone with your private key controls your coins.

**Proof-of-Work (PoW)**: The consensus mechanism where miners solve computational puzzles to validate transactions. Used by Bitcoin, Litecoin, etc.

**Public Key**: A cryptographic code derived from the private key, shareable with others to receive payments. Like an email address.

## R
**Ripple (XRP)**: A cryptocurrency and payment protocol designed for fast, low-cost international bank transfers.

## S
**Scrypt**: A memory-hard mining algorithm used by Litecoin. Resistant to ASIC mining, favoring CPU/GPU miners.

**Smart Contract**: Self-executing code on a blockchain that automatically performs actions when predetermined conditions are met.

**Stablecoin**: A cryptocurrency designed to maintain a stable value, typically pegged to a fiat currency like USD.

## T
**Token**: A digital asset issued on top of an existing blockchain (e.g., ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum), representing utility or value.

**Transaction Fee**: A small payment to miners for including a transaction in a block. Higher fees can prioritize faster processing.

**2FA (Two-Factor Authentication)**: An additional security layer requiring both a password and a second factor (phone, authenticator app).

## W
**Wallet**: Software or hardware that stores the private keys needed to access and manage cryptocurrency on the blockchain.

**Whitepaper**: A document published by a cryptocurrency project explaining its technology, goals, timeline, and tokenomics.

## X
**XRP**: The native digital asset of the Ripple payment network. Used for liquidity in cross-border payments.

## Z
**Zero-Knowledge Proof (zk-SNARK)**: A cryptographic method allowing one party to prove a statement is true without revealing any underlying information. Used by Zcash for private transactions.
