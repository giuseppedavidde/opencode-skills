# Chapter 1: History of Money & Bitcoin's Birth

## Core Idea
Cryptocurrencies are the latest step in a long evolution of currency — from barter to representative tokens to coins/paper to digital credit cards, and now to decentralized digital cash. Bitcoin was born directly from the 2008 financial crisis as a solution to centralized banking failures.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Barter-to-Bitcoin Timeline**: barter → shells/representative units → coins/paper → credit cards → cryptocurrencies
- **Centralized Failure → Decentralized Solution**: The 2008 crisis exposed that banks fail and governments print money at will, devaluing savings. Bitcoin solves both by removing central control.
- **Satoshi's Trilogy**: 2008 whitepaper ("Bitcoin: A Peer to Peer Electronic Cash System"), 2009 network launch, 2011 Nakamoto withdrawal to prove decentralization

## Key Concepts
- **Fiat currency weakness**: Governments can print unlimited money, causing inflation and devaluing savings
- **2008 crisis trigger**: Banks gave risky loans, defaulted, went bankrupt; taxpayer bailouts caused outrage
- **Double-spend problem solved**: Early digital cash failed because the same coins could be spent twice; Bitcoin's proof-of-work prevents this
- **Cryptocurrency definition**: A digital currency secured through cryptography, with no central authority or government oversight

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming crypto is "just like credit cards" — the underlying decentralized system is fundamentally different
- Expecting crypto to replace fiat overnight — it's still niche and volatile

## Key Takeaways
- Currency has always evolved to solve problems of trust, transport, and scarcity
- Bitcoin was a direct response to banks losing customer money in 2008
- Cryptocurrencies remove the need for trusted third parties entirely
