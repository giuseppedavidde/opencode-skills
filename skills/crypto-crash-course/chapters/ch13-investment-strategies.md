# Chapter 13: Investment Strategies

## Core Idea
Three main approaches exist for profiting from cryptocurrencies: long-term holding (hodling) for multi-year appreciation, short-term trading that exploits daily volatility, and Contracts for Differences (CFDs) for trading price movements without owning coins. Beginners should start with long-term holding and progress to active strategies.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Time Horizon Spectrum**: Long-term (years) → Medium-term (weeks/months) → Short-term/day trading (hours/days)
- **CFD Simulation Model**: Trade price direction without owning the underlying asset; margin calls protect from negative balances
- **Strategy Progression Path**: Beginner → long-term hold → medium-term swing → short-term/day trade

## Key Concepts
### Long-Term Investing
- Ignore daily ups and downs; focus on multi-year trends
- Cryptocurrency market predicted to reach 10x current size over several years
- Simply owning coins is an investment — time in the market beats timing the market
- Recommended for beginners and those unable to watch markets constantly

### Short-Term Trading
- Buy low, sell high repeatedly for compounding small profits
- Crypto volatility (multiple % point daily swings) is ideal for short-term traders
- Requires global 24/7 market awareness and cryptocurrency-specific news monitoring
- Don't rely solely on Bitcoin — new coins spike temporarily on attention

### Investing in a Company
- High risk of scams — "double your money" promises are almost always fraudulent
- Legitimate companies are hard to distinguish from sophisticated Ponzi schemes
- If it sounds too good to be true, it almost always is

### Contracts for Differences (CFDs)
- Trade price direction without owning coins
- Margin call automatically closes positions before negative balance
- Demo accounts (e.g., Plus500) for risk-free practice
- Strategies: accumulate (flawed due to premium), day trade, medium-term swing, long trend ride
- Market driven by fear and greed; news cycle dictates sentiment

## Anti-patterns
- Starting with short-term trading as a complete beginner
- Promising guaranteed returns or "double your money" schemes
- Holding CFD positions long-term (premiums eat profits)
- Letting emotions drive trading decisions during volatility

## Key Takeaways
- Long-term holding is the safest and most appropriate strategy for beginners
- Short-term trading requires experience, constant monitoring, and emotional discipline
- CFDs allow price speculation without ownership but have fees that penalize long holds
- Avoid any investment promising unrealistic returns
