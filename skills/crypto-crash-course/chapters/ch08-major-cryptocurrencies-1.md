# Chapter 8: Major Cryptocurrencies (Part 1)

## Core Idea
Beyond Bitcoin, several major cryptocurrencies serve distinct purposes. Ethereum enables smart contracts and decentralized apps (dapps). Ripple/XRP focuses on cross-border payments. Monero prioritizes privacy. Litecoin positions itself as "silver to Bitcoin's gold" for everyday transactions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Purpose-Based Classification**: Each cryptocurrency targets a specific problem — store of value (Bitcoin), smart contracts (Ethereum), payments (Ripple, Litecoin), privacy (Monero)
- **First-Mover vs. Specialized Competitor**: Bitcoin was first; others improve on specific dimensions (speed, privacy, programmability)

## Key Concepts
### Ethereum
- Platform for decentralized applications (dapps) using Smart Contracts
- Smart Contracts: self-executing scripts that trigger when conditions are met
- Ether (ETH) is the currency — rewards miners AND pays for contract execution
- EVM (Ethereum Virtual Machine): Turing-complete, runs on a global public node network
- Blocks mined every ~15 seconds (vs. Bitcoin's 10 minutes); no ASIC required for mining

### Ripple / XRP
- Purpose: fix outdated international payment infrastructure
- RippleNet connects banks, payment providers, and exchanges globally
- XRP settles cross-border payments in ~4 seconds with 1,500+ TPS capacity
- Real-time tracing and low cost vs. 3-5 day traditional bank transfers

### Monero
- Privacy-focused: transactions are untraceable and completely private
- Addresses are disguised — sender, recipient, and amount are hidden
- Users are their own bank; no third party can see wallet balances or history
- Useful for businesses negotiating prices and individuals avoiding targeting

### Litecoin
- "Silver to Bitcoin's Gold" — designed for everyday transactions
- Scrypt algorithm (memory-hard, ASIC-resistant)
- Blocks mined every 2.5 minutes (4x faster than Bitcoin)
- Atomic Swap feature: direct exchange between cryptocurrencies without an intermediary

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming all coins compete with Bitcoin — many serve completely different purposes
- Ignoring transaction speed differences when choosing a currency for active use
- Overlooking privacy implications — not all blockchains are anonymous

## Key Takeaways
- Each major cryptocurrency solves a different problem
- Ethereum's Smart Contracts enable programmable money and dapps
- Ripple focuses on banking infrastructure; Monero on privacy; Litecoin on daily spending
- Understanding each coin's purpose is essential before investing
