# Options Trading Glossary (Beginner-Friendly)

**Ask Price** — The price sellers want for an option contract. Always higher than the bid price.

**At the Money (ATM)** — An option whose strike price equals the current market price of the underlying asset.

**Bear Market** — A market where prices are falling or expected to fall. Associated with put strategies.

**Bid Price** — The price buyers are willing to pay for an option contract.

**Binary Option** — An option with only two outcomes: fixed payout if correct, total loss if wrong. No partial payouts.

**Breakeven Point** — The price the underlying must reach for a trade to become profitable (including premium and fees).

**Bull Market** — A market where prices are rising or expected to rise. Associated with call strategies.

**Call Option** — Contract giving the buyer the right (not obligation) to buy an asset at a fixed strike price before expiry.

**Cash Flow Statement** — Financial document showing actual cash inflows and outflows. Harder to manipulate than earnings.

**CFD (Contract for Difference)** — Derivative allowing traders to speculate on price movements without owning the underlying asset.

**Covered Call** — Strategy: own the underlying stock and sell (write) a call option against it. Generates premium income.

**Credit Spread** — An options spread where the premium received exceeds the premium paid (net credit).

**Debit Spread** — An options spread where the premium paid exceeds the premium received (net debit).

**Delta** — A Greek measuring how much an option's price changes per $1 move in the underlying asset.

**Derivative** — A financial instrument whose value derives from an underlying asset (stock, currency, index, etc.).

**Divergent Yield** — Annual dividend divided by share price. High yield can indicate health OR low growth prospects.

**Expiration (Expiry)** — The date when an option contract ceases to exist. After expiry, the option is worthless.

**Fundamental Analysis** — Evaluating an asset by examining related economic, financial, and qualitative factors.

**Greeks** — Risk measures (Delta, Gamma, Theta, Vega, Rho) that quantify option price sensitivity to various factors.

**In the Money (ITM)** — A call option where the strike price is below market price; a put where strike is above market.

**Iron Condor** — Strategy: two credit spreads (put spread + call spread) profiting from low volatility.

**Leverage** — Using borrowed capital or derivatives to amplify potential returns (and losses).

**Liquidity** — How easily an option can be bought or sold without affecting its price.

**Long Position** — Buying an option (or stock) with the expectation that its value will increase.

**Married Put** — Strategy: buy the underlying stock AND buy a put option for downside protection.

**Net Credit** — The net premium received when selling an option spread (money received minus money paid).

**Net Debit** — The net premium paid when buying an option spread (money paid minus money received).

**Out of the Money (OTM)** — A call option where strike is above market; a put where strike is below market. No intrinsic value.

**Premium** — The price paid to buy an option contract (per share, multiplied by 100 for true cost).

**Put Option** — Contract giving the buyer the right (not obligation) to sell an asset at a fixed strike price before expiry.

**Roll** — Closing an existing option position and opening a new one with different strike or expiration.

**Short Position** — Selling an option (or stock) expecting its value to decrease.

**Spread** — The difference between bid and ask price. Also: a strategy combining multiple options.

**Straddle** — Buying or selling both a call and a put with the same strike and expiry.

**Strangle** — Buying or selling both a call and a put with different strikes (both OTM) and same expiry.

**Strike Price** — The fixed price at which the option buyer can buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying.

**Technical Analysis** — Evaluating assets by analyzing statistical trends from trading activity (price, volume).

**Theta** — Greek measuring rate of time decay. Options lose value as expiration approaches.

**Time Decay** — The erosion of an option's value over time. Accelerates as expiration nears.

**Underlying Asset** — The stock, currency, index, or commodity that an option's value is based on.

**Vertical Spread** — An options strategy involving buying and selling options of the same type with different strikes.

**Volatility** — The rate and magnitude of price changes in an asset. High volatility = larger potential moves.

**Writer** — The seller of an option contract (obligated to fulfill if exercised).

**ZCB (Zero-Coupon Bond)** — Bond with no periodic interest; purchased at discount, pays full face value at maturity.
