# Chapter 1: Options Trading Advanced

## Core Idea
Options trading is a binary prediction: choose **call** (price goes up) or **put** (price goes down) before expiry. You don't own the underlying asset — you predict its direction. Wrong prediction = loss of full wager. Right prediction = ~90% net profit.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Binary Outcome Model** — only two possible results per trade, fixed payout, fixed loss
- **Insurance Analogy for Puts** — puts function like insurance: you pay a premium (option price) to protect against downside; the seller collects premiums and covers losses when they occur
- **Down Payment Analogy for Calls** — a call option is like a refundable down payment that locks in a future purchase price

## Key Concepts
- **Call Option**: Right to buy an asset at a fixed strike price before expiry. Used when you believe the price will rise. Profit = (market price - strike price) - premium paid
- **Put Option**: Right to sell an asset at a fixed strike price before expiry. Used when you believe the price will fall. Profit = (strike price - market price) - premium paid
- **Premium**: The cost of buying an option (e.g., $0.10 per share = $10 per contract)
- **Strike Price**: The price at which you can buy (call) or sell (put) the underlying asset
- **Expiry Date**: The deadline by which the option must be exercised or it expires worthless
- **Leverage**: Options amplify gains and losses relative to small price movements in the underlying

## Anti-patterns
- Treating options as gambling rather than researched decisions
- Using options as primary income source (cash flow is unpredictable)
- Jumping into advanced strategies without mastering basic calls and puts
- Letting the "easy money" perception lead to oversized bets

## Key Takeaways
- Options require predicting direction AND timing (expiry)
- Fixed payout (~90% profit) and fixed loss (full premium) per trade
- Practice with small amounts first; use demo accounts
- Options are a side income stream, not a replacement for a salary
