# Chapter 8: An Example of Trade

## Core Idea
ETFs and fund structures provide medium-to-long-term investment vehicles that can complement options trading. Capital-guaranteed structures combining zero-coupon bonds with options/ETFs offer downside protection with upside participation. Fundamental metrics (ROE, P/E, dividend yield) guide stock selection.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Capital-Guaranteed Investing** — Combine zero-coupon bonds (guarantee principal) with options or ETFs (upside potential). The bond portion shrinks with higher rates/longer duration. Formula: invest PV of target amount in ZCB, invest remainder in growth assets.
- **Sector Rotation** — Markets cycle through economic sectors. Identify stage of primary trend, then overweight sectors with growing relative strength and underweight declining ones. ETFs enable instant sector exposure without buying individual stocks.
- **Fundamental Stock Selection** — Use ROE, P/E ratio, P/B ratio, dividend yield, and analyst ratings/target prices to screen equities.

## Key Concepts
- **ETF (Exchange Traded Fund)**: Tracks an index/sector; lower costs than mutual funds; highly transparent
- **Active vs Passive Management**: Active funds try to beat benchmark (expensive, rare to succeed); passive ETFs track benchmark (low cost, predictable)
- **Zero-Coupon Bond (ZCB)**: Bond with no periodic interest; purchased at discount, pays face value at maturity
- **ROE (Return on Equity)**: Net income / shareholder equity. Higher ROE suggests value creation
- **P/E Ratio**: Price / Earnings. Low = potentially undervalued or low expectations
- **P/B Ratio**: Price / Book Value. <1 means trading below asset value
- **Dividend Yield**: Annual dividend / share price. High can mean healthy company or low growth prospects
- **Target Price**: Analyst's price prediction. Compare multiple analyst ratings for consensus

## Anti-patterns
- Relying solely on dividend yield without examining business plans
- Using analyst ratings as a sole decision factor (not a crystal ball)
- Ignoring ETF costs and tracking error
- Building capital-guaranteed structures without understanding interest rate risk

## Key Takeaways
- ZCB + options/ETF = DIY capital-guaranteed investment
- Sector rotation via ETFs captures macroeconomic trends
- Use multiple fundamentals (ROE, P/E, P/B, yield) together, never in isolation
- Analyst upgrades/downgrades provide useful signals but require context
- Active fund managers rarely outperform low-cost ETFs over the long term
