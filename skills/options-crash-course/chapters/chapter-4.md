# Chapter 4: Financial Leverage from Experts

## Core Idea
Data is useless without a framework. Fundamental analysis examines the underlying health of a company/economy through financial statements and qualitative factors. It answers "what should the price be?" rather than "what did the price do?"

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Fundamental Analysis Baseline Method** — Establish a baseline of company + industry performance, then adjust for macro/worldwide issues. Compare historical norms to current state to identify assets near boom/bust phase transitions
- **Quantitative + Qualitative Split** — Hard numbers (financial statements) and soft factors (business model, competitive advantage, management) together tell the full story
- **Phase Timing** — Best entry points are at the end of post-bust or post-boom periods, where market freedom is highest and risk tolerance is max

## Key Concepts
- **Balance Sheet**: Snapshot of assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time
- **Income Statement**: Revenue, expenses, and profit over a period (quarterly/annual)
- **Cash Flow Statement**: Actual cash in/out; harder to manipulate than earnings
- **SEC Filings (10-K, 10-Q)**: Required disclosures via EDGAR system at sec.gov
- **Business Model**: How a company makes money (found in 10-K or website)
- **Competitive Advantage**: Operational effectiveness (same thing, better) vs Strategic positioning (unique approach)
- **Macro Factors**: Technology shifts, political changes, global economic trends
- **Relative Strength**: How strongly an asset is performing compared to its sector

## Anti-patterns
- Relying only on qualitative "gut feel" without examining financial statements
- Ignoring cash flow statements (easier to manipulate earnings than cash)
- Overlooking macro/geopolitical trends that affect entire sectors
- Buying during boom peaks or bust troughs instead of at transition points

## Key Takeaways
- Start with fundamental analysis to build a thesis, then use technical analysis for timing
- Cash flow statements are the most reliable financial document
- Use SEC.gov/EDGAR for official financial filings
- The earlier in a boom/bust phase you enter, the more risk you can handle
- 95% of growth companies started with a major structural change
