# Chapter 3: Platforms and Tools for Options Trading

## Core Idea
Your broker determines your costs, tools, and available research. For beginners, low-cost brokers matter most. Bid/ask spreads reveal supply/demand dynamics for any option contract. The size of your investment fund dictates how many trades you can make and how much risk you can absorb.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **10% Risk Rule** — Never risk more than 10% of your total investment fund on a single trade. At $2,000 fund: $200/trade max. This limits you to ~6 trades/month, giving time to learn
- **Bid/Ask Spread as Market Signal** — Spread size indicates option liquidity and potential direction. Wide spread + low volume = possible reversal ahead
- **Per-Share Pricing** — Options are listed per share but must be purchased in 100-share increments (multiply listed price by 100 for true cost)

## Key Concepts
- **Bid Price**: What buyers are willing to pay for an option
- **Ask Price**: What sellers want for an option
- **Spread**: Ask - Bid. Immediate loss per share if buying and selling instantly
- **Contract Size**: 1 option contract = 100 shares
- **Option Ticker Format**: C-MSFT Jul 20 (Call/Put, Ticker, Month Year)
- **Recommended Minimum Fund**: $2,000 absolute minimum; $4,000–$5,000 ideal
- **Broker Recommendations**: Options Review (lowest fees for small trades), Charles Schwab (best research tools)

## Anti-patterns
- Ignoring the bid/ask spread when calculating potential profit
- Assuming price per share scales linearly across multiple contracts
- Starting with less than $2,000 (brokerage fees eat too much)
- Using a single broker for both cheap trades AND research tools

## Key Takeaways
- Multiply option price by 100 to get true contract cost
- Spread = immediate loss on round-trip; minimize it by choosing liquid options
- Risk < 10% of total fund per trade
- $2,000 minimum to start; expect max ~6 trades in first month
- Use separate brokers for trading (low fees) and research (best tools)
