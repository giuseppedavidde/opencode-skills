# Chapter 7: Scenario Planning & Trade Management

## Core Idea
Scenario planning operationalizes analysis into actionable trades. Use "If X, then Y" conditional logic, manage risk through layered stop placement, and take profits at HVN/VPOC targets. The goal is repeatable, rules-based execution, not prediction.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **One-Move vs Two-Move Scenarios**
  - **One-Move**: Price is already favorably positioned. You only need the entry trigger at the zone. Example: price is above a potential Spring — wait for SOSbar and buy stop above.
  - **Two-Move**: Price must first reach the trading zone (move 1), then trigger (move 2). Example: price is above the Creek but you want to buy at the BUEC test — first wait for the bearish pullback to the zone, then wait for the bullish trigger.

- **Entry Triggers (Intentionality Candles)**
  - **SOSbar (Sign of Strength)**: Wide-range bullish candle, close in upper third, relatively high volume. Confirms buying initiative.
  - **SOWbar (Sign of Weakness)**: Wide-range bearish candle, close in lower third, relatively high volume. Confirms selling initiative.
  - **Order Flow confirmation**: Look for initiative (ASK imbalances for buys, BID imbalances for sells) inside the trigger candle.

- **Entry Orders**: Use BUY STOP orders (above current price for longs) and SELL STOP orders (below current price for shorts). Stop orders filter out false triggers — if the move doesn't have momentum to trigger the stop, it lacks conviction. Do NOT use limit orders to anticipate a move.

## Key Concepts
- **Stop Loss Placement Layered**:
  1. Beyond the trigger candle's opposite extreme (minimum).
  2. Beyond the structure level (Creek/Ice for more room).
  3. Beyond an LVN (Low Volume Node = expected rejection zone).
  4. Beyond VPOC of the profile (widest, only if R:R acceptable).
  Each layer should have more operating levels protecting it.

- **Take Profit Targets (priority order)**:
  1. **HVN in the direction of trade**: Price is magnetically attracted to high-volume zones.
  2. **VPOC of the target HVN**: Precision level within the HVN zone.
  3. **Naked VPOC**: Previous session VPOC not yet tested.
  4. **Developing VPOC (DVPOC)**: Levels that were VPOC during a session but are not the final VPOC.
  5. **Liquidity zones**: Previous relevant highs/lows when no volume reference exists.

- **Handling "Price Went Without Us"**: Do NOT chase. If the analysis was correct, be satisfied with the insight — capital preservation is paramount. Wait for the next trading zone. Entering in desperation leads to losses.

- **Label Changes & Flexibility**: Labels are dynamic. A potential Spring that fails to produce any SOS becomes a simple test of weakness. Always give greater relevance to the latest information.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using limit orders to pre-empt the trigger**: This is betting, not trading. You're predicting two moves ahead when you can only plan one.
- **Ignoring alternative scenarios**: If price reaches a resistance zone, always prepare for BOTH breakout (continuation) AND shakeout (reversal). Have plans for both.
- **Moving stops to breakeven too early**: Manage risk actively. Wait for price to reach the first relevant target zone before considering breakeven.

## Key Takeaways
1. Know your context → identify your zone → plan your scenario (1 or 2 moves) → wait for intentionality → execute with stop order.
2. Stop loss is a function of structure and volume levels, not fixed dollar amounts.
3. Take profit at HVN/VPOC levels — let the magnetic nature of volume work in your favor.
