# Chapter 6: Wyckoff 2.0 — Context & Trading Framework

## Core Idea
Wyckoff 2.0 combines the logical framework of Wyckoff methodology (structures, events, phases, 3 laws) with Volume Profile objectivity (value zones, VPOC, HVN/LVN) and Order Flow precision (absorption/initiative). The result is a complete, repeatable trading system from context analysis to trade execution.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 4 Pillars of Wyckoff 2.0**
  1. **Wyckoff Methodology**: Context from structures, phases, events. The "what and why."
  2. **Auction Theory**: Understanding equilibrium vs imbalance. Balance = ranges = cause. Imbalance = trends = effect.
  3. **Volume Profile**: Objective identification of trading zones and levels. The "where."
  4. **Order Flow**: Precision timing at the zone. The "when."

- **Operating Scheme (4-Step Checklist)**
  1. **Context Analysis**: Range or trend? At extremes, inside, interacting with value zone, or away from value zone?
  2. **Trading Zone Identification**: Structure levels (Creek, Ice, LPS, UTAD). Volume levels (VAH, VAL, VPOC, VWAP). Volume zones (HVN, LVN).
  3. **Scenario Approach**: Continuous validation protocol ("If X, then Y"). Always include an alternative scenario.
  4. **Position Management**: Entry trigger (SOS/SOW bar or Order Flow pattern) → Stop (beyond LVN/trigger candle) → Target (HVN, naked VPOC, previous liquidity zone).

## Key Concepts
- **Context Analysis — 4 Trading Situations**:
  1. **Range at extremes**: Classic Wyckoff — wait for Spring or Upthrust at structure boundaries. Volume Profile — reversal at VAH/VAL.
  2. **Range inside**: Trade in favor of the last HVN. Look for LPS inside the range if accumulation is suspected. Need enough distance to justify risk.
  3. **Trend interacting with value zone**: After breakout, wait for test of broken structure/VA extreme. Phase D = BUEC test; Phase E = continuation.
  4. **Trend away from value zone**: Pullback to any trading level (previous session levels, weekly VWAP, VPOC of last impulse, LVN). Wait for minor structure development.

- **Distinguishing Accumulation from Distribution** (6-clue system):
  1. **Phase A test location**: ST at bottom = weakness; ST at top = strength.
  2. **Phase B test/reaction**: Test at top + structural failure below = accumulation. Test at bottom + structural failure above = distribution.
  3. **Phase C shakeout**: The dominant clue — Spring = accumulation; UTAD = distribution.
  4. **Phase D price/volume action**: SOS/SOW bars confirming the shakeout.
  5. **Overall volume**: Decreasing during range = accumulation; high/unusual = distribution.
  6. **Weis Wave analysis**: Bullish waves predominant + increasing = accumulation. Bearish waves predominant = distribution.

- **Scenario Planning**: Only propose one move at a time. One-move scenario: price is already positioned favorably relative to the trading zone. Two-move scenario: price must first reach the zone (move 1) then trigger (move 2).

- **Continuous Validation Protocol**: "If price breaks Creek, wait for test to look for buys. If failed breakout, wait for opposite test to short." Always maintain an alternative scenario.

## Anti-patterns
- **Predicting beyond the next move**: Only scenario plan for the immediate next price movement. Don't assume you know Phases D and E from Phase B.
- **Ignoring higher timeframe context**: Always start analysis from higher TF (weekly/daily) and move down. A lower TF Spring may be meaningless against a weekly distribution.
- **Sticking to a scenario after invalidation**: If the price does what you didn't expect, the new information is more relevant than your old analysis.

## Key Takeaways
1. Always answer: What is the context? (range/trend, which sub-type). What levels matter? (structure + VP). What is the scenario? ("If X, then Y"). How will I manage the trade?
2. The shakeout is the dominant event — bias in its direction unless clearly invalidated.
3. Trade in favor of the last accumulation/distribution until the market develops a structure in the opposite direction OR loses the last identified value zone.
