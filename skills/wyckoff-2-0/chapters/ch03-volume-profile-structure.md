# Chapter 3: Volume Profile — Structure & Components

## Core Idea
The Volume Profile is an objective representation of volume at each price level (horizontal volume), showing WHERE trading occurred, unlike vertical volume which shows WHEN. It reveals value zones, rejection areas, and institutional interest points.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Horizontal vs Vertical Volume**: Vertical volume (classic) tells you when large traders were active. Horizontal volume (Volume Profile) tells you where they were active. They are complementary, not substitutes.

- **Profile Composition** (Gaussian distribution model)
  - **Value Area (VA)**: The central 68.2% of total volume, bounded by Value Area High (VAH) and Value Area Low (VAL). Represents the acceptance/equilibrium zone.
  - **Extremes (High/Low)**: The highest/lowest price reached. Finished auction = decreasing volume at extremes (Low Volume Node). Unfinished auction = High Volume Node at the extreme, suggesting a future revisit.
  - **Volume Point of Control (VPOC)**: The single price level with the most volume. Represents the fairest price, where most institutional contracts were traded. Price above VPOC = buyer control; below = seller control.
  - **VWAP (Volume Weighted Average Price)**: The average price weighted by volume. Key institutional benchmark. Above VWAP = "we bought low"; below = "we sold high".
  - **High Volume Node (HVN)**: Zones of high trading — balance, magnetism, likely value acceptance. Past HVNs act as price magnets.
  - **Low Volume Node (LVN)**: Zones of low trading — imbalance, rejection. Act as support/resistance. Rejection takes two forms: V-turn or rapid movement through.

## Key Concepts
- **Market Memory**: Short-term. Recent trading zones are more important than older ones. The most recent equilibrium zone is where price is most likely to return first.
- **Finished vs Unfinished Auction**: A finished auction shows volume tapering at the extreme (rejection). An unfinished auction shows a sharp cut-off with high volume at the extreme (will likely be revisited).
- **Profile Types**:
  - **Fixed Range**: Manual, static. Best for analyzing specific structures or trend legs.
  - **Session**: Dynamic, per day. Used for intraday trading.
  - **Composite**: Aggregates multiple sessions. Best for context analysis — identifies macro HVNs and LVNs.
- **P-shape Profile**: Bullish imbalance first, then range. Suggests strength, future distribution/reaccumulation.
- **b-shape Profile**: Bearish imbalance first, then range. Suggests weakness, future accumulation/redistribution.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading at VPOC**: Avoid entering trades near VPOC — it's the fairest price and the market will oscillate around it during equilibrium.
- **Overvaluing old profiles**: The market has short memory — prioritize the most recent equilibrium zone over older, larger ones.
- **Confusing Market Profile with Volume Profile**: MP uses TPOs (time-based); VP uses volume. They look similar but will differ where price moves fast at high volume.

## Key Takeaways
1. VAH/VAL define the acceptance zone; VPOC defines market control. Always know where price sits relative to all three.
2. Use composite profiles for context (macros HVN/LVN) and fixed/session profiles for trade execution.
3. The profile shape (P/b) gives early structural bias — P suggests bullish imbalance, b suggests bearish imbalance.
