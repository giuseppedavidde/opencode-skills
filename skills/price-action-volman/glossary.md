# Glossary of Price Action Terms

**Bracket Order (OCO)** — An order type enclosing an open position with both a target and a stop. When one side is hit, the other is automatically cancelled. Standard: 20-pip target, 10-pip stop. (Ch 4)

**Break for Failure** — A counter-break trade taken when a breakout shows all normal entry characteristics but goes against the dominant pressure. The trap falls on those who accepted the break, and the counter-break rides their forced exits back in line with dominance. (Ch 7)

**Buildup / Pre-breakout Tension** — A tight cluster of alternating bars at a support or resistance boundary indicating a tug-of-war between bulls and bears. The essential prerequisite for a high-odds breakout. Without it, decline the trade. (Ch 2, Ch 5)

**Ceiling Test** — After a failed breakout attempt, price retraces inside the pattern and launches a new attack on the same boundary. The re-break often carries high follow-through odds. (Ch 2)

**Combi (Pattern Break Combi)** — A two-bar setup: a tall powerbar followed by a small inside bar. The break of the inside bar (or powerbar) triggers entry. Signals concentrated tension within buildup. (Ch 5)

**Contrarian** — A trader who positions against the prevailing break, anticipating failure. Their presence is a constant threat to breakout trades and must be factored into every assessment. (Ch 2, Ch 7)

**Dominant Pressure** — The prevailing directional force in the chart, identified by the overall slope. Higher highs and higher lows = bull dominance. Never trade against it without experience. (Ch 2)

**Double Pressure** — The mechanism driving follow-through: both bulls buying and bears covering their shorts temporarily join forces on the same side of the market. (Ch 2)

**Entry Bar** — The bar that breaks the signal bar's high/low, triggering market order entry. (Ch 5)

**False Break Trap** — A breakout that pierces a barrier without prior buildup and instantly reverses. Contrarians design these to catch impatient breakout traders. (Ch 2)

**False High / False Low** — A break of a significant high or low that immediately reverses, trapping late entrants. Distinguished from a proper break by the absence of buildup. (Ch 2)

**Follow-through** — Sustained price movement in the breakout direction, driven by double pressure. (Ch 2)

**Market Order** — An order fired "at the market" for immediate execution. Used for entry to ensure the break is caught. (Ch 4)

**Pattern Break** — The standard breakout setup: buildup at a pattern boundary, a signal bar closing against it, entry on the next bar breaking the signal bar. (Ch 5)

**Pattern Break Pullback** — A second-instance entry after the initial break was untradeable. Price either clings at the barrier or pulls back to test it, offering a new signal bar. (Ch 5)

**Proper Break** — A breakout with clear buildup at the boundary, a tight signal bar, and the close against the barrier. Highest probability of follow-through. (Ch 2)

**Pullback Reversal** — A trade in line with dominance after a correction has run its course. Correction types: price (diagonal, 50-60% retracement) or time (horizontal flag). The 25ema filter determines readiness. (Ch 2, Ch 5)

**Round Number Effect** — Psychological price levels (e.g., 1.3500, 1.3600) that act as support/resistance. Price often stalls, fights, or reverses at these levels. (Ch 2)

**Signal Bar** — The crucial bar within buildup that sets up the trade. In a bull breakout, a bullish bar closing against the pattern barrier. Its break triggers entry. (Ch 5)

**Slippage** — The difference between the expected fill price and the actual fill price when using market orders. Faster conditions increase slippage. Accept as a minor cost. (Ch 4)

**Support and Resistance** — Price levels from which the market previously bounced. Once broken, former support becomes resistance and vice versa. (Ch 2)

**Tease Break Trap** — A breakout with some buildup but not at the critical boundary. Prices may initially counter the break, then succeed on a second attempt. Still risky for tight stops. (Ch 2)

**25 Exponential Moving Average (25ema)** — A pressure gauge plotted on the chart. Sloping up = bulls in control; sloping down = bears. Used as a filter for pullback reversals and dominance assessment. Not a trading signal. (Ch 3)

**20/10 Bracket** — Standard management: 20-pip target, 10-pip stop. The target aligns with typical follow-through distance; the stop is tight enough to prevent hope-and-pray. (Ch 4)
