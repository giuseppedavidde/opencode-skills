# Chapter 7: Skipping Trades and Trading Breaks for Failure

## Core Idea
Trade selection is as important as entry technique. Saving one or two unnecessary losses per week makes the difference between success and failure. The break-for-failure setup turns this selectivity into a weapon.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Break for Failure (Trap Trade)**: When a breakout has all the characteristics of a tradable event (boundary, buildup, signal bar) but clearly goes against the dominant pressure, it may be traded in the opposite direction. The trap falls shut on those who accepted the counter-dominance break; the counter-break back in line with dominance can generate immediate follow-through.
- **Conditions-over-setup principle**: A beautiful setup in poor conditions is still a losing trade. The broader pressure picture always trumps the local pattern.

## Key Concepts
- **Why skip**: The root of unnecessary losses is spending too much attention on the setup and too little on the surrounding conditions and pressures.
- **Trap trade requirements**: (a) the break must be against dominant pressure, (b) the setup must meet all normal criteria (boundary, buildup, signal bar), (c) the counter-break entry is taken on a signal bar in the opposite direction.
- **Strategic benefit of selectivity**: Watching others take bad trades tells you where trapped participants will soon need to bail out, potentially fueling your direction.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trading every setup that appears**: Most common pitfall. Conditions must endorse the trade.
- **Forcing the break-for-failure on weak breaks**: Not every false breakout should be faded. The break must look genuinely tradable to those who took it.
- **Taking traps against a very strong trend**: Dominance must be clear but not so extreme that the trap gets overwhelmed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Skipping poor trades is a skill — master it before adding advanced setups.
2. The break-for-failure is one of the most potent setups when conditions align.
3. Study the conditions, not just the pattern. The broader picture decides.
