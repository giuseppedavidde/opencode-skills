# Chapter 10: Trade Size — Compounding

## Core Idea
Two traders taking identical trades with identical results can have vastly different bottom lines — the sole difference is compounding. Weekly reinvestment of profits dramatically outperforms fixed position sizing over time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Compounding mechanics**: Each week's gains are incorporated into the next week's trading volume. A trader scoring 2.5 points/week (25 pips/week with 10-pip stop) on a $5,000 account with 2% risk grows to $52,000 in 48 weeks vs. $17,000 for a non-compounding trader — on the exact same trades.
- **Compound interest calculation**: Use standard compound interest calculators. The key variable is consistent point scoring per week, not per-trade heroics.

## Key Concepts
- **Fixed vs. compounding**: Fixed = $250/week withdrawal style. Compounding = reinvesting each week's gain into position size. Both traders score the same points; compounding produces 3x+ the annual result.
- **Practical constraints**: Futures traders face contract size steps; live traders may need to withdraw for expenses. The ideal is to keep the account purely for trading and let it grow.

## Anti-patterns
- **Increasing risk % to accelerate growth**: Stick to consistent risk per trade (e.g., 2%). Compounding multiplies results without increasing risk per trade.
- **Pulling profits out habitually**: If compounding is the goal, keep earnings in the account. Only withdraw when necessary.

## Key Takeaways
1. Compounding transforms consistent small gains into exponential growth.
2. The same trades, same points — compounding vs. fixed = 3x+ difference.
3. Keep the trading account separate and let it compound uninterrupted.
