# Chapter 2: Price Action Principles — Theory

## Core Idea
All sound trading techniques derive from seven elementary concepts that repeat in any technical chart. Master these before any strategy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Double Pressure**: For follow-through, bulls and bears must temporarily join forces (bulls buying + bears covering). Anticipate these pops from buildup clusters.
- **Support & Resistance**: Former S/R levels reverse roles when broken. Use them to gauge dominance, not to blindly trade breaks or bounces.
- **False Break / Tease Break / Proper Break**: False = no buildup, instant reversal. Tease = partial buildup, may succeed second time. Proper = clear buildup at the boundary, highest odds.
- **False Highs and Lows**: A break of a high/low that immediately reverses, trapping late entrants. Distinguish from proper breaks by absence of buildup.
- **Pullback Reversals**: Corrections in price (diagonal, 50-60% retracement) or time (horizontal flag). Trade in line with dominant pressure after the correction reaches the 25ema.
- **Ceiling Test**: After a failed breakout, price retests the boundary from inside the pattern. The re-break carries high follow-through odds.
- **Round Number Effect**: Psychological levels (e.g., 1.3500) attract price action — stalls, fights, reversals. Treat as additional S/R confirmation.

## Key Concepts
- **Buildup**: A tight cluster of alternating bars at a boundary — the essential prerequisite for a high-odds break.
- **Dominant Pressure**: Identified by the overall slope. In a bull market, corrections fail to undercut former lows. Never trade against it without experience.
- **Three pre-entry checks**: (a) break in line with dominance? (b) market ranging or trending? (c) obstacles to target?

## Anti-patterns
- **Shorting a break at point a after a strong bull run**: Even with a topping pattern, defying dominance burns accounts.
- **Treating every break as tradable**: Most breaks fail. Buildup is the non-negotiable filter.

## Key Takeaways
1. Double pressure is the engine of follow-through; buildup is its signature.
2. False breaks exist because contrarians are always hunting. Let them validate your side.
3. If the stop must be placed wide, the break is unattractive for continuation.
