# Chapter 3: Price Action Principles — Practice

## Core Idea
Translate theory into real chart reading. Each annotated chart shows multiple core principles at work simultaneously — round number fights, false breaks, tease breaks, proper breaks, pullbacks, ceiling tests, and false highs/lows.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Practical 25ema Application**: Plot the 25 exponential moving average as a pressure gauge. Sloping up with bars above = bulls on top; sloping down = bears. Not a trading signal, a filter.
- **Chart setup conventions**: Classic candlestick (white bull / black bear), 6-7 hours of 5-min action per chart, slight horizontal spacing between bars, compressed vertical scale for calm appearance.
- **Buildup recognition in practice**: Not all sideways action is buildup. Distinguish between active tug-of-war at a level vs. dead price action in a low-volume lull.

## Key Concepts
- **Buildup vs. dead action**: A tight cluster in active trade = tension. A long sideways phase in thin volume = nothingness.
- **Dominance shift cues**: When the ruling party can no longer recoup from setbacks, a power shift may be ahead.
- **One-pip chart setting**: Essential for clean barrier alignment. Pipette-based charts cause premature entries.

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-plotting historical data**: More than a day's worth of 5-min action on screen creates conflicting information.
- **Stretching the price axis vertically**: Creates a jagged, aggressive feel. Compress vertically for calm objectivity.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every chart contains multiple principle examples — train your eye to see them simultaneously.
2. The 25ema is a reference gauge, not a rule. Use it to confirm pressure, not to generate signals.
3. Backtest at least one full year on 5-minute before trading live.
