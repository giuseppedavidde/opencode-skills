# Chapter 4: Volume Price Analysis — First Principles

## Core Idea
VPA reduces to one question: is volume validating price or showing an anomaly? Six principles guide the analysis: art not science, patience, relativity, practice, technical context, and the binary validation/anomaly framework.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Six Guiding Principles**: Art Not Science, Patience, All Relative, Practice, Technical Analysis Support, Validation or Anomaly
- **Validation Examples**: Wide spread + high vol = genuine; narrow spread + low vol = genuine
- **Anomaly Examples**: Wide spread + low vol = trap up; narrow spread + high vol = absorption/weakness
- **Three-Step VPA Process**: Micro (single candle) → Macro (candle cluster) → Global (full chart)
- **Effort vs Result Applied**: To individual candles AND to trends (rising prices = rising vol; falling prices = rising vol)
- **Cause & Effect Applied**: Small cause (few candles) = small effect; large cause (extended consolidation) = large effect

## Key Concepts
- Two levels of validation: (1) candle-level, (2) trend-level
- Rising market requires effort to rise; falling market ALSO requires effort to fall
- Market is like an oil tanker — does NOT reverse on a dime; takes multiple candles
- Rain shower analogy: reversal builds gradually, sellers/buyers mopped up over time
- Volume is only ever compared to recent history on same feed
- First anomaly sign = alarm bell, not trade signal

## Anti-patterns
- Entering immediately on first signal without waiting for confirmation
- Expecting markets to reverse instantly — patience is mandatory
- Comparing volume across different feeds or instruments

## Key Takeaways
- Only two states exist: validation or anomaly
- Effort vs result applies to both single bars and entire trends
- Always look for alarm bells (anomalies) as early warning, not entry triggers
- Use the 3-step process: micro → macro → global for every analysis
