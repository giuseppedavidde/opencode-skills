# Chapter 10: Volume Price Analysis Examples

## Core Idea
VPA applied to real charts across multiple markets: US stocks (Honeywell, Duke Energy), ETFs (SLV, GLD), spot forex (AUD/USD), futures (YM, ES E-mini), and tick charts (Coffee). The same principles work identically in every market.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stock Example (HON)**: Daily. Shooting star → wide spread down → stopping volume → hammer → low-vol test → breakout. Patience required.
- **Stock Example (DUK)**: Daily. 18-month journey. Low volume top → waterfall → stopping volume → low-vol test → gap up on high vol (genuine) → exhaustion with ultra-high vol on narrow spreads.
- **ETF Example (SLV)**: 5-min. Waterfall → stopping volume → hammer → test → breakout → failure. Demonstrates scalping-level VPA.
- **ETF Example (GLD)**: 15-min. Bearish context. Gap down on extreme vol → repeated stopping volume → ultra-high vol on narrow spreads = extreme weakness → further gap down.
- **Forex Example (AUD/USD)**: 15-min. Rising → shooting star + high vol → reversal short → volume confirms pullbacks as pauses, not reversals → exit on stopping volume.
- **Weekly Forex (AUD/USD)**: Selling climax on weekly chart. 18-month cycle. Accumulation → markup → exhaustion (wide bars, no volume) → climax.
- **Futures (YM E-mini)**: 5-min. Gap up open → rising vol = genuine → pullbacks on falling vol = normal → gradual decline in buying vol = warning.
- **Futures (ES E-mini)**: 10-min. Classic trap up open: wide spread + LOW volume = fakeout. Next day genuine: wide spread + HIGH volume.
- **Tick Chart (Coffee)**: 80-tick. Operators spike vol → weakness (narrow spread on ultra-high vol) → consolidation → waterfall.

## Key Concepts
- VPA works in ALL markets: stocks, ETFs, forex, futures — cash and derivatives
- VPA works in ALL chart types: time-based and tick-based
- Low volume tests after climax = highest-probability entry signals
- Ultra-high volume on narrow spreads = climax (buying or selling)
- Use multiple examples to calibrate your "volume eye" for high/low/average on each instrument

## Anti-patterns
- Assuming a gap up is automatically bullish — check volume first
- Ignoring the broader trend context (e.g., silver was bearish, GLD had bearish context)
- Being fooled by extreme volume bars that distort the scale for the rest of the session

## Key Takeaways
- VPA is universal — one methodology, all markets
- Worked examples build pattern recognition for live trading
- Volume always tells the truth, even when price action is ambiguous
- Study these examples repeatedly to internalise the VPA thought process
