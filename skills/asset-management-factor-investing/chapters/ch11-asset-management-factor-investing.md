# Chapter 11: "Real" Assets

## Core Idea
Many "real" assets — inflation-indexed bonds (linkers), commodities, and real estate — turn out not to be that "real." While a single linker delivers a constant real return, linkers *as an asset class* have almost no correlation with inflation. Among commodities, only energy has been a decent inflation hedge; gold, surprisingly, is a poor inflation hedge. Real estate has some, but far from complete, inflation-hedging ability. **Boring T-bills** are actually the best at hedging inflation. The chapter distinguishes inflation *hedging* (the correlation of returns with inflation) from the long-run *level* of returns (an asset can have a high long-run mean and still be a lousy inflation hedge).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Inflation hedging vs long-run return** — hedging is a *co-movement* statement (corr(return, inflation) ≈ 1); long-run return is a *mean* statement. A high-return asset can hedge poorly; a low-return one (T-bills) can hedge well.
- **Rising and falling inflation / no single inflation rate** — inflation is non-uniform across goods, ages, and cohorts; CPI isn't everyone's inflation.
- **Treasury bills as the best inflation hedge** — short maturity lets nominal T-bill yields reset quickly with inflation; rolling T-bills track CPI much better than any long "real" asset.
- **Real bonds (linkers/TIPS)** — a single linker pays a guaranteed real coupon+principal; but the *asset class* aggregate has near-zero inflation correlation because real yields move dominantly with real-rate and liquidity shocks.
- **Real-bonds-for-retirement illusion** — a TIPS ladder funds a known real liability, but offers no equity premia and leaves retirees exposed to longevity and growth risk; do not hold 100% TIPS.
- **Real bonds are lousy inflation hedges** — the asset-class level of TIPS is buffered by real-yield volatility, not inflation; as a class, TIPS co-move with nominal Treasuries.
- **Real yields** — can be negative for extended periods; driven by liquidity, safe-haven demand, and duration risk, not just growth.
- **Break-even inflation compensation** — the spread between nominal and linker yields; *compensation* for inflation, not a forecast, and distorted by liquidity premia in each market.
- **Illiquidity in TIPS** — large breakeven spreads especially pre-2004; illiquidity is itself a return driver of TIPS.
- **TIPS and taxes** — phantom income from inflation indexation is taxed; hold TIPS in tax-deferred accounts.
- **Commodity factors** — commodity returns decompose into **spot, cash/collateral, and roll** returns; only the roll and spot parts respond to energy-demand inflation.
- **Energy as the only decent commodity hedge** — oil and gas spot returns correlate with inflation because demand-pull inflation is energy-intensive; industrial and precious metals are weak.
- **Gold is a poor inflation hedge** — long-run real gold return has been ≈ 0; gold co-moves with real interest rates and safe-haven demand, not CPI.
- **Direct vs indirect real estate** — direct (bricks & mortar) vs REITs; REITs are securitized, trade like equities, and carry equity beta.
- **Real estate and inflation** — some long-run hedging ability (rents and values adjust to inflation), but far from complete and obscured by smoothing and selection biases.

## Key Concepts
- **Smoothing bias** — appraisal-based real estate indices smooth returns, hiding true volatility.
- **Selection bias** — transaction indices select properties that *trade*, overstating average appreciation.
- **Idiosyncratic risk is large** — each property has enormous location, tenant, and capital expenditure idiosyncrasies.
- **Heterogeneity & leverage** — properties are heterogeneous; vehicles typically use high leverage, embedding more financing-rate and credit risk.
- **Active management** — direct real estate requires ongoing capex and active management; it is not a passive holding.
- **Break-even inflation ≠ forecast** — quoting TIPS-implied breakeven as "the market's inflation expectation" ignores liquidity and risk premia.
- **Negative real yields** — long periods (post-2008) have produced deeply negative real yields; do not assume real yields are positive or stable.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating linkers as an inflation hedge at the asset-class level** — a linker locks a *known* real liability, but a portfolio of TIPS does *not* track inflation.
- **Buying gold for inflation protection** — the empirical correlation of gold with CPI is near zero over long horizons; gold hedges real-rate and crisis risk, not inflation.
- **Owning 100% TIPS for retirement** — guarantees a real liability but forgoes the equity and factor premia needed for longevity and growth risk over 30+ years.
- **Holding TIPS in taxable accounts** — phantom inflation accruals are taxed as income even when unrealized; shelter TIPS first.
- **Confusing real-estate appraisals with mark-to-market** — smoothed, selection-biased indices understate true real-estate volatility and inflate Sharpe ratios.
- **Assuming "real" means inflation-hedged** — the label "real asset" is descriptive, not predictive; require measured inflation correlation before assigning any asset an inflation-hedge role.

## Key Takeaways
1. **Separate hedging from long-run return** — inflation hedging is a correlation property; do not justify an asset by its long-run mean when you need co-movement with inflation.
2. **T-bills are the best inflation hedge** — short nominal instruments reset with inflation; boring and effective.
3. **Linkers fund known real liabilities**, not inflation-hedging portfolios — an individual TIPS ladder, not a TIPS fund, matches mandatory real spending.
4. **Energy is the only commodity inflation hedge worth the name** — gold and most commodities fail the test.
5. **Real estate's inflation protection is partial** — direct real estate offers some long-run hedging but is smoothed/selection-biased, leveraged, and active-manager-intensive.
6. **Hold "real" assets for real-yield and crisis risk premia** — many real assets reward liquidity droughts and real-rate shocks more than inflation; size them honestly for what they actually hedge.
