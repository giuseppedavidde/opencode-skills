# Chapter 17: How to Spot Explosive Opportunities

## Core Idea
Three forms of analysis identify trading opportunities: **fundamental** (company value), **technical** (price/volume patterns), and **sentiment** (crowd psychology). Successful traders combine them; most specialize in one approach.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Fundamental Analysis**: Supply/demand factors, products, earnings, management, economic data. Determines if an asset is over/undervalued
- **Technical Analysis**: Price and volume statistics, charts, patterns. Moving averages, momentum indicators (MACD), Bollinger bands, support/resistance
- **Sentiment Analysis**: Crowd psychology. Contrarian approach — when most are bullish, sell; when most are bearish, buy
- **Key Sentiment Tools**: VIX (Volatility Index), put/call ratios (contrary indicator), advisor sentiment, short interest ratio, mutual fund cash levels

## Key Concepts
- **Contrarian Approach**: The crowd is usually wrong at extremes. Buy when panic selling (blow-off bottom/capitulation), sell when euphoria peaks (blow-off top)
- **Momentum Investing**: Short-term. Look for stocks with >20% daily price moves, strong volume, 60/90-day trend confirmation
- **Low-priced stocks** (<$20): Higher percentage returns, less capital at risk, more diversification with same capital
- **Volume signals**: >300,000 shares/day minimum for liquidity. Rising volume confirms trends
- **Everyday opportunity spotting**: Hot products in stores, children's trends, workplace observations
- **Data feeds**: Real-time (costly, for day traders), delayed (cheaper), end-of-day (cheapest)

## Key Takeaways
1. Technical + fundamental analysis are **complementary**, not mutually exclusive
2. Sentiment analysis via VIX and put/call ratios reveals **market extremes**
3. The contrarian approach: be fearful when others are greedy, greedy when others are fearful
4. Use multiple time frames to avoid mistaking short-term noise for long-term trends
