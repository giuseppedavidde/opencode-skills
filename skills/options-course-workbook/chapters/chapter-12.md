# Chapter 12: Choosing the Right Broker

## Core Idea
Broker selection is one of the most important trading decisions. Beyond commissions, consider **execution quality, online tools, research**, and how the broker handles options orders.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Broker Types**: Full-service (advice + execution, higher commissions), discount/self-directed (lower cost, do-it-yourself), online brokers (internet trading, often with live broker backup)
- **Key Selection Criteria**: Commission costs, quality of executions, research tools, online platform, customer service
- **Regulatory Landscape**: SEC regulates exchanges; NASD regulates brokers; OCC clears and guarantees options contracts

## Key Concepts
- **Payment for order flow**: Broker routes order to exchange for cash — can conflict with best execution
- **Duty of best execution**: Broker must shop for best available prices
- **Churning**: Excessive trading to generate commissions — illegal
- **Front running**: Trading ahead of customer orders — illegal
- **Options approval form**: Required to open account; broker assesses experience and net worth
- **Fill**: The price at which a trade is executed

## Key Takeaways
1. Lowest commissions don't guarantee lowest total cost — **execution quality matters**
2. Full-service vs self-directed depends on your need for advice vs desire for low costs
3. Use Optionetics Broker Review or Barron's rankings to compare brokers
4. Always complete the options approval form honestly — it protects both you and the broker
