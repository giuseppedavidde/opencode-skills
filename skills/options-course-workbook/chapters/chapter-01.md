# Chapter 1: Options Trading — A Primer

## Core Idea
Success comes from systematic trading, patience, and defining risk before every trade. Options require understanding **duration, direction, and magnitude** — the three pillars of every strategy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Why Trade Options?** Flexibility: profit in up, down, or sideways markets using a matrix of strategies
- **Trader vs Investor**: Traders are active, short-term, focused on risk management and volatility. Investors are passive, long-term, buy-and-hold
- **Capital Allocation Rule**: Only use funds that are readily available and can be risked without emotional distress. Never trade money needed for bills or life events

## Key Concepts
- **Paper trading**: Simulating trades without real money to learn strategy mechanics
- **Start small**: Minimize losses during the learning curve
- **Interview brokers**: Find one that suits your needs before committing
- **Specialize**: Master a few strategies rather than all of them
- **Risk management**: The most important factor for low-stress investing — define risk before entry

## Key Takeaways
1. A license to trade does not equal trading competence
2. Successful traders have patience and persistence — expectations of overnight riches are unrealistic
3. Flexibility is the greatest advantage of options — cultivate strategies for every market condition
4. Consistent risk management separates successful traders from the rest
