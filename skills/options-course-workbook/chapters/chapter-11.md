# Chapter 11: Increasing Your Profits with Adjustments

## Core Idea
Option positions are not static — they are **actively managed**. When the underlying moves, delta changes. Adjustments bring the position back to delta neutral, lock in profits, or salvage losing trades.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Adjustment Definition**: Modifying an open position by buying/selling additional options, stock, or futures to alter the risk/reward profile
- **Three Responses to Market Movement**: (1) Exit the trade, (2) Maintain as-is, (3) Adjust
- **Two Sides of a Delta Neutral Trade**: Directional bet + hedge. Adjust one side to rebalance
- **Types of Adjustments**:
  - **Time-based**: At regular intervals (weekly, biweekly)
  - **Event-based**: Triggered by earnings, FDA decisions, management changes

## Key Concepts
- **Stock delta is fixed** (+100 long, −100 short); **option delta is variable** (changes with price and time)
- ATM options are best for adjustments due to **high liquidity** and **thin bid-ask spreads**
- Positive delta = call/moves with underlying; Negative delta = put/moves opposite underlying
- Adjustments can also be used to take profits: sell part of the winning side, return to delta neutral

## Key Takeaways
1. Options have **variable deltas** — a position that starts delta neutral won't stay that way
2. Adjustment = buy or sell instruments to bring delta back to zero
3. ATM options offer the best liquidity for making adjustments
4. Event-based adjustments anticipate catalysts; time-based adjustments enforce discipline
