# Chapter 13: Processing Your Trade

## Core Idea
Understanding the **trade execution process** — from order placement to fill — helps traders choose appropriate order types and set realistic expectations for execution quality.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Primary U.S. Stock Exchanges**: NYSE, AMEX, Nasdaq
- **Options Exchanges**: CBOE (largest), ISE, BOX, PHLX, AMEX, Pacific Exchange
- **Primary Regulators**: SEC (securities), CFTC (commodities futures)
- **Market Participants**: Specialists (NYSE), market makers (options, OTC), floor brokers, retail brokers
- **Execution Systems**: DOT (electronic for small orders), open outcry (commodities)

## Key Concepts
- **Market order**: Immediate fill at current price. Good for liquid securities only
- **Limit order**: Execute only at specified price or better. Protects against bad fills. **Preferred for options**
- **Good 'Til Cancelled (GTC)**: Open until filled or cancelled
- **Day order**: Expires if not filled by end of trading day
- Floor traders profit from the **bid-ask spread** — retail traders buy at ask, sell at bid
- Higher volatility = **wider bid-ask spread**
- Essential order components: action (buy/sell), quantity, contract, month, strike, type, price

## Key Takeaways
1. Use **limit orders** for options — market orders risk bad fills in less-liquid contracts
2. The CBOE is the largest and oldest options exchange (est. 1973)
3. Market makers provide liquidity by taking the opposite side of trades
4. Clearly specify every component when placing an order to avoid errors
