# Chapter 2: The Big Picture

## Core Idea
Options are **derivatives** — their value derives from underlying instruments (stocks, indexes, futures, ETFs). Understanding the broader trading ecosystem is essential before specializing in options.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Trading Instrument Hierarchy**:
  1. **Stocks**: Unit of ownership in a company; value based on assets, earnings, demand
  2. **Futures**: Obligation to deliver/receive a commodity; used by hedgers and speculators
  3. **Options**: Right but not obligation to buy/sell at a specific price within a specific time
- **Supply & Demand**: More buyers = prices bid up; more sellers = prices offered down. The market is a great discounter of news — expectations drive price more than actual results
- **Stock Classifications**: Large-cap (>$5B), mid-cap ($500M–$5B), small-cap ($150M–$500M), micro-cap (<$150M)

## Key Concepts
- **Liquidity**: Trading volume enabling easy entry/exit. Minimum 300,000 shares/day for stocks; open interest of 100+ contracts for options
- **Volatility**: Measures expected fluctuation. Highly volatile stocks have higher option premiums. Two types: historical (past) and implied (expected future)
- **Leverage**: Control large positions with small capital outlay
- **Indexes**: Measure value changes in a group of stocks (DJIA, S&P 500). Many have optionable ETFs
- **Financial Commodities**: Debt instruments, currencies, indexes whose value depends on interest rates

## Key Takeaways
1. The two most important factors for opportunity are **liquidity** and **volatility**
2. Combine futures with options to create limited-risk trades
3. Options premium = price paid for rights; strike price = execution price
4. Most successful traders specialize — they don't need every instrument
