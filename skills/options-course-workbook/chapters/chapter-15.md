# Chapter 15: A Short Course in Economic Analyses

## Core Idea
**Interest rates drive asset prices**. Their movement affects stocks, bonds, and options through volatility. Understanding the bond market — which opens **one hour before stocks** — provides early insight into market direction.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Interest Rate → Bond Price Relationship**: Inverse. Rates up = bond prices down; rates down = bond prices up
- **Bonds and Stocks**: Generally move in the same direction. Falling rates benefit both; rising rates hurt both
- **Treasury Instruments**: T-bills (<90 days), T-notes (5–10 years), T-bonds (up to 30 years). The 10-year note is the key barometer for mortgage rates
- **Divergence**: When stocks rise despite rising rates (driven by strong earnings), a divergence between stocks and bonds occurs

## Key Concepts
- **Fed Funds Rate**: Short-term rate controlled by the Federal Reserve to stimulate or cool the economy
- **Yield**: Annual interest ÷ market price of the bond
- Stable or falling rates = favorable for stocks (upward bias)
- Rapidly increasing rates = avoid buying stocks
- Watch CNBC/Bloomberg at 8:30 AM ET for CBOT bond market open — it sets the tone for stocks
- Volatility spikes when economic reports cause sudden rate moves

## Key Takeaways
1. Bond prices and interest rates move in **opposite directions**
2. **Stable or falling rates** = bullish for stocks; **rising rates** = bearish
3. Watch the bond market before stocks open — it reacts to economic data first
4. Sudden interest rate moves create **volatility spikes** — opportunities for options traders
