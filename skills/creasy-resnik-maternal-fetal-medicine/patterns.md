# Clinical Patterns in Maternal-Fetal Medicine

## Suspected fetal growth restriction
**When you see it**: Estimated fetal weight below the 10th percentile, oligohydramnios, or maternal hypertension.
**How to confirm**: Serial biometry every 2 weeks, umbilical artery Doppler, biophysical profile, and ductus venosus Doppler if arterial flow is abnormal.
**Management**: Corticosteroids before 34 weeks. Deliver after 32 weeks with reversed umbilical end-diastolic flow and after 34 weeks with absent flow.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: A single estimate is inaccurate; Doppler changes precede acidemia.

## Non-reassuring fetal heart rate
**When you see it**: Category II with minimal or absent variability, recurrent late or variable decelerations, or tachycardia; Category III with absent variability plus decelerations, bradycardia, or sinusoidal pattern.
**How to confirm**: Pattern recognition; scalp or vibroacoustic stimulation clarifies Category II.
**Management**: Stop oxytocin, place left lateral, give IV fluids and oxygen, use tocolysis for tachysystole, and amnioinfuse for variable decelerations. Deliver if no improvement, especially with Category III.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Category II has a false-positive rate above 99% for cerebral palsy. Avoid cesarean for a responsive pattern.

## Hypertensive emergency in pregnancy
**When you see it**: Blood pressure at least 160/105-110 mm Hg for more than 15 minutes, with symptoms or abnormal platelets, liver enzymes, or creatinine.
**How to confirm**: Repeat blood pressure; check proteinuria, platelets, AST/ALT, LDH, and creatinine.
**Management**: Labetalol 10-20 mg IV, doubled every 10 minutes to 80 mg (maximum 300 mg), or hydralazine 5-10 mg IV every 20 minutes, or nifedipine 10 mg orally. Magnesium sulfate 4-6 g IV then 1-2 g/hr. Deliver at 34 weeks.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Target 135-145/95-100 mm Hg; rapid lowering reduces placental perfusion. Lost reflexes suggest magnesium toxicity.

## Preterm labor
**When you see it**: Contractions with cervical change before 37 weeks; cervical length below 30 mm or fetal fibronectin 50 ng/mL or more.
**How to confirm**: Transvaginal cervical length, fetal fibronectin, and group B streptococcal (GBS) status.
**Management**: Betamethasone 12 mg IM twice, 24 hours apart, at 24-34 weeks. Magnesium sulfate 4-6 g then 1-2 g/hr for 12-24 hours before 32 weeks. Nifedipine 10-20 mg every 3-6 hours, then 30-60 mg every 8-12 hours for 48 hours. GBS prophylaxis.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Overdiagnosis occurs in 40-70%. Antibiotics do not prolong pregnancy with intact membranes. Avoid nifedipine with magnesium.

## Preterm premature rupture of membranes
**When you see it**: Membrane rupture before 37 weeks and before labor.
**How to confirm**: Sterile speculum for pooling, ferning, and nitrazine; avoid digital examination.
**Management**: Deliver at 34-36 weeks. At 23-31 weeks give ampicillin 2 g IV every 6 hours plus erythromycin 250 mg IV every 6 hours for 48 hours, then oral amoxicillin and erythromycin for 5 days. Give corticosteroids and GBS prophylaxis.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Watch for infection, abruption, and cord prolapse. Low amniotic fluid predicts shorter latency but not outcome.

## Postpartum hemorrhage
**When you see it**: Heavy bleeding after delivery. Uterine atony is usual; also consider lacerations, retained placenta, accreta, and coagulopathy.
**How to confirm**: Assess tone, inspect the genital tract, use ultrasound for retained tissue, and check hemoglobin, platelets, fibrinogen, and coagulation.
**Management**: Oxytocin 20-30 units in 1000 mL at 100 mU/min or less. Add methylergonovine 0.2 mg IM, carboprost 250 µg IM, or misoprostol 800-1000 µg. Escalate to balloon, B-Lynch, arterial ligation, or hysterectomy.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Methylergonovine is contraindicated in hypertension, carboprost in asthma. Oxytocin bolus causes hypotension.

## Placenta previa with accreta risk
**When you see it**: Painless third-trimester bleeding, often with an abnormal lie; prior cesarean raises accreta risk.
**How to confirm**: Transvaginal ultrasound; no digital or speculum examination until previa is excluded. Use color Doppler and MRI if accreta is suspected.
**Management**: Admit, stabilize, give corticosteroids before 34 weeks, and perform cesarean at 36 weeks if the placenta lies within 2 cm of the cervix. For accreta, plan cesarean hysterectomy at 34-35 weeks with a multidisciplinary team, fundal incision, and placenta left in situ.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Transabdominal ultrasound overdiagnoses previa. Accreta hemorrhage can be massive.

## Maternal sepsis
**When you see it**: Infection with temperature above 38 or below 36°C, heart rate above 90, respiratory rate above 20, and white cells above 12,000 or below 4000/mm³. Chorioamnionitis adds uterine tenderness and foul fluid.
**How to confirm**: Cultures, lactate, and organ function. Amniotic fluid glucose below 5 mg/dL suggests a positive culture.
**Management**: Broad-spectrum IV antibiotics and source control, including delivery or uterine evacuation. Give fluids and vasopressors if needed.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Pregnancy causes tachycardia and leukocytosis. Do not delay antibiotics for cultures.

## Diabetic pregnancy with macrosomia
**When you see it**: Gestational or pregestational diabetes with estimated fetal weight above the 90th percentile or polyhydramnios.
**How to confirm**: A 75-g OGTT with thresholds 92, 180, and 153 mg/dL, or a 100-g OGTT with 95, 180, 155, and 140 mg/dL; HbA1c.
**Management**: Target preprandial below 95, 1-hour below 140, and 2-hour below 120 mg/dL. Use insulin or glyburide and prepare for shoulder dystocia.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Forty to fifty percent of shoulder dystocias occur below 4000 g. Cesarean for weight above 4500 g requires more than 1000 operations to prevent one brachial plexus injury.

## Thyroid disease in pregnancy
**When you see it**: TSH outside the trimester range; upper limits are 2.5, 3.0, and 3.0 mIU/L.
**How to confirm**: TSH and free T4; thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulins for Graves disease.
**Management**: Thyroxine for hypothyroidism. Propylthiouracil in the first trimester, then methimazole. For thyroid storm, give a 600-mg propylthiouracil load, then 200-300 mg.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: hCG lowers TSH early; propylthiouracil is hepatotoxic; methimazole causes embryopathy; immunoglobulins cause fetal or neonatal Graves.

## Anesthetic risk in the parturient
**When you see it**: Labor analgesia or cesarean delivery with obesity, preeclampsia, airway edema, or coagulopathy.
**How to confirm**: Airway examination, platelet count, and coagulation studies.
**Management**: Prefer neuraxial anesthesia; platelets of 75,000/mm³ or more are acceptable in stable preeclampsia. Maintain left uterine displacement. Use rapid-sequence induction with cricoid pressure; give phenylephrine for hypotension when heart rate is above 70.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Failed intubation occurs in 1 of 275 attempts; reduced sphincter tone increases aspiration; magnesium potentiates nondepolarizing relaxants.

## Fetal anemia from alloimmunization
**When you see it**: A red cell antibody known to cause hemolytic disease, MCA-PSV above 1.5 MoM, or hydrops.
**How to confirm**: Weekly MCA-PSV from 16-18 weeks in a quiescent fetus; cordocentesis for hematocrit when above 1.5 MoM.
**Management**: Intrauterine transfusion; deliver when mature.
**Trade-offs / caveats**: Fetal activity falsely lowers MCA-PSV.
