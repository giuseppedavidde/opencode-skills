# Chapter 25: Urogenital Imaging

## Core Idea
Fetal genitourinary anomalies are diagnosed by the effect of obstruction, dysplasia, or abnormal parenchyma on amniotic fluid volume and on the size, echogenicity, and cystic architecture of the kidneys and bladder. The chapter treats six lesions as separate diagnostic units: bladder exstrophy, echogenic dysplastic kidneys, multicystic dysplastic kidney (MCDK), posterior urethral valves (PUV), pyelectasis, and ureterocele. Prognosis is driven less by the anatomic label than by whether the process is unilateral or bilateral and by amniotic fluid volume. Because amniotic fluid in the second half of pregnancy depends on fetal urine, oligohydramnios is the central marker of functional renal compromise.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Structured anomaly template.** Each section uses Definition, Incidence and Pathogenesis, Differential Diagnosis, Key Diagnostic Features, Associated Anomalies, Antenatal/Obstetric/Neonatal Management, and Prognosis.

**Pyelectasis threshold systems.** Anteroposterior renal pelvis diameter is the measured variable. Three published schemes are given:
- Ouzounian: ≥5 mm at any gestational age.
- Corteville: >4 mm at 16–33 weeks; >7 mm at 33–40 weeks.
- Anderson: >4 mm at 16–23 weeks; >6 mm at 23–30 weeks; >8 mm at 30–40 weeks.

**PUV prognostic stratification.** Fetal mortality 30–40% overall, stratified by kidney appearance, urine concentration, and amniotic fluid: 10% mortality with nondysplastic kidneys and hypotonic urine; 70% with abnormal kidneys or any abnormal urine parameter; 95% with severe oligohydramnios or anhydramnios.

**Ureterocele classification (American Academy of Pediatrics).** Intravesical 25%; ectopic 75% (bladder neck, urethra, or pelvis).

## Key Concepts
- Bladder exstrophy: 1/25,000–1/40,000 live births, male-to-female 2:1; persistent absent bladder for >30 minutes with normal amniotic fluid; isoechoic mass below the cord insertion; displaced phallus; delivery at a tertiary center; bladder closure within 72 hours, genital reconstruction at 6–12 months, bladder neck reconstruction at 4–5 years.
- Exstrophy outcomes: urinary incontinence 25%, erectile dysfunction 25%, male infertility 90%, female infertility 75%, bladder or colon malignancy 17%.
- Echogenic kidneys: autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD) 1/1000, autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (ARPKD) 1/40,000, obstructive cystic dysplasia 1/8000. Kidneys are echogenic when brighter than liver; size may be normal before 20 weeks.
- ADPKD: enlarged echogenic kidneys, normal bladder, normal fluid, affected parent. ARPKD: small or absent bladder and oligohydramnios. Hyperechoic parenchyma predicts abnormal renal function in 80%.
- MCDK: 1/4000 live births, male-to-female 2:1; multiple noncommunicating cysts with loss of reniform outline; 25% have contralateral urologic defects; 35% have nonrenal anomalies; described in over 80 syndromes; bilateral disease causes anhydramnios and pulmonary hypoplasia. Postnatal ultrasound every 3–6 months.
- PUV: 1/8000–1/25,000, almost exclusively male; megacystis, bladder wall >3 mm, "keyhole" posterior urethra, bilateral hydronephrosis, oligohydramnios. Three serial vesicocenteses 48–72 hours apart are required. Favorable urine: sodium <100 mg/dL, chloride <90 mg/dL, osmolarity <200 mOsm/L, calcium <8 mg/dL, total protein <20 mg/dL, β2-microglobulin <4 mg/dL.
- Pyelectasis: 2–5% of pregnancies; caliectasis implies more severe hydronephrosis. About 90% of mid-trimester pyelectasis resolves; about one third of persistent moderate-to-severe cases (>7 mm or with caliectasis) need postnatal surgery.
- Ureterocele: 1/5000 live births, male-to-female 1:5; thin-walled cyst in the bladder; 80% accompany a duplicated collecting system with an obstructed upper pole; 10% are bilateral and mimic a septated bladder. Prenatal diagnosis lowers preoperative urinary infections by 85% and postoperative infections by 50%.

## Anti-patterns
- Diagnosing an absent bladder before 30 minutes of observation, or without excluding recent fetal urination, dysplastic kidneys, renal agenesis, and placental insufficiency.
- Treating isolated pyelectasis as an aneuploidy marker. As an isolated finding it does not increase aneuploidy risk; karyotype only for additional anomalies.
- Assuming normal amniotic fluid or normal first-trimester kidneys exclude renal disease. Both can be normal with polycystic disease.
- Reporting echogenic kidneys without measuring them against gestational reference tables and examining the parental kidneys.
- Missing the contralateral kidney and ureter in MCDK or ureterocele, and missing bladder outlet obstruction when bilateral hydronephrosis is present.
- Offering fetal therapy for PUV when amniotic fluid is normal or kidneys are already bilaterally dysplastic; such fetuses are unlikely to benefit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Measure the renal pelvis in the anteroposterior plane; use gestational-age-specific cutoffs, and escalate if caliectasis is present.
2. In bilateral hydronephrosis or a thick-walled bladder, evaluate the urethra and perform serial vesicocenteses before considering shunting.
3. Interpret echogenic kidneys with the bladder and fluid: enlarged kidneys with normal fluid and a parent with cysts suggests ADPKD; small kidneys with oligohydramnios suggests ARPKD or dysplasia.
4. Bilateral MCDK or anhydramnios carries a poor prognosis from pulmonary hypoplasia; counsel accordingly.
5. Deliver at a tertiary center when urinary obstruction is severe, and arrange postnatal ultrasound, voiding cystourethrogram, and renography.
