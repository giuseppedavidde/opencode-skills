# Chapter 15: Fetal Lung Development and Surfactant

## Core Idea
The lung develops in five overlapping stages, and only the last two — saccular and alveolar — support gas exchange at the gestational ages where preterm delivery occurs. Surfactant, a phospholipid-rich film dominated by saturated phosphatidylcholine, lowers surface tension so that inflation becomes uniform and the lung retains volume on deflation. Fetal lung fluid, maintained by active chloride secretion and a small tracheal pressure gradient, is the mechanical stimulus for lung growth; loss of that fluid or of chest space produces pulmonary hypoplasia. Antenatal glucocorticoids and chorioamnionitis both accelerate lung maturation, and this induced maturation explains why many very preterm infants have little or no respiratory distress syndrome (RDS).

## Frameworks Introduced
**Five-stage morphologic classification (human).**
- **Embryonic, 3–7 weeks:** lung bud from foregut at ~22 days, lung bud by 26 days, bifurcation into mainstem bronchi on day 28. Regulators: TTF-1, FGF-10, Gli2/Gli3, retinoic acid, HOX genes. Defects: tracheoesophageal fistula, pulmonary agenesis, lobation defects.
- **Pseudoglandular, 5–17 weeks:** 15–20 generations of airways, cartilage, glands, smooth muscle, early vasculature, diaphragm closure. Defects: sequestration, cystic adenomatoid malformation, lymphangiectasia, diaphragmatic hernia.
- **Canalicular, 16–26 weeks:** acinus appears, double capillary network fuses, air–blood barrier forms by ~21 weeks, type I and II cells differentiate. Failure of capillary fusion causes alveolar-capillary dysplasia.
- **Saccular, 24–36 weeks:** saccules divide through ~3 generations into respiratory bronchioles and ~3 more into alveolar ducts; microvascular expansion increases gas-exchange area.
- **Alveolar, 32 weeks through childhood:** secondary septation; ~100 million alveoli at term, ~500 million in the adult; maximal formation between ~36 weeks and several months postnatally.

**Pulmonary hypoplasia differential (Box 15-1).** Thoracic compression (renal agenesis/Potter, urinary outflow obstruction, oligohydramnios before 28 weeks, extra-amniotic development); decreased intrathoracic space (diaphragmatic hernia, effusions, abdominal distention, thoracic dystrophy); decreased fetal breathing (CNS damage, Werdnig-Hoffmann/spinal muscular atrophy, other neuropathies); other (primary hypoplasia, trisomy 21, multiple anomalies, erythroblastosis).

## Key Concepts
- Fetal lung fluid volume near term is ~**25 mL/kg**, close to the postnatal functional residual capacity. Active chloride transport drives production of **4–5 mL/kg/hr** in later gestation; a 4-kg sheep fetus produces ~**400 mL/day**. Tracheal pressure exceeds amniotic pressure by ~**2 mmHg**, and that outflow resistance maintains lung volume.
- Surfactant is ~80% phospholipid and ~8% protein by mass. About **50%** of phosphatidylcholine is saturated, principally dipalmitoylphosphatidylcholine, the main surface-active species. Phosphatidylglycerol is **4–15%** of phospholipid. Alveolar lining fluid volume in the normal air-breathing lung is only ~**0.3 mL/kg**.
- SP-A and SP-D are collectins of innate host defense. SP-B is a 79-amino-acid homodimer of ~18 kDa; SP-B deficiency causes lethal neonatal RDS and functionally also causes SP-C deficiency. SP-D forms a 560-kDa multimer and rises with acute lung injury; no human SP-D deficiency state is described.
- Surfactant kinetics: peak incorporation of ¹³C from glucose into airway phosphatidylcholine occurs at **77 ± 8 hours** (about 70 hours in RDS infants); half-life after peak is ~**100 hours**. Exogenous surfactant half-life in RDS infants is ~**34–35 hours**. Radiolabeled phospholipid clearance in term lambs has a half-life of ~**6 days**.
- Recycling: about **90%** of phospholipid is endocytosed and re-secreted intact in the term and preterm lung; recycling is only ~25% efficient in the adult lung, where macrophages catabolize ~50% of surfactant. Few macrophages are present in the preterm lung at birth.
- Pressure–volume behavior: surfactant-deficient preterm rabbit lungs do not inflate until pressure exceeds **25 cm H₂O**; surfactant treatment lowers opening pressure to ~**15 cm H₂O**. Surfactant increases maximal volume at 35 cm H₂O by **2.5-fold** and retains ~**36%** of lung volume on deflation to 5 cm H₂O.
- RDS frequency by gestation: ~**10%** at 35 weeks when the amniotic L/S ratio exceeds 2; ~**50%** at 30 weeks; close to **100%** below 28 weeks. In 9,575 infants below 29 weeks (2003–2007), **93%** had RDS. In later CPAP-era trials of similar infants, only **30–60%** required mechanical ventilation or surfactant.
- Lung maturation, defined by absence of RDS and by the L/S ratio, normally occurs after about **35 weeks**. Female fetuses mature about **1 week** before males.
- Antenatal corticosteroids change fetal sheep lung structure within **24 hours**, but surfactant content does not increase for several days. Net clinical effect: reduced RDS and infant death, no reduction in BPD, and decreased alveolar septation.
- The maturational response to intra-amniotic inflammation is more consistent and greater than the response to maternal corticosteroids; chorioamnionitis and antenatal steroids interact to improve lung function.
- Pulmonary hypoplasia is found by low lung weight in **15–20%** of unselected infant autopsies. With gentle ventilation, selective ECMO, and delayed hernia repair, survival for diaphragmatic hernia without other anomalies reaches **80%**.
- Surfactant therapy reduces RDS mortality, overall infant mortality, pneumothorax, and oxygen and ventilatory requirements, but does not consistently reduce BPD, patent ductus arteriosus, or intraventricular hemorrhage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming a mature L/S ratio excludes RDS.** The RDS curve and the L/S curve diverge; ~10% of infants still develop RDS when the L/S ratio exceeds 2, and late preterm infants can have morbidity despite maturity testing.
- **Attributing all preterm respiratory disease to surfactant deficiency.** Transient tachypnea of the newborn, pulmonary hypoplasia, alveolar-capillary dysplasia, and inflammatory lung injury produce similar early pictures.
- **Withholding antenatal corticosteroids because surfactant is available.** The two act synergistically; steroid exposure makes endogenous surfactant more resistant to protein and inflammatory inhibition and reduces the exogenous dose needed.
- **Ventilating a hypoplastic lung aggressively.** Attempting normal gas exchange with excessive ventilation causes severe lung injury; gentle ventilation with selective ECMO is the approach associated with 80% survival in diaphragmatic hernia.
- **Reading growth restriction as protection.** Fetal growth restriction decreases alveolar number and vessel development and predicts more RDS and BPD; very preterm growth-restricted fetuses do not gain the mortality benefit from antenatal corticosteroids.
- **Expecting surfactant to prevent BPD.** Survival of the highest-risk infants offsets any BPD reduction; surfactant does not reduce patent ductus arteriosus or intraventricular hemorrhage.
- **Ignoring fluid mechanics at birth.** The near-term lung holds ~25 mL/kg of fluid with essentially no protein; delivery, intubation, and ventilation can constitute a crystalloid challenge of ~25 mL/kg.

## Key Takeaways
- Date lung maturity by stage: gas exchange is structurally possible only after the canalicular stage (~21 weeks), and surfactant-driven stability is a saccular and alveolar achievement.
- Expect ~10% RDS at 35 weeks, ~50% at 30 weeks, and near 100% below 28 weeks; apply these figures when counseling before elective delivery.
- Use antenatal corticosteroids for structural maturation within 24 hours and surfactant increase over several days; do not expect a BPD reduction.
- Protect fetal lung volume: oligohydramnios before 28 weeks and any space-occupying thoracic lesion are mechanisms of hypoplasia, not incidental findings.
- Target surfactant physiology: opening pressure falls from >25 to ~15 cm H₂O, and deflation stability (~36% volume retained at 5 cm H₂O) is what restores functional residual capacity.
- Diagnose hypoplasia clinically and by imaging; rigorous autopsy criteria (airway number, radial alveolar count, lung DNA) are not routine.
- Treat the late preterm and early term period as a risk window; respiratory morbidity falls with each week gained toward 39 weeks.
