# Chapter 37: Nonimmune Hydrops

## Core Idea
Hydrops fetalis is generalized skin edema with serous effusions, and nonimmune causes now dominate the diagnosis. The chapter argues that NIH is a heterogeneous final common pathway rather than a disease, so prognosis and treatment follow from identifying the underlying cause. Etiologic diagnosis is therefore the central clinical act, because mortality ranges from 52% to 98% and specific causes carry specific fetal therapies.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Diagnostic definition of NIH.** Two definitions coexist. Mahony's definition is generalized skin edema with or without a serous effusion. The more widely used definition requires edema plus at least one effusion, or effusions in at least two spaces, from the set: ascites, pleural effusion, pericardial effusion, skin edema.

**Severity scoring systems.** Hutchison scored hydrops by the total number of involved serous spaces; the score did not predict outcome because overall mortality approached 100%. Saltzman quantified each effusion separately and could discriminate anemic from nonanemic causes.

**Etiologic classification (Box 37-1).** Causes are grouped as cardiovascular (structural malformation, tachyarrhythmia, bradyarrhythmia, high-output failure), chromosomal, hematologic, thoracic, infection, malformation sequence, twinning, metabolic, and neoplastic.

**Diagnostic algorithm.** Ultrasonography first to define anatomy, effusions, MCA peak systolic velocity, ductus venosus Doppler, and echocardiogram. Maternal blood type and indirect Coombs, hemoglobinopathy screen, Kleihauer-Betke, syphilis, parvovirus and TORCH titers, then fetal karyotype by FISH or amniotic fluid PCR, and frozen amniotic fluid for lysosomal storage disease.

## Key Concepts
- NIH accounts for 76% to 87% of hydropic fetuses in series; by 2002 one center reported 80% nonimmune.
- Incidence at delivery is 1 in 1500 to 1 in 3800; ultrasound referral centers report 1 in 150 to 1 in 766.
- A cause is identified before delivery in 51% to 85% of cases and in up to 95% after delivery with autopsy and karyotype.
- Hydramnios accompanies 40% to 75% of NIH cases; oligohydramnios is an ominous, late finding.
- Mirror syndrome (pseudotoxemia): maternal edema, pulmonary edema, hypertension, proteinuria, with elevated sFlt-1 and sVEGFR-1; it resolves when fetal hydrops resolves or after delivery.
- Cardiovascular disease is the largest single category. In 104 fetuses with pericardial effusion, 36% had other hydrops and 38% had structural heart disease.
- In 73 fetuses with congenital heart disease, 15% had trisomy and 41% died before 28 days.
- Alpha-thalassemia with homozygous alpha-thalassemia-1 produces hemoglobin Bart, which delivers almost no oxygen and is a leading cause in Asia and the eastern Mediterranean.
- MCA peak systolic velocity predicts fetal anemia from immune and nonimmune causes, including parvovirus.
- Pleuroamniotic shunting for isolated hydrothorax yields survival in 40% to 70%; aneuploidy risk with isolated pleural effusion is high.
- TTTS hydrops may affect the recipient (volume overload) or the donor (anemia); laser ablation is warranted when hydrops is present.
- An end-diastolic biventricular dimension below the 95th percentile predicted survival in one series of 19 mixed-etiology cases.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating NIH as a single diagnosis and quoting a single prognosis instead of pursuing etiology.
- Omitting Kleihauer-Betke, parvovirus serology, or karyotype because hydrops is "idiopathic" on initial scan.
- Relying on optical density 450 in amniotic fluid; values are often elevated but the result is not clinically useful.
- Assuming a normal MCA Doppler excludes anemia, or that hydrops with structural heart disease merits aggressive fetal therapy.
- Continuing tocolysis when maternal preeclampsia or hemorrhage has developed; maternal status governs management regardless of fetal outcome.

## Key Takeaways
1. Confirm NIH by edema plus two effusions or edema plus one effusion, then search systematically for a cause.
2. Measure MCA peak systolic velocity and perform fetal echocardiography in every case.
3. Before viability the prognosis is grave; counsel termination and avoid prolonged tocolysis for a lethal diagnosis.
4. For idiopathic third-trimester NIH, prolong pregnancy to 32 to 34 weeks unless fetal or maternal decompensation appears.
5. Treat symptomatic hydramnios with therapeutic amniocentesis or indomethacin; shunt isolated hydrothorax when appropriate.
6. Recognize mirror syndrome as a maternal emergency that resolves only with resolution of fetal hydrops or delivery.
