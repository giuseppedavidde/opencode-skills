# Chapter 52: Cardiac Diseases

## Core Idea

Pregnancy imposes a large volume and output load on the heart, and normal pregnancy reproduces many signs of cardiac disease: dyspnea, edema, a third heart sound, systolic murmurs in more than 95% of women, and chamber enlargement with trivial valvular regurgitation. Diagnosis therefore depends on discriminating specific warning findings rather than on the presence of symptoms alone. Risk is determined by the lesion and its severity, not by the diagnosis in isolation. A few lesions carry mortality high enough to contraindicate pregnancy. Management rests on pre-conception counseling, co-management by maternal-fetal medicine and cardiology, lesion-specific hemodynamic goals, and meticulous anticoagulation for mechanical valves.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **ACC/AHA high-risk valvular lesion list (Box 52-1).** Severe aortic stenosis with or without symptoms; aortic regurgitation with NYHA class III to IV symptoms; mitral stenosis with NYHA class II to IV; mitral regurgitation with NYHA class III to IV; aortic or mitral disease with pulmonary pressure greater than 75% of systemic pressure; aortic or mitral disease with ejection fraction below 40%; mechanical prosthetic valve requiring anticoagulation; Marfan syndrome with or without aortic regurgitation.
- **Low-risk valvular lesion list (Box 52-2).** Asymptomatic aortic stenosis with mean gradient below 25 mm Hg, valve area above 1.5 cm², and ejection fraction above 50%; NYHA class I to II aortic or mitral regurgitation with normal systolic function; mitral valve prolapse without or with mild to moderate regurgitation; mild mitral stenosis with valve area above 1.5 cm² and gradient below 5 mm Hg without severe pulmonary hypertension; mild to moderate pulmonary stenosis.
- **Absolute contraindications to pregnancy.** Dilated cardiomyopathy or left ventricular dysfunction of any cause with ejection fraction below 40%; severe pulmonary hypertension of any cause; Marfan syndrome with aortic root diameter greater than 4 cm; Eisenmenger syndrome. Termination is recommended if pregnancy occurs; suction curettage before 13 weeks carries lower risk than later termination.
- **NYHA functional classification** used to grade symptoms and select the risk category.
- **Mechanical prosthetic valve anticoagulation (ACCP/ACC-AHA).** Dose-adjusted low-molecular-weight heparin twice daily to keep anti-Xa at 0.7 to 1.2 U/mL; dose-adjusted unfractionated heparin to keep aPTT at least twice control; warfarin with INR target 3.0 (range 2.5 to 3.5); stop warfarin and start continuous intravenous unfractionated heparin 2 to 3 weeks before delivery; resume heparin 4 to 6 hours after delivery; low-dose aspirin 75 to 100 mg in the second and third trimesters.
- **Endocarditis prophylaxis.** Indicated for dental procedures in patients with prosthetic valves, not for uncomplicated delivery.
- **Peripartum cardiomyopathy criteria.** Dilated cardiomyopathy in the last month of pregnancy or the first 5 months postpartum, no pre-existing heart disease, ejection fraction below 45%, and no other identifiable cause.

## Key Concepts

- Blood volume rises by almost 50% by 30 weeks; heart rate rises 10 to 20 beats/min; cardiac output is 30% to 45% above nonpregnant values by 20 weeks and peaks at 20 to 26 weeks.
- Stroke volume dominates early; heart rate dominates later. Supine positioning in late pregnancy compresses the inferior vena cava and lowers output, favoring left lateral positioning in labor.
- Physiologic anemia: plasma volume rises more than red cell mass, giving hematocrit about 33% and hemoglobin about 11 g/dL.
- Warning symptoms: severe dyspnea, syncope with exertion, hemoptysis, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, exertional chest pain. Warning signs: fourth heart sound, cyanosis, clubbing, diastolic murmur, sustained arrhythmia, loud harsh systolic murmur.
- Peripartum cardiomyopathy incidence is 1 per 3000 to 4000 live births; reported mortality is 15% to 60%. About 20% have a fulminant course, 30% to 50% recover partially, and the remainder recover well. Failure of ejection fraction to improve within 2 to 3 months predicts long-term dysfunction.
- Recurrent peripartum cardiomyopathy: heart failure recurs in 20% of women whose ejection fraction normalized, and in 40% of those with persistent dysfunction, with 19% maternal mortality in the latter group.
- Eisenmenger syndrome: in one series mean systolic pulmonary arterial pressure was 113 mm Hg, with two maternal deaths among 13 pregnancies. Idiopathic pulmonary hypertension carries mortality approaching 50%.
- Aortic stenosis with a mechanical prosthesis carries reported maternal mortality up to 17%; moderate stenosis should be corrected before pregnancy.
- Cardiac surgery with cardiopulmonary bypass carries 10% to 15% fetal mortality, so surgery should avoid bypass when possible.
- Warfarin embryopathy (nasal hypoplasia, optic atrophy, digital abnormalities, intellectual impairment) is reported in 4% to 67% of first-trimester exposures.
- Prosthetic valve endocarditis carries mortality up to 40%.

## Anti-patterns

- Interpreting a systolic murmur, third heart sound, or dependent edema as proof of heart disease; these are normal in pregnancy.
- Dismissing exertional syncope, hemoptysis, or paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea as normal pregnancy symptoms.
- Using echocardiographic chamber enlargement or trivial four-valve regurgitation alone to diagnose cardiomyopathy.
- Continuing an ACE inhibitor in pregnancy; hydralazine is the vasodilator of choice in peripartum cardiomyopathy.
- Using nifedipine in hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, because vasodilation worsens outflow obstruction.
- Allowing labor and delivery in a patient taking warfarin; cesarean delivery is recommended to avoid fetal intracranial hemorrhage.
- Assuming a recovered peripartum cardiomyopathy makes a subsequent pregnancy low risk.

## Key Takeaways

1. Evaluate any pregnant woman with suspected heart disease at least once by a cardiologist familiar with pregnancy, and co-manage high-risk lesions jointly with maternal-fetal medicine.
2. Screen for the absolute contraindications (ejection fraction below 40%, severe pulmonary hypertension, Marfan with aortic root above 4 cm, Eisenmenger) and counsel before conception.
3. Prefer vaginal delivery with a low threshold for forceps or vacuum assistance; reserve cesarean delivery for Marfan syndrome, aortic aneurysm, and obstetric indications.
4. Manage mechanical valves with a planned anticoagulation regimen, targeting anti-Xa 0.7 to 1.2 U/mL, aPTT at least twice control, or INR 2.5 to 3.5, and convert to heparin by about 35 weeks.
5. Give antibiotic prophylaxis for dental procedures in prosthetic valve patients, but not for uncomplicated delivery.
6. Treat peripartum cardiomyopathy with standard heart failure therapy substituting hydralazine for ACE inhibitors, and counsel about a 20% to 40% recurrence risk.
7. In hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, avoid hypovolemia, maintain venous return, avoid inotropes, and manage the second stage in the left lateral position.
