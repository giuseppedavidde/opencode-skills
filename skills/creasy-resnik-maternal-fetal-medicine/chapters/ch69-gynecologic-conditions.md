# Chapter 69: Benign Gynecologic Conditions in Pregnancy

## Core Idea
Benign gynecologic lesions are usually discovered incidentally at the first-trimester ultrasound, and the central question is whether they will distort the uterine cavity, torse, or obstruct labor. Pregnancy alters myoma size and adnexal mass behavior in ways that favor expectancy: about 40% of myomas stay the same size and 69–89% of ovarian cysts resolve spontaneously. The chapter argues for restraint — intervention is reserved for symptoms, suspicious morphology, torsion, or obstetric indication — because most adverse associations are modest and confounded.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Sonographic classification of adnexal masses.** Simple cyst (anechoic, no septa or vegetations); endometriosis-like or corpus luteum-like (hypoechoic, homogeneous or trabecular, no papillae); dermoid-like (mixed hyperechoic and hypoechoic content with shadows); complex benign (septa, thick content, no papillae); borderline (smooth capsule, intracystic papillae, no gross solid parts); suspect (solid parts, irregular capsule, ascites, irregular vascularization).

**Zanetta triage for adnexal masses.** Masses with suspect features go to surgery at diagnosis; all other categories may be followed expectantly.

**Congenital uterine anomaly classification (types I–VI).** I, hypoplasia/agenesis; II, unicornuate; III, didelphys; IV, bicornuate; V, septate; VI, arcuate.

**Myoma-related obstetric risk framework.** Risk is driven by submucous position, cavity distortion, size (especially >5 cm), number (>3), and lower uterine segment location rather than by size alone for labor outcomes.

## Key Concepts
- Leiomyoma prevalence in pregnancy is 1.6–4%. Approximately 40% (range 25–60%) remain stable in size; myomas larger than 5 cm are more likely to grow, and fewer than 10% of myomas smaller than 5 cm grow. Growth is most common in the first trimester.
- Rapid myoma growth does not suggest malignancy. Degenerative changes in pregnancy mimic those after embolization.
- Intramural myomas and miscarriage: 20% versus 13% in controls (odds ratio 1.82, 95% CI 1.43–2.30).
- Preterm delivery with myomas: odds ratio 1.5 (95% CI 1.3–1.7) in systematic review.
- Fetal death with growth restriction and myomas: odds ratio 2.1 (95% CI 1.2–3.6); with more than three myomas, 2.2 (1.1–4.6); with myomas larger than 5 cm, 2.6 (1.5–4.5).
- Placenta previa: 3.5% with myomas versus 1.8% without; for myomas larger than 5 cm, odds ratios 2.1 (1.4–3.1) and 2.4. Lower uterine segment myomas confer additional abruption risk.
- Fetal malpresentation with myomas: odds ratio 1.6–4.0.
- Cesarean delivery with myomas: odds ratio 3.7 (95% CI 3.5–3.9). Dystocia and fetal distress cesarean rates are not increased in labor-eligible women, and women with myomas who start labor have similar vaginal delivery success to the general population.
- Postpartum hemorrhage with myomas: cumulative odds ratio 1.8 (95% CI 1.4–2.2); size does not appear to matter.
- Placental abruption evidence is conflicting: some series show 11% versus 0.7% in women admitted antepartum, with odds ratios 2.6–3.8; prospective ultrasound cohorts show no association.
- Adnexal masses: 8.8% in the first 5 weeks, 6.1% before 14 weeks. Spontaneous resolution occurs in 89% (including 82% of cysts >6 cm), 72%, and 69% (masses >5 cm).
- Benign ovarian pathology in pregnancy is dominated by corpus luteum cysts, mature teratomas, and cystadenomas.
- Malignancy is found in 1–3% of surgically removed adnexal masses. In 9,375 women with a discharge diagnosis of ovarian mass, malignancy was 2.1% (0.9% ovarian cancer, 1.2% low malignant potential). In 470 women operated during pregnancy, 4.3% were malignant.
- Mean diameter is 7.6 cm for nonmalignant tumors and 11.5 cm for malignant neoplasms; all masses smaller than 6 cm were benign. Size alone cannot predict malignancy.
- Complications of adnexal masses occur in 4% (7 of 166): malignancy, torsion, rupture, hemorrhage, and dystocia.
- Congenital uterine anomalies occur in 4.3% of fertile women, 3.4% of infertile women, and complicate 1 in 594 pregnancies. Mean prevalence in recurrent pregnancy loss is 12.6% (range 1.8–37.6%).
- Distribution of uterine anomalies: septate 34.9%, bicornuate 26.0%, arcuate 18.3%, unicornuate 9.6%, didelphys 8.2%, agenesis 2.9%.
- Septate uterus carries the poorest reproductive outcome, mainly from spontaneous abortion. Septate, bicornuate, and didelphys uteri have preterm delivery rates two to three times higher than expected.
- Urinary tract anomalies occur in about 60% of congenital uterine anomalies; imaging of the urinary tract is indicated.
- Three-dimensional ultrasound correctly identified 30 of 30 uterine anomalies versus 10 of 30 by hysterosalpingography.

## Anti-patterns
- Recommending myomectomy solely to improve fertility without counseling that randomized evidence is lacking.
- Interpreting rapid myoma growth in pregnancy as possible malignancy.
- Assuming a large myoma predicts failed vaginal delivery; labor outcomes in labor-eligible women are similar to the general population.
- Operating on every adnexal mass; most resolve, including many larger than 5 cm.
- Using cyst size alone to predict malignancy; masses below 6 cm were uniformly benign, but larger size is nonspecific.
- Diagnosing a uterine anomaly by MRI alone; unicornuate uterus with a rudimentary horn is frequently misread as bicornuate or didelphys.
- Overlooking urinary tract anomalies when a Müllerian anomaly is found.
- Attributing abruption to myomas as established fact; the evidence is conflicting.

## Key Takeaways
1. Follow most myomas expectantly: about 40% do not change, and rapid growth is not a malignancy signal.
2. Counsel that myomas raise the risk of cesarean delivery (odds ratio 3.7), postpartum hemorrhage (1.8), malpresentation (1.6–4.0), and placenta previa (approximately 2-fold), but not of failed vaginal delivery once labor begins.
3. Follow simple, endometriosis-like, dermoid-like, and complex benign cysts expectantly; operate for suspect morphology, torsion, rupture, or symptoms.
4. Remember that all adnexal masses smaller than 6 cm in pregnancy were benign in the cited series, and that malignancy accounts for only 1–3% of surgically removed masses.
5. Classify uterine anomalies with three-dimensional ultrasound or saline sonohysterography, and image the urinary tract because 60% have associated renal anomalies.
6. Anticipate preterm delivery and miscarriage with a septate uterus, and counsel that septate, bicornuate, and didelphys uteri carry two to three times the expected preterm birth rate.
