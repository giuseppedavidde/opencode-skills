# Chapter 2: Normal Early Development

## Core Idea

Early development is a sequence of timed interactions between trophoblast and decidua. Implantation converts a free blastocyst into an interstitial structure surrounded by decidua. Trophoblast then separates into a villous lineage that exchanges with maternal blood and an extravillous lineage that invades and remodels spiral arterioles. The chapter's central argument is that placental architecture is not passive scaffolding; it is the product of controlled invasion, and the diseases of placentation (accreta, molar pregnancy, vasa previa) are failures of that control.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Hertig staging of the pre-implantation embryo.** The 58-cell morula is described as the point of divergence into embryonic and trophoblastic lineages.

**Trophoblast lineage model.** Villous cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast form the exchange surface. The extravillous ("X") cell is a separate lineage that migrates into decidua and myometrium, fuses into placental giant cells, and invades spiral arterioles.

**Decidual anatomy.** Decidua basalis lies between blastocyst and myometrium. Decidua capsularis becomes part of the chorion laeve. Decidua vera lines the rest of the cavity. The presence of decidua basalis is what prevents accreta.

**Villous maturation sequence.** Primary villi are trophoblastic columns. Secondary villi acquire a mesenchymal core. Tertiary villi acquire fetal capillaries.

## Key Concepts

- The fertilised ovum reaches the uterine cavity about day 4 after fertilisation.
- A 6-day specimen contained 5 embryonic cells and 53 trophoblastic cells.
- Implantation occurs about day 6 or 7, within a hormonally defined window of receptivity.
- The implanting blastocyst measures about 0.1 mm and can be seen only under a dissecting microscope.
- By 14 days the conceptus is a polypoid protrusion with a differentiated embryo, amnion, and yolk sac.
- A true fetal circulation is active by 21 days. Initial fetal erythrocytes come from the yolk sac; fetal hematopoietic islands take over after the second month.
- By day 30 a truly villous ovum is formed.
- Syncytiotrophoblast is the functionally dominant layer; it produces cell-free DNA that enters the maternal circulation.
- Cytotrophoblastic mitoses become rare after 36 weeks in normal placentas.
- The amnion is a single layer of ectodermal epithelium without blood vessels. It does not fully line the chorionic sac until about 12 weeks and never fuses completely with the chorion.
- A calcified yolk sac remnant may persist at the placental periphery as a 3-mm yellow disk.
- Hofbauer cells are fetal macrophages that derive from circulating fetal blood.
- Extravillous trophoblast occludes spiral arterioles early, so only a filtrate of maternal blood enters the intervillous space in the first trimester. Low-resistance flow is measurable from day 20.
- Placenta accreta follows absence of decidua basalis. Prior curettage and prior cesarean section are the main antecedents; increta and percreta reflect deeper invasion.
- Velamentous cord insertion is explained by either eccentric implantation or by atrophy of the placental pole.
- Surface placental arteries cross over veins. There are no anastomoses between superficial vessels; each terminal arterial branch supplies one cotyledon.
- Assisted reproduction increases monochorionic twinning even after single embryo transfer.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the decidua as inert packing. It actively controls trophoblast invasion and immune cell traffic.
- Confusing amnion nodosum with squamous metaplasia of the amnion. Both are squamous plaques but only metaplasia is normal near the cord insertion.
- Assuming a hydatidiform mole never contained an embryo. If villous stroma and vessels are embryonal in origin, a mole must have had an embryo at some stage.
- Reading a velamentous cord as an isolated finding. It carries vasa previa and exsanguination risk.
- Expecting the amnion to strip cleanly in all placentas. In meconium-stained placentas the plane becomes obvious.

## Key Takeaways

1. Time implantation at day 6 to 7 and the fetal circulation at day 21 when dating early pregnancy events.
2. Look for absent decidua basalis at cesarean or curettage as the mechanism of accreta.
3. Treat a monochorionic twin pregnancy after ART as a known excess risk, not a random event.
4. Inspect the cord insertion site for velamentous insertion in every twin placenta.
5. Use villous morphology, not gestational sac size, to crudely estimate gestational age histologically.
6. Remember that the syncytiotrophoblast, not the cytotrophoblast, is the source of cell-free DNA.
