# Chapter 40: Preterm Labor and Birth

## Core Idea
Preterm birth is defined by time rather than by a distinct pathology, and the diagnosis of preterm labor is imprecise because symptoms are common in normal pregnancy. Management therefore has two goals: arrest labor long enough to transfer the mother to an appropriate hospital and to give antenatal corticosteroids, and reduce neonatal morbidity with antibiotics and magnesium sulfate. Prevention is a separate strategy built on cervical length screening and progestational agents.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Gestational age terminology.** Preterm is before 37 0/7 weeks; late preterm is 34 0/7 to 36 6/7; term is 37 0/7 to 41 6/7, subdivided into early term (37 0/7 to 38 6/7) and full term (39 0/7 to 41 6/7); post-term is 42 weeks or more. Low birth weight is under 2500 g, very low birth weight under 1500 g, extremely low birth weight under 1000 g.

**Clinical diagnosis of preterm labor.** Persistent uterine contractions of six or more per hour plus cervical dilation of 3 cm or more, or effacement of 80% or more, or membrane rupture or bleeding.

**Preterm labor evaluation protocol.** Transvaginal cervical length is measured in symptomatic women. A length under 20 mm leads to treatment at physician discretion. A length of 20 to 29 mm triggers fetal fibronectin testing: negative (<50 ng/mL) permits observation for 24 hours if contractions persist; positive (≥50 ng/mL) leads to management. A length over 30 mm argues against preterm labor.

**Progesterone prevention algorithm.** Singleton with prior spontaneous preterm birth: 17-OHPC 250 mg intramuscularly weekly from 15 0/7 to 36 6/7 weeks with transvaginal cervical length every 14 days from 16 to 24 weeks, or every 7 days if the cervix is under 25 mm. Universal cervical length screening at 18 to 24 weeks for all women. If length is 21 to 25 mm, repeat in 7 to 10 days and start vaginal progesterone 200 mg daily (suppositories or capsules) or 90 mg gel daily until 36 6/7 weeks. If length is under 25 mm, consider cerclage, especially below 15 mm or with multiple prior second-trimester losses.

## Key Concepts
- The 50th, 10th, and 3rd percentile cervical lengths are 40, 30, and 25 mm at 16 to 22 weeks, and 35, 25, and 15 mm at 22 to 32 weeks.
- A cervical length of 25 mm or less at 22 to 24 weeks carries a relative risk of 6.5 for birth before 35 weeks and 7.7 for birth before 32 weeks.
- Cervical length before 14 to 15 weeks has little relationship to preterm birth risk because normal effacement begins around 32 weeks.
- Fetal fibronectin cutoff is 50 ng/mL; values below 50 ng/mL are negative.
- Preterm birth occurs in only 35% to 40% of untreated women with a short cervix and in 25% to 30% with a positive second-trimester fetal fibronectin.
- Antenatal corticosteroids: betamethasone 12 mg intramuscularly twice, 24 hours apart (6 mg acetate plus 6 mg phosphate per dose), or dexamethasone 6 mg intramuscularly four times every 12 hours.
- A single rescue steroid course may be given before 32 6/7 weeks if more than 2 weeks have elapsed since the initial course. More than two courses are not recommended.
- Magnesium sulfate for neuroprotection: 4 to 6 g intravenous bolus over 30 minutes, then 1 to 2 g per hour for 12 to 24 hours. Number needed to treat to prevent one cerebral palsy case is 56 at under 32 to 34 weeks and 46 at under 30 weeks.
- Nifedipine: 10 to 20 mg orally, repeated every 3 to 6 hours until contractions are rare, then 30 or 60 mg long-acting every 8 to 12 hours for 48 hours. Avoid more than 60 mg total initial dosing.
- Indomethacin: 50 mg oral load then 25 to 50 mg every 6 hours, limited to 48 hours and to gestations under 32 weeks. Assess amniotic fluid volume and ductal flow before use.
- Tocolysis is contraindicated at 37 weeks or more, with fetal demise or lethal anomaly, chorioamnionitis, fetal compromise requiring prompt delivery, or severe maternal disease such as preeclampsia or abruption.
- Do not combine calcium channel blockers with beta-mimetics; magnesium with calcium channel blockers may cause skeletal muscle blockade.
- Antibiotics for intact membranes do not prolong pregnancy; antibiotics are for group B Streptococcus prophylaxis with penicillin or ampicillin.
- Progesterone and cerclage do not reduce preterm birth in multifetal gestation.

## Anti-patterns
- Diagnosing preterm labor on contractions alone without documented cervical change; false-positive diagnosis is common with low thresholds.
- Prescribing progesterone to a woman with a prior preterm birth but a normal cervix; it does not reduce risk in that group.
- Giving repeated steroid courses routinely; only one rescue course is supported.
- Using indomethacin beyond 48 hours or after 32 weeks, risking ductal constriction, oligohydramnios, and neonatal pulmonary hypertension.
- Combining nifedipine with magnesium or a beta-mimetic.
- Assuming cerclage helps twins; it may increase preterm birth risk in twins with a short cervix.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose preterm labor with six or more contractions per hour plus cervical dilation of 3 cm, effacement of 80%, or ruptured membranes.
2. Use transvaginal cervical length and fetal fibronectin to triage symptomatic women and avoid unnecessary treatment.
3. Give betamethasone 12 mg intramuscularly twice 24 hours apart to every candidate between 24 and 34 weeks.
4. Give magnesium sulfate 4 to 6 g bolus then 1 to 2 g/hour for 12 to 24 hours when delivery before 32 to 34 weeks is expected.
5. Use one tocolytic for 48 hours to permit steroid administration and transfer; choose nifedipine or indomethacin (under 32 weeks, 48 hours maximum).
6. Screen cervical length at 18 to 24 weeks and treat a short cervix with vaginal progesterone 200 mg daily; add cerclage for prior preterm birth with length under 25 mm, especially under 15 mm.
