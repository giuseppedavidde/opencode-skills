# Chapter 3: Amniotic Fluid Dynamics

## Core Idea

Amniotic fluid volume is the balance of production and resorption, and the balance changes with gestational age. Fetal urine and lung liquid are the main sources. Fetal swallowing and the intramembranous pathway are the main sinks. The chapter's argument is that only intramembranous flow is modulated in response to a primary change in amniotic fluid volume. That makes the intramembranous pathway the leading candidate for volume homeostasis. The clinical corollary is that oligohydramnios and polyhydramnios are usually disorders of a specific inflow or outflow, not of a single "fluid level".

## Frameworks Introduced

**Source–sink model of amniotic fluid.** Sources: fetal urine, fetal lung liquid, transudation across cord and skin, fetal metabolism. Sinks: fetal swallowing and intramembranous absorption into fetal vessels.

**Intramembranous (IM) flow model.** Water crosses the amnion from the amniotic cavity into fetal vessels on the placental surface. Flow is driven by osmotic and hydrostatic forces and is described by the Starling equation. Aquaporins (AQP1, AQP3, AQP8, AQP9) are proposed as the molecular route.

**Feedback regulation model.** Expansion of amniotic fluid volume increases IM resorption; the effect appears in sheep after esophageal ligation and after volume infusion.

## Key Concepts

- Amniotic fluid volume is less than 10 mL at 8 weeks, about 630 mL at 22 weeks, and about 770 mL at 28 weeks.
- Volume plateaus between 30 and 36 to 38 weeks, then falls. Mean volume at 41 weeks is about 515 mL.
- After term the decline averages 33% of volume per week, which explains the excess of oligohydramnios post-term.
- Rate of increase is about 10 mL/week at the start of the fetal period, rising to 50 to 60 mL/week at 19 to 25 weeks.
- Rate of decrease averages 60 to 70 mL/week at 40 weeks.
- First-trimester fluid is isotonic with maternal or fetal plasma and has very low protein and oxygen content.
- Later fluid is hypotonic: osmolality falls 20 to 30 mOsm/kg below maternal serum, to about 85% to 90% of serum.
- Amniotic urea, creatinine, and uric acid reach two to three times fetal plasma concentrations.
- Fetal urine output rises from about 110 mL/kg/24 h at 25 weeks to nearly 200 mL/kg/24 h at term, roughly 25% of body weight per day or almost 1000 mL/day near term.
- Fetal swallowing reaches up to 760 mL/day near term, less than daily urine output.
- Fetal lung liquid production is about 100 mL/day/kg in the lamb. Half enters the fluid and half is swallowed, so net lung contribution is about one-sixth that of urine.
- Fluid turns over completely about once per day.
- Human amnion diffusional water permeability is about 2.2 × 10⁻⁴ cm/sec; osmotic permeability about 1.5 × 10⁻³ cm/sec. The amnion is a "leaky" epithelium.
- Ovine amniotic filtration coefficient is about 0.00137 mL/min/mm Hg per kg fetal weight.
- The entire intramembranous route is probably regulated; swallowing and urine output are modulated but do not maintain volume.
- Maternal dehydration causes oligohydramnios on an osmotic basis, and rehydration can increase fetal urine flow and fluid volume.
- Maternal hypoproteinemia with low oncotic pressure may increase amniotic fluid.
- IM flow modelling has been used to predict the success of treatments for twin-twin transfusion syndrome.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the normal fall in amniotic fluid near term as pathology. It is a programmed decline, not a disorder.
- Assuming fetal lung liquid is a major volume source. Its net contribution is small.
- Attributing all oligohydramnios to membrane rupture. Renal agenesis, urinary obstruction, and maternal dehydration produce the same picture.
- Attributing all polyhydramnios to diabetes. Esophageal atresia, aneuploidy, and idiopathic cases are common.
- Using amniotic fluid sodium or osmolality to date pregnancy. Composition changes with gestational age but is not a dating test.

## Key Takeaways

1. Interpret amniotic fluid volume against gestational age, using the 8-, 22-, 28-, and 41-week anchors.
2. Expect a 33% weekly fall in volume after term when counselling about post-term oligohydramnios.
3. Check maternal hydration before attributing mild oligohydramnios to placental insufficiency.
4. Treat an absent bladder with oligohydramnios as a renal tract problem until proven otherwise.
5. Remember that the intramembranous pathway, not swallowing, is the plausible homeostatic regulator.
6. Expect dramatic polyhydramnios with esophageal obstruction in the human, unlike the sheep model.
