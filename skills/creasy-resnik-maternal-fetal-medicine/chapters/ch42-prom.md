# Chapter 42: Premature Rupture of the Membranes

## Core Idea
Rupture of the membranes before the onset of contractions is premature rupture of the membranes (PROM). At term it reflects physiologic membrane weakening and delivery should be expedited. Preterm PROM reflects pathologic weakening, usually infection or inflammation, and management becomes a balance between the hazards of prematurity and the hazards of expectant management: intrauterine infection, abruption, cord compression, and pulmonary hypoplasia. Gestational age at rupture is the single most important determinant of the plan.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Management algorithm by gestational age (Figure 42-2).** Confirmation of rupture, ultrasound for gestational age, growth, and anomalies, cervical cultures for Chlamydia and gonorrhea, anovaginal group B Streptococcus culture, and urine culture. Then: previable PROM under 23 weeks, early preterm PROM 23 to 31 weeks, preterm PROM 32 to 33 weeks, late preterm PROM 34 to 36 weeks, and term PROM at 37 weeks or more.

**Diagnostic sequence.** Sterile speculum examination for fluid passing from the cervical canal; nitrazine paper turning blue above pH 6.0 to 6.5 (amniotic fluid pH 7.1 to 7.3, vaginal secretions 4.5 to 6.0); ferning of an air-dried sample; ultrasound documentation of oligohydramnios; and, if needed, ultrasound-guided amniocentesis with indigo carmine dye.

**NICHD-MFMU antibiotic protocol.** Intravenous ampicillin 2 g every 6 hours plus erythromycin 250 mg every 6 hours for 48 hours, then oral amoxicillin 250 mg every 8 hours plus enteric-coated erythromycin base 333 mg every 8 hours for 5 days.

**Chorioamnionitis criteria.** Maternal fever above 38.0 degrees Celsius (100.4 degrees Fahrenheit) with uterine tenderness, or with maternal or fetal tachycardia, in the absence of another source.

## Key Concepts
- PROM affects about 8% of term pregnancies; 95% of these women deliver within 28 hours of rupture.
- Amniotic fluid cultures after preterm PROM are positive in 25% to 35%.
- A prior preterm birth after PROM confers a 3.3-fold risk of recurrence from the same cause (13.5% versus 4.1%) and a 13.5-fold risk of delivery before 28 weeks (1.8% versus 0.13%).
- Nulliparas with cervical length under 25 mm and a positive cervicovaginal fibronectin at 22 to 24 weeks have a 16.7% chance of preterm birth from PROM. Prior PROM, short cervix, and positive fibronectin together raise the risk of birth before 35 weeks from PROM 10.9-fold (25% versus 2.3%).
- When PROM occurs before 34 weeks, 93% deliver within 1 week; 50% to 60% of conservatively managed women deliver within 1 week. Near the limit of viability, 60% to 70% deliver within 1 week, but 1 in 5 has latency of 4 weeks or more.
- Chorioamnionitis complicates 9% of term PROM, rising to 24% when rupture lasts more than 24 hours; it complicates 13% to 60% of preterm PROM remote from term.
- Endometritis occurs in 2% to 13%; placental abruption in 4% to 12%; maternal sepsis in 0.8% and death in 0.14% near the limit of viability.
- Neonatal sepsis is twice as common after preterm PROM as after preterm birth from preterm labor.
- Pulmonary hypoplasia occurs in 0% to 26.5% of infants after PROM at 16 to 26 weeks (mean 5.9%), approaching 50% with PROM before 19 weeks and 74% to 82% with PROM at 15 to 16 weeks, amniotic fluid index 2 cm or less, and 28-day latency; it is uncommon (0% to 1.4%) after 24 to 26 weeks.
- At term, oxytocin induction shortens rupture duration (17.2 versus 33.3 hours) and lowers chorioamnionitis (4.0% versus 8.6%) and postpartum febrile morbidity (1.9% versus 3.6%) without increasing cesarean delivery (13.7% versus 14.1%).
- At 34 to 36 weeks, expectant management prolongs pregnancy by days, raises chorioamnionitis (16% versus 2%), and lowers cord pH (7.35 versus 7.25); these women should be delivered.
- At 32 to 33 weeks, conservative management with steroids and antibiotics is reasonable if pulmonary maturity is not documented; delivery should be considered if elective delivery is planned within 7 days of steroid benefit.
- At 23 to 31 weeks, manage expectantly to 34 weeks unless infection, abruption, advanced labor, or non-reassuring fetal status intervenes.
- Antenatal corticosteroids after preterm PROM reduce RDS (20% versus 35.4%), intraventricular hemorrhage (7.5% versus 15.9%), and necrotizing enterocolitis (0.8% versus 4.6%) without increasing maternal or neonatal infection.
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate is associated with necrotizing enterocolitis (RR 4.60) and should be avoided.
- Preterm PROM complicates about one fourth of pregnancies with a cerclage and one half of emergent cerclages; remove the cerclage on admission.

## Anti-patterns
- Performing digital cervical examinations in suspected PROM; they shorten latency and introduce infection.
- Using nitrazine or ferning alone when blood, semen, alkaline antiseptic, or bacterial vaginosis can produce false-positive results.
- Expectant management at term or at 34 to 36 weeks, where it adds infection risk without fetal benefit.
- Retaining a cerclage after preterm PROM; removal on admission is recommended.
- Treating previable PROM with immediate delivery by default before counseling on pulmonary hypoplasia and neonatal outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Confirm PROM by speculum examination with pooling, nitrazine, and ferning; reserve indigo carmine amnioinfusion for equivocal cases.
2. At 37 weeks or more, induce labor; do not manage expectantly.
3. At 34 to 36 weeks, deliver actively.
4. At 23 to 31 weeks, give corticosteroids and the ampicillin-erythromycin regimen and manage expectantly to 34 weeks.
5. Diagnose chorioamnionitis clinically and deliver with broad-spectrum antibiotics.
6. Counsel that previable PROM before 23 weeks carries a high risk of lethal pulmonary hypoplasia and fetal death, and individualize expectant management.
