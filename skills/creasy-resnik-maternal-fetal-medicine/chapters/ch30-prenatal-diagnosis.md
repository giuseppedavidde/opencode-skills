# Chapter 30: Prenatal Diagnosis of Congenital Disorders

## Core Idea
Prenatal diagnosis is a layered process: pedigree and population screening identify risk; serum and ultrasound refine it; invasive testing or cell-free DNA confirms it; genetic counseling interprets it. Screening and diagnosis are distinct, and a positive screen only changes the pretest probability. Test performance depends on the cutoff, the population prevalence, and gestational age. A risk estimate should combine independent likelihood ratios, and invasive testing should be reserved for women whose adjusted risk justifies the procedure-related loss.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Screening performance framework.** Sensitivity is the proportion of affected pregnancies that screen positive; specificity is the proportion of unaffected pregnancies that screen negative. Predictive values depend on prevalence. For binary markers, likelihood ratio = sensitivity ÷ false-positive rate; independent likelihood ratios multiply the a priori risk.

**First-trimester combined screening.** NT plus free β-hCG plus PAPP-A at 11–13+6 weeks. Pooled data from 167,210 pregnancies and 604 Down syndrome cases: 88.2% detection at a 5% false-positive rate.

**Second-trimester serum screening.** Triple screen (AFP, hCG, unconjugated estriol) detects 57–67% at a 5% false-positive rate. Quadruple screen (adds inhibin A) detects 60–72% depending on cutoff. Integrated screening (first-trimester NT and PAPP-A plus second-trimester quad) yields about 95% detection at 5%, or 85% at 0.9%.

**Cell-free DNA screening.** Massively parallel, targeted, or single-nucleotide polymorphism sequencing of maternal plasma. Trisomy 21 detection exceeds 98% with a false-positive rate as low as 0.2%; trisomy 18 detection is about 97–100%; trisomy 13 is lower (79–92%). About 5% of samples are uninterpretable, more often with body mass index >30.

**Invasive testing.** Amniocentesis at 15–18 weeks, chorionic villus sampling (CVS) at 10–13 weeks, or percutaneous umbilical blood sampling (PUBS), with karyotype, fluorescence in situ hybridization, microarray, or molecular testing.

## Key Concepts
- Maternal age risk is continuous, not a threshold. At 12 weeks, Down syndrome risk is 1/1068 at age 20, 1/249 at 35, 1/68 at 40, and 1/16 at 45. The age-35 cutoff was historical, based on procedure risk and resource availability.
- NT alone detects about 60% of Down syndrome at a 5% screen-positive rate. Adding the nasal bone raises detection: absent nasal bone is present in 73% of trisomy 21 and 0.5% of euploid fetuses; the likelihood ratio is about 87-fold in high-risk series.
- Second-trimester soft markers: nuchal fold >6 mm is the strongest, with a 20-fold likelihood ratio when isolated (35% of affected versus 0.7% of normal). Others include absent or hypoplastic nasal bone, hyperechoic bowel, short femur, short humerus, echogenic intracardiac focus, and pyelectasis.
- An isolated choroid plexus cyst has a likelihood ratio of only 1.5 for trisomy 18; 30–35% of trisomy 18 fetuses have one, but 65–90% of those have other findings.
- No soft marker alone is an indication for invasive testing; markers modify an already calculated risk.
- Maternal serum AFP screening is best at 16–18 weeks. Cutoffs of 2.0–2.5 MoM detect almost 100% of anencephaly and 85–92% of open spina bifida, with a 2–5% false-positive rate. Amniotic fluid AFP plus acetylcholinesterase detects 96–99% of open spina bifida.
- Unexplained AFP elevation above 2.5 MoM carries a 27% risk of adverse outcome overall: 16% at 2.5–2.9 MoM, 29% at 3.0–5.0 MoM, and 70% above 5.0 MoM. Risks include growth restriction, fetal death, preterm birth, oligohydramnios, abruption, and preeclampsia.
- AFP also rises in congenital nephrosis (>5–6 MoM serum, >10 MoM amniotic fluid), omphalocele, gastroschisis, and duodenal atresia.
- Carrier screening: cystic fibrosis 1/25–29 in whites, 1/46 in Hispanics, 1/29 in Ashkenazi Jews; Tay-Sachs 1/27–30 in Ashkenazi Jews and 1/250–300 in others, with leukocyte (not serum) enzyme testing in pregnancy.
- Spinal muscular atrophy: 1/10,000; 90–94% carrier detection by SMN1 deletion testing, lower in African Americans. Fragile X: full mutation >200 CGG repeats, premutation 59–200, prevalence 1/259 women; expansion risk rises with repeat size, and paternal transmission does not produce full mutations.
- Amniocentesis: 20–22-gauge needle, 20–30 mL, discarding the first 1–2 mL. With ultrasound guidance, excess loss is 0.33% (95% CI 0.09–0.56) and total loss about 0.6%. Amnionitis occurs in 0.1%, Rh isoimmunization in about 1% without prophylaxis, and fluid leak or bleeding in 2–3%. Three insertions carry a 3.8% loss rate versus 1.2% for one pass.
- CVS: equal safety to amniocentesis with adequate experience; transcervical bleeding in 7–10%, transabdominal 1% or less. Mosaicism occurs in about 1% and is confirmed in the fetus in 10–40%. Limb-reduction defects followed very early CVS at 56–66 days; the 10–13-week window is not associated with increased risk.
- PUBS: fetal loss 1.1% per procedure and 1.3% per patient in the North American registry (7462 procedures); about 2% above background. Main complications are chorioamnionitis, membrane rupture, puncture-site bleeding, bradycardia, and thrombosis.
- Chromosomal microarray detects clinically significant microdeletions or duplications in about 1.5% of cases and variants of uncertain significance in about 0.6%; a balanced translocation occurs in about 1/2000 prenatal cases.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a "positive" screen as a diagnosis, or a "negative" screen as absence of disease. A 1:250 positive and a 1:290 negative carry similar actual risk.
- Using maternal age alone as the sole indication for invasive testing while omitting serum or ultrasound screening.
- Interpreting soft markers in isolation, without the a priori risk and a complete anatomic survey.
- Using a maternal serum titer or an isolated marker to reassure a patient with a prior affected pregnancy.
- Performing CVS before 10 weeks, or interpreting confined placental mosaicism as fetal aneuploidy without confirmation.
- Offering cell-free DNA as a diagnostic test, or implying that a negative result excludes all anomalies.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build the risk estimate from maternal age, NT, serum analytes, and ultrasound likelihood ratios, not from any single result.
2. Use first-trimester combined screening (about 88% detection at 5% false-positive rate) or integrated screening (about 95%); reserve cell-free DNA for high-performance screening, not diagnosis.
3. Investigate AFP >2.5 MoM with targeted ultrasound and amniotic fluid AFP plus acetylcholinesterase.
4. Quote procedure risk with ultrasound guidance: about 0.3% excess loss after amniocentesis and about 1% after PUBS.
5. Confirm abnormal screening or ultrasound findings with karyotype or microarray and genetic counseling.
