# Chapter 13: Behavioral States in the Fetus: Relationship to Fetal Health and Development

## Core Idea
The fetus organizes its activity into discrete behavioral states that are homologous to postnatal NREM sleep, REM sleep, and wakefulness. These states are defined by the stable temporal coincidence of three variables — gross body movements, eye movements, and fetal heart rate (FHR) pattern — and by synchronous change of all three at transition. State organization is a developmental achievement: it is not reliably present before approximately 36 weeks' gestation, when brainstem control circuitry matures. Because hypoxemia alters state activity in a graded, adaptive sequence, behavioral parameters can be used to assess fetal health, but only if their maturational norms are respected.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Nijhuis four-state classification (human fetus).** Adapted from Prechtl's neonatal scheme; states are assigned only when parameters remain concordant over prolonged recording and change together at transition.
- **State 1F** — quiescence with only occasional brief gross body movements, eye movements absent, FHR stable with narrow oscillation bandwidth. Equivalent to NREM/quiet sleep.
- **State 2F** — frequent gross body movements, eye movements continually present, FHR with wider oscillation bandwidth and frequent accelerations during movement. Equivalent to REM/active sleep.
- **State 3F** — no gross body movements, eye movements continually present, FHR stable but with wider bandwidth than 1F. Rare after birth.
- **State 4F** — frequent vigorous gross body movements, eye movements continually present, FHR unstable with large long-lasting accelerations. Awake-like.

**Ovine electrocortical (ECoG) state model.** The animal framework underlying the human scheme: low-voltage/high-frequency (LV/HF) activity corresponds to REM, high-voltage/low-frequency (HV/LF) to NREM, plus an awake-like state identified by blood pressure, heart rate, and evoked responses.

**Hierarchical hypoxia response model.** With progressive oxygen reduction, electrocortical activity is preserved until metabolic acidosis supervenes; movement and breathing reduce earlier. This ordering is the physiologic rationale for biophysical surveillance.

## Key Concepts
- Well-differentiated ECoG patterns appear in the ovine fetus from approximately 120 days (term ~145 days); early gestation is dominated by LV/HF-REM (>50% of time), with ~30% HV/LF-NREM and only brief wakefulness.
- By term, LV/HF-REM falls to approximately 40%, with the remainder redistributed to wakefulness and HV/LF-NREM. Postnatally REM falls below 10%.
- Human behavioral states are first clearly defined at approximately **36 weeks**. At that age state 2F occupies ~40% of recording time, state 1F ~25%, state 4F only briefly, and ~20% of time no state is identifiable.
- Coincidence of 1F and 2F rises with gestation: at 25–30 weeks 6% and 17%; at 32 weeks 15% and 46%; at 36 weeks 20% and 38%; at 40 weeks 36% and 42%. Time with no coincident state falls from 29% at 32 weeks to 9% at 40 weeks.
- At term, NREM plus REM states occupy approximately 80% of time — comparable to the preterm neonate.
- The activity–quiescence cycle lengthens from approximately 20 minutes at 28 weeks to approximately 60 minutes at term, matching the newborn REM–NREM cycle.
- In the ovine fetus, breathing movements occur only in LV/HF-REM, but about one third of that state is not accompanied by breathing; breathing therefore cannot define the state.
- Fetal micturition is inhibited during low-variability (1F-like) epochs and facilitated during high-variability epochs with eye and breathing movements.
- Regular mouthing movements occur only during quiescence and are a concomitant of state 1F.
- Chronic hypoxemia sufficient to cause growth restriction reduces LV/HF ECoG time by approximately 30%, modestly increases HV/LF time, and reduces breathing to approximately 20% of time — interpreted as a shift to lower oxidative demand.
- In human IUGR, breathing and gross body movements are generally reduced but overlap substantially with population norms; the diagnostic value of movement counts alone is therefore limited.
- A normal biophysical variable carries a negative predictive value of approximately 95% for normal perinatal outcome, similar across breathing, movement, and nonstress testing; combining variables improves positive predictive value, and body movement adds more than breathing or heart rate testing.
- Biophysical profile scoring over 70 minutes is held to be more predictive than shorter windows because it spans a full state cycle.

## Anti-patterns
- **Diagnosing behavioral states before 36 weeks.** Before that age, activity and quiescence alternate, and parameters may transiently mimic states, but they lack stable temporal linkage and synchronous transition.
- **Sequential rather than concurrent observation.** Scoring biophysical variables one after another ignores their interdependence with state organization; a fetus quiet in state 1F is not necessarily compromised.
- **Treating reduced movements as an early hypoxia marker.** In the ovine model, marked reduction in movement occurs only once acidemia is established; movements index moderate-to-severe, not mild, hypoxemia.
- **Using fetal breathing as an isolated test at 26–33 weeks.** Breathing is more often falsely abnormal at 26–33 weeks than at 34–41 weeks because of lower physiologic incidence at younger ages.
- **Interpreting a nonreactive nonstress test before 32 weeks as pathologic.** Acceleration amplitude normally is lower before 32 weeks, so false nonreactivity is common; computerized analysis accounting for gestational age is preferable.
- **Assuming absent breathing in oligohydramnios proves pulmonary hypoplasia.** Fetuses with preterm rupture of membranes or renal agenesis studied after 29 weeks can still make breathing movements; lung hypoplasia in oligohydramnios is primarily from chronically reduced lung expansion, not from inhibition of breathing.

## Key Takeaways
- Assign states only at or after approximately 36 weeks, using concurrent body movement, eye movement, and FHR recording over a period long enough to capture a complete cycle (approximately 60 minutes at term).
- Expect state 2F to predominate at term (~40%) and state 1F ~25%; a 20-minute quiet epoch alone is not abnormal.
- Read a fall in movement or breathing as a late, not early, sign of hypoxemia; electrocortical activity is preserved until acidosis appears.
- Use a normal biophysical profile for its ~95% negative predictive value, and combine variables when a positive (abnormal) result must be trusted.
- Apply gestational-age-specific norms to breathing and heart rate reactivity, particularly between 26 and 33 weeks.
- Remember that fetal breathing supports lung growth by opposing lung recoil and by phasic cell distortion; its absence is a mechanism of, not merely a marker for, pulmonary hypoplasia.
