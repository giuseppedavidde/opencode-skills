# Chapter 5: Biology of Parturition

## Core Idea

Parturition is an inflammatory process that converts a quiescent uterus into a contractile organ. The conversion requires functional progesterone withdrawal, functional estrogen activation, prostaglandin synthesis, oxytocin receptor upregulation, and gap junction formation. The chapter argues that the fetus sets the timing through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis and a placental clock. The clock is set near implantation but retains some flexibility. Cervical remodelling begins months before the first contraction, so cervix and myometrium are separate but coordinated targets.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Four-phase cervical remodelling model.** Softening occupies most of pregnancy. Ripening occupies the last 1 to 2 weeks. Dilation occurs in active labor. Postpartum repair restores tissue integrity.

**Functional progesterone withdrawal model.** The human progesterone receptor has two isoforms: PR-A (94 kDa) and PR-B (116 kDa). PR-B activates progesterone-responsive genes; PR-A generally represses PR-B. Labor is associated with a rise in the myometrial PR-A/PR-B ratio, so progesterone action falls without a fall in circulating progesterone.

**Placental clock model.** Placental CRH rises exponentially, driven by cortisol from the fetal adrenal. CRH-binding protein falls before labor, so free CRH rises.

**Contraction-associated protein (CAP) model.** Estrogen upregulates ion channels, oxytocin receptors, prostaglandin receptors, and gap junctions. Progesterone suppresses them.

**Electromechanical coupling model.** Action potentials spread through the myometrium; contraction force depends on spike frequency and on the number of recruited cells.

## Key Concepts

- Uterine weight rises from 70 g non-pregnant to 1100 to 1200 g at term; volume rises from 10 mL to 5 L.
- Uterine blood flow rises from 2% of cardiac output to 17% at term. Of this, 80% to 90% goes to the placenta.
- The cervix contains about 10% smooth muscle; the rest is collagen I and III, elastin, and proteoglycans.
- Maternal CRH rises from 10 to 100 pg/mL in non-pregnant women to 500 to 3000 pg/mL in the third trimester, then falls abruptly after delivery.
- Progesterone from the corpus luteum maintains early pregnancy until the placenta takes over at 7 to 9 weeks.
- Mifepristone induces abortion before 7 weeks (49 days).
- Progesterone concentrations remain high at term and do not fall at the onset of labor. The withdrawal is functional, not absolute.
- Aromatase deficiency blocks cervical ripening, which shows that placental estrogens are needed for preparation for labor.
- Prostaglandins rise in maternal plasma, urine, and amniotic fluid before contractions begin, which supports causation rather than consequence.
- Fetal membranes produce mainly PGE₂; decidua produces mainly PGF₂α; myometrium produces mainly prostacyclin.
- PGF₂α, thromboxane, PGE₁, and PGF₂ promote contractility. PGE₂, PGD₂, and prostacyclin inhibit it.
- Chorionic 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase (PGDH) degrades prostaglandins and forms a metabolic barrier. PGDH-expressing trophoblasts are reduced in chorioamnionitis-associated preterm labor.
- Oxytocin has a biological half-life of 3 to 4 minutes and is degraded by placental oxytocinase.
- Myometrial oxytocin receptor concentration rises 50- to 100-fold in the first trimester and a further 200- to 300-fold later in pregnancy.
- Fetal oxytocin secretion rises from about 1 mU/min before labor to about 3 mU/min after spontaneous labor.
- Myometrial action potentials are 12 to 25 mV, from a resting potential of −65 to −80 mV.
- Contraction intensity is 25 to 30 mm Hg in early labor and 60 to 65 mm Hg in active labor.
- A cervical length below 1.5 cm or 2.0 cm at 16 to 24 weeks is a significant predictor of preterm birth.
- Weekly intramuscular 17α-hydroxyprogesterone caproate from 18 to 22 weeks reduced delivery before 37 weeks in women with a prior preterm birth. Vaginal progesterone reduced preterm delivery in women with a short cervix.

## Anti-patterns

- Teaching that progesterone falls before human labor. It does not; the receptor ratio changes instead.
- Treating oxytocin as the initiator of labor. It sustains and amplifies a process already underway.
- Treating cervical softening as the start of labor. It begins in the first trimester.
- Using maternal CRH as a clinical predictor. Intrapatient and interpatient variability make it unreliable.
- Assuming all prostaglandins stimulate the uterus. Several prostanoids inhibit contractility.
- Equating a long cervix with safety. Remodelling can precede shortening by weeks.

## Key Takeaways

1. Read labor as an inflammatory cascade, not a single hormonal switch.
2. Target the cervix and the myometrium as separate clinical problems.
3. Use progesterone supplementation only in the populations tested: prior preterm birth, or a short cervix.
4. Expect cervical length below 2.0 cm at 16 to 24 weeks to carry a high preterm risk.
5. Remember that PGDH in the chorion is a functional barrier against prostaglandin-driven labor.
6. Use contraction pressure, not patient report, when quantifying uterine activity.
