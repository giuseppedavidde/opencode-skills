# Chapter 62: Gastrointestinal Disease in Pregnancy

## Core Idea
Progesterone relaxes smooth muscle and the gravid uterus displaces bowel, so normal pregnancy produces reflux, delayed orocecal transit, constipation, and nausea that overlap with organic disease. The diagnostic problem is separating physiologic complaint from pathology without unnecessary radiation or endoscopy, and the therapeutic problem is that many effective agents carry unfavorable pregnancy categories. The chapter argues for a stepwise approach: lifestyle and antacids first, then histamine-2 receptor antagonists, then proton pump inhibitors, with endoscopy reserved for alarm features. Peptic ulcer disease usually improves in pregnancy, whereas appendicitis is more often perforated at diagnosis because the presentation is atypical and imaging is delayed.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Stepwise GERD/PUD algorithm.** Lifestyle and dietary modification → antacids or sucralfate → H₂-receptor antagonist → proton pump inhibitor or esophagogastroduodenoscopy (EGD) if symptoms persist, especially after the first trimester.

**Nausea and vomiting of pregnancy (NVP) treatment ladder.** Nonpharmacologic (ginger 500–1,500 mg/day in divided doses, vitamin B₆, acupressure at P6) → vitamin B₆ 10–25 mg every 6 hours → antihistamines (doxylamine 12.5–25 mg every 8 h) → phenothiazines or dopamine antagonists → glucocorticoids for refractory hyperemesis.

**Hyperemesis severity pathway.** History and weight loss → orthostatic vital signs and electrolytes → intravenous crystalloid (up to 2 L lactated Ringer solution) with potassium replacement → antiemetics → methylprednisolone or hydrocortisone → total parenteral nutrition in prolonged cases.

**ASGE indications for endoscopy in pregnancy.** Significant or ongoing gastrointestinal bleeding; severe or refractory nausea, vomiting, or abdominal pain; dysphagia or odynophagia; strong suspicion of upper gastrointestinal or colonic mass; severe diarrhea with negative noninvasive evaluation; choledocholithiasis or cholangitis; biliary or pancreatic duct injury.

**Acute appendicitis imaging sequence.** Ultrasound (graded compression) → MRI (no ionizing radiation) → limited helical CT (300 mrad) when needed; radiography has no diagnostic role.

## Key Concepts
- NVP affects 50–80% of pregnant women; symptoms begin at 4–10 weeks and resolve by 20 weeks; 3% have symptoms only in the third trimester. Only 2% have nausea confined to morning; 80% have all-day symptoms.
- Hyperemesis gravidarum affects approximately 0.5% of live births and is associated with gestational trophoblastic disease, triploidy, and trisomy 21.
- Vitamin B₆ 10–25 mg every 6 hours is first-line pharmacotherapy. Doxylamine 12.5–25 mg every 8 h (category A); diphenhydramine 25–50 mg every 8 h; meclizine 25 mg every 6 h; dimenhydrinate 50–100 mg every 4–6 h; promethazine 25 mg every 4–6 h; prochlorperazine 5–10 mg every 6 h; metoclopramide 10 mg every 6 h (tardive dyskinesia risk with prolonged use); droperidol 1.25–2.5 mg IM/IV only (torsades warning).
- Methylprednisolone 16 mg every 8 hours with a 2-week taper: avoid before 10 weeks and limit to less than 6 weeks. Intravenous hydrocortisone 300 mg daily for 3 days then taper.
- Correct hyperemesis electrolytes slowly to avoid central pontine myelinolysis; add potassium until hypokalemia resolves.
- Peptic ulcer disease: of 313 pregnancies after a PUD diagnosis, 45% were asymptomatic, 44% improved, 12% unchanged. Misoprostol is category X (abortifacient); tetracycline is category D.
- More than 2,000 pregnancies exposed to cimetidine or ranitidine show no increased malformation risk; famotidine and nizatidine have less data, and nizatidine has conflicting animal data.
- Acute appendicitis: right lower quadrant pain in about 80%, right upper quadrant pain in 10–55%, nausea/vomiting absent in about 20%. Mean white blood cell count is about 16,000 cells/mm³; leukocytosis alone is not predictive.
- Perforation risk rises with trimester: odds ratio 1.0 in first, 2.1 in second, 1.6 in third trimester; only 50% of operated cases are confirmed histologically.
- Ultrasound sensitivity 66–100%, specificity 95%; CT sensitivity 92%, specificity 95–99%; MRI sensitivity 91%, specificity 98%. Normal appendix on MRI is ≤6 mm, or filled with air or oral contrast; a fluid-filled appendix >6–7 mm is abnormal.
- Inflammatory bowel disease: one third of women with active ulcerative colitis or Crohn disease flare in pregnancy, equal to the nonpregnant annual risk; disease activity at conception tends to persist. Cesarean delivery occurs in up to 44%. Surgery is required in 20% of ulcerative colitis and 70% of Crohn disease over a lifetime.
- Continue IBD maintenance therapy through pregnancy; exclude *Clostridium difficile* and other infection before escalating immunosuppression. Flexible sigmoidoscopy is safe and usually sufficient.
- Constipation: polyethylene glycol 8–32 oz daily is preferred; lactulose or sorbitol 15–30 mL once or twice daily; docusate 100 mg twice daily; senna 17–34 mg; bisacodyl 10 mg rectal or 30 mg oral (limit long-term use to 3 days/week); magnesium-containing laxatives carry magnesium toxicity risk.
- Hemorrhoids: conservative therapy first; sclerotherapy with 5% phenol is safe; rubber band ligation for reducibly prolapsed internal hemorrhoids; surgical hemorrhoidectomy reserved for incarcerated prolapse or failed office measures.
- Position pregnant patients in the left lateral tilt, not supine, in the second and third trimesters to avoid aortocaval compression.
- Endoscopic ultrasound is preferred for common bile duct stones in pregnancy because it avoids radiation and does not carry the pancreatitis risk of endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating all-day nausea as "morning sickness" and dismissing it; only 2% of cases are morning-only.
- Rushing hyperemesis electrolyte correction, risking central pontine myelinolysis.
- Using misoprostol for peptic ulcer disease in pregnancy — it is category X and an abortifacient.
- Excluding appendicitis because the white cell count is only modestly elevated or pain is not in the right lower quadrant.
- Delaying appendectomy for imaging; perforation odds are 2.1 in the second trimester.
- Stopping IBD maintenance drugs at conception, which converts quiescent disease into an active flare.
- Starting immunosuppression for an IBD flare without stool studies for *C. difficile*.
- Placing a pregnant patient supine for endoscopy or surgery in the second or third trimester.

## Key Takeaways
1. Start NVP treatment with vitamin B₆ 10–25 mg every 6 hours, adding doxylamine if needed; escalate to antihistamines, then dopamine antagonists.
2. Reserve glucocorticoids for refractory hyperemesis: methylprednisolone 16 mg every 8 hours with taper, never before 10 weeks or beyond 6 weeks.
3. In GERD or PUD, use antacids/sucralfate, then H₂-blockers, then a proton pump inhibitor; perform EGD only for alarm features.
4. Suspect appendicitis with right lower quadrant pain in 80% of cases; proceed to ultrasound, then MRI, then limited CT, and accept a 50% histologic confirmation rate as expected.
5. Keep IBD patients on 5-aminosalicylates, thiopurines, or biologics; check stool studies before treating a flare as inflammatory.
6. Use polyethylene glycol as first-line therapy for constipation and reserve stimulant laxatives for short courses.
