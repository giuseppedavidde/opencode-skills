# Chapter 4: Multiple Gestation: The Biology of Twinning

## Core Idea

Twinning is classified by two independent axes: zygosity (monozygotic or dizygotic) and chorionicity (dichorionic or monochorionic). Zygosity determines genetic identity; chorionicity determines risk. Monochorionic placentation carries shared cotyledons and interfetal anastomoses, and therefore carries the twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome and its consequences. The chapter argues that the older terms "identical" and "fraternal" obscure both axes and should be replaced by monozygotic/dizygotic and by a placental examination at birth.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Weinberg differential rule.** The number of monozygotic pairs is estimated as (like-sex pairs − unlike-sex pairs) ÷ number of pregnancies. It assumes all unlike-sex pairs are dizygotic.

**Hellin's hypothesis.** When the twinning rate is n, the triplet rate is n², the quadruplet rate is n³, and so on.

**Monozygotic timing model.** Division within the first 2 days yields dichorionic placentation. Division at about day 3 yields dichorionic diamniotic twins. Later division yields monochorionic diamniotic, then monoamniotic, then conjoined twins.

**Twin-to-twin transfusion model.** An arteriovenous shunt exists when one cotyledon is fed by an artery from one twin and drained by a vein to the other. Without compensating artery-to-artery or vein-to-vein anastomoses, the donor becomes hypovolemic and anemic and the recipient becomes hypervolemic and plethoric.

## Key Concepts

- Monozygotic twinning is remarkably constant at 3.5 to 4 per 1000 pregnancies. Dizygotic twinning varies widely with population, maternal age, parity, and nutrition.
- Multiples represent 3.3% of US births, or about 1 in 30 gestations. At least 1 in 43 births is a twin and 1 in 1341 pregnancies yields triplets.
- About one third of US twins are monozygotic.
- Dizygotic twinning rises with maternal age to about 35 years and then falls abruptly. It also rises with parity and falls during famine.
- Chorionicity by ultrasound: a maximal dividing membrane thickness of 2 mm identifies monochorionicity with 82% accuracy. Oligohydramnios is the main limitation.
- Diamniotic dichorionic membranes contain four layers: two amnions and two chorions with degenerated trophoblast between them.
- Diamniotic monochorionic membranes contain two amnions only.
- Monoamniotic twins occur in about 1% of twin pregnancies and carry 50% to 60% mortality from cord entanglement. No further mortality from entanglement is expected after 32 weeks.
- Perinatal mortality is about 25% for diamniotic monochorionic twins and about 8.9% for dichorionic twins.
- About 20% to 30% of monozygotic twins have dichorionic placentation.
- Velamentous cord insertion is six to nine times more common in twin placentas.
- Vasa previa can cause fetal exsanguination and death within 3 minutes of membrane rupture. The second twin can also exsanguinate through interfetal anastomoses.
- TTTS presents with recipient hydramnios, usually between 20 and 30 weeks, and a "stuck" donor with a dry sac and amnion nodosum.
- Interfetal anastomoses are not invariable in monochorionic twins.
- Arteriovenous shunts may be single or multiple and may run in opposing directions.
- In one series of 668 twin pairs: 35% were dizygotic by sex, 20% were monozygotic by monochorionicity, and 45% were same-sex dichorionic. Of that last group, 36% proved dizygotic and 8% monozygotic.
- Blood chimerism in dizygotic twins follows chorion fusion and is rare in humans.

## Anti-patterns

- Labelling twins "identical" on the basis of sex alone. Same-sex dichorionic pairs are often dizygotic.
- Assuming a monochorionic placenta proves monozygosity in every case. Rare exceptions exist.
- Assuming every monochorionic twin placenta has anastomoses. Some do not.
- Diagnosing TTTS from a size difference alone. The syndrome requires the polyhydramnios/oligohydramnios sequence.
- Ignoring a velamentous cord because the pregnancy is dichorionic. Vasa previa is not confined to monochorionic pairs.
- Deciding zygosity from appearance in adulthood. DNA polymorphism is the reliable method.

## Key Takeaways

1. Determine chorionicity, not zygosity, as the first step in twin management.
2. Measure the dividing membrane; a maximum of 2 mm supports monochorionicity.
3. Examine every twin placenta at birth and record the findings in the chart.
4. Anticipate TTTS from 20 weeks in every monochorionic diamniotic pregnancy.
5. Screen for vasa previa when a velamentous insertion is seen.
6. Use DNA polymorphism to settle zygosity when same-sex dichorionic twins remain ambiguous.
