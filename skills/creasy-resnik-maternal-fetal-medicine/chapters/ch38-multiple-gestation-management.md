# Chapter 38: Multiple Gestation: Clinical Characteristics and Management

## Core Idea
Multiple gestation is a high-risk state driven by three forces: prematurity, monochorionicity, and growth restriction. Chorionicity, not zygosity, determines risk, so assigning chorionicity accurately and early is the pivotal clinical act. Monochorionic twins share a placental circulation and therefore carry the specific hazards of twin-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS), twin anemia-polycythemia sequence (TAPS), and co-twin death.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Chorionicity assignment.** First-trimester transvaginal ultrasound is the reference standard. Findings supporting dichorionicity: two separate gestational sacs with thick echogenic rings before 8 weeks, two yolk sacs, three or four membrane layers at placental insertion, membrane thickness greater than 2 mm, and the twin-peak (lambda) sign. A composite of one placenta, sex concordance, thin membrane, and absent lambda sign gives a positive predictive value of 92% for monochorionicity. First-trimester accuracy approaches 100%.

**Quintero staging of TTTS.** Stage I: donor bladder visible, normal Doppler. Stage II: donor bladder absent, normal Doppler. Stage III: donor bladder absent with critically abnormal Doppler. Stage IV: hydrops. Stage V: death of one or both. Stage does not reliably predict outcome, and progression is not orderly.

**Management of severe TTTS.** For stages II to IV diagnosed before 24 to 26 weeks, selective fetoscopic laser coagulation of placental anastomoses is first-line. Reduction amniocentesis and amniotic septostomy have been abandoned for severe disease.

**Delivery route.** Vertex-vertex twins: plan vaginal birth absent obstetric indication. Nonvertex second twin: vaginal breech delivery is reasonable at estimated weight 1500 to 3500 g if the second twin is not substantially larger and the head is not hyperextended. Cesarean delivery for all gestations with three or more viable fetuses.

## Key Concepts
- Multiple gestation exceeds 3% of US live births; the twin rate is 33.2 per 1000 births, a 76% rise since 1980.
- Mean gestational age at birth: twins 35.3 weeks, triplets 31.9, quadruplets 29.5.
- Birth before 32 weeks: 1.6% of singletons, 11% of twins, 37% of triplets.
- Stillbirth per 1000: 6.8 singletons, 16.1 twins, 21.5 triplets. Infant mortality per 1000: 5, 23.4, 51.2.
- About 20% of twins are monochorionic; monochorionicity carries higher perinatal mortality.
- Growth discordance above 18% predicts perinatal morbidity in 1028 unselected twins (ESPRIT), independent of chorionicity; above 20% also predicts adverse outcome. Discordance is an indication for surveillance, not immediate delivery.
- TTTS affects about 15% of monochorionic twins although arteriovenous anastomoses exist in up to 70% of monochorionic placentas.
- TTTS criteria: maximal vertical pocket under 2 cm in the donor sac and over 8 cm in the recipient sac before 20 weeks, or over 10 cm after 20 weeks.
- Eurofoetus trial (142 women): laser versus amnioreduction gave delivery at 33 versus 29 weeks and survival of at least one infant in 76% versus 51%.
- Pooled laser series (1367 cases): perinatal survival 66%, at least one survivor 72%, complete loss before 24 weeks 7%, pPROM 15%, residual anastomoses 19%, recurrent TTTS 10%.
- TAPS is diagnosed when middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity exceeds 1.5 MoM in one twin and occurs in up to 13% after TTTS.
- Monoamniotic twins are about 1% of monozygotic gestations; mortality has fallen from over 50% to about 13% in recent series.
- Death of one monochorionic co-twin carries a 26% risk of profound neurologic injury in the survivor.
- Reducing triplets to twins raises miscarriage before 24 weeks from 4% to 8% but lowers delivery at 24 to 32 weeks from 27% to 10%.

## Anti-patterns
- Assigning chorionicity by placental count alone; one apparent placental mass has only 42% positive predictive value for monochorionicity.
- Injecting potassium chloride for selective termination when dichorionicity is not confirmed.
- Treating growth discordance as an automatic delivery indication rather than a trigger for surveillance.
- Expectant management of a monochorionic pregnancy with a clearly premorbid fetus, given that neurologic injury begins at co-twin death.
- Overestimating maternal disseminated intravascular coagulation risk after a single fetal death; recent reviews found no clinical cases.
- Assuming TTTS requires growth discordance; acute TTTS produces fluid discordance without size discordance.

## Key Takeaways
1. Determine chorionicity by first-trimester transvaginal ultrasound in every multiple gestation and document it.
2. Diagnose TTTS with the paired maximal vertical pocket criteria and stage by Quintero, but do not use stage alone for prognosis.
3. Offer fetoscopic laser for Quintero stage II to IV before 24 to 26 weeks; counsel 66% perinatal survival and 12% neurologic impairment.
4. Plan delivery at 37 weeks for uncomplicated twins, or about 34 weeks after successful laser therapy.
5. Offer vaginal breech delivery for a nonvertex second twin of 1500 to 3500 g; plan cesarean delivery for three or more fetuses.
6. Treat monochorionic co-twin death as a neurologic emergency for the survivor and deliver by 37 weeks or at documented lung maturity.
