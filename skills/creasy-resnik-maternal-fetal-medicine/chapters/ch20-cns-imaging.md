# Chapter 20: Central Nervous System Imaging

## Core Idea
Fetal CNS imaging is organized around a small number of binary distinctions: is the midline intact, is the ventricular system dilated, is the posterior fossa normal, and is there a skull defect. Each entity in the chapter is presented as a definition, a differential, key diagnostic features, associated anomalies, management, and prognosis, and the same template applies whether the finding is a "soft marker" such as a choroid plexus cyst or a lethal malformation such as alobar holoprosencephaly. Ventriculomegaly is explicitly a sign, not a diagnosis; its meaning depends on the associated anomalies, karyotype, and infection status. Prognosis is driven less by the size of the fluid space than by the presence of additional CNS and extracranial malformations.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Ventriculomegaly severity classification.** Diameter of one or both lateral ventricles ≥10 mm defines ventriculomegaly. Borderline/mild: **10–12 mm**; moderate: **12.1–14.9 mm**; severe: **≥15 mm**. Many publications group mild and moderate as 10–14.9 mm. "Hydrocephaly" is often reserved for measurements >15 mm.

**Cephalocele classification.** Cranial defect along a suture with herniation of brain and/or meninges: encephalocele if brain tissue is present, meningocele if only CSF. Occipital, parietal, or frontal; 80% occipital in the Western Hemisphere.

**Holoprosencephaly spectrum.** Alobar (complete failure of forebrain division, monoventricle, absent falx, corpus callosum, and cavum septi pellucidi, fused thalami), semilobar (posterior separation only, fused anterior horns), lobar (absent CSP and corpus callosum in the affected area), and middle interhemispheric variant (failure of separation of posterior frontal and parietal lobes, absent corpus callosum body, heterotopias).

**Posterior fossa differential.** Dandy-Walker malformation versus posterior fossa arachnoid cyst, Blake's pouch cyst, mega cisterna magna, and vermian hypoplasia.

**Arnold-Chiari II pathway.** Open spinal defect → CSF leak → failure of posterior fossa development → hindbrain herniation → noncommunicating ventriculomegaly in 80–90%.

## Key Concepts
- **Cephalocele:** incidence 1 in 3,500 to 1 in 5,000 live births; 80% occipital; 65% have a major malformation; hydrocephaly in 80% of occipital lesions; mortality up to 80% when diagnosed prenatally.
- **Choroid plexus cysts:** 0.18–3.6% of second-trimester fetuses; usually <10 mm (range 3–20 mm) and must exceed 2 mm; associated with trisomy 18.
- **Porencephaly:** perinatal arterial ischemic stroke occurs in 1:2,500 to 1:5,000 term neonates; 64% of affected infants have a thrombophilic marker; COL4A1 mutation causes autosomal dominant disease.
- **Holoprosencephaly:** occurs in **1:250 embryos** but **1:10,000 live births**; more than **90%** of affected embryos die spontaneously. **25–45%** of newborns have a karyotype abnormality (trisomy 13, trisomy 18, triploidy), and about **25%** have syndromes such as Smith-Lemli-Opitz or Pallister-Hall; SHH, ZIC2, SIX3, and TGIF mutations occur. **33%** die within the first day, **58%** within the first month, and only **29%** survive 1 year; isolated HPE has the best survival, with **54%** living.
- **Aqueductal stenosis:** congenital dilation of the lateral ventricles occurs in **0.3–1.5 per 1,000 births**; AS causes **30–40%** of fetal ventriculomegaly and is idiopathic in about **75%**. X-linked hydrocephaly affects **1:30,000 males** (L1CAM, MASA syndrome, SPG1). Associated findings: severe bilateral VM >15 mm with dangling choroid, third-ventricle dilation, thin or compressed mantle, absent or fenestrated CSP, normal posterior fossa, periventricular calcification with infection, abducted thumbs in **50–60%**, chromosomal aberrations in **11%**, extracranial anomalies in **30%**, multiple anomalies in **25%**. It is a diagnosis of exclusion.
- **Arnold-Chiari II:** incidence equals that of spina bifida, **1.90 per 10,000 live births**; the defect originates at closure of the posterior neural tube in week 4. Elevated maternal serum AFP; "lemon" and "banana" signs in **>95%** of cases between **16 and 24 weeks**; hydrocephaly in **80–90%**; chromosomal abnormalities in about **10%**. First-trimester sign: nonvisualization of the intracranial translucency.
- **Mild ventriculomegaly:** overall incidence 1:1,000 to 2:1,000 births; **1.4/1,000** in low-risk and **22/1,000** in high-risk populations; **7.8 per 10,000** at 18–24 weeks. Isolated mild VM resolves before 24 weeks in as many as **62%**. Associated structural or chromosomal malformation in about **41%**; in 355 confirmed cases, aneuploidy **11%** (trisomy 21 most common, then 18 and 13), structural anomalies with normal karyotype **43%** (brain anomalies most common), congenital infection **0.8%**. A systematic review found aneuploidy **3%** and infection **0.4%** in mild VM. Males are diagnosed more often.
- **Dandy-Walker malformation:** incidence **1 per 30,000 births**; present in **4–12%** of infantile hydrocephaly; developmental arrest at 4–6 weeks. Enlarged posterior fossa with elevated tentorium and torcular, fourth ventricle dilating into a large cisterna magna, and absent or hypoplastic vermis with splayed hemispheres. Ventriculomegaly in **70–80%**; associated anomalies in up to **60%** prenatally (50–70% postnatally); CNS anomalies are the commonest. Intrauterine demise in about **14%**; **80%** develop hydrocephaly by 3 months; infant mortality **10–66%** — **63%** with multiple malformations versus **37%** isolated; two or more affected organ systems confer a **6-fold** death risk and one additional finding **2.27-fold**. Up to one third of survivors develop normally, and favorable outcome is seen with a normal lobulated vermis and no associated anomalies.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating ventriculomegaly as a diagnosis.** It is a sign requiring a search for open spina bifida, Chiari II, corpus callosum agenesis, infection, and aneuploidy.
- **Calling every choroidal lucency a cyst.** A lesion must exceed 2 mm to meet criteria; isolated small cysts are a variant, not an indication for invasive testing by themselves.
- **Diagnosing aqueductal stenosis without excluding other causes.** It is a diagnosis of exclusion; posterior fossa structures must be normal.
- **Missing the posterior fossa in Dandy-Walker.** Mega cisterna magna, Blake's pouch cyst, and arachnoid cyst are the principal mimics and have different prognoses.
- **Assuming an isolated Dandy-Walker malformation guarantees normal outcome.** Up to one third of survivors are normal at best, and hydrocephaly often progresses postnatally.
- **Ignoring the karyotype and infection workup in "mild" ventriculomegaly.** Aneuploidy is present in roughly 3–11% and infection in 0.4–0.8%.
- **Reading holoprosencephaly as a surgical problem.** Severe forms carry high intrauterine and neonatal mortality; counseling should address prognosis and pregnancy options.

## Key Takeaways
- Measure the atria; apply 10–12 mm (mild), 12.1–14.9 mm (moderate), and ≥15 mm (severe), and look for dangling choroid as a sign of progressive dilation.
- For every ventriculomegaly, examine the spine, posterior fossa, corpus callosum, and CSP, and offer karyotype and infection studies.
- Use the lemon and banana signs at 16–24 weeks for Chiari II; they are present in >95% of cases.
- Distinguish cephalocele (skull defect, sac) from meningocele (CSF only) and document brain content, because hydrocephaly occurs in 80% of occipital lesions.
- Treat a porencephalic cavity as evidence of a prior destructive vascular or infectious event; assess for thrombophilia and COL4A1.
- Diagnose Dandy-Walker by the combination of large posterior fossa, elevated tentorium/torcular, and vermian hypoplasia; counsel that 80% develop hydrocephaly by 3 months.
- Report associated anomalies, because they — not the size of the fluid space — determine prognosis.
