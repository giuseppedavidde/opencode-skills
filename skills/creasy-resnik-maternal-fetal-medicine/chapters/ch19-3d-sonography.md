# Chapter 19: Clinical Applications of Three-Dimensional Sonography in Obstetrics

## Core Idea
Three- and four-dimensional ultrasound — volume sonography — is an adjunct to two-dimensional imaging, not a replacement. Its central advantage is that a single automated volume sweep stores all three orthogonal planes, so the examination can be reworked, resliced, and rendered after the patient has left, removing interobserver variation in obtaining the plane. Routine 3D screening is not recommended; targeted acquisition is triggered by a 2D finding or a clinical indication. The most diagnostically useful output is usually the multiplanar display rather than the surface-rendered image that attracts attention.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Volume acquisition and display model.** A mechanical 3D probe performs an automated sweep at constant speed; data are displayed in X, Y, and Z planes with a reference dot for multiplanar localization, alongside an optional rendered image. Measurements can be made from multiplanar images and, on some systems, from rendered images.

**Tool-selection matrix (Table 19-2).** Match the tool to the clinical question:
- Cleft lip and palate — multiplanar (MP), tomographic ultrasound imaging (TUI), surface rendering (SR), electronic scalpel (ES).
- Forehead — MP for angle measurement, SR, ES.
- Orbits — MP, TUI.
- Metopic suture — MP, SR, maximum intensity projection (MIP).
- Cardiac — MP, TUI, STIC, renderings.
- Fetal mandible — MP, TUI.
- Skeletal — MP, MIP, ES.
- Neural tube defects — MP, TUI, ES.
- Brain abnormalities — MP, TUI, SR, inverse mode.

**Rendering and editing modes.** Surface rendering for soft tissue such as face and extremities; maximum intensity projection for bone, curved structures, spine and ribs; volume rendering for skeleton; TUI for sequential slices through a volume; electronic scalpel and inverse mode to delete unwanted echoes; thick-slice scanning for vermis and palate; 3D Doppler algorithms for brain, heart, and placenta.

**Standardized cleft lip/palate protocol.** 2D imaging first, then 3D volume acquisition in the sagittal plane centered on the palate or the axial plane centered on the maxilla, followed by multiplanar display and reconstruction. The fetus ideally lies supine with the face toward the anterior uterine wall. The "flipped face" view examines jaw, lips, alveolar ridge, palate, and maxilla in sequence from below; the reverse-face view examines the palate coronally from behind.

**STIC (spatiotemporal image correlation).** An automated slow sweep acquires cardiac frames at a rapid rate, producing a 4D cine sequence from one satisfactory volume. The heart can then be rotated in any plane around a cursor dot; in transposition, the parallel great vessels are shown in one plane and the ventriculoarterial connection in another. Gating can be added for motion; real-time 3D echocardiography minimizes the need for cardiac or respiratory gating.

## Key Concepts
- Nuchal translucency can be measured from stored 3D volumes only when the nuchal skin is clearly seen on 2D: reslicing reproduced the measurement in **95%** of sagittal volumes but only **60%** of random volumes.
- Müllerian duct anomalies occur in approximately **3.2%** of the general healthy population, with septate uterus the most common and the highest risk for recurrent first-trimester loss. For 3D detection of septate uterus, sensitivity **98.4%**, specificity **100%**, PPV **100%**, NPV **96%**. The coronal plane allows measurement of intercornual distance, septal length, and depth of the external fundal indentation.
- Interstitial ectopic: the "interstitial line" — a hyperechoic line from the lateral endometrial cavity into the mid-portion of the gestational sac, corresponding to the interstitial fallopian tube — plus a continuous myometrial mantle around the sac is highly specific.
- Retronasal triangle (two frontal processes of the maxilla plus the primary palate) is consistently imaged from **11 + 0 to 13 + 6 weeks**; loss of the triangle signals cleft palate. An absent mandibular gap below the triangle signals micrognathia.
- Acrania is visible at **12 weeks**, anencephaly at **11 weeks** (no osseous structures above the orbit, irrespective of neural tissue present). The "dangling choroid" sign marks first-trimester hydrocephalus; choroid plexus can be measured from **11 weeks**.
- Nasal bone: ideal midsagittal plane at **45°**; reliable demonstration when the acquisition angle is **30–60°**, unsatisfactory outside that range.
- In a series of 460 infants with clefts, almost **37%** had other anomalies — **46%** with cleft palate, **36%** with cleft lip and palate, **13%** with cleft lip alone.
- Palate anatomy: the incisive foramen marks the primary/secondary boundary; the soft palate is about **half** the length of the bony palate. True midline sagittal plane was achieved in only **69.6%** of 618 women by 2D, versus effectively all by 3D manipulation.
- Metopic suture normally fuses by the **32nd week**. All dominant cranial sutures and fontanelles except the sagittal suture and posterior fontanelle can be reliably evaluated by volume acquisition in sagittal and transverse planes; advanced gestation and cephalic presentation reduce visualization.
- Mandibular indices: inferior facial angle (IFA) below **50°** defines retrognathia; mandibular width/maxillary width (MD/MX) ratio below **0.785** defines micrognathia. These require midsagittal and two axial planes.
- One third of cardiac structures, particularly valves, are not well visualized by 3D because of resolution limits; motion artifact limits ungated 3D in the rapid fetal heart.
- Lung volume by VOCAL consistently **underestimates** lung volume compared with MRI, and measurements at **30°** rotation are significantly smaller than at smaller rotation angles.
- Hippocampus-fornix identified bilaterally in **94%** of fetuses beyond **14 weeks**. Cerebellar vermis can be measured from **18 weeks** regardless of fetal position; vermian length and the primary fissure are the key features.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using 3D for primary fetal evaluation.** Routine 3D is not recommended; targeted acquisition follows a 2D finding or clinical indication.
- **Judging the study by the rendered image.** Multiplanar data usually carry the diagnostic information; a pretty surface rendering can conceal a defect.
- **Diagnosing micrognathia on a single apparent sagittal view.** Obliquity of the plane creates false micrognathia; correct by reorienting in multiplanar display.
- **Assuming an absent cavum septi pellucidi means agenesis of the corpus callosum.** The converse implication holds only in one direction.
- **Treating 3D lung volumetry as interchangeable with MRI.** VOCAL underestimates volume, and the rotation angle changes the result.
- **Expecting 3D to resolve valves or a fast heart without gating.** Resolution and motion artifact are real limits.
- **Extrapolating cleft detection performance to low-risk screening.** Reported accuracy comes from high-risk referred populations and has not been shown in unselected screening.
- **Offering 3D for bonding.** Evidence does not show increased bonding over 2D.

## Key Takeaways
- Acquire volumes only when a question exists, then analyze in multiplanar mode before rendering.
- Use the coronal plane for uterine anomalies: 3D separates septate from bicornuate with sensitivity 98.4% and specificity 100%.
- Use the retronasal triangle at 11 + 0 to 13 + 6 weeks for first-trimester cleft palate and micrognathia screening.
- Apply the mandibular thresholds — IFA <50°, MD/MX <0.785 — only from correct midsagittal and axial planes.
- Use MIP for bone and curved structures, surface rendering for soft tissue and extremities, TUI for sequential slices and cardiac sweeps.
- Obtain one good STIC volume rather than struggling with freehand outflow tract sweeps; expect valve detail to remain limited.
- Store still images, not volumes, unless research, teaching, or quality assurance justifies retention.
