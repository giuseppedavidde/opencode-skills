# Chapter 41: Cervical Insufficiency

## Core Idea
Cervical insufficiency is a diagnosis of exclusion, not a synonym for a short cervix. The traditional definition required painless dilation, recurrent second-trimester birth, and absence of other causes, but ultrasound and treatment trials showed that cervical shortening is usually the first step of preterm parturition rather than a structural defect. The chapter therefore separates women with true structural cervical weakness, who benefit from cerclage, from women whose short cervix reflects another mechanism, who are treated medically with progesterone.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Historical diagnostic criteria.** (1) Painless cervical dilation leading to (2) recurrent (3) second-trimester births in the (4) absence of other causes. These criteria are now recognized as unable to separate structural weakness from early preterm parturition.

**Cerclage indication categories (Table 41-1).** History-indicated: historical criteria such as recurrent early preterm births or second-trimester losses, placed at 12 to 14 weeks, prophylactic. Ultrasound-indicated: cervical length under 25 mm before 24 weeks in a singleton with prior spontaneous preterm birth, placed at 14 to 23 weeks, therapeutic or salvage. Physical examination-indicated: cervical changes such as 1 cm or more dilation or prolapsed membranes, placed at 16 to 23 weeks, rescue or emergency. Transabdominal: after a prior history-indicated cerclage that resulted in preterm birth before 33 weeks, placed at 11 to 12 weeks.

**Cervical remodeling model.** Four overlapping phases: softening, ripening (shortening, dilation, change in position), dilation in response to contractions, and postpartum repair.

**Differential diagnosis (Box 41-2).** Maternal or fetal infection, uterine distention (hydramnios, multiple gestation), bleeding or placental disease, other fetal disease causing preterm birth (structural or karyotypic anomaly), and other maternal disease (poorly controlled diabetes). These disorders precede cervical change.

## Key Concepts
- The cervix is predominantly fibrous; smooth muscle content rises from 6.4% in the lower third near the external os to 29% in the upper third near the internal os.
- Women who deliver at term shorten only about 0.36 mm per week until 32 to 34 weeks. Women who later have spontaneous preterm birth begin shortening as early as 16 to 24 weeks and progress faster: 0.96 mm per week with preterm labor and 0.82 mm per week with preterm ruptured membranes.
- Risk factors are acquired (cervical laceration, D&E, D&C, elective abortion, LEEP, cold-knife or laser conization, hysteroscopy, prior second-trimester birth before 28 weeks) or congenital (collagen disorders such as Ehlers-Danlos, uterine anomalies, diethylstilbestrol exposure).
- Diagnosis is clinical: speculum visualization, digital palpation, and provocative suprapubic or fundal pressure or Valsalva to reveal membranes.
- Laboratory tests are not useful. Fetal fibronectin has no role in diagnosis or management. Intra-amniotic infection contraindicates cerclage and must be excluded.
- Only women with three or more early preterm births or second-trimester losses are candidates for history-indicated cerclage.
- History-indicated cerclage is placed at 12 to 14 weeks; ultrasound-indicated at 14 to 23 weeks; physical examination-indicated at 16 to 23 weeks; transabdominal at 11 to 12 weeks.
- Ultrasound-indicated cerclage in singleton gestation with prior preterm birth and cervical length under 25 mm reduces birth before 35 weeks from 41% to 28% (RR 0.70) and composite perinatal morbidity and mortality from 25% to 16% (RR 0.64).
- The greatest cerclage benefit is at cervical length 15.9 mm or less: birth before 35 weeks 35.0% with cerclage versus 58.1% without (RR 0.59).
- In singletons without prior preterm birth, cerclage for short cervix showed a nonsignificant 24% reduction in birth before 35 weeks (26% versus 33%, RR 0.76); evidence is insufficient in this group.
- Cerclage does not benefit twins and may increase preterm birth risk in twins with a short cervix.
- A cerclage height of at least 18 mm is associated with 4% preterm birth versus 33% when placed below 18 mm; aim for more than 2 cm above the external os.
- Chorioamnionitis complicates 1% to 2% of ultrasound-indicated cerclages and subclinical intra-amniotic infection about 20% or more of physical examination-indicated cerclages.
- Physical examination-indicated cerclage with a dilated cervix and visible membranes prolongs pregnancy by about 1 month compared with expectant management.
- The cerclage should be removed at 36 weeks if pregnancy is otherwise uncomplicated; remove earlier with pPROM or advanced preterm labor.
- Up to 80% of asymptomatic women with a second-trimester short cervix have contractions; four or more per hour doubles preterm birth risk.

## Anti-patterns
- Placing a cerclage without excluding intra-amniotic infection.
- Using fetal fibronectin or cervical cultures to diagnose cervical insufficiency.
- Offering history-indicated cerclage to a woman with only one prior preterm birth; the threshold is three or more.
- Placing a Shirodkar or McDonald stitch low on the cervix; cerclage height below 18 mm predicts failure.
- Leaving a cerclage in place through pPROM or advanced labor with tension on the stitch.
- Assuming a term delivery after cerclage confirms the diagnosis of cervical insufficiency.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose cervical insufficiency only after excluding infection, uterine distention, bleeding, placental disease, and fetal anomaly.
2. Screen cervical length by transvaginal ultrasound in women with prior spontaneous preterm birth, starting at 16 weeks and repeating every 2 weeks to 23 weeks.
3. Offer ultrasound-indicated cerclage for cervical length under 25 mm with prior preterm birth; expect a 30% relative reduction in birth before 35 weeks.
4. Place the stitch as high as possible, targeting a cerclage height above 2 cm.
5. Resume progesterone prophylaxis in parallel; most women with prior preterm birth should receive it regardless of cervical length.
6. Remove the cerclage at 36 weeks, or earlier for ruptured membranes or advanced labor.
