# Chapter 32: Assessment of Fetal Health

## Core Idea
Antepartum fetal testing is a screening program for fetal hypoxemia and acidosis, not a diagnostic test of fetal well-being. No single modality satisfies all requirements, because normal fetal behavior varies with sleep state, gestational age, and maternal condition. The chapter argues for disorder-specific surveillance built from multiple variables: heart rate monitoring, biophysical profile (BPP), amniotic fluid assessment, and Doppler. The clinical task is to balance the risk of stillbirth from decompensation against the risk of neonatal death and morbidity from iatrogenic preterm delivery.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Ideal fetal monitoring test: nine characteristics.** Broad information across conditions and gestational ages; specificity, sensitivity, and timeliness; low false-positive rate, especially at early gestational ages; high sensitivity for modest compromise; durable negative predictive value (commonly 7 days); multiple variables; detection across mechanisms (asphyxia, poisoning, metabolic disease, anemia); feasibility in inpatient and outpatient settings at modest cost; and measurable benefit in high-risk populations.

**Biophysical profile (five variables, 10 points).** Fetal breathing movements: at least one episode of >30 seconds within 30 minutes; hiccups count. Body or limb movements: at least three discrete movements in 30 minutes. Fetal tone: active extension with rapid return to flexion, or brisk repositioning. Nonstress test (NST): at least two accelerations of at least 15 beats/min lasting at least 15 seconds in a 20-minute window. Amniotic fluid: at least one pocket >2 cm in two planes, free of cord.

**BPP interpretation and perinatal mortality per 1000.** 10/10, 8/8, or 8/10 with normal fluid: no acute intervention, mortality <1/1000. 8/10 with oligohydramnios: chronic compromise, 89/1000. 6/10 with normal fluid: equivocal, about 61/1000; repeat immediately, and if persistent at 6/10 deliver the mature fetus. 4/10: acute asphyxia likely, 91/1000; deliver. 2/10: 125/1000; deliver. 0/10: severe acute asphyxia, 600/1000; immediate delivery if viable.

**Contraction stress test (CST)/oxytocin challenge test.** Establish at least three contractions in 10 minutes. Negative: no decelerations. Positive: late decelerations with at least three consecutive contractions, or with at least 50% of contractions when fewer than three occur in 10 minutes. Equivocal and suspicious categories depend on deceleration frequency and contraction adequacy.

## Key Concepts
- Fetal heart rate falls with gestational age: mean 155 beats/min at 20 weeks, 144 at 30 weeks, and 140 at term, reflecting rising vagal tone. Normal fetuses vary by 20 beats/min around these values.
- NST reactivity: two accelerations of at least 15 beats/min lasting at least 15 seconds in 20 minutes. For fetuses below 32 weeks, accelerations of 10 beats/min lasting 10 seconds are acceptable if variability is normal.
- A 30-minute NST is nonreactive in 10–12% of third-trimester fetuses; this falls below 6% by 40 minutes. The usual cause is a sleep cycle longer than average; a nonreactive test with preserved variability and no decelerations is not evidence of compromise.
- Fetal behavioral state 1F (quiet sleep) produces a nonreactive NST and usually resolves by 40 minutes, though intervals up to 2 hours are possible; state 2F (active sleep) produces accelerations.
- Amniotic fluid: deepest vertical pocket <2 cm suggests oligohydramnios; >8 cm suggests polyhydramnios; 8–12 cm usually reflects idiopathic polyhydramnios, diabetes with macrosomia, or structural anomalies. Pockets >12 cm are more often associated with aneuploidy and neurologic lesions.
- In randomized trials, the deepest vertical pocket produced fewer diagnoses of oligohydramnios and fewer inductions than the amniotic fluid index, with similar neonatal intensive care admission, Apgar scores, umbilical artery pH <7.1, and nonreassuring heart rates.
- Umbilical artery Doppler reflects placental vascular resistance; abnormalities progress from reduced end-diastolic flow to absent to reversed end-diastolic flow. Absent or reversed end-diastolic flow identifies severe placental insufficiency.
- Middle cerebral artery Doppler shows brain sparing, with reduced resistance and increased diastolic velocity. The cerebroplacental ratio compares middle cerebral artery and umbilical artery resistance.
- Ductus venosus Doppler: normal is a four-phase waveform with a positive a-wave. Absent or reversed a-wave indicates cardiac decompensation and is a short-term predictor of adverse outcome. Venous Doppler and BPP are not strictly concordant.
- Deterioration sequence in severe growth restriction: reduced umbilical artery end-diastolic flow, then absent or reversed flow (completed in about 20% of severely growth-restricted fetuses), then flattening of the NST, reversed ductus venosus a-wave, loss of breathing, loss of tone, and finally loss of all movement.
- Timing: ACOG guidelines suggest starting surveillance at 32–34 weeks. Testing frequency ranges from every 2–6 weeks for growth review to continuous monitoring for severe growth restriction with abnormal venous Doppler. Earlier and more frequent testing is used when the threshold of viability is 24 weeks and growth restriction is severe.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a nonreactive NST as fetal compromise without excluding sleep, drugs, or a central nervous system anomaly.
- Using a BPP score of 6/10 as an indication for immediate delivery without repeat testing in a mature fetus, or as reassurance in an immature fetus.
- Applying the NST acceleration criteria of term fetuses to a 28-week fetus without allowing the 10-beat/10-second threshold.
- Using the amniotic fluid index rather than the deepest vertical pocket for surveillance, and acting on the resulting excess diagnoses of oligohydramnios.
- Ordering a full BPP when the NST is reactive and the only question is amniotic fluid volume.
- Ignoring the clinical context: a BPP of 8/10 in a growth-restricted fetus with absent end-diastolic flow is not equivalent to 8/10 in a normally grown fetus.
- Continuing a fixed testing interval in a fetus whose Doppler or fluid status is deteriorating.

## Key Takeaways
1. Choose the test by the mechanism of risk: NST and BPP for acute hypoxemia, Doppler for placental insufficiency, and serial fluid assessment for chronic compromise.
2. Interpret the BPP with the fluid volume: 8/10 with oligohydramnios is chronic compromise, not a normal score.
3. Use 30 minutes for the NST and 40 minutes before calling it nonreactive; back it with a BPP.
4. Start surveillance at 32–34 weeks for most high-risk conditions; start earlier and test more often for severe growth restriction or abnormal Doppler.
5. Act on a persistent 6/10 or any score of 4/10 or less; a score of 0/10 requires immediate delivery if viable.
