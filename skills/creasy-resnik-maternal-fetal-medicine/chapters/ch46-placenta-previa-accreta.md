# Chapter 46: Placenta Previa, Placenta Accreta, Abruptio Placentae, and Vasa Previa

## Core Idea
These four conditions share only one feature: hemorrhage in late pregnancy. Their etiology, diagnosis, and management are distinct, and ultrasound has replaced clinical guesswork in distinguishing them. The chapter's central argument is that precise anatomic diagnosis before bleeding occurs allows planned delivery, and that planned delivery by an experienced multidisciplinary team is what prevents most maternal deaths.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Anatomic classification by transvaginal ultrasound.** Complete previa: placenta completely covers the internal os. Marginal previa: placental edge within 2.5 cm of the internal os. Low-lying placenta: extends into the lower segment but precise limits undefined, or diagnosed before the third trimester. A distance greater than 2 to 3 cm from the cervix carries no increased bleeding risk. Definitive diagnosis should be avoided before the third trimester because at least 90% of early low-lying placentas resolve.

**Accreta invasive spectrum.** Accreta: trophoblastic invasion beyond Nitabuch's fibrinoid layer. Increta: invasion into myometrium. Percreta: invasion beyond the serosa, sometimes involving bladder or pelvic organs.

**Obstetric hemorrhage protocol (Box 46-1).** Immediate type and cross-match, hematocrit, coagulation studies including fibrinogen, and a wall clot that should clot within 6 minutes; four units of type-specific or O-negative blood made available; laboratory stays four units ahead; two units of fresh-frozen plasma thawed and a 10-pack of platelets available.

**Management algorithm for abruption.** Individualize by maternal and fetal status and gestational age. Maternal or fetal compromise mandates immediate delivery, usually by cesarean. After 34 weeks, do not delay delivery. Between 20 and 34 weeks with a stable mother and fetus, conservative management with betamethasone may be attempted.

## Key Concepts
- Bleeding in the second half of pregnancy complicates about 6% of pregnancies. Placenta previa explains 7%, significant abruption 13%, and the remaining 80% is early labor, local genital tract lesions, or no identifiable source.
- Placenta previa affects about 1 in 200 pregnancies (0.5%) at term. A single prior cesarean or prior previa raises the risk to as high as 5%. Risk is 2% after age 35 and 5% after age 40.
- The classic presentation is painless third-trimester bleeding, but up to 10% have no bleeding until labor. All painless bleeding after 20 weeks is treated as previa until excluded.
- Transvaginal ultrasound is superior to transabdominal and is safe. Digital or speculum examination is contraindicated until previa is excluded.
- Delivery is recommended at or just after 36 weeks for placenta previa; amniocentesis for lung maturity is usually not worth the bleeding risk.
- Cesarean delivery is required when the placenta lies within 2 cm of the cervix; a placenta more than 2 cm from the os permits labor.
- In previa with one prior cesarean, accreta risk is 10% to 25%; with two or more prior cesareans it exceeds 50%.
- Ultrasound for accreta has sensitivity about 0.8 and specificity 0.95. In 80 women with previa studied with color Doppler, 16 were diagnosed antenatally and 14 confirmed histologically (sensitivity 0.82, specificity 0.97).
- Accreta is best managed by planned cesarean hysterectomy at 34 to 35 weeks after maternal corticosteroids, with a multidisciplinary team; predelivery diagnosis reduces blood loss and morbidity.
- Conservative accreta management (placenta left in situ, with or without methotrexate) led to 18 primary and 18 delayed hysterectomies among 167 women, severe morbidity in 10, and one death from methotrexate.
- Abruption complicates about 1% of pregnancies; abruption causing fetal death occurs in about 1 in 420 deliveries. Twenty percent of those fetal deaths occur after hospital presentation, and 30% within 2 hours of admission.
- The incidence of abruption peaks at 24 to 26 weeks. About 10% of all preterm births result from abruption.
- The strongest risk factor for abruption is a prior abruption, with up to a 20-fold increase; recurrence after two prior abruptions is 25%.
- Chronic hypertension raises abruption risk fivefold and eightfold with superimposed preeclampsia. Smoking raises risk up to 2.5-fold with a dose-response effect.
- More than 50% of the placenta must be separated before fetal death is likely. About 10% of abruptions are concealed.
- At least 50% of abruptions produce no ultrasound findings; ultrasound is used mainly to exclude previa.
- Coagulopathy develops in about 10% of abruptions, usually related to severity, fetal demise, or massive hemorrhage.
- Posterior abruption may present with severe back pain alone. Abruption should be considered in apparent idiopathic preterm labor.
- Vasa previa carries a 10% risk of membrane rupture before labor and high fetal mortality; planned delivery is at 34 to 35 weeks after betamethasone, with cervical length surveillance weekly from 30 weeks.

## Anti-patterns
- Performing a digital or speculum examination for third-trimester bleeding before excluding previa by ultrasound.
- Diagnosing placenta previa definitively before the third trimester; most low-lying placentas resolve.
- Relying on ultrasound to confirm abruption; half are invisible on scan.
- Using tocolytics routinely in abruption.

## Key Takeaways
1. Treat any painless bleeding after 20 weeks as placenta previa until transvaginal ultrasound excludes it.
2. Classify the placenta by distance from the internal os and plan cesarean delivery when it lies within 2 cm, at about 36 weeks.
3. Screen every previa for accreta with ultrasound and, when needed, MRI; the combination of previa and two or more prior cesareans exceeds 50% risk.
4. Plan accreta delivery at 34 to 35 weeks after steroids with a multidisciplinary team and blood bank preparation.
5. In abruption, stabilize the mother, monitor the fetus continuously, check coagulation and fibrinogen, and deliver immediately for maternal or fetal compromise or after 34 weeks.
6. Diagnose vasa previa antenatally by ultrasound and deliver by planned cesarean at 34 to 35 weeks.
