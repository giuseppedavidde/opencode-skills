# Chapter 58: Respiratory Diseases in Pregnancy

## Core Idea

Pregnancy increases minute ventilation by about 50% through a larger tidal volume, producing a compensated respiratory alkalosis with a PaCO2 below 30 mm Hg and a slightly higher PaO2. This adaptation narrows the reserve available when pulmonary disease develops, so a "normal" blood gas in a dyspneic pregnant woman may signal serious compromise. Most diagnostic tests and medications used for respiratory disease are safe in pregnancy, and management should follow nonpregnant algorithms. Asthma is the commonest serious medical condition, and its control directly determines obstetric outcome.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Respiratory physiologic changes.** Tidal volume rises from about 500 to 700 mL with no change in respiratory rate; minute ventilation rises from 7.5 to 10.5 L/min; functional residual capacity falls 18%; residual volume falls about 20% (1200 to 1000 mL); vital capacity is unchanged. PaCO2 falls below 30 mm Hg, PaO2 rises to 101 to 104 mm Hg, bicarbonate falls, and pH stays about 7.40. The diaphragm rises 4 cm, the transverse chest diameter increases 2 cm, and the subcostal angle opens from 68 to 103 degrees.
- **Asthma severity and control classification (Table 58-2, NAEPP).** Well controlled: symptoms and short-acting beta-agonist use 2 days per week or less, nighttime awakening less than twice per month, no activity limitation, FEV1 or peak flow above 80%. Not well controlled: symptoms more than 2 days per week but not daily, awakening more than twice per month, minor limitation, FEV1 60% to 80%. Very poorly controlled: daily symptoms, awakening more than once per week, some limitation, FEV1 below 60%.
- **Step-care asthma therapy (Table 58-5).** Step 1: no controller. Step 2: low-dose inhaled corticosteroid (alternative leukotriene receptor antagonist, theophylline, or cromolyn). Step 3: medium-dose inhaled corticosteroid, deliberately chosen over low-dose corticosteroid plus long-acting beta-agonist because of limited LABA safety data in pregnancy. Step 4: medium-dose corticosteroid plus LABA. Step 5: high-dose corticosteroid plus LABA. Step 6: high-dose corticosteroid plus LABA plus oral prednisone. Step up one step for not well controlled and two steps for very poorly controlled.
- **Tuberculosis screening and treatment.** Screen high-risk women with intradermal purified protein derivative; a reaction of 10 mm or more after BCG is treated as infection. Molecular tests (MTD, Amplicor) detect Mycobacterium tuberculosis with sensitivity above 95% in smear-positive and 40% to 77% in smear-negative specimens. Latent infection: isoniazid 300 mg/day starting after the first trimester for 6 to 9 months, with pyridoxine to prevent neuropathy. A 12-week weekly isoniazid-rifapentine regimen under direct observation is an alternative.
- **Pneumonia assessment.** Streptococcus pneumoniae is the commonest bacterial pathogen and Haemophilus influenzae the second. Use American Thoracic Society or British Thoracic Society admission criteria, which avoided admission in 25% and 66% of pregnant patients respectively without complications.

## Key Concepts

- Asthma exacerbation rates in pregnancy rise with severity: 12.6% with mild disease (2.3% hospitalized), 25.7% with moderate disease (6.8% hospitalized), and 51.9% with severe disease (26.9% hospitalized).
- Asthma course in pregnancy: about 23% improve and 30% worsen.
- Untreated or undertreated asthma is associated with preeclampsia, preterm birth, low birth weight, growth restriction, perinatal mortality, and first-trimester exacerbation-related congenital malformations.
- Starting an inhaled corticosteroid reduces acute exacerbations from 17% to 4%.
- Oral prednisone 40 to 60 mg/day in one or two divided doses is recommended for asthma exacerbations.
- Theophylline is an alternative or adjunct, with a target serum concentration of 5 to 12 µg/mL in pregnancy.
- Pneumonia complicates about 1 in 367 deliveries. Preterm labor occurred in 11 of 21 gestations in one series, and mean birth weight was 2770 g versus 3173 g in controls.
- Varicella infects 0.7 per 1000 pregnancies; varicella pneumonia develops in 5.2% of pregnant women with varicella and carries 35% to 40% maternal mortality, versus 11% to 17% in nonpregnant adults. Smoking and more than 100 skin lesions increase risk. Acyclovir 7.5 mg/kg intravenously every 8 hours is recommended.
- Pneumocystis jiroveci pneumonia occurs when CD4 is below 200 cells/mm³, is the leading cause of pregnancy-associated AIDS death, and had 50% mortality in a review of 22 pregnant cases; 59% required mechanical ventilation with 31% survival.
- Tuberculosis kills more than 1 million women per year, and 646 million women and girls are infected worldwide. In 1991 New York City, 33% of cases were resistant to at least one drug and 19% were resistant to both isoniazid and rifampin.
- Cystic fibrosis affects 1 in 3200 white live births, with median survival of 37 years in 2006; 209 women with CF were pregnant in 2006. Pre-pregnancy pulmonary status is the strongest predictor of outcome, and progressive deterioration with hypoxemia despite maximal therapy is an indication to consider termination.
- Restrictive lung disease with pulmonary hypertension may carry mortality as high as 50%.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting a PaCO2 of 40 mm Hg as normal in a dyspneic pregnant woman; it indicates hypoventilation relative to pregnancy.
- Stopping inhaled corticosteroids in pregnancy for fear of fetal harm.
- Using a long-acting beta-agonist without an inhaled corticosteroid.
- Withholding chest radiography when pneumonia is suspected; the fetal dose is negligible.
- Treating latent tuberculosis only after delivery in a woman at high risk of progression.
- Attributing third-trimester dyspnea to pregnancy alone and missing pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, or cardiomyopathy.
- Delaying acyclovir in varicella pneumonia.

## Key Takeaways

1. Interpret arterial blood gases against pregnancy norms: PaCO2 below 30 mm Hg and PaO2 of 101 to 104 mm Hg are expected.
2. Continue inhaled corticosteroids throughout pregnancy; do not step down controller therapy during pregnancy if control is fragile.
3. Step up one level for not well controlled asthma and two levels for very poorly controlled disease; use prednisone 40 to 60 mg/day for exacerbations.
4. Vaccinate against influenza and varicella before or during pregnancy when indicated, and treat varicella pneumonia with intravenous acyclovir.
5. Screen high-risk women for tuberculosis with PPD and treat active disease with a rifampin-based regimen; give isoniazid prophylaxis after the first trimester.
6. Use standard admission criteria for pneumonia and treat empirically for pneumococcus and Haemophilus influenzae.
7. Counsel women with cystic fibrosis before conception, optimize nutrition and pulmonary function, and monitor closely for postpartum deterioration.
