# Chapter 29: First-Trimester Imaging

## Core Idea
Between 11 and 13+6 weeks, a structured anatomic survey can detect a defined set of major anomalies before the second-trimester scan. The same window is used for nuchal translucency (NT) measurement, which must follow strict technical rules to be reproducible. Detection depends on knowing which structures are normally developed at this age, which anomalies should be reliably excluded, and which findings are physiologic variants.

## Frameworks Introduced
**First-trimester anatomy protocol.** Views of the head (cranium), transverse brain (midline falx), transverse thorax with heart, abdomen (stomach, bladder, cord insertion), and extremities (long bones, hands, feet). A standardized protocol achieves complete anatomy in 95% of fetuses at 12–13 weeks.

**NT quality criteria.** Crown-rump length 45–84 mm (11–13+6 weeks); true midsagittal view showing the nasal tip, third and fourth ventricles, and palate, without the orbit or zygoma; image magnified so the head, neck, and upper thorax fill the screen; caliper movement in 0.1-mm increments; head in line with the spine and neck neutral; whole NT line visible and crisp; amnion seen separately; crossbar caliper placed perpendicular to the long axis on the inner border of the nuchal line; largest technically good measurement reported; average of three crown-rump measurements reported.

## Key Concepts
- Physiologic midgut herniation is normal until the crown-rump length reaches 45–54 mm; herniation is abnormal after 61 mm. Liver in the cord base is always abnormal.
- The fetal stomach is visible by 10 weeks; the falx and choroid plexus by 10 weeks; absence of lateralization suggests holoprosencephaly. Cranial ossification begins at 11 weeks and is complete by 14 weeks.
- The fetal bladder is seen by 12 weeks. Megacystis is a bladder length ≥7 mm and is associated with aneuploidy and obstructive uropathy. Genitalia are not fully developed until the end of the 14th week.
- Anomalies that should be reliably detected: acrania/anencephaly, alobar holoprosencephaly, omphalocele, gastroschisis, megacystis, and gross limb abnormalities. Myelomeningocele appears as an acorn-shaped cranium with an irregular spine.
- Anomalies that may escape first-trimester detection: agenesis of the corpus callosum, cerebellar vermian abnormalities, coarctation of the aorta, and ventricular hypoplasia, because normal development or the pathologic process continues later.
- A normal first-trimester renal appearance does not exclude polycystic kidneys, and normal amniotic fluid does not exclude a significant renal anomaly.
- NT increases with crown-rump length. Combined NT, free β-hCG, and PAPP-A detects 79–90% of trisomy 21 at a 5% screen-positive rate, and about 90% of trisomy 18 at 2%. NT alone detects about 60% of Down syndrome at a 5% screen-positive rate.
- When NT is >3 mm, serum screening adds minimal benefit; when NT is >4 mm, it adds none.
- NT ≥3.5 mm warrants detailed sonography and fetal echocardiography even with a normal karyotype and microarray. Echocardiography is recommended at the 95th or 99th percentile, depending on the desired performance.
- A nuchal cord is present in 5–10% and adds about 0.8 mm to the measurement; measure above and below the cord and average, which is the only situation in which NT values are averaged.
- Neck extension increases NT by 0.6 mm; flexion decreases it by 0.4 mm.
- Absent nasal bone in the first trimester is present in 73% of trisomy 21 and 0.5% of euploid fetuses, but absence varies with ethnicity: 2.5% in white, 9.0% in Afro-Caribbean, and 5.0% in southern Asian euploid fetuses. Likelihood ratios for trisomy 21 are 31.3, 8.8, and 14.2, respectively.
- Tricuspid regurgitation is present in about 8% of normal fetuses and 65% of trisomy 21 fetuses. Combining it with NT and PAPP-A is projected to detect 95% at a 5% false-positive rate.
- NT measurement may be infeasible with high maternal body mass index, fibroids, or unfavorable fetal position. Semi-automated measurement is acceptable if image criteria are met and the operator verifies caliper placement.

## Anti-patterns
- Measuring NT before 11 weeks or beyond 13+6 weeks, or with a crown-rump length outside 45–84 mm.
- Measuring in a flexed or extended neck, through the amnion, or with calipers placed inside the lucency.
- Using an abdominal or transvaginal view that does not show the tip of the nose and palate, or including the orbit or zygoma.
- Calling midgut herniation an omphalocele before a crown-rump length of 61 mm.
- Reporting a normal first-trimester anatomy scan as exclusion of all anomalies, especially corpus callosum, cerebellar, and cardiac lesions.
- Treating an isolated NT of 2.5–3.0 mm as diagnostic of aneuploidy, or omitting karyotype when NT is markedly increased.

## Key Takeaways
1. Perform the 11–13+6-week scan with a standardized protocol; expect detection of acrania, holoprosencephaly, abdominal wall defects, megacystis, and gross limb defects.
2. Measure NT only in a true midsagittal plane, neutral neck, with 0.1-mm calipers and the amnion separate.
3. Offer combined NT and biochemistry for aneuploidy screening; add nasal bone, tricuspid regurgitation, or ductus venosus only with appropriate training.
4. Refer NT ≥3.5 mm for detailed anatomy and fetal echocardiography regardless of karyotype.
5. Repeat the anatomic survey in the second trimester; the first-trimester scan cannot exclude later-developing anomalies.
