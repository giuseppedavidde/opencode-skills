# Chapter 43: Clinical Aspects of Normal and Abnormal Labor

## Core Idea
Normal labor is a continuous process, not two separate stages, and its expected duration is longer than the classic Friedman curves suggested. Modern cohort data show a gradual latent-to-active transition, no deceleration phase, and a 5th-percentile dilation rate below 1 cm per hour. Clinical decisions should therefore rest on a graphical record of progress and on defined arrest criteria rather than on rigid historical time limits, because overly early intervention causes cesarean delivery and operative trauma without improving outcome.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Friedman labor framework.** Latent phase from onset of regular contractions to the beginning of the active phase, and active phase from about 3 to 4 cm to full dilation. Historical minimum active-phase rates were 1.2 cm per hour in nulliparas and 1.5 cm per hour in multiparas. Active-phase arrest was defined as no cervical dilation for 2 hours.

**Contemporary labor curve (Zhang, Consortium on Safe Labor).** Active labor lasts about 5.5 hours rather than 2.5 hours, there is no deceleration phase, at least 2 hours without dilation in the active phase is common, and the 5th percentile for dilation is below 1 cm per hour. A 2012 NICHD, SMFM, and ACOG summary concluded that the historical 1.2 and 1.5 cm per hour criteria are no longer valid.

**Prolonged latent phase.** Twenty hours in nulliparas and 14 hours in multiparas (95th percentile); over 12 and 6 hours respectively predicts adverse outcome.

**Arrest criteria used to justify cesarean delivery.** A minimum of 4 hours of oxytocin-augmented labor with a contraction pattern above 200 Montevideo units, or a minimum of 6 hours of oxytocin augmentation if that contraction pattern cannot be achieved. This approach yielded a 92% vaginal delivery rate.

**Bishop pelvic score.** Dilation (0 cm = 0, 1 to 2 cm = 1, 3 to 4 cm = 2, 5 to 6 cm = 3), effacement (0% to 30% = 0, 40% to 50% = 1, 60% to 70% = 2, 80% = 3), station (-3 = 0, -2 = 1, -1 or 0 = 2, +1 or +2 = 3), consistency (firm = 0, medium = 1, soft = 2), and position (posterior = 0, middle = 1, anterior = 2).

**Active management of labor (Dublin protocol).** Admit only in painful labor with complete effacement, ruptured membranes, or bloody show; amniotomy soon after admission; oxytocin if progress is less than 1 cm per hour over 2 hours, starting at 4 mU per minute and increasing by 6 mU per minute every 15 minutes to a maximum of 40 mU per minute and seven contractions per 15 minutes; continuous monitoring only for meconium or after fetal scalp pH; one-to-one nursing.

## Key Concepts
- Prolonged latent phase defined as over 12 hours in nulliparas and over 6 hours in multiparas was associated with more labor abnormalities, cesarean delivery, low Apgar scores, and neonatal resuscitation.
- Protraction disorder is a rate of dilation below 1.2 cm per hour in nulliparas or below 1.5 cm per hour in multiparas. For descent, the rates are 1.0 cm per hour in nulliparas and 2.0 cm per hour in multiparas.
- Arrest disorder is cessation of dilation or descent for 2 hours after normal preceding progress; 45% of arrest disorders were associated with cephalopelvic disproportion.
- Descent from +1/3 to +3/3 station can take up to 3 hours, with another 30 minutes for delivery.
- Elective induction requires a Bishop score of 6 or greater, confirmed dating, a 14-week examination consistent with dates, and ultrasound estimation before 20 weeks.
- Elective induction doubles the cesarean rate, almost entirely in nulliparas with an unfavorable cervix, and increases neonatal respiratory morbidity.
- Failed induction should not be diagnosed before 12 hours of oxytocin stimulation after membrane rupture.
- Starting oxytocin at 2 or 4 mU per minute rather than 1 mU per minute shortens labor by 0.8 and 1.3 hours without changing cesarean or perinatal outcomes.
- Second-stage duration alone does not predict perinatal outcome when fetal distress and traumatic delivery are excluded. Fifty-five percent of women with a second stage of 3 hours or more delivered vaginally, but maternal morbidity (chorioamnionitis, severe lacerations, uterine atony, transfusion) and NICU admission rise.
- Shoulder dystocia occurs in 0.24% to 2.00% of vaginal deliveries (1.5% in 132,098 Consortium deliveries); birth injury occurred in 5.2%, including 64 nerve palsies, with no neonatal deaths. Forty to fifty percent occur under 4000 g, and no risk factor is found in 50%.
- The McRoberts maneuver and suprapubic pressure are the usual first responses; delivery of the posterior shoulder has the highest success rate, and injury risk rises with the number of maneuvers.

## Anti-patterns
- Diagnosing active-phase arrest after only 2 hours of oxytocin; use 4 hours, or 6 hours if 200 Montevideo units cannot be reached, and avoid a rigid 2-hour second-stage limit.
- Inducing labor electively with a Bishop score below 6, especially in a nullipara.
- Using cervical dilation alone without plotting progress against time; partograms reduce prolonged labor and emergency cesarean delivery.
- Blaming the clinician for a permanent brachial plexus palsy; many injuries reflect chronic intrauterine nerve compression, and permanent injury does not prove excessive traction.
- Diagnosing failed induction before 12 hours of adequate oxytocin after membrane rupture.

## Key Takeaways
1. Record labor graphically and judge progress against contemporary curves, not 1950s thresholds.
2. Diagnose active-phase arrest only after 4 hours of adequate oxytocin (or 6 hours if adequate contractions cannot be achieved).
3. Do not impose a fixed second-stage time limit when mother and fetus are stable and descent or rotation is occurring.
4. Require a Bishop score of 6 or more, confirmed dating, and a documented indication before elective induction.
5. Manage shoulder dystocia with McRoberts and suprapubic pressure first, then posterior shoulder delivery, and document the sequence.
6. Use oxytocin augmentation in protraction and arrest disorders after excluding disproportion, with reassessment at 4 to 6 hours.
