# Chapter 8: Endocrinology of Pregnancy

## Core Idea

The fetus, placenta, and mother act as one endocrine unit. The placenta synthesises peptide and steroid hormones without neural connections to either mother or fetus. The chapter's argument is that the placenta is a complete endocrine organ, and that most pregnancy hormones are produced by a two- or three-compartment pathway rather than by a single tissue. The clinical consequences are the luteal-placental shift, the diagnostic use of hCG, and the metabolic adaptation that redirects fuel to the fetus.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Fetoplacental unit model.** Steroid synthesis requires cooperation. The fetus supplies androgen precursors; the placenta supplies sulfatase and aromatase; the mother supplies cholesterol and clearance.

**Luteal-placental shift.** Progesterone production moves from the corpus luteum to the placenta between the 7th and 9th weeks.

**Two-cell, two-gonadotropin analogy for the placenta.** Fetal adrenal DHEAS is 16α-hydroxylated in the fetal liver, converted to DHEA by placental sulfatase, and aromatised to estriol.

**Maternal metabolic adaptation model.** Human chorionic somatomammotropin and placental growth hormone produce insulin resistance, fasting hypoglycemia, and lipolysis, so glucose is reserved for the fetus.

## Key Concepts

- Implantation occurs about 8 to 10 days after ovulation.
- Implantation rate per embryo is 17% to 37% after day-3 transfer and 30% to 50% per good-quality blastocyst.
- hCG peaks at about 100,000 mU/mL at 9 to 10 weeks. The early doubling time is 30.9 ± 3.7 hours.
- An hCG rise of 35% over 2 days gives the best sensitivity and specificity for distinguishing a normal pregnancy from a pregnancy of unknown location.
- A gestational sac should be visible on endovaginal ultrasound when hCG reaches 1100 to 1500 mU/mL. Absence suggests an abnormal or ectopic gestation.
- Removal of the corpus luteum before 7 weeks usually causes abortion. Removal after 9 weeks has little effect. Progesterone support is needed if luteal function fails before 9 to 10 weeks.
- 17α-hydroxyprogesterone is elevated for the first 6 weeks, then falls and becomes undetectable by mid-pregnancy.
- Estriol is the major estrogen of pregnancy and accounts for more than 90% of urinary estrogen. Maternal serum estriol reaches 12 to 20 ng/mL at term.
- About 60% of estradiol-17β and estrone near term derives from fetal androgen precursors; about 40% derives from maternal DHEAS.
- Human chorionic somatomammotropin is a 191-amino-acid, 22-kDa single chain with two disulfide bonds. It has up to 96% homology with growth hormone and about 67% with prolactin.
- The hCS/hGH gene cluster lies on chromosome 17.
- Placental hCS production is 1 to 4 g/day; maternal serum levels are 5 to 15 µg/mL. It is the most abundant placental secretory product.
- Pregnancies with very low or absent hCS have delivered healthy infants, so hCS is not essential.
- Placental growth hormone is secreted continuously, stimulates IGF-1, and suppresses pituitary growth hormone in the second half of pregnancy.
- Maternal prolactin rises to about 125 to 180 ng/mL in the first trimester.
- Pregnancy produces more estrogen than a normally ovulating woman could produce in 150 years.
- Metabolic changes include hyperinsulinemia, insulin resistance, fasting hypoglycemia, hyperlipidemia, and hypoaminoacidemia.
- Human placental lactogen and placental growth hormone are the main drivers of insulin resistance.
- Fetal ovarian steroidogenesis is inactive; fetal Leydig cells produce testosterone in the first trimester under hCG stimulation.

## Anti-patterns

- Interpreting a single hCG value as diagnostic of viable pregnancy. Use the rise and the ultrasound threshold together.
- Teaching that progesterone falls at labor. Levels remain high; the receptor ratio changes.
- Using hCS as a test of fetal well-being. Production correlates with placental mass, not with fetal status.
- Treating a low estriol as placental failure without a differential. Sulfatase deficiency, aromatase deficiency, anencephaly, and fetal demise all lower estriol.
- Assuming placental hormones require neural input. The placenta has none.
- Ignoring the luteal-placental shift when planning early progesterone support.

## Key Takeaways

1. Use the 35% two-day hCG rise and the 1100 to 1500 mU/mL ultrasound threshold together.
2. Support the corpus luteum until 9 to 10 weeks if luteal function is compromised.
3. Read fasting hypoglycemia and postprandial hyperglycemia as expected adaptations, not as disease.
4. Expect hCS to track placental mass rather than fetal health.
5. Interpret low estriol with a differential that includes enzyme deficiency and anencephaly.
6. Remember that placental steroidogenesis is a two-compartment process.
