# Chapter 18: Doppler Ultrasound: Select Fetal and Maternal Applications

## Core Idea
Gray-scale ultrasound shows structure but not hemodynamics. Doppler detects frequency shifts from moving blood, and each vessel has a characteristic flow velocity waveform that can be reduced to ratios — systolic/diastolic (S/D), resistance index (RI), and pulsatility index (PI) — which are angle-independent and comparable across gestations. Four vessels carry most of the clinical value: the umbilical artery for placental impedance, the middle cerebral artery for anemia and brain sparing, the ductus venosus for right-heart pressure and impending decompensation, and the uterine artery for placentation. Doppler findings are markers of risk that direct surveillance; they are not by themselves indications for delivery.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Index definitions (Fig. 18-1).** With PSV, EDV, MDV, and TAPV measured from the waveform:
- S/D = PSV ÷ EDV
- RI = (PSV − EDV) ÷ PSV
- PI = (PSV − EDV) ÷ TAPV

Because these are ratios, absolute velocity calibration is unnecessary, but the insonation angle must still be kept as close to 0° as possible to preserve the systolic and diastolic components.

**Umbilical artery interrogation protocol.** Confirm vessel number by color Doppler around the fetal bladder, angling from the cord insertion toward the pelvis; beware of mistaking both internal iliac arteries for umbilical arteries. Sample a free-floating mid-cord loop, avoid compression between extremities or against the uterine wall, keep the color scale and gain appropriate, and set the gate to sample only the artery unless both artery and vein are intended.

**MCA protocol.** Axial plane through thalami and cavum septi pellucidi; color or power box anterior to the thalami over the circle of Willis; small sample volume as close as possible to the MCA origin; either near or far vessel is acceptable; angle as close to 0° as possible. Measure the highest point of the waveform. PSV falls with distance from the origin, so the sampling site must be standardized.

**Ductus venosus protocol.** Identify the intra-abdominal umbilical vein, follow its sharp posterior turn, and sample at the DV origin before the IVC/right atrial junction; sagittal/oblique parasagittal or cross-sectional abdominal views. Record during fetal rest without breathing.

**Uterine artery protocol.** Place the probe above the inguinal canal, identify the iliac vessels along the pelvic side wall, then rotate medially until the uterine artery is seen crossing the iliac vessels.

## Key Concepts
- Single umbilical artery prevalence ranges from 0.2% to 11% depending on population; in a cohort of 203,240 fetuses and neonates, 0.44% had a single UA and 0.37% an isolated single UA. Single UA carried a **6.77-fold** risk of congenital anomaly and a **15.35-fold** risk of chromosomal abnormality. In chromosomally normal cases the commonest anomalies were genitourinary (6.48%), cardiovascular (6.25%), and musculoskeletal (5.44%).
- Isolated single UA was associated with placental abnormalities (OR **3.63**, 95% CI 3.01–4.39), hydramnios (OR **2.80**, 95% CI 1.42–5.49), and amniocentesis (OR **2.52**, 95% CI 1.82–3.51), plus higher rates of prematurity, growth restriction, and adverse neonatal outcome.
- In normal pregnancy, end-diastolic velocity in the umbilical artery rises progressively as placental vascular impedance falls, so all UA indices decrease across the third trimester.
- In growth restriction, UA waveforms deteriorate in a characteristic sequence: rising S/D, then absent end-diastolic flow, then reversed end-diastolic flow. UA Doppler in small-for-gestational-age pregnancies and preeclampsia reduces perinatal mortality, but the finding is a marker of a high-risk condition requiring further surveillance, not an indication for delivery.
- Reference MCA values (Table 18-1): 1.0 MoM is 25.5 cm/s at 20 weeks and 64.4 cm/s at 40 weeks; the 1.5 MoM threshold is **38.2 cm/s** at 20 weeks and **96.6 cm/s** at 40 weeks.
- Fetal anemia: MCA PSV **>1.5 multiples of the median** predicts moderate or severe anemia with **100% sensitivity** and a **12% false-positive rate** in red-cell alloimmunization; the 1.5 MoM threshold is 38.2 cm/s at 20 weeks and 96.6 cm/s at 40 weeks. Confirm with cordocentesis before transfusion.
- Brain sparing: the cerebroplacental ratio (CPR) = **MCA RI ÷ UA RI**. A falling CPR, a falling MCA PI with rising cerebral end-diastolic velocity, or both indicate redistribution.
- Ductus venosus diameter is ~**0.7 mm** at 18 weeks and ~**1.7 mm** at 40 weeks. Normal flow is triphasic with continuous forward flow, including during atrial systole.
- Reverse or absent DV flow during atrial contraction occurs in approximately **90%** of fetuses with chromosomal anomalies but in only **3.1%** of chromosomally normal fetuses. In chromosomally normal fetuses with increased nuchal translucency, abnormal DV flow identifies an underlying major cardiac defect in **31–63%**.
- In the second and third trimesters, absent or reversed DV flow during atrial systole predicts worse perinatal outcome and is a very strong indicator of fetal demise within **1 week**. In most growth-restricted fetuses, venous flow deterioration precedes biophysical profile deterioration.
- Uterine artery impedance falls with gestation; the initial fall until **24–26 weeks** is attributed to trophoblastic invasion of the spiral arteries, with a later contribution from hormonal effects on arterial elasticity. Impedance is lower on the placental side than on the contralateral side.

## Anti-patterns
- **Mistaking the internal iliac arteries for umbilical arteries.** Both may be seen when the probe angle is wrong; confirm two arteries encircling the bladder.
- **Compressing the cord.** External compression from extremities or the uterine wall changes resistance and distorts the waveform.
- **Measuring MCA PSV away from the origin.** PSV decreases with distance, so a non-standardized site produces values that cannot be compared with the nomogram.
- **Sampling during fetal breathing.** Peak velocity and waveform shape become highly variable and unreliable.
- **Applying excessive transducer pressure to the head.** Apparent end-diastolic flow falls, mimicking increased resistance.
- **Using an abnormal UA Doppler as a delivery trigger.** It identifies a high-risk state; management is surveillance and delivery timing in context, not the index alone.
- **Reading absent or reversed DV atrial flow as merely abnormal.** It is a strong predictor of demise within a week and should prompt urgent escalation.
- **Assuming a normal umbilical artery Doppler excludes fetal compromise.** Brain sparing can occur with preserved UA indices; the CPR and MCA PI add information.

## Key Takeaways
- Document the three-vessel cord routinely; a single UA warrants anomaly survey and consideration of karyotype given the 6.77-fold and 15.35-fold risks.
- Use MCA PSV >1.5 MoM as the anemia threshold (38.2 cm/s at 20 weeks, 96.6 cm/s at 40 weeks) and confirm with cordocentesis before transfusion.
- Calculate CPR as MCA RI ÷ UA RI and trend it with MCA PI when assessing brain sparing.
- Grade UA deterioration as rising S/D → absent end-diastolic flow → reversed end-diastolic flow, and escalate surveillance with each step.
- Sample the DV at its origin during fetal rest; absent or reversed atrial systolic flow predicts demise within 1 week.
- Interpret uterine artery Doppler as evidence about placentation, not as a proven outcome intervention; impedance should fall by 24–26 weeks and be lower on the placental side.
