# Chapter 45: Stillbirth

## Core Idea
Stillbirth is defined as fetal death at 20 weeks or more and remains one of the most common adverse pregnancy outcomes, with striking racial and geographic disparities. Most cases have identifiable risk factors, and a structured evaluation yields a probable or possible cause in two thirds of cases. Because recurrence risk is substantially elevated, the chapter links etiologic diagnosis to surveillance and delivery planning in the next pregnancy.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Diagnostic evaluation hierarchy.** Four tests should be offered in every stillbirth: placental examination, fetal postmortem examination, cytogenetic analysis, and Kleihauer-Betke testing for fetal-to-maternal hemorrhage (ideally before induction). Further testing is then directed by those results and by clinical circumstances: antiphospholipid antibodies, alloimmune antibody screen, glucose or hemoglobin A1c, parvovirus serology, syphilis screen, and inherited thrombophilia only in selected cases.

**Recurrence-risk framework.** A prior stillbirth confers about a sixfold increase in risk overall, with subtype-specific gradients: 10-fold for stillbirth at 20 to 28 weeks, 2.5-fold after 29 weeks, 12-fold for intrapartum stillbirth, and 4-fold for antepartum stillbirth.

**Subsequent-pregnancy surveillance plan (Box 45-4).** Serial ultrasound from 28 weeks for growth, fetal movement counting from 28 weeks, antepartum fetal surveillance from 32 weeks or 1 to 2 weeks before the gestational age of the previous stillbirth, and elective induction at 39 weeks or earlier with documented lung maturity.

**Delivery protocol (Box 45-3).** Dilation and evacuation for uterine size 13 to 22 weeks with doxycycline 100 mg before and 200 mg after, or metronidazole 500 mg twice daily for 5 days, plus misoprostol 200 micrograms in the posterior fornix 4 hours before or laminaria. Induction: misoprostol 200 to 400 micrograms vaginally or orally every 4 hours when uterine size is under 28 weeks, and 25 to 50 micrograms every 4 hours when over 28 weeks.

## Key Concepts
- An estimated 3.2 million stillbirths at 28 weeks or more occur worldwide each year; 98% are in low- and middle-income countries.
- In the United States, stillbirth occurs in 1 in 160 pregnancies, about 26,000 per year, with an overall rate of 6.2 per 1000 births.
- The US rate for non-Hispanic black women is 10.73 per 1000 versus 4.81 for non-Hispanic white women, a 2.2-fold difference; the Hispanic rate is 5.29.
- Maternal age shows a U-shaped risk: 13.12 per 1000 under age 15 and 13.02 at 45 or older, versus 5.4 at 30 to 34 years. Relative risk is 1.32 at 35 to 39 years and 1.88 at 40 or older.
- Stillbirth is 2.8-fold higher in twins and 4.8-fold higher in triplets than in singletons.
- Singleton pregnancies conceived by IVF or ICSI have more than four times the stillbirth risk (OR 4.44).
- A prior preterm or small-for-gestational-age birth confers about a fivefold increase (OR 5.0).
- Overweight raises stillbirth odds 1.47 and obesity 2.07; obesity raises risk 3.5- to 4.6-fold after 37 weeks.
- Smoking raises the odds 36% (OR 1.36), and cessation between pregnancies restores the risk toward that of nonsmokers.
- A prior cesarean delivery is associated with a 1.3-fold increase in antepartum stillbirth.
- In the SCRN study of 512 stillbirths, placental histology was positive in 52.3%, postmortem in 31.4%, and karyotype in 9.0%; at least one was positive in 66.4%. Second-trimester D&E has a 4% complication rate versus 29% for induction.
- Other SCRN yields: anticardiolipin antibodies 4.8%, lupus anticoagulant 3.2%, fetomaternal hemorrhage screen 4.6%, alloimmune antibody screen 3.6%, glucose or hemoglobin A1c 2.9%, parvovirus IgM 2.0%, syphilis 0.4%.
- In a Dutch series of 1025 stillbirths, placental examination identified a cause in 95.7%, postmortem in 72.6%, and cytogenetics in 29.0%. Kleihauer-Betke was positive in 11.9% but established cause in only 1.3%.
- About 50% of stillbirths are associated with fetal growth restriction.
- Between 80% and 90% of women enter spontaneous labor within 2 weeks of fetal death. Consumptive coagulopathy usually occurs after 4 weeks; coagulation abnormalities develop in 3% to 4% over 4 to 8 weeks.
- Cesarean delivery for fetal death should be limited to maternal indications. With a prior classic incision, repeat cesarean is appropriate because labor carries a 1% to 12% rupture risk.
- Inherited thrombophilia testing is not routine; it may be considered with severe placental pathology, growth restriction, or a personal history of thrombosis.

## Anti-patterns
- Omitting placental histology or autopsy because the family declines; these are the highest-yield tests and should be offered by default.
- Attributing stillbirth to "unexplained" before completing placental, postmortem, and cytogenetic evaluation.
- Ordering inherited thrombophilia panels routinely; results rarely change management and prophylaxis is unproven.
- Performing cesarean delivery for fetal death without a maternal indication.
- Waiting for spontaneous labor beyond 4 weeks without surveillance for consumptive coagulopathy.
- Failing to offer bereavement support and a follow-up results visit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define stillbirth as fetal death at 20 weeks or more and record gestational age and cause precisely.
2. Offer placental histology, postmortem examination, karyotype, and Kleihauer-Betke testing in every case.
3. Choose D&E for 13 to 22 weeks when feasible, and misoprostol induction otherwise, with dose reduced after 28 weeks.
4. Counsel a sixfold recurrence risk and stratify by gestational age of the prior loss.
5. In the next pregnancy, start growth scans and kick counts at 28 weeks, antepartum testing at 32 weeks or 1 to 2 weeks before the prior loss, and plan delivery at 39 weeks.
6. Modify risk before conception: stop smoking, normalize body mass index to 18.5 to 24.9, and treat hypertension, diabetes, and antiphospholipid syndrome.
