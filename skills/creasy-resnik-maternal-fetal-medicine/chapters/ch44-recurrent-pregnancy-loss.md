# Chapter 44: Recurrent Pregnancy Loss

## Core Idea
Recurrent pregnancy loss (RPL) is a real disease process, not a statistical accident: the observed frequency of 1% for three consecutive losses exceeds the 0.4% predicted by chance, and prognosis depends heavily on prior outcomes. Only a few bona fide causes are established, and a cause is found in less than half of couples. Evaluation should therefore target the treatable causes, avoid unproven testing, and include psychological support.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Definition (American Society for Reproductive Medicine).** Two or more failed pregnancies, not necessarily consecutive, with focus on losses before 20 weeks. The chapter distinguishes early pregnancy loss (under 10 weeks) from early fetal death (10 to 20 weeks).

**Antiphospholipid antibody syndrome (APS) criteria.** Clinical criteria: deep venous or arterial thrombosis, or characteristic obstetric complications. Laboratory criteria: medium to high titers of IgG or IgM anticardiolipin antibodies, IgG or IgM anti-beta-2-glycoprotein-1 antibodies at or above the 99th percentile, or lupus anticoagulant on two or more occasions at least 12 weeks apart. At least one clinical and one laboratory criterion are required.

**APS obstetric criteria.** At least one fetal death at 10 weeks or later; at least one preterm birth before 35 weeks from severe preeclampsia or placental insufficiency; or at least three consecutive spontaneous abortions before 10 weeks with genetic, anatomic, and endocrine causes excluded.

**Structured evaluation checklist.** Genetic: parental karyotypes, karyotype of miscarriage specimens, placental histology for trophoblast inclusions. Endocrine: TSH, thyroperoxidase and thyroglobulin antibodies, prolactin. Thrombophilia: antiphospholipid antibodies if clinical criteria are met; inherited thrombophilia testing only with personal or first-degree relative venous thromboembolism. Anatomic: hysterosalpingogram, sonohysterogram, hysteroscopy, or three-dimensional ultrasound. Counseling: caffeine, smoking, alcohol, weight, psychological support.

## Key Concepts
- Miscarriage affects about 50% of pregnancies when conception through embryonic development is counted; 5% of couples have two miscarriages and 1% have three or more.
- Cytogenetic abnormalities are found in 50% to 60% of spontaneous abortion specimens: autosomal trisomy 60%, monosomy X 20%, polyploidy 20%.
- Trisomy risk rises from 2% to 3% in a woman's twenties to 25% or more in her forties.
- Spontaneous abortion rates by maternal age: under 12% at 20 to 29 years, 15% at 30 to 34, 24.6% at 35 to 39, 51% at 40 to 44, and 93.4% above 44.
- Compared with women under 35, the adjusted odds ratio for spontaneous abortion is 2.0 at 35 to 39 years and 2.4 at 40 years or older; for chromosomal abnormality it is 4.0 and 9.9 respectively.
- Unexplained RPL carries about a 75% chance of a successful future pregnancy; untreated APS carries a pregnancy failure rate up to 90%.
- Between 5% and 15% of women with recurrent spontaneous abortion have antiphospholipid antibodies, versus 2% to 5% of the general obstetric population.
- Lupus anticoagulant has the most consistent association with fetal loss (odds ratios 3.0 to 4.8).
- Heparin plus low-dose aspirin gives a live birth rate of 74.3% versus 55.8% with aspirin alone in APS (RR 1.3, number needed to treat 5.6).
- Unfractionated heparin and low-molecular-weight heparin appear equally effective in APS; either is acceptable.
- There is no proven benefit of anticoagulation for inherited thrombophilia in RPL, and inherited thrombophilia testing does not change management in early recurrent loss.
- Factor V Leiden is not associated with first-trimester miscarriage but is associated with two or more second- or third-trimester losses (OR 10.7) and recurrent stillbirth (OR 7.83).
- Progesterone supplementation reduced pregnancy loss in women with three or more consecutive losses (OR 0.38).
- Bromocriptine improves live birth rates in RPL with hyperprolactinemia.
- Hysteroscopic resection of a cavity-distorting submucous myoma reduced miscarriage from 21.7% to 0% and raised live birth from 23.3% to 52.0%; non-distorting myomas had no effect.
- There are no randomized trials validating metroplasty for a septate uterus, and open metroplasty is rarely recommended for bicornuate or didelphys uteri.
- Obesity with body mass index 25 or greater raises miscarriage risk (OR 1.67); recurrent early miscarriage is more common with body mass index above 30.
- Preimplantation genetic screening is not indicated for RPL; it does not improve live birth or reduce miscarriage.

## Anti-patterns
- Ordering a full inherited thrombophilia panel in every couple with early recurrent loss; results do not change management.
- Treating inherited thrombophilia with heparin outside APS; no benefit and potential harm from heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, osteoporosis, and fracture.
- Offering preimplantation genetic screening as a treatment for RPL.
- Attributing RPL to a septate uterus without correction, or correcting a bicornuate uterus surgically.
- Overlooking the psychological burden; depression, anxiety, guilt, and post-traumatic stress disorder are common.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use the ASRM definition: two or more failed pregnancies, not necessarily consecutive.
2. Test for antiphospholipid antibodies when clinical criteria are met and treat confirmed APS with heparin plus low-dose aspirin.
3. Obtain karyotype of the products of conception and placental histology whenever possible.
4. Screen with TSH, thyroid antibodies, and prolactin; treat overt thyroid disease and hyperprolactinemia.
5. Image the uterine cavity by sonohysterography or three-dimensional ultrasound and resect cavity-distorting lesions.
6. Counsel on lifestyle modification, provide psychological support, and quote a 75% success rate for unexplained RPL.
