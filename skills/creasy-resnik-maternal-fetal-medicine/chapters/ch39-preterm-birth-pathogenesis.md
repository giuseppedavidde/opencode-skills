# Chapter 39: Pathogenesis of Spontaneous Preterm Birth

## Core Idea
Preterm birth (PTB) is a syndrome, not a single disease. Term and preterm parturition share a final common pathway of increased uterine contractility, cervical ripening, and membrane rupture, but in PTB that pathway is activated pathologically. The chapter replaces the older spontaneous-versus-indicated taxonomy with a phenotype-based classification and maps each phenotype to a mechanism: infection, decidual bleeding, overdistension, cervical remodeling, stress, and placental abnormality.

## Frameworks Introduced

**GAPPS phenotypic classification.** PTB is categorized on three axes: (1) the maternal, placental, or fetal condition present; (2) presence or absence of signs of initiated parturition (cervical shortening, pPROM, contractions, dilation, bleeding); (3) the pathway to delivery, spontaneous or caregiver-initiated. This system includes births at 37 0/7 to 38 6/7 weeks, so reported rates run about 28% higher than under the older definition.

**Traditional classification.** PTB is spontaneous or indicated; spontaneous PTB is further divided into preterm labor with intact membranes and preterm labor after premature rupture of membranes.

**Common pathway model.** Parturition requires anatomic, biochemical, immunologic, and endocrine changes in myometrium, cervix, and membranes. Each mechanism converges on prostaglandin synthesis, matrix metalloproteinase activation, and gap junction formation.

**Mass-restricted (MR) score.** Amniotic fluid is scored 0 to 4 by presence of neutrophil defensin-1, neutrophil defensin-2, calgranulin-C, and calgranulin-A. MR 3 to 4 indicates severe inflammation and predicts a shorter amniocentesis-to-delivery interval.

## Key Concepts
- PTB is birth from viability to 37 completed weeks. Viability is usually 20 to 22 weeks but varies; a birth weight of 500 g is used where dating is unreliable.
- US PTB rate was about 12.0% in 2010; births before 34 weeks were 3.50%, low birth weight 8.15%, very low birth weight 1.45%.
- About 75% of PTBs are spontaneous: 45% (range 23.2% to 64.1%) from preterm labor with intact membranes and 30% (range 7.1% to 51.2%) after PROM.
- Intrauterine infection may contribute to about 25% of PTB. Organisms most often cultured are Ureaplasma urealyticum, Mycoplasma hominis, Gardnerella vaginalis, group B Streptococcus, Bacteroides species, and Escherichia coli; infection is often polymicrobial.
- Subclinical intra-amniotic infection and mid-trimester intra-amniotic inflammation both raise the risk of later spontaneous PTB.
- A fetal plasma IL-6 above 11 pg/mL marks the fetal inflammatory response syndrome, with shorter cordocentesis-to-delivery intervals and greater neonatal morbidity.
- The cervix is almost 90% collagen. Preterm cervical ripening differs histologically from term ripening; PTB is not accelerated term cervical change.
- Cervical insufficiency is not explained by low collagen content or a "muscular cervix"; that hypothesis is unsupported.
- Fetal fibronectin in cervicovaginal secretions reflects elastase-mediated degradation of cellular fibronectin and membrane-decidual separation, and is one of the most clinically useful biomarkers.
- Placental abruption is a binary event driven by inflammation or aberrant coagulation; decidual hemorrhage is itself a risk factor for preterm contractions and pPROM.
- Placenta previa complicates 0.3% to 0.8% of pregnancies; over 50% of affected women deliver preterm, the first bleed occurs at a median of about 30 weeks, and the median interval from first bleed to delivery is 20 days.
- Polyhydramnios causes PTB in 18.5% of mild cases (amniotic fluid index 25 to 30 cm) and more often when severe; 50% to 60% of polyhydramnios is idiopathic.
- Maternal stress operates through the placental corticotropin-releasing hormone system, which rises exponentially in the second half of pregnancy; an earlier rise is associated with preterm delivery.

## Anti-patterns
- Treating a short cervix as proof of cervical insufficiency; shortening is often the first step of preterm parturition rather than a structural defect.
- Assuming all preterm births are either "spontaneous" or "indicated" without recording the phenotype; this obscures mechanism.
- Relying on placental histology for antenatal management; it is available only after birth and serves counseling and research.
- Interpreting absent clinical infection as absent intra-amniotic inflammation; subclinical inflammation is common.
- Attributing PTB in a multiple gestation solely to overdistension while ignoring infection, cervical change, and placental pathology.

## Key Takeaways
1. Classify every preterm birth by phenotype: maternal, placental, or fetal condition, presence of parturition signs, and spontaneous versus caregiver-initiated pathway.
2. Treat intra-amniotic infection and inflammation as a leading, often subclinical, driver of early PTB.
3. Use cervicovaginal fetal fibronectin and cervical length as the best antenatal markers of premature cervical change.
4. Recognize decidual bleeding and abruption as inflammatory triggers of preterm labor and membrane rupture.
5. Count placenta previa, polyhydramnios, multiple gestation, and fetal anomaly as direct mechanical or placental causes.
6. Remember that roughly three quarters of preterm births begin spontaneously; prevention must target the mechanism.
