# Chapter 14: Placental Respiratory Gas Exchange and Fetal Oxygenation

## Core Idea
Oxygen moves from atmosphere to fetal tissue through six alternating steps of bulk transport and diffusion, with the placenta as the rate-limiting step. Because the placenta consumes oxygen, maternal and fetal blood do not equilibrate at the venous end; umbilical venous PO2 tracks uterine venous PO2, not maternal arterial PO2. The fetus tolerates a PO2 that would define severe hypoxia postnatally because cardiac output is about 460 mL/min/kg, giving a flow-to-oxygen-consumption ratio roughly 2.5 times the adult value. Carbon dioxide is far more diffusible, so maternal respiratory acid-base changes reach the fetus within minutes.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Six-step oxygen transport chain.** Ventilation maintaining alveolar PO2; diffusion into maternal blood; maternal circulation to the uterus; diffusion across the placental membrane; umbilical circulation to the fetus; diffusion into fetal tissue. Each step has a measurable PO2 drop that localizes where fetal oxygenation is lost.

**Fick principle applied to the gravid uterus.** Uterine oxygen uptake = uterine flow × (maternal arterial − uterine venous oxygen content). Umbilical uptake uses the same calculation with umbilical venous minus arterial content. The difference between the two is uteroplacental oxygen consumption.

**Venous equilibration model.** Maternal and fetal streams flow concurrently, so the transmembrane PO2 difference tends toward zero at the venous end. Umbilical venous PO2 therefore depends on uterine venous PO2 and not on maternal arterial PO2.

**Two-pathway third-trimester placenta.** Uptake occurs through a high-resistance pathway (stem and intermediate villi) and a low-resistance pathway (terminal villi, locally flow-limited). Capillary loops in terminal villi create a countercurrent arrangement that lowers the PO2 of blood leaving the villus.

## Key Concepts
- Reference near-term sheep: uterine flow 1263 mL/min, maternal oxygen capacity 7.0 mM, arterial PO2 72 mmHg, uterine venous PO2 40 mmHg, uterine uptake **1644 µmol/min**; umbilical flow 600 mL/min, fetal capacity 6.8 mM, umbilical venous saturation 82% (PO2 28 mmHg), arterial saturation 57% (PO2 19 mmHg), umbilical uptake **1027 µmol/min**; uteroplacental consumption **617 µmol/min**.
- Maternal arterial blood at sea level is 96% saturated at PO2 ~100 mmHg; normal human umbilical venous blood is about 81% saturated at PO2 ~35 mmHg.
- Near-term ovine umbilical arterial values are about 55% saturation and 19 mmHg. With human fetal oxygen consumption of 300 µmol/min/kg and umbilical flow of 110 mL/min/kg, calculated human umbilical arterial saturation is 47% and PO2 about 20 mmHg.
- Streaming of umbilical venous blood into the left ventricle raises ascending aortic saturation to about 63% (PO2 ~22 mmHg in sheep); the aortic isthmus arteriovenous content difference is 0.45 ± 0.02 mM. Only the left hepatic lobe receives nearly pure umbilical venous blood.
- At mid-gestation in sheep (74 days), uteroplacental tissues consume about **4.5 times** more oxygen than the fetus, 70–80% of it by the villous tree. Uterine flow is nearly five times umbilical flow, with a flow-to-uptake ratio of about 0.9 mL/µmol.
- Umbilical venous saturation falls from about 90% (SD 3.5%) at mid-gestation to **81% ± 7%** near term; the lower normal limit is about **67%** (PO2 ~27 mmHg at pH 7.37). Reported human limits of 20 mmHg correspond to only ~47% saturation, which is below normal.
- Fetal oxygen consumption is about 300 µmol/min/kg, roughly twice the adult basal value (~150 µmol/min/kg). Protein turnover and accretion account for about **18%** of ovine fetal oxygen consumption; umbilical uptake per kilogram falls only 24% in severely growth-restricted lambs whose growth is near zero.
- Fetal hemoglobin has higher oxygen affinity than adult hemoglobin, shifting the curve left; in sheep, maternal hemoglobin type alters the maternal curve while fetal curves overlap because fetal blood is dominated by fetal hemoglobin.
- Placental oxygen consumption maintains the uterine-to-umbilical venous PO2 difference. The human placenta does not equilibrate because of the thick epithelial membrane and because membrane and stromal oxygen consumption approximate capillary uptake.
- Hyperoxia: raising maternal arterial PO2 to 500 mmHg raises oxygen content ~1 mM, uterine venous PO2 by **11.5 mmHg**, and umbilical venous PO2 by nearly the same amount, but umbilical arterial PO2 by only **~4 mmHg**, because the fetal arterial point lies on the steep part of the curve.
- In practice, 55% oxygen for several days in growth-restricted pregnancies increased umbilical venous PO2 and saturation and improved Doppler indices.
- Oxygen limits: 100% at atmospheric pressure for a few hours only; 50% is considered safe. Maternal hyperoxia at atmospheric pressure is not expected to raise fetal PO2 to toxic levels.
- Carbon dioxide: ovine fetal respiratory quotient is about **0.94**; umbilical venous PCO2 is about **3 mmHg** above uterine venous PCO2. Maternal respiratory disturbances are mirrored in the fetus within minutes.
- Placental bicarbonate permeability differs by type: the epitheliochorial sheep placenta is nearly impermeable, so fetal bicarbonate remains normal for days during maternal metabolic acidosis; hemochorial placentas are more permeable to chloride and probably bicarbonate.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using maternal arterial PO2 to predict fetal oxygenation.** Fetal PO2 follows uterine venous PO2.
- **Assuming maternal hyperoxia cannot help chronic hypoxia.** Oxygen content, not pressure, is the currency; 55% oxygen improved fetal content and Doppler indices.
- **Assuming reduced umbilical flow must lower venous PO2.** Slower flow prolongs transit and tends to raise it; the low PO2 of growth restriction reflects placental structural change.
- **Treating the placenta as an inert membrane.** Placental oxygen consumption and villous architecture prevent equilibration.
- **Judging fetal PO2 by adult standards.** High fetal cardiac output relative to consumption makes a low PO2 adequate.

## Key Takeaways
- Compute fetal oxygen delivery from content and flow, not PO2 alone; the ovine reference set is uterine 1644, umbilical 1027, uteroplacental 617 µmol/min.
- Expect near-term umbilical venous saturation of 81% ± 7% with a lower limit of about 67%.
- Expect umbilical arterial saturation near 55% in sheep and about 47% in the human fetus, with ascending aortic saturation about 63%.
- Use 50% inspired oxygen for chronic fetal hypoxia and reserve 100% for a few hours.
- Read fetal PCO2 against maternal PCO2; a raised value indicates maternal hypercapnia, placental failure, or both.
- Growth restriction lowers umbilical venous PO2 through placental structural change despite slower umbilical flow.
