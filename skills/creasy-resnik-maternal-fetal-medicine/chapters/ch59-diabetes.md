# Chapter 59: Diabetes in Pregnancy

## Core Idea

Diabetes in pregnancy is a spectrum from pregestational type 1 and type 2 disease to gestational diabetes, and the distinction matters because pregestational disease carries the risk of congenital malformation determined by periconceptional glycemia. Maternal hyperglycemia drives fetal hyperinsulinemia, which produces macrosomia, birth injury, neonatal hypoglycemia, and excess perinatal mortality. Risk is proportional to the degree of hyperglycemia, so meticulous control before conception and throughout gestation is the central intervention. Modern management combines medical nutrition therapy, glucose monitoring, insulin or oral agents, planned delivery, and intrapartum glycemic control.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Diabetes classification (ADA).** Type 1 diabetes from beta-cell destruction with absolute insulin deficiency; type 2 diabetes from progressive insulin secretory defect on a background of insulin resistance; other specific types (genetic beta-cell defects such as glucokinase MODY, pancreatic disease such as cystic fibrosis, drug-induced); and gestational diabetes.
- **IADPSG one-step criteria (75-g, 2-hour OGTT at 24 to 28 weeks).** Gestational diabetes if one or more values equals or exceeds: fasting 92 mg/dL (5.1 mmol/L), 1 hour 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L), 2 hours 153 mg/dL (8.5 mmol/L). Overt diabetes if fasting 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or higher, HbA1c 6.5% or higher, or random glucose 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or higher. ACOG has not endorsed this approach.
- **Two-step Carpenter-Coustan protocol (ACOG preferred).** 50-g, 1-hour glucose challenge with a threshold of 135 or 140 mg/dL, followed by a 100-g, 3-hour OGTT. Diagnostic values: fasting 95 mg/dL (5.3 mmol/L), 1 hour 180 mg/dL (10.0), 2 hours 155 mg/dL (8.6), 3 hours 140 mg/dL (7.8). Two or more values must be met or exceeded. Prerequisites: overnight fast of 8 to 14 hours, 3 days of carbohydrate loading with at least 150 g daily, seated and not smoking during the test.
- **Glycemic targets.** Preprandial 65 to 95 mg/dL; postprandial no higher than 130 to 140 mg/dL at 1 hour. Normal pregnant women maintain postprandial glucose at 70 to 120 mg/dL. During labor, target 80 to 110 mg/dL (some protocols 90 to 110).
- **Insulin regimen framework.** Basal-bolus with rapid-acting analog (lispro or aspart, peak 1 hour, duration 2 hours), intermediate NPH (peak 4 to 8 hours), or long-acting detemir (about 20 hours) or glargine (under 24 hours). Total daily insulin requirement rises about 280% from preconception (33.3 ± 7.8 units/day) to term.
- **Oral agent framework.** Glyburide titrated before meals so that the peak coincides with postprandial glucose, taken 30 to 60 minutes before eating; metformin as an alternative. Neither is clearly associated with increased maternal or neonatal risk compared with insulin, but long-term outcome data are limited.
- **Delivery framework.** Deliver at 39 weeks or later unless maternal or fetal indications require earlier delivery; verify fetal lung maturity with more than 3% phosphatidylglycerol before 38 weeks; induce before 42 weeks. Discuss primary cesarean delivery when estimated fetal weight exceeds 4500 g.

## Key Concepts

- In 2011, 366 million people worldwide had diabetes, projected to reach 552 million by 2030; diabetes caused 4.6 million deaths and $465 billion in health care costs, 11% of adult health spending.
- In the United States, about 20 million people have diagnosed diabetes and an estimated 79 million adults have prediabetes; 35% of adults aged 20 or older and 50% aged 65 or older have prediabetes.
- Compared with offspring of nondiabetic mothers, offspring of diabetic mothers have doubled birth injury, increased cesarean delivery, and quadrupled neonatal intensive care admission.
- Fetal and neonatal mortality remain about threefold higher with type 1 or type 2 diabetes, and major congenital anomalies are about threefold more common (71.6 per 1000; relative risk 3.8).
- First-trimester HbA1c below 8.5% is associated with 3.4% congenital anomalies, versus 22.4% when HbA1c exceeds 8.5%. Each 1% rise in HbA1c above 6.3% raises anomaly risk (adjusted odds ratio 1.3), and pre-existing nephropathy raises it further (adjusted odds ratio 2.5).
- Insulin sensitivity falls 50% to 60% in late gestation.
- Gestational diabetes: 5% of cases carry a glucokinase mutation (MODY), which can produce a fetal phenotype ranging from growth restriction to macrosomia depending on whether mother, fetus, or both carry the defect.
- ACOG two-step screening is performed at 24 to 28 weeks; early screening is offered to high-risk women.
- In the ACHOIS trial, insulin therapy targeting premeal glucose below 99 mg/dL and 2-hour postprandial below 126 mg/dL reduced serious perinatal complications from 4% to 1%.
- Glyburide: across eight randomized trials, glycemic control and cesarean rates were similar to insulin; 4% to 18% required conversion to insulin; one cohort reported preeclampsia 12% versus 6% with insulin.
- Metformin: across two randomized trials, glycemic control was similar to insulin, neonatal hypoglycemia was less frequent, and anomaly rates did not differ.
- Macrosomia above 4000 g accounts for 42% to 74% of shoulder dystocia and 56% to 76% of brachial plexus injuries. Shoulder dystocia rises tenfold, to 5% to 7%, at birth weights of 4000 g or more.
- Ultrasound prediction of macrosomia is poor: estimated fetal weight correctly predicted macrosomia in only 18% of cases in one series, and a policy of preemptive cesarean for estimated weight above 4500 g prevented at most one case of brachial palsy.
- Postpartum insulin requirements fall to 30% to 50% of the late-pregnancy preprandial dose. Type 1 patients often enter a remission phase with markedly reduced requirements.
- Exclusive breastfeeding is associated with lower childhood diabetes (odds ratio 0.41) and lower postpartum fasting glucose.

## Anti-patterns

- Deferring preconception counseling and glycemic optimization in type 1 or type 2 diabetes.
- Diagnosing gestational diabetes with a 50-g challenge alone instead of completing the diagnostic OGTT.
- Using a single abnormal value on the 100-g OGTT to diagnose gestational diabetes; two values are required.
- Targeting HbA1c below normal during pregnancy without regard to hypoglycemia risk.
- Assuming ultrasound estimated fetal weight reliably identifies macrosomia.
- Inducing labor before 39 weeks for "impending macrosomia" without lung maturity assessment or a compelling indication.
- Continuing pre-pregnancy insulin doses unchanged after delivery.
- Missing neonatal hypoglycemia in an infant of a diabetic mother.

## Key Takeaways

1. Optimize HbA1c before conception, aiming well below 8.5% and ideally near 6.3%, and prescribe folate.
2. Screen for gestational diabetes at 24 to 28 weeks with the two-step 50-g challenge and 100-g OGTT, or with the one-step 75-g OGTT if the practice adopts IADPSG criteria.
3. Target preprandial glucose 65 to 95 mg/dL and 1-hour postprandial 130 to 140 mg/dL or less.
4. Start medical nutrition therapy and glucose monitoring first; add insulin or an oral agent when targets are not met.
5. Plan delivery at 39 weeks or later unless indicated earlier, verify lung maturity before 38 weeks, and induce before 42 weeks.
6. Manage labor with combined insulin and glucose infusion targeting 80 to 110 mg/dL, and monitor capillary glucose hourly.
7. Reduce insulin to 30% to 50% of the late-pregnancy dose postpartum, monitor the neonate for hypoglycemia for at least 24 hours, and encourage breastfeeding.
