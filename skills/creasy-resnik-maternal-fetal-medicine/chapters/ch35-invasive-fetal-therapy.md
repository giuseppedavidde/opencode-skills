# Chapter 35: Invasive Fetal Therapy

## Core Idea
Fetal therapy is justified only when the natural history is known, postnatal treatment is inadequate, and the intervention has been shown to reverse the pathophysiology in animal models. Each procedure trades a fetal benefit against maternal risk, preterm labor, and membrane complications, so selection and center experience dominate outcomes. The chapter divides therapy into surgery on the fetus (open repair, shunting, valvuloplasty, cord occlusion), surgery on the placenta and membranes (laser for twin-twin transfusion syndrome, amniotic band release), and transplacental pharmacotherapy.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Criteria for fetal surgery (IFMSS consensus).** (1) Accurate diagnosis and staging with exclusion of associated anomalies. (2) Documented natural history and established prognosis. (3) No effective postnatal therapy. (4) Proven feasibility in animal models, with reversal of deleterious effects. (5) Performance in a specialized multidisciplinary fetal treatment center under strict protocols, ethics committee approval, and informed consent.

**Quintero staging of twin-twin transfusion syndrome (TTTS).** Stage I: donor bladder visible, normal Doppler. Stage II: donor bladder not visible, normal Doppler in both twins. Stage III: abnormal Doppler in either twin — absent or reversed end-diastolic flow in the umbilical artery, reversed a-wave in the ductus venosus, or pulsatile umbilical venous flow. Stage IV: hydrops, usually in the recipient. Stage V: intrauterine fetal death. Polyhydramnios is a deepest vertical pocket >8 cm (some European centers use >10 cm after 20 weeks); oligohydramnios is <2 cm.

**Sacrococcygeal teratoma (Altman) types.** Type I external only; type II external with intrapelvic extension; type III external with abdominal extension; type IV presacral only.

**Amniotic band classification.** Bands without constriction; constriction without vascular compromise; severe constriction with progressive arterial compromise; bowing or fracture; intrauterine amputation.

## Key Concepts
- Open fetal surgery requires general anesthesia, tocolysis, a stapled hysterotomy, partial fetal exteriorization, fetal analgesia and paralysis, and layered uterine closure. Complications include preterm contractions, maternal morbidity from tocolysis, membrane rupture, and uterine dehiscence.
- TTTS: untreated mid-trimester mortality exceeds 80%. Fetoscopic laser coagulation is first-line for stage II–IV disease between 15 and 26 weeks. Compared with amnioreduction, laser achieves survival of at least one twin to 28 days in 76% versus 56%, a higher median delivery age (33.3 versus 29.0 weeks), less delivery before 32 weeks (42% versus 69%), and less cystic periventricular leukomalacia (6% versus 14%).
- After laser: single intrauterine fetal death about 33%, double death about 4%; recurrent TTTS about 14%; twin anemia-polycythemia sequence (TAPS) up to 13% of double survivors, versus under 1% spontaneously. About 60% of fetal deaths occur within 24 hours and 75% within 1 week; missed anastomoses are found in 33% of examined placentas.
- TAPS is defined by middle cerebral artery peak systolic velocity >1.5 MoM in one twin and <0.8 MoM in the other; the recipient usually becomes anemic. Measure MCA-PSV weekly for the first month after laser, then every 2 weeks.
- Selective feticide by cord occlusion in monochorionic twins has about 79% co-twin survival; complications include membrane rupture, incomplete coagulation, and late fetal death up to 10 weeks later.
- TRAP sequence: prophylactic intervention at 16–18 weeks produced 90% survival and 90% delivery after 32 weeks in one series; spontaneous cessation of acardiac flow occurs. Fetoscopic laser has an 80% survival rate in the largest series.
- Sacrococcygeal teratoma: hydrops, placentomegaly, and growth >150 cm³/week predict mortality; tumor-to-fetal weight ratio >0.12 before 24 weeks predicted hydrops in one study. Cesarean delivery is reserved for large tumors or dystocia risk.
- Congenital diaphragmatic hernia (CDH): prevalence 1–4/10,000; 13% right-sided; associated anomalies raise mortality above 85%; isolated CDH mortality remains about 30%. Prognosis is estimated by observed-to-expected lung-to-head ratio (O/E LHR) and liver herniation. With LHR <1.0, survival was 11.1% with expectant management versus 57.1% after fetoscopic endoluminal tracheal occlusion (FETO). FETO is performed at 26–28 weeks and reversed at about 34 weeks.
- Lower urinary tract obstruction: vesicoamniotic shunting is offered with oligohydramnios and favorable urine — sodium <100 mg/dL, chloride <90 mg/dL, osmolarity <200 mOsm/L, calcium <8 mg/dL, total protein <20 mg/dL, and β2-microglobulin <6 mg/L. Even with favorable profiles, up to half of survivors have renal impairment.
- Fetal cardiac intervention: aortic valvuloplasty has technical success in about 70%; complications include bradycardia requiring resuscitation (17–38%), hemopericardium (13%), ventricular thrombosis (15–20%), and intrauterine fetal death (8–13%). Only 33–67% of successful cases achieve significant left ventricular growth.
- Myelomeningocele: the MOMS trial enrolled fetuses at 20 weeks 0 days to 25 weeks 6 days. Prenatal repair reduced shunt placement (40% versus 82%) and improved independent walking at 30 months (42% versus 21%). Mean delivery was 34.1 versus 37.3 weeks. Maternal risks included membrane separation (26% versus 0%), oligohydramnios (21% versus 4%), spontaneous membrane rupture (46% versus 8%), and hysterotomy dehiscence (10%).
- Amniotic band syndrome occurs in about 1/1200 to 1/15,000 live births. Follow weekly while Doppler studies are normal; offer fetoscopic release for progressive vascular compromise. In 10 fetoscopic releases, membrane rupture before 34 weeks occurred in 78% and preterm birth in 67%; complete release was possible in one third.

## Anti-patterns
- Offering fetal surgery without excluding associated anomalies, aneuploidy, or a lethal prognosis.
- Using Quintero stage alone to decide treatment; stage I disease may be managed expectantly or with amnioreduction, and stage II–IV disease is the usual laser indication.
- Performing laser without planning for TAPS, recurrent TTTS, and single fetal death surveillance.
- Assuming a favorable fetal urine profile guarantees normal renal function; up to half of survivors have renal impairment.
- Attempting open repair of myelomeningocele after 26 weeks; the MOMS window is 20–25+6 weeks.
- Diagnosing amniotic bands and operating on them without progressive constriction or vascular compromise.
- Underestimating maternal risk: membrane separation, preterm rupture, pulmonary edema, and hysterotomy dehiscence are procedure-related, not rare.

## Key Takeaways
1. Apply the five fetal surgery criteria before any intervention, and perform procedures only in experienced multidisciplinary centers.
2. For TTTS, use Quintero staging and offer fetoscopic laser for stage II–IV disease at 15–26 weeks; counsel about 76% survival of at least one twin.
3. For CDH, use O/E LHR and liver position; FETO improves survival in severe cases but requires balloon removal at about 34 weeks.
4. For LUTO, select by serial vesicocenteses and urine electrolytes; counsel that shunt improves perinatal survival but not necessarily long-term renal function.
5. For myelomeningocele, prenatal repair before 26 weeks reduces shunting and improves walking but increases preterm delivery and membrane complications.
