# Chapter 7: Maternal Cardiovascular, Respiratory, and Renal Adaptation to Pregnancy

## Core Idea

Pregnancy is a high-volume, low-resistance state. Plasma volume expands more than red cell mass, cardiac output rises while systemic vascular resistance falls, and blood pressure falls in mid-pregnancy. The respiratory system hyperventilates to a compensated respiratory alkalosis. The kidney dilates and filters 50% more, which lowers normal creatinine and urea values. The chapter's argument is that these changes are predictable and reversible, and that their main clinical importance is to prevent misreading normal pregnancy as disease.

## Frameworks Introduced

**Hypervolemia–vasodilation sequence.** Plasma volume expansion begins at 6 to 8 weeks, possibly driven by nitric oxide–mediated vasodilation, which activates the renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system and retains sodium and water.

**Hemodynamic profile framework.** Cardiac output equals heart rate times stroke volume. Stroke volume dominates the early rise; heart rate maintains output late. Systemic vascular resistance is calculated from mean arterial pressure, central venous pressure, and cardiac output.

**Colloid osmotic pressure–wedge gradient.** Pulmonary edema risk is assessed by the difference between colloid osmotic pressure and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure, not by wedge pressure alone.

**Renal adaptation framework.** Renal plasma flow rises more than glomerular filtration rate early, so filtration fraction falls until the third trimester. Sodium retention is net positive despite forces that promote excretion.

## Key Concepts

- Plasma volume rises 45% (1200 to 1600 mL) and peaks at 4700 to 5200 mL at 32 weeks.
- Red cell mass rises 20% to 30% (250 to 450 mL) by term.
- Total iron requirement is about 1000 mg: 500 mg for increased erythropoiesis, 300 mg transferred to the fetus, and 200 mg for daily losses.
- Cardiac output rises 30% to 50%; half of the rise occurs by 8 weeks.
- Heart rate rises from 5 weeks and peaks 15 to 20 beats/min above baseline by 32 weeks.
- Stroke volume peaks in the first half of pregnancy and declines toward term.
- Systemic vascular resistance falls about 21% (1530 to 1210 dyne-sec-cm⁻⁵). Pulmonary vascular resistance falls about 34%.
- Colloid osmotic pressure falls about 14%, from 20.8 to 18.0 mm Hg. The colloid osmotic pressure to wedge pressure gradient falls from 14.5 to 10.5 mm Hg.
- Uterine blood flow rises to 500 to 800 mL/min, from 2% to 17% of cardiac output. Renal blood flow rises about 50%.
- Systolic pressure is relatively stable. Diastolic pressure falls by a maximum of 10 mm Hg at 28 weeks.
- Korotkoff phase IV readings average 13 mm Hg higher than phase V readings.
- A systolic murmur develops in more than 95% of pregnant women. A grade greater than 2/4 or any diastolic murmur is abnormal. About 14% have a continuous mammary vessel murmur.
- A loud third heart sound is heard in up to 90%; a fourth heart sound in less than 5%.
- Vaginal delivery loses about 500 mL of blood; cesarean delivery about 1000 mL.
- Cardiac output returns to prelabor values about 1 hour after delivery. Left atrial dimensions normalize within 10 days; left ventricular dimensions take 4 to 6 months.
- Respiratory volumes: tidal volume 450 to 600 mL (+33%); expiratory reserve volume 700 to 550 mL (−20%); residual volume 1000 to 800 mL (−20%); functional residual capacity 1700 to 1350 mL (−20%); total lung capacity 4200 to 4000 mL; vital capacity unchanged at 3200 mL.
- Arterial PCO₂ falls from 39 mm Hg to 28 to 31 mm Hg. Serum bicarbonate falls to 18 to 22 mEq/L. Arterial pH is 7.44.
- Oxygen consumption rises 20% to 40%.
- Renal plasma flow rises 60% to 80% by mid-second trimester, then settles at 50% above baseline in the third.
- Glomerular filtration rate rises 50% by the end of the first trimester. Creatinine clearance is 110 to 150 mL/min.
- Serum creatinine falls from 0.83 mg/dL to 0.7, 0.6, and 0.5 mg/dL across the three trimesters. Blood urea nitrogen falls from 12 mg/dL to 11, 9, and 10 mg/dL.
- Net sodium retention is about 950 mg. Net potassium retention is 300 to 350 mEq.
- Urinary protein excretion normally stays below 300 mg per 24 hours.
- Kidney length increases about 1 cm. Hydronephrosis and hydroureter may persist 3 to 4 months postpartum.
- Stress incontinence occurs in 29% to 41% of women at term.

## Anti-patterns

- Diagnosing anemia using non-pregnant haemoglobin thresholds. Hemodilution is physiological.
- Accepting a serum creatinine of 0.9 mg/dL as normal in pregnancy. It suggests renal impairment.
- Treating a systolic murmur as pathological. Grade 2/4 or softer systolic murmurs are expected.
- Attributing dyspnea to disease without arterial blood gas measurement. A compensated respiratory alkalosis is normal.
- Measuring blood pressure in different positions across visits. Position changes readings by 10 mm Hg.
- Positioning a term patient supine for assessment. Supine hypotension reduces cardiac output.
- Interpreting postpartum hydronephrosis as obstruction. It can persist for months.

## Key Takeaways

1. Interpret creatinine and urea against pregnancy-specific ranges.
2. Use the colloid osmotic pressure to wedge gradient when assessing pulmonary edema risk.
3. Accept a PCO₂ of 28 to 31 mm Hg and bicarbonate of 18 to 22 mEq/L as normal.
4. Investigate any diastolic murmur or systolic murmur louder than grade 2/4.
5. Keep the patient in the left lateral position for hemodynamic measurement.
6. Expect blood pressure to nadir at about 28 weeks and to return toward baseline by term.
