# Chapter 61: Other Endocrine Disorders of Pregnancy

## Core Idea
Pregnancy remodels every endocrine axis: the pituitary doubles or triples in size, prolactin rises roughly tenfold, cortisol-binding globulin and free cortisol rise, and vasopressinase accelerates vasopressin clearance. These physiologic changes mask or mimic disease, so diagnosis requires trimester-specific interpretation rather than nonpregnant thresholds. The central argument is that most nonthyroid endocrine disease can be managed through pregnancy if the clinician anticipates decompensation, uses the correct confirmatory test, and times intervention (surgery, delivery, or drugs) to fetal and maternal risk. Untreated disease — Cushing syndrome, pheochromocytoma, hypercalcemia, adrenal crisis — carries substantial fetal and maternal mortality; treated disease approaches normal outcomes.

## Frameworks Introduced
**Prolactinoma size-based risk stratification.** Microadenoma (<10 mm) versus macroadenoma (≥10 mm); prior surgery or radiation versus untreated. This determines counseling on tumor expansion and the need for serial visual field testing and MRI.

**Cushing syndrome diagnostic sequence in pregnancy.** Overnight/low-dose dexamethasone suppression is unreliable in pregnancy. Sequence: 24-hour urinary free cortisol (UFC) → high-dose dexamethasone suppression (8 mg/day for ≥2 days, ≥80% suppression = pituitary-dependent) → plasma ACTH (undetectable <10 pg/mL suggests adrenal source) → MRI pituitary/adrenals.

**Diabetes insipidus: five pregnancy-specific forms.** (1) pre-existing DI; (2) transient AVP-resistant, DDAVP-responsive DI from excess vasopressinase (often with acute fatty liver or HELLP); (3) transient DI in latent central or nephrogenic DI; (4) post-delivery DI with pituitary apoplexy/Sheehan; (5) de novo nephrogenic DI resistant to AVP and DDAVP.

**Adrenal crisis protocol.** Draw electrolytes, cortisol, ACTH, then give 100 mg hydrocortisone sodium succinate IV with normal saline plus 5% glucose; 300–400 mg hydrocortisone/24 h continuous infusion; taper to oral by 24 h if recovering.

**Hypercalcemia treatment ladder.** Hydration; stop offending drugs; restrict calcium; saline 200–500 mL/h with furosemide 20–60 mg IV every 2–4 h; oral phosphate (Neutra-Phos 500–750 mg every 6–8 h); dialysis; calcitonin (short-acting, does not cross placenta); mithramycin (fetotoxic) only in life-threatening crisis.

## Key Concepts
- Pituitary volume rises 136% by end of pregnancy; 40%, 75%, and up to 120% increases in second trimester, third trimester, and immediate postpartum.
- Prolactin increases approximately 10-fold; lactotroph responsiveness to TRH is preserved in normal pregnancy but blunted/absent in prolactinoma.
- Untreated microadenomas (n=352): visual disturbance 2.3%, headache 4.8%, diabetes insipidus 0.6%. Untreated macroadenomas (n=144): visual disturbance 15.3%, headache 15.3%, DI 1.4%.
- Symptomatic enlargement: 2.6% of microadenomas; 5% of treated macroadenomas; 31% of untreated macroadenomas (8.5% required surgery). Visual symptoms begin at mean 14 weeks.
- Acromegaly: failure of GH suppression below 2 ng/mL on glucose tolerance test is diagnostic; IGF-1 is unreliable in pregnancy because placental GH raises it. Carbohydrate intolerance up to 50%, overt diabetes up to 20%, hypertension 25–35%; tumor expansion ~10%.
- Acromegaly in 59 pregnancies: gestational diabetes 6.8%, hypertensive disorders 13.8%; somatostatin analogues (octreotide, lanreotide) stopped before conception but usable for expanding tumor.
- Cushing syndrome: mean gestational age at diagnosis 18 weeks; adrenal adenoma accounts for about half of cases (versus 15% nonpregnant); adrenal carcinoma 10%; pituitary-dependent disease one third (versus 70%); ectopic ACTH rare. UFC >3× upper limit of normal in second/third trimester suggests Cushing syndrome.
- Untreated Cushing: 76% live births; treated (mean 20 weeks): 89%. Metyrapone is the drug of choice; ketoconazole and aminoglutethimide have teratogenic/virilizing risk; mitotane is teratogenic and contraindicated.
- Sheehan syndrome with hormone replacement: 87% live births, 13% abortion, no stillbirth or maternal death (15 pregnancies). Without replacement: 58% live births, 42% abortion, one stillbirth, three maternal deaths.
- Hypopituitarism replacement: levothyroxine 0.1–0.2 mg/day, keep free T4 mid-normal (TSH is not useful); cortisol 20 mg morning plus 10 mg evening, or prednisone 5 mg plus 2.5 mg; no mineralocorticoid needed.
- Diabetes insipidus: pre-existing DI deteriorates in 58%, improves in 20%, unchanged in 15%. DDAVP 10–20 µg intranasally once or twice daily, or 0.05–0.1 mg orally twice daily; minimal transfer into breast milk; breastfeeding permitted.
- Serum sodium of 140 mEq/L should be considered hypernatremia in pregnancy.
- Adrenal insufficiency: normal cortisol response is >18 µg/dL with increment >7 µg/dL, but mean increments are 18, 23, and 26 µg/dL in first, second, and third trimesters. Cosyntropin 250 µg IV with samples at 0, 30, 60 minutes.
- Doubling or tripling of cortisol dose during stress; labor stress dose 300 mg hydrocortisone over 24 hours with taper.
- Addison disease (1,188 deliveries): preterm birth OR 2.40, low birth weight OR 3.5, cesarean OR 1.74.
- Pheochromocytoma: 90% adrenal medulla, 12% malignant, prevalence 1 in 54,000 at term; urinary fractionated metanephrines are the most sensitive test. Phentolamine 1–5 mg for hypertensive crisis. Never give β-blockade before α-blockade. Tumors found before 24 weeks: resection (45% of antenatal surgeries laparoscopic, zero maternal mortality); later: cesarean with simultaneous or subsequent excision under blockade. Maternal mortality diagnosed in pregnancy fell from 18% (pre-1969) to 5% (1998–2008); fetal mortality from 50% to 12%.
- Congenital adrenal hyperplasia: 21-hydroxylase deficiency is 90–95% of cases, incidence about 1 in 14,000; autosomal recessive, 25% recurrence risk.
- Primary hyperparathyroidism: 20% asymptomatic; renal calculi 36%, pancreatitis 13%, hypertension 10%, bone disease 19%, hypercalcemic crisis 8%; maternal mortality 1 of 73. Fetal/neonatal: 30% spontaneous abortion or stillbirth, 50% tetany, 25% neonatal death. Surgery is best in the second trimester; hypercalcemic crisis is serum calcium >14 mg/dL.
- Hypoparathyroidism: Chvostek and Trousseau signs, QT prolongation. Acute hypocalcemia: 10 mL of 10% calcium gluconate over 10 minutes, then 0.5–2.0 mg/kg/h elemental calcium. Maintain calcium with 1.0–1.5 g calcium salts. Maternal hypocalcemia causes fetal hyperparathyroidism with bone demineralization; of 16 reported cases, 6 died within 3 months.
- Pregnancy and lactation increase calcium demand by about 0.3 g/day; bone resorption rises by 14 weeks and formation only by 28 weeks. Lactational bone loss does not continue past 6 months and recovers with return of menses.

## Anti-patterns
- Applying nonpregnant cortisol or calcium thresholds to pregnant women; total calcium falls in pregnancy and can mask hyperparathyroidism — measure ionized calcium.
- Using IGF-1 or a low-dose dexamethasone suppression test to diagnose acromegaly or Cushing syndrome in pregnancy.
- Withdrawing dopamine agonist abruptly in a macroadenoma without a plan for visual field surveillance and MRI.
- Giving a β-blocker alone in pheochromocytoma, which causes unopposed α-vasoconstriction and a hypertensive surge.
- Treating hypercalcemia with bisphosphonates or mithramycin without weighing fetal toxicity; they are reserved for life-threatening crisis.
- Attributing prolonged nausea, weakness, and postural hypotension in a thin pregnant woman to hyperemesis alone, missing Addison disease.
- Assuming a normal luteal or menstrual pattern excludes acromegaly (only 31% were eumenorrheic).
- Reassuring on diabetes insipidus because serum sodium is "normal" at 140 mEq/L.

## Key Takeaways
1. Screen and treat subclinical and overt hypothyroidism before conception where possible; untreated maternal endocrine disease drives most of the excess fetal loss.
2. In prolactinoma, size and prior treatment dictate counseling: untreated macroadenomas have 31% symptomatic expansion versus 2.6% for microadenomas.
3. Confirm Cushing syndrome with 24-hour UFC plus high-dose dexamethasone suppression (8 mg/day, ≥80% suppression) and ACTH; metyrapone is the safest drug, surgery is preferred in the first trimester.
4. Treat suspected adrenal crisis immediately with 100 mg hydrocortisone IV and saline-dextrose before confirmatory results return.
5. Diagnose pheochromocytoma with urinary fractionated metanephrines; block with α before β; deliver by cesarean under general anesthesia if tumor is unresected.
6. Correct symptomatic hypocalcemia with intravenous calcium gluconate and maintain normocalcemia to prevent fetal hyperparathyroidism and neonatal death.
