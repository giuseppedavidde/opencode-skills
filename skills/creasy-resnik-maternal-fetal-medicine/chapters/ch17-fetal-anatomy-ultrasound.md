# Chapter 17: Performing and Documenting the Fetal Anatomy Ultrasound Examination

## Core Idea
The mid-trimester "anatomy scan" is a structured checkpoint, not a general look at the fetus: it compares biometric dating with the pregnancy, screens for structural anomalies before viability, and can change obstetric management by identifying maternal findings such as placenta previa or fibroids. Its content is defined by billing and professional standards (CPT and ISUOG), and the report must state not only what was seen but what was poorly visualized. Detection is imperfect: the Eurofetus study found that slightly more than half of postnatally identified structural defects had been seen on mid-trimester ultrasound, and a later literature review reported approximately 60% detection of major anomalies at 18–20 weeks.

## Frameworks Introduced
**CPT-based examination hierarchy.**
- **76801** — first trimester, pregnancy under 14 weeks 0 days: number of sacs and fetuses, sac or crown-rump length for dating, description of uterus and adnexa, qualitative amniotic fluid assessment.
- **76805** — routine obstetric screening at or after 14 weeks 0 days ("standard examination"): cardiac activity, fetal number and chorionicity, structural dimensions with gestational age estimates, major organ anatomy, placental appearance and location, amniotic fluid, uterine and adnexal anatomy including lower uterine segment and cervix.
- **76811** — detailed fetal and maternal evaluation, the "Level II scan": all 76805 elements plus third and fourth ventricles, cerebellar lobes with measurement, cerebellar vermis, cisterna magna with measurement, nuchal thickness (15–20 weeks), palate, thoracic masses/effusions/diaphragmatic integrity, ribs, and ascites.
- **76816** — follow-up examination after a detailed study.

**Sequenced scan protocol.** (1) Sagittal and transverse overview of uterine contents to establish fetal number, viability, lie, placental position, and amniotic fluid, which then guides transducer placement. (2) Biometry against established dating. (3) Systematic anatomy by organ system. (4) Placenta, cord, and amniotic fluid. (5) Uterus, adnexa, lower uterine segment, and cervix, with endovaginal scanning if transabdominal images are suboptimal or abnormal.

**Standardized biometric planes.** Each measurement has prescribed landmarks to make values reproducible and comparable with nomograms.
- **BPD** — axial plane at the thalami; linear midline falx interrupted by cavum septi pellucidi and thalami; insonation 90° to the falx; no cerebellum visible; measured outer table of proximal parietal bone to inner table of distal parietal bone, bisecting the thalami.
- **HC** — same plane as BPD; ellipse traced along outer cranial bone edges, or two orthogonal diameters (transverse outer-table to outer-table; occipital-frontal along the midline falx). **HC = 1.62 × (BPD + OFD)**.
- **AC** — circular cross-section cephalad to the umbilical vein entry, showing the portal sinus and stomach bubble, with kidneys and cord insertion not visible. **AC = 1.57 × (LAD + TAD)**, both diameters outer skin to outer skin.
- **FL** — femur at about 45° to the spine, insonation 90°, both ossified metaphyses visible; maximum ossified diaphyseal length, excluding the distal femoral epiphysis.

**Report template.** Patient identifiers, indication, type of examination, referring provider, gestational age by established dates, number of fetuses and chorionicity, biometry with predicted gestational age, principal findings (normal, abnormal, poorly visualized), differential diagnosis, and recommendations for management or further imaging. Missing elements must be reported together with the technical reason (gestational age, maternal habitus).

## Key Concepts
- Required anatomy (CPT 76805, ISUOG): cranial bones, falx, cavum septi pellucidi, thalami, lateral ventricles, cerebellum, cisterna magna, orbits, facial profile, upper lip; four-chamber heart, cardiac axis, left and right ventricular outflow tracts, lung parenchyma; stomach, intestines, abdominal cord insertion, kidneys, bladder and umbilical arteries; spine in longitudinal and transverse planes; all four extremities with hand and foot posture; genitalia.
- Placental and cord elements: location, appearance (cysts, lucencies, masses, accessory lobe), cord vessel number, placental cord insertion. Amniotic fluid by maximum vertical pocket, four-quadrant amniotic fluid index, or subjective assessment.
- Cervical assessment: transabdominal visualization is the screen; endovaginal scanning is required when the transabdominal view is suboptimal or abnormal.
- 76811 is justified only by a known or strongly suspected anatomic or genetic abnormality, or increased risk from family history, maternal age, or maternal condition (diabetes, teratogen exposure, abnormal aneuploidy screening). Performing a more thorough routine scan does not qualify.
- 76811 should generally be performed once per pregnancy by a single practice; after the detailed examination and risk assessment, subsequent imaging is coded as follow-up (76816).
- Listed elements are necessary but not sufficient. A family history of polydactyly obliges counting fingers and toes even though digits are not a standard required element.
- Images must be labeled and stored, usually for at least 5 years or as local statute requires.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating the anatomy scan as a single "normal/abnormal" verdict.** Detection of major anomalies is only about 60% at 18–20 weeks and slightly over half in the unselected Eurofetus population; a normal scan is a risk reduction, not an exclusion.
- **Omitting poorly visualized structures.** Failure to document what could not be seen, and why, is a reporting defect and removes the basis for follow-up imaging.
- **Upcoding to 76811 for a thorough routine scan.** The detailed code requires an indication; thoroughness alone does not justify it.
- **Repeating 76811 serially.** The detailed evaluation is intended once per pregnancy, with later studies coded as follow-up.
- **Measuring in the wrong plane.** BPD at a level showing cerebellum, AC at the level of the kidneys or cord insertion, or FL including the distal epiphysis produces values that do not belong on the nomogram.
- **Relying on transabdominal cervical imaging when it is inadequate.** A short or poorly seen cervix on transabdominal scan requires endovaginal confirmation.
- **Forgetting the mother.** Uterine contour (arcuate, bicornuate, septate uteri, myomata), adnexal masses, placenta previa, vasa praevia, and obstructing fibroids are part of the examination and can alter delivery planning.

## Key Takeaways
- Perform the scan in a fixed sequence: overview, biometry, anatomy, placenta/cord/fluid, then uterus/adnexa/cervix.
- Use the prescribed planes and formulas: BPD outer-to-inner at the thalami, HC = 1.62 × (BPD + OFD), AC = 1.57 × (LAD + TAD), FL ossified diaphysis only.
- Document every required element, and explicitly list any that were not obtained with the technical reason.
- Reserve 76811 for a defined indication, once per pregnancy; use 76816 for subsequent follow-up.
- Include the nuchal thickness measurement at 15–20 weeks when performing the detailed examination.
- Report cardiac axis and both outflow tracts, not just the four-chamber view; these are the elements most often omitted and most relevant to conotruncal disease.
- Store labeled images for at least 5 years and ensure the report is usable by clinicians who did not perform the study.
