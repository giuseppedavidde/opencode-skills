# Chapter 3: The Scientific Method and Technical Analysis

## Core Idea
TA's central problem is **erroneous knowledge** — a body of dogma and myth sustained by informal, intuitive methods of knowledge acquisition. Adopting the scientific method, with its logical and philosophical foundations (deduction, induction, falsification), is the only rational path to distinguishing genuine predictive patterns from illusory ones.

## Frameworks Introduced

### 1. The Logic Hierarchy
Aristotle's legacy provides the foundation:
- **Deductive logic**: Reasoning from general premises to specific conclusions. Valid categorical syllogisms produce conclusions that must be true if premises are true. Conditional syllogisms ("If P, then Q") have valid forms (_modus ponens_, _modus tollens_) and invalid forms (the fallacy of affirming the consequent).
- **Inductive logic**: Reasoning from specific observations to general conclusions. Always uncertain — conclusions go beyond what is logically contained in the premises.

### 2. Falsificationism (Popper)
The demarcation between science and pseudoscience: a theory is scientific only if it makes predictions that can potentially be **falsified** by observation. Confirmatory evidence, while nice, can never prove a theory true because any finite set of observations is consistent with infinitely many explanations (_underdetermination of theories_). Science progresses not by verification but by **conjecture and refutation**.

### 3. The Hypothetico-Deductive Method
The scientific workflow applied to TA:
1. **Observation**: Identify a putative pattern in market data
2. **Hypothesis formation**: Propose that a specific rule captures this pattern profitably
3. **Deduction of testable predictions**: If the rule has predictive power, then its out-of-sample/detrended performance should exceed chance
4. **Empirical testing**: Back-test with rigorous statistical procedures
5. **Falsification or corroboration**: Reject or tentatively accept the hypothesis

### 4. The Discernible-Difference Test
A proposition has legitimate cognitive content only if its truth or falsity makes a **discernible difference** in observable outcomes. Most traditional TA predictions fail this test because they are too vague to ever be proven wrong.

## Key Concepts
- **Underdetermination of theories**: Any set of observations is consistent with infinite alternative explanations — data alone cannot determine which theory is correct
- **Fallacy of affirming the consequent**: "If rule works, then profitable back-test. Profitable back-test observed. Therefore rule works." This is logically invalid.
- **Necessary vs. sufficient conditions**: Confirmatory evidence is necessary (a true theory must be consistent with observations) but never sufficient to establish truth
- **Objectification of subjective TA**: Chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, etc.) can potentially be rendered objective via computational pattern recognition (kernel regression, fuzzy logic), making them testable
- **Openness tempered with skepticism**: Scientific attitude balances receptivity to new ideas with demand for rigorous evidence

## Anti-patterns
- **Confirmation-seeking**: Testing a TA claim by looking only for instances where it worked, ignoring failures
- **Ad-hoc immunization**: Adding auxiliary hypotheses to explain away disconfirming evidence rather than abandoning the theory
- **Vague predictions**: Making forecasts so imprecise that no possible outcome could falsify them
- **Authority-based belief**: Accepting a TA method because a famous practitioner or founder endorsed it, rather than because of evidence
- **Correlation-equals-causation**: Assuming a pattern that worked historically must reflect a genuine causal mechanism

## Key Takeaways
1. Scientific knowledge is distinguished by its **falsifiability** — a theory that cannot be wrong cannot be right either.
2. The hypothetico-deductive method provides a systematic framework for testing TA claims.
3. Most traditional TA fails the discernible-difference test due to vague, untestable predictions.
4. The fallacy of affirming the consequent is the single most common logical error in TA — profitable back-tests do not prove predictive power.
5. TA's evolution from folk art to observational science is underway among quants and systematic hedge funds, but faces resistance from vested interests.
